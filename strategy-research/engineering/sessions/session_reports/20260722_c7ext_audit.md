### G6 verdict provenance — **DEFECT. Bypassable. STOP condition.**

The gate checks `_VERDICT_FIELDS = ("verdict_c7", "hypothesis_verdict", "verdict")`.
The campaign does not record verdicts in those fields. It records them in **`outcome`**.

Empirically, against the live evaluator:

```
A  {"id":"x","outcome":"kill_mechanism_falsified","evidence_runs":["run_999"]}   -> ACCEPTED
B  {"id":"y","verdict_c7":"promote","pass_rule_evaluation_ref":"does/not/exist.yaml"} -> ACCEPTED
C  {"id":"z","final_verdict":"refine","c7_verdict":"refine"}                    -> ACCEPTED
CONTROL {"id":"xs","verdict_c7":"refine"}                                       -> BLOCKED
```

- **A** is the fatal one. A hand-written KB finding recording an outright *kill*,
  with no evaluator provenance, passes. And it is not a hypothetical shape: it is
  the shape `_write_kb_findings_entry` itself produces. That function's own stub
  writer (`run_phase1_research.py:2995-3016`) emits `outcome` + `outcome_reason`
  and **never** emits `verdict_c7`/`verdict`/`hypothesis_verdict`, and never emits
  a `pass_rule_evaluation_ref`. So G6's validator is a structural no-op on every
  entry the orchestrator itself writes. Confirmed against the live files:
  `grep -c pass_rule_evaluation_ref campaign_knowledge_base.yaml config/campaign_queue.yaml`
  -> **0 and 0**. Not one entry in the campaign carries the provenance G6 requires;
  not one is rejected.
- **B** shows the ref is never resolved. Any truthy string is provenance.
- **C** shows the field list is a denylist of three names, trivially side-stepped.

**Second G6 bypass — the queue is not validated at all.** The commit says "no verdict
enters the KB/**queue** without evaluator provenance." `validate_verdict_provenance`
has exactly one call site in the entire repo (`run_phase1_research.py:3030`, inside
`_write_kb_findings_entry`). `run_campaign.py::_save_queue` — the only queue writer,
called from 9 sites — does not call it. `config/campaign_queue.yaml`'s `outcome`
field is written with no provenance check of any kind.

Third: the KB call site only fires when the orchestrator writes a finding. A hand
edit to `campaign_knowledge_base.yaml` is validated only if some *later* orchestrator
run happens to write a finding. Since a hand edit by an agent is exactly how the
XS_momentum entry got there, this is the live threat model, and it is unguarded
between runs. There is no lint/CI entry point that validates the KB standalone.

**Net:** the link G6 was written to close — "a research-path tool wrote `verdict_c7`
into the KB" — is closed for the literal field name `verdict_c7` and open for the
field the campaign actually uses.

Note `panel_backtester.py` is exonerated as a *writer*: 545 lines, no KB/queue write,
no verdict emission (`grep knowledge_base|queue|verdict|save_yaml` -> no hits). The
XS_momentum KB entry was hand-authored. That makes the hand-edit path, not the tool
path, the one that needs the gate — and that is the path G6 leaves open.

> protocol_result.yaml, pass_rule_evaluation.yaml, or verdict_interpretation.yaml exist

H-041-C-v2 was rejected by an **LLM validation stage** (`validation_decision.yaml`,

## 6. The consequential question: is a kill buried under a generic threshold?

**No. No kill in the archive exists because of the generic promotion fallback.**
Evidence, run by run:

- **The five prescreen kills (043, 044, 047, 050, 053)** never reached the promotion
  block. They terminated at `prescreen_route: kill_no_ic` before any walk-forward
  backtest, on two thresholds: `_SIG_THRESHOLD = 0.10` (`tools/prescreen_signal.py:75`)
  and `safety_factor: 2.0` (`config/cost_model.yaml:62`). Those are campaign-standing,
  documented, cross-run constants — categorically different from a silent per-run
  default, and run_057's own brief explicitly ratifies them ("Standing campaign gates
  ... apply as configured in code/config; no additional LLM-authored thresholds are
  decision-bearing"). None of the five is a plausible buried edge: measured pooled ICs
  are −0.021, +0.017, `None`, +0.018, −0.050, with p ∈ [0.29, 1.0], and edge-to-cost
  ratios of 0.24–0.74 against a required 2.0. Nothing survives on any threshold.
  (run_047 deserves a footnote: it was killed on `pooled_ic: None, n_eff: 9` — i.e.
  *unmeasurable*, which its own `disconfirming_outcome` says is "merely underpowered"
  and explicitly **not** grounds for a kill. The KB already records it as
  `invalidated_artifact`, not a kill, so the record is not wrong — but the prescreen
  route that produced it was.)

- **run_057 / P4_ts_trend, the one recorded kill.** Fallback thresholds in play:
  `max_abs_drawdown_pct_lt: 30` and `median_sharpe_gt: 0`, generic-derived (copied
  from `baseline_v2.json`), and quoted in the headline verdict string. Measured:
  ETHUSDT max DD **36.87%** (would breach the 30 bar), BTCUSDT 20.9% (would not).
  **But the kill does not depend on them.** The decision-bearing criteria are the
  brief's own: ETHUSDT median Sharpe **−0.686** against a registered bar of ≥0.5791,
  and min_trade_count **0** against a registered floor of ≥3, on both symbols.
  A negative Sharpe fails any non-negative bar; zero trades fails any positive floor.
  **The kill survives the brief's own stated intent by a wide margin.**

**The residual risk is real but it is a different defect.** run_057's brief registered
a criterion (b) — "per-episode expectancy of taken entries > 0 (sign plus expectancy
± SE)" — and the machinery recorded it **UNTESTED** ("no matching metric keyword").
The measured value was **+433.73 bps, SE 441.39, t=0.98, n=42**: a positive point
estimate, insignificant, that fails the brief's own sign±SE test by 7.7 bps
(433.73 − 441.39 = −7.66) — i.e. it would have failed criterion (b) too, but *narrowly*,
and nobody evaluated it. Combined with min_trade_count = 0 on both symbols (the ER
gate left almost nothing to measure), the honest description of run_057 is closer to
"the gated variant produced too few trades to evaluate" than to "the mechanism is
falsified" — which is what `kill_er_gate_mechanism_falsified` asserts.

So: **no buried edge attributable to a generic threshold.** One kill (run_057) rests
on a registered criterion it fails decisively, alongside a second registered criterion
that was never evaluated and whose point estimate leans positive. That is worth
re-opening on its own merits, and it is worth correcting for the same ungated-verdict
reason XS_momentum was corrected — but it is not the generic-fallback story.

## 7. Summary

**Ratification: DO NOT RATIFY.**

Two STOP conditions fired:
- G6 is bypassable (§3.6) — via the `outcome` field, via an unverified ref, via a
  renamed field, and entirely on the queue side, which has no call site at all.
- The independent verdict count is **1**, not 2 (§4) — H-041-C-v2 never executed its
  pass_rule, per the repository's own KB entry.

What the commit gets right, and should be kept: G1–G5 and G7 are real, they trip on
their intended inputs, and every one of them is backed by tests that fail when the
gate is removed (M1–M8). The five repointed tests are legitimately repointed, not
weakened. The XS_momentum record correction is correct on its own terms and the
reasoning behind it — "failing a promote bar is not a kill"; "a measurement is not a
verdict" — is sound.

Carry-forwards, in priority order:
- **D-4 (blocking):** extend G6 to `outcome`/`outcome_reason`, resolve
  `pass_rule_evaluation_ref` to an existing artifact rather than accepting any truthy
  string, and add a call site on the queue writer plus a standalone KB lint that runs
  independently of an orchestrator write.
- **D-5 (blocking):** correct the verdict count to 1 and re-record H-041-C-v2's
  outcome as a validation-stage rejection, not a gated verdict; fix
  `test_campaign_honest_verdict_count`, which currently string-matches `completed_rejected`
  and tests nothing about gatedness.
- **D-6:** run_057 / P4_ts_trend carries a recorded kill with no `pass_rule_evaluation.yaml`
  and a pass_rule the evaluator structurally cannot read (nested under
  `machine_constraints`). Same defect class as XS_momentum, left uncorrected. Fix the
  nesting lookup, and re-adjudicate the kill — criterion (b) was never evaluated and
  leans positive.
- **D-2:** G4 detects nothing; it requires prose for self-declared anomalies. Add at
  least one mechanical detector.
- **D-3:** G7 guards protocol *generation* only. The generic block is still live and
  loadable from `baseline_v1/v2.json` and five other committed protocol files, and
  `_resolve_protocol_path` still defaults to `baseline_v1.json` on two branches.
- **D-1:** no test pins a `FAIL -> kill/terminate` routing through the *public* entry
  point any more; M9 is caught by one test via the PASS path only.
- **D-7 (minor):** G1's product allowlist is exact-match on a free-text brief field;
  G2/G3 are presence-only and accept placeholder or wrong-typed values.
