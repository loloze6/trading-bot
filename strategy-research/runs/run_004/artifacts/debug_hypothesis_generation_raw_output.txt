```yaml
hypothesis_id: HG-BTCUSDT-1h-001
thesis: >
  A mean-reversion strategy that buys dips during periods of high selling volume and
  sells rips during periods of high buying volume will be profitable on BTCUSDT
  on a 1-hour timeframe.
rationale: >
  High volume spikes can indicate capitulation or exhaustion. Buying after a high
  selling volume spike (indicating potential capitulation) and selling after a high
  buying volume spike (indicating potential exhaustion) can capture short-term
  reversals. This is expected to be robust across regimes as it relies on
  fundamental market dynamics of supply and demand rather than directional trends.
signal_concept: >
  Long Entry: When the current 1-hour candle closes with a significant increase in
  volume (e.g., > 2 standard deviations above the 20-period moving average of volume)
  and the price has decreased (close < open).
  Short Entry: When the current 1-hour candle closes with a significant increase in
  volume (e.g., > 2 standard deviations above the 20-period moving average of volume)
  and the price has increased (close > open).
  Exit: A fixed take-profit of 0.5% and a fixed stop-loss of 1% for both long and
  short positions.
target_market: BTCUSDT
timeframe: 1h
assumptions:
  - Volume spikes are indicative of significant market participant activity leading to
    reversals.
  - The chosen exit parameters (0.5% TP, 1% SL) are appropriate for capturing mean-reversion
    opportunities without excessive risk.
  - Standard OHLCV data is sufficient to identify these volume-confirmed anomalies.
  - The existing backtest framework can accurately simulate this strategy.
expected_failure_modes:
  - The strategy may fail in strongly trending markets where high volume confirms the
    trend rather than signaling exhaustion.
  - Transaction costs (fees and slippage) may erode profitability, especially with frequent
    trades.
  - The definition of "significant increase in volume" may require further tuning to avoid
    false signals.
  - The fixed take-profit and stop-loss levels may not be optimal across different volatility
    regimes.
```
