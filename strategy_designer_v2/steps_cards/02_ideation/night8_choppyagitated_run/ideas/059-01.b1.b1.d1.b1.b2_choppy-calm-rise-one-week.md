Idea: In a choppy, calm market, the larger a coin's rise over the past 7 days measured in multiples of its usual 7-day swing over the past 30 days, the larger its further rise over the next 7 days.
Consequence: A trader in a quiet, trendless coin should expect a week's rise that is large against its swing to carry on over the following week, and a small rise to give back.
Why:
- F1: Step 5 found the grading the idea predicts but not the rise itself. The 21-day slope t is 2.00, but the mean excess return of its 103 cases (48 days) is -0.61 %. The idea's size is an unusual move of -0.88 % per case (t -0.59, 309 days, strength -0.051), so a larger scaled rise does better than a smaller one without the cases beating the coin's drift.
- F3: Over the next 7 days the grading is clear. There are 89 cases on 45 days, slope t 3.17, and the core six coins alone give slope t 2.66. The upper half of forecasts earns +3.5 % above drift (t 1.60) against -2.2 % for the lower half. The slope is positive in 2018, 2020 and 2021 (t 1.94, 1.92, 1.83) and negative only in 2019 (t -0.96). The mean excess is small: +0.69 % (t 0.30), and -0.09 % on the core coins.
- F5: The weakest rises are what pull the mean below the drift. The lowest forecast tercile is followed by -6.2 % at 7 days and -13.0 % at 14 days, against +6.8 %/+1.7 % (7 days) and +20.5 %/+11.5 % (14 days) for the mid and high terciles. So the slope passes while the mean of all cases fails.
- F6: The continuation is not smooth in its horizon, so the horizon decides what the test sees. At 10 days the slope t is only 0.49 (65 cases), against 3.17 at 7 days, 2.47 at 14 days and 0.13 at 21 days.
