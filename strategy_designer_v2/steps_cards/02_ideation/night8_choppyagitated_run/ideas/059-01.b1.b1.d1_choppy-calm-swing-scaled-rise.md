Idea: In a choppy, calm market, the larger a coin's rise over the past 7 days measured in multiples of its usual 7-day swing over the past 30 days, the larger its further rise over the next 14 days. The rise is scaled by the coin's own swing, not ranked against the coin's recent history of such rises, so only rises that stand out from the coin's usual swing carry the continuation.
Consequence: A trader should go long only when a quiet, trendless coin's weekly rise is large in units of its own usual swing, and ignore modest rises that merely look large against recent weeks, since those give the move back.
Why:
- F1: The idea's cases do no better than holding the coin, which is why the book trails holding: over 589 cases the unusual move per case over 14 days is -0.04 % (t -0.04, 377 days), against a total move of +2.17 % that is 9.7 times the 0.22 % cost of a trade, so fees are not the problem and the missing excess is.
- F2: Inside the profile, the excess return lives only in rises that are large against the coin's own usual weekly swing. On the 14-day grid, cells with a positive forecast and a raw scaled 7-day rise above 0.5 earn +22.1 % excess (t 2.20, 22 cases, 9 days). Cells with a positive forecast and a smaller raw rise give back -11.0 % (t -2.55, 20 cases, 17 days), and cells with no signal earn -3.2 % (t -0.81, 38 cases).
- F3: The book's weakest forecasts are its losers, because the forecast is re-standardised over its own past 60 values, which lets small raw rises open longs. Grid cells with a forecast of 0-5 lose -11.9 % excess (t -3.09, 12 cases), while forecasts of 15-20 earn +23.2 % (t 2.41, 18 cases). Trades entered at a forecast of 0-5 average -2.33 % net (32 trades), against +7.23 % for 15-20 (163 trades).
- F4: The same split holds on 7 days, so a shorter hold does not rescue the weak cases. Raw scaled rises of 0-0.5 in the profile lose -5.7 % excess (t -3.47, 32 cases), while rises of 0.5-1 gain +7.2 % (t 2.19, 21 cases). On 14 days, rises above 1 gain +26.5 % (t 2.06, 13 cases), so the further rise grows with the raw scaled size.
