Idea: When a coin's daily swings over the past 30 days are large compared with its own recent history and its price has made steady net progress over those 30 days rather than chopping, a 7-day rise tends to be partly given back with a decline over the next 7 days. The larger that 7-day rise is relative to the coin's typical daily swing over the past 30 days, the larger the decline.
Consequence: In agitated markets that have been trending, a trader should fade a large weekly up-move rather than chase it, and should not fade it in agitated chop.
Why:
- F4: The reversal the brief describes does appear, but in the opposite situation. When 30-day volatility is in the coin's top third and the 30-day efficiency ratio is above 0.3 (agitated and trending), the past 7-day move is given back by -6.73 % over the next 7 days (64 cases, 36 days, t -1.99). That is a strength of -0.47 of a usual 7-day move, with rho -0.26 between the scaled 7-day move and the unusual move.

- F6: The same pattern shows with a causal volatility rank. Among bars whose 30-day volatility ranks in the top fifth of the window so far, the move reverses where the efficiency ratio is above 0.3 (-4.51 %, 67 cases, t -1.65, rho -0.19). It does not reverse where the ratio is 0.3 or below (+0.43 %, 104 cases, t 0.21).
- F7: The tested idea's size cannot pay, but the agitated-trending reversal could. The tested idea is -0.31 % per case (t -0.46), against a cost of 0.19 % a trade. The reversal in F4 is about 6.7 % per case, some 35 times that cost.
