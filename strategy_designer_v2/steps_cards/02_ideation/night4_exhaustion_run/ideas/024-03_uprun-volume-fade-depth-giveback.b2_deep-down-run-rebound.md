Idea: After an unbroken streak of falling four-hour periods whose loss is large relative to the coin's typical four-hour move over the past 30 days, the coin rises over the next three days. The larger the streak's loss in typical four-hour moves, the larger the rise.
Consequence: After a long run of falling bars far beyond the coin's usual move, a trader would buy for a three-day rebound, sized by the run's depth, rather than short the continuation.
Why:
- F5: Falls behave the other way, as the brief says, and with a graded size. After an unbroken run of falling 4h bars, the 18-bar unusual move toward a rebound by loss-size quintile is −0.59 % (t −2.72), −0.31 %, −0.37 %, +0.01 % and +1.18 % (t 4.14, 1058 cases). The top quintile has a strength of 0.13 and a total move of +1.59 %, about 8 times the 0.19 % cost.
- F6: The rebound after large falls holds in most years and on every coin, but volume fade barely changes it.
