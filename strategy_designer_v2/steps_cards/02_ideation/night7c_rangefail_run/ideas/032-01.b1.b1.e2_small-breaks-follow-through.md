Idea: In a coin whose price has gone nowhere over the past 60 days, a daily close above its prior 60-day high is followed by a further rise over the next 20 days. The smaller the distance of that close above the prior high, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader should prefer a modest close just past a quiet range's top over a large jump past it, which tends to give its gain back.
Why:
- F4: The idea's comparison holds. Breaks above the 60-day high in flat coins (ER ≤ 0.3) beat the same breaks in trending coins: at 10 days +9.2 % (t 2.00, 58 cases) against +2.7 % (t 0.58, 31 cases); at 20 days +5.7 % against +2.0 % (t 0.32, 22 cases).
- F6: Small breaks follow through more than large ones. By distance of the close above the prior 60-day high, the 20-day unusual move is +13.9 % in the smallest tercile, +5.1 % in the middle and −1.9 % in the largest (Spearman −0.19, 39 cases).
