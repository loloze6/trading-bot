Idea: In a rising market, the next-day rise after a day closing in the lower half of its range grows with that day's range against its 30-day swing multiplied by how near the close sits to the day's low.
Consequence: The discount left by a forced sweep is largest when the day is both wide against the coin's normal swing and ends at its low, so the next-day repayment is largest there.
