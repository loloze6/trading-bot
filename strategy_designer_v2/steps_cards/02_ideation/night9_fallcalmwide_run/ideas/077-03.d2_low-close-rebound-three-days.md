Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, a day closing below the middle of its range is followed by a rise over the next three days, larger the further the close sits below the middle, multiplied by the day's range against its 30-day swing.
Consequence: A trader holds the rebound after a wide low close for three days, which keeps the larger move and trades less often than a one-day hold.
Why:
- F2: The effect is on the long side: in-position long bars gain +1.10 % the next day in their direction (t 2.58, 186 bars, sd 5.8 %), against +0.69 % for shorts (t 1.34, 231 bars, sd 7.9 %); shorts lose in w11, w12 and w14 (summed gains −0.037, −0.092, −0.151) while longs are positive in 11 of 12 windows.
- F3: The long side is graded and lasts beyond one day: longs in the top third of |forecast| rise +2.93 % the next day (t 3.75, 67 cases), +2.66 % over 2 days and +3.39 % over 3 days, against +0.64 % (lowest third) and −0.20 % (middle third); shorts in the top third gain only +1.19 % (t 1.28), and their gain shrinks to +0.42 % at 2 days.
- F5: Costs are not what fails: fees sum to −0.20 against summed gross gains of 2.05, about 10 %, and the move per case (+0.36 %) is 1.6 times the 0.22 % cost of a trade; the failing measure comes from the size of the short-side falls, not from the fees.
