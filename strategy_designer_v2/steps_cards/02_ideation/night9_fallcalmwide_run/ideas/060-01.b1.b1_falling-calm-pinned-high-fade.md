Idea: In a falling, calm market, a day whose high-to-low range is wide against the coin's 30-day swing of daily returns and that closes at the very top of its range, pinned to its high, is followed by a fall over the next day, larger the closer the close sits to the day's high.
Consequence: In a falling, calm coin, only a wide day that ends essentially at its high is worth fading for one day, and closes merely in the upper part of the range are no reason to short.
Why:
- F1: Step 5's main measure is on the predicted side but short of the bar: +0.30 % next-day short-direction excess, t 1.00, over 321 cases on 186 days. In folds B and C alone, the same cases give +1.25 % (t 2.65, 105 cases, 63 days), so folds A and D must sit near zero or below and pull the whole result down; the effect is mixed across the data, not uniformly weak.
- F3: The effect lives where the close pins the day's high. Among active cases, closes in the top tenth of the range give +2.13 % next day (t 2.99, 29 cases, 22 days, hit 79 %), against +0.69 % (t 1.18, 60 cases, 39 days) for the rest of the top quarter. By close-location tercile, the lowest gives +0.08 % (t 0.11), against +2.12 % (t 2.73) and +1.60 % (t 2.48) for the two upper ones.
- F4: The day's width does not grade the effect. The width terciles give +1.28 %, +1.30 % and +0.90 %, and the Spearman correlation of width with the next-day excess is −0.09, against +0.18 for close location. Among top-tenth closes, ranges of 1.5–2 usual moves even give −1.72 % (t −1.30), against +2.51 % (t 3.15, 22 cases) for ranges of 2–3 usual moves, so the width cut matters but more width adds nothing.
- F5: The plan's 1-day horizon is where the effect shows; it fades over longer holds. The 2-day excess is +0.63 % (t 0.93) and the 3-day +0.81 % (t 0.75), against +1.25 % (t 2.65) at 1 day. At 3 days the widest tercile turns negative (−1.16 %).
- F6: Per case the move is too small to pay its cost. The total move is +0.13 % against a 0.22 % trade cost (0.6 times). The unusual move is +0.26 % (1.2 times, t 0.87), so only the top-of-range cases of F3, with about +2 % per case, could cover it.
