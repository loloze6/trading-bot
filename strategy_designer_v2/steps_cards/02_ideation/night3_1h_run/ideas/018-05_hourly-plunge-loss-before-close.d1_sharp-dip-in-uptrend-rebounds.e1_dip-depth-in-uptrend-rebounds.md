Idea: When a coin's 30-day return is positive, the deeper its 24-hour fall in multiples of its 30-day average absolute daily return, the more the coin rises above its normal drift over the next 3 days.
Consequence: A sharp one-day drop inside a month-long rise is a reason to buy for about 3 days, sized by the depth of the drop rather than by the strength of the month.
Why:
- F4: The idea's situation is real when read on the plain grid, and graded by the depth of the fall. With a positive 30-day return, the 72-hour unusual move by depth of the 24-hour fall (in multiples of the 30-day mean absolute 24-hour return) is:
- F5: The same falls when the 30-day return is not positive are followed by no rebound. Falls of at least one usual daily move give +0.09 % over 72 hours (t 0.23, 421 cases), and falls of at least two give +0.66 % (t 1.0, 146 cases). Falls of at least two in an uptrend give +2.94 % (t 4.12, 86 cases).
- F6: The idea's second grading, by the strength of the month's rise, is not there. Among uptrend dips of at least one usual daily move, the 72-hour unusual move by tercile of the 30-day return in its usual size is +1.51 %, +3.98 % and −0.22 % from weakest to strongest (Spearman −0.086, p 0.16, 263 cases).

- F8: Acting at the hour the situation first holds earns no more than acting at that day's close. The 72-hour unusual move is +0.44 % from onset and +0.47 % from the daily close, a difference of −0.02 % (t −0.19, 1760 cases, mean lag 12 hours). This is the brief's own refutation condition.
