Idea: When a coin's 30-day return is positive, the larger that return in multiples of its usual 30-day move, the more the coin rises above its normal drift over the next 2 weeks.
Consequence: A strong month-long rise is a reason to stay long for weeks, which is the hold this book actually profits from.
Why:
- F1: The book's gain is mostly the market's rise, held through a few large long trades. Its mean daily return is 0.1232 % (t 3.14, 2898 days), of which 0.0554 % is the market's own rise and 0.0678 % is timing, against 0.1179 % for holding the coins. Without its best 5 % of days the mean falls to −0.1614 %. Without its best 5 % of trades, the net sum of 12353 % becomes −665 %.
- F2: The book earns from long holds of weeks, not from the plan's 3 days. The 860 trades held over 14 days carry 10219 % of the 12353 % net sum, and trades of 3 to 7 days earn 0.02 % each. The mean trade lasts 243 hours, and 70 % of entries are at the forecast's cap of 20.
- F3: The book earns where the 30-day return is positive, but mostly away from the dip itself. Bars with a positive 30-day return carry 73 % of the position's gain, at a mean position of 0.85 against 0.14 when the 30-day return is not positive. Inside those up states, bars with a 24-hour fall of at least one usual daily move carry only 28 % of the gain, against 41 % for bars without such a fall.
- F11: A plain month-long rise predicts the next 3 days only weakly. Across all grid cells, the top two quintiles of the 30-day return give a 72-hour unusual move of +0.35 % and +0.40 % (t 1.34 and 1.10, 820 cases each). The bottom quintiles give −0.09 % and −0.51 %.
