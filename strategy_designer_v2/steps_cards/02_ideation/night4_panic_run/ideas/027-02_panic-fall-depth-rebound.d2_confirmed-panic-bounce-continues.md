Idea: Among 24-hour stretches where a coin's range is several times its 30-day average and the coin fell, the larger the coin's rise over the 24 hours after the stretch, the higher its return over the following 14 days.
Consequence: A trader waits one day after a panic and buys in proportion to the first bounce, which shows that the forced selling is over.
Why:
- F4: What happens in the first day after entry sorts the outcome. Entries whose coin rose more than 4.3 % over the next 24 hours returned +34.3 % on average while held (median +11.9 %, 43 cases). The other two terciles returned +2.6 % and -0.6 %. Over the first 72 hours, the top tercile returned +34.7 % against -2.4 % and +4.1 %.
- F1: The book's deepest fall, -44.7 % over 313 days, spans windows w12 to w14, while the coins fell -68.2 % in the same days. Window w14 alone lost a summed -36.5 % of daily returns. The book's mean daily return was -0.115 % in w12–w14 against +0.064 % in the other windows, which sinks both the Calmar (0.29) and the mean daily return (0.042 %).
