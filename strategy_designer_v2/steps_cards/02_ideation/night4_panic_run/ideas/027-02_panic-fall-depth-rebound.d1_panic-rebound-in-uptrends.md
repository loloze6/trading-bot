Idea: Among 24-hour stretches where a coin's range is several times its 30-day average, the deeper the coin's fall over that stretch, the higher its return over the next 14 days, only while the coin's 90-day return is positive, and the more so the larger that 90-day rise.
Consequence: A trader buys panics in coins whose last three months rose, and refuses panics inside a longer decline, where the forced selling is part of a bear market rather than a one-off discount.
Why:
- F1: The book's deepest fall, -44.7 % over 313 days, spans windows w12 to w14, while the coins fell -68.2 % in the same days. Window w14 alone lost a summed -36.5 % of daily returns. The book's mean daily return was -0.115 % in w12–w14 against +0.064 % in the other windows, which sinks both the Calmar (0.29) and the mean daily return (0.042 %).
- F2: The 15 entries taken in w12–w14 returned -14.1 % on average while held, with 20 % winners. The other 113 entries returned +15.7 % with 63 % winners. The two groups differ in the coin's 90-day (540-bar) return before entry, -23.5 % against +37.1 %, but not in its 14-day (84-bar) return, +13.2 % against +9.9 %.
- F3: The rebound grows with the coin's longer trend before the panic. By tercile of the 30-day (180-bar) return, held returns were +5.3 %, +8.3 % and +14.5 % (31 cases each). By tercile of the distance from the 30-day mean price they were +3.9 %, +9.8 % and +14.2 %. By tercile of the 90-day return they were +4.3 %, +1.1 % and +15.4 %, with 100 % winners in the top tercile (8 cases; slope of the 84-bar return on it, t 0.95, 23 cases). The 9 entries of 2018 lost -33.4 % on average.
