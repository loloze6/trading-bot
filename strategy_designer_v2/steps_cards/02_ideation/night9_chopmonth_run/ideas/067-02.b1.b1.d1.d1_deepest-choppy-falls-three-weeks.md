Idea: In a choppy market, the further a coin's close sits below its close of 30 days earlier, measured as a plain percent of that earlier close, the more it falls over the next 21 days, with the decline growing faster than the depth so that the deepest month's falls carry nearly all of it and shallow falls carry almost none.
Consequence: A trader shorts only the deepest month's falls in a choppy market and holds them about three weeks, so that the move per trade is many times its cost and the shallow falls that lose against the coins' rise are left alone.
Why:
- F1: The book's gain is diluted by the shallow falls and by the coins' own rise, so it never covers what it risks. It is in a position on 16 % of coin-days and earns 0.016 % per position day, against the 0.05 % required. Its 458 trades are all short and average -2.21 % before fees (-2.31 % after). Short bars with a forecast between -2 and 0 lose 0.143 in summed gain, while bars from -10 to -2 make +0.448 and bars at -10 or below make +0.231. The normal drift works against the short by -1.15 % per case, which cuts the total move to +0.50 % although the unusual move is +1.65 %.
