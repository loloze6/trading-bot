Idea: The larger a coin's positive 10-day return relative to its own daily volatility over the past 60 days, the more it keeps rising over the next 5 days beyond its normal drift.
Consequence: A trader would lean long harder into a coin whose recent two-week rise is large for its volatility, and would stay small when the rise is modest.
Why:
- F3: Of the book's summed pre-cost gain, 17.9 comes from held bars that did not close up (streak 0) and only 2.0 from bars at streak 1, while the per-bar gain at streaks 2, 3, 4 and 5+ is 0.32 %, 0.32 %, 0.49 % and 0.71 %; the streak does not carry the gain by itself.
- F4: Inside 30-day-up stretches, the streak's own 5-day unusual move is not monotone (streak 0: +0.04 %, 1: +0.50 %, 2: +1.87 % t 1.62, 3: +2.25 % t 1.70, 4+: +0.23 % t 0.22), and with the volatility-scaled 30-day return in the same regression the streak's 5-day coefficient has t 1.27 against 1.53 for the 30-day return, so the streak adds little beyond trend.
- F8: Among bars with a positive volatility-scaled 10-day return, the slope of the 5-day scaled unusual move on that return is 0.145 (t 2.89, 2352 cases on 365 days) and the 10-day slope is 0.219 (t 2.40); the top quartile gives +1.46 % over 5 days (t 1.74, strength 0.17) and +2.52 % over 10 days (strength 0.23).
