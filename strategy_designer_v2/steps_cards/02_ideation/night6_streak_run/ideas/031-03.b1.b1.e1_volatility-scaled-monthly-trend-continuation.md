Idea: The larger a coin's 30-day return relative to its own daily volatility over the past 60 days, the more it rises over the next 10 days beyond its normal drift.
Consequence: A trader would size a long by how strong the coin's volatility-adjusted month-long rise is, and would hold it for about ten days rather than react to single up closes.
Why:
- F2: The book's daily gain tracks a plain "long whenever the 30-day return is positive" unit with a correlation of 0.81 (that unit earns 0.088 % a day against the book's 0.118 %), so the 30-day trend regime, not the streak, carries most of the book.
- F5: Across all bars, the 30-day return grades the next move: the 5-day unusual move by 30-day-return quintile runs −0.50 %, −1.19 % (t −1.97), −0.75 %, +0.60 % and +1.23 % (t 1.64; 921 cases), and over 10 days the top quintile gives +2.47 % (t 1.66).
- F6: Scaled by the coin's 60-day volatility, the 30-day return predicts the volatility-scaled unusual move with a slope of 0.064 (t 1.99, 4603 cases) at 5 days, and the 90-day return does so with a slope of 0.095 (t 2.45, 718 cases) at 10 days.
- F7: Among bars with a positive volatility-scaled 30-day return, the top quartile (mean 2.42 usual moves) is followed by a 10-day unusual move of +3.76 % (t 1.94, 290 cases on 91 days, strength 0.28, total move +5.27 %), against −1.21 % for the bottom quartile; this total move is about 24 times the 0.22 % cost of a trade.
- F9: The book's gain is spread across coins (ETH 12.6 %, BTC 11.6 %, ADA 10.8 %, the top five 55 %) but concentrated in time: 2023 and 2024 give 31 % and 36 % of it, while 2018 (−4.7 %) and 2022 (−7.2 %) lose; the 10-day trend slope is negative only in those two years (−0.53 and −0.27).
