Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days, having closed below it the day before, it tends to rise over the next 10 days. The more times it turned down there before, the larger the rise, and the more so the higher its 20-day return up to that close.
Consequence: A close through a repeated resistance is a reason to buy when it comes on top of a 20-day rise, and a reason to refuse the buy when it comes after a 20-day fall.
Why:
- F1: The Calmar fails because the falls are long and the coins share them. The book's deepest fall is −36.6 % against a yearly return of 19.5 %. It lasted 452 days to its trough, across w6 to w10. Holding the same coins summed −54 % of daily returns over that stretch. The next falls are −27.4 % (w12 to w14, 283 days) and −27.1 % (w16, 27 days). Holding fell much more in each, −102 % and −79 % in summed daily returns, so the book was long through broad declines.
- F3: The losses come from a few days with large positions. The worst 2 % of held days (251 days) sum to −20.5 in daily gain, against +11.2 for all held days together. On those days the median allocation is 0.87, against 0.34 on all held days. The 20-day return before them is +12.3 % (median), against +2.3 % on all held days, and the 20-to-60-day volatility ratio is 1.10 against 0.96.
- F4: Breaks that come after a 20-day decline are followed by a fall. In the lowest fifth of the 20-day return, the excess return over the next 10 days is −2.7 % (t −3.3, 377 cases). In the highest fifth it is +4.1 % (t 3.3, 377 cases), and the middle three fifths range from +0.8 % to +1.5 % (t ≤ 1.9).
- F6: The idea as tested is too weak to stand on its own. Its unusual move is +0.31 % per case over 10 days (strength 0.009, t 0.62, 1,084 cases), only 1.6 times a round trip of 0.19 %. Most of the book's gain is the coins' drift (+1.17 % per case), which is why it is exposed to the coins' shared falls (F1). Inside the trending half, the unusual move is +4 to +5 % (F4, F5), about 20 times the cost.
- F7: Trade results depend on the market's direction by year. The mean net per trade is −2.7 % in 2018 (207 trades) and 0.0 % in 2022 (215 trades), the two years when holding's mean daily return was negative. In 2020 it is +9.4 % and in 2024 +10.6 %.
