Idea: When a coin's close sits under a price where it turned up several times in the past 90 days, the deeper the close lies under that level and the more times the level held, the more the coin rises over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A deep break of a repeatedly held support is a reason to buy the rebound and to refuse the breakdown short, which F1 shows loses.
Why:
- F2: Inside broken support, the move grades the opposite way to the idea. On the 5-day grid, cases are split by quartile of each coin's log raw value (touch count × depth under the level). The strength of the next 5 days runs -0.076 (t -2.23, 594 cases), -0.077 (t -2.16, 590), -0.024 (t -0.59, 589) and +0.064 (t 1.48, 593). The deepest, most-held breaks are followed by a rise of +0.70 % over the coin's drift, while the shallowest are followed by a fall of -0.82 % below it.
- F3: The F2 gradient appears on every coin and in both halves of the years. Spearman's rho between the log raw value and the next 5 days' strength is positive on 6 of 6 coins (0.02 to 0.11, 358 to 425 cases each). The quartile means climb from -0.107 to +0.068 in 2018–2021 and from -0.052 to +0.058 in 2022–2025.
- F4: The deepest-quartile rebound is large enough to pay. Its unusual move is +0.70 % per case over 5 days, 3.7 times the 0.19 % cost of a buy and a sell. A long bet also earns the coin's normal drift of about +0.58 % over 5 days, so the total move is about 1.3 %, roughly 7 times the cost.
- F1: The tested short book lost against a rising market. The book's mean daily return is -0.051 % while the coins' is +0.118 %, and the two correlate at -0.82. The mean position runs from -0.36 to -0.54 on all 6 coins. Every coin's short trades lose on average after fees: from -0.91 % per trade on ETH to -4.85 % on XRP, over 596 to 773 trades per coin.
