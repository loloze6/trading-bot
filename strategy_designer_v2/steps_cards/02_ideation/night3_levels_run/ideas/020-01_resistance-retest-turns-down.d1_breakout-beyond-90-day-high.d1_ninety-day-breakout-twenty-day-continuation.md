Idea: The further a coin's daily close stands above its highest close of the previous 90 days, the more it rises over the next 20 days.
Consequence: A trader should hold a long after a close beyond the 90-day high for about three weeks, larger the further beyond, rather than leaving it after five days.
Why:
- F2: The breakout's move keeps growing well past the 5-day horizon that was tested. The mean total move after a breakout case is +1.4 % over 5 days, +3.2 % over 10, +4.8 % over 20 and +6.9 % over 40 (175, 172, 162 and 124 cases). The unusual move is +0.8 %, +2.0 %, +2.4 % and +2.5 % (t 0.81, 1.32, 1.14, 0.64).
- F3: The trades the book held longer earn almost all of its gain. Trades of up to 5.5 days average +0.85 % net (282 trades). Trades of 5.5 to 10 days average +13.6 % (93), 10 to 20 days +33.0 % (50) and 20 to 40 days +44.3 % (25). The median trade still lasts only 5 days.
- F4: The size of the breakout grades the longer move but not the 5-day one. By forecast quartile, the 20-day unusual move is -1.4 %, +0.5 %, +2.7 % and +8.1 %, and the 10-day one rises to +5.6 % in the top quartile. The 5-day move peaks in the third quartile (+5.4 %) and falls back to +0.7 % in the top one, which is why the 5-day slope has t 0.37.
- F1: The book is almost always out of the market, which keeps its mean daily return at 0.000165 against the 0.0005 required. It holds a position on only 17.6 % of bars, and its mean position is 0.036 of the book. When it is in, the position is only 0.15 to 0.38 of the book, because the forecast is 0.9 to 1.7 on average. Between 28 % and 48 % of those in-position bars already carry a forecast of 0, depending on the coin.
