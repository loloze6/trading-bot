Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days, after a rise over its past 20 days, it tends to keep rising over the next 20 days. The more times the level held, the further the close sits above it and the larger the 20-day rise, the larger the rise that follows.
Consequence: A strong break of a repeatedly held level after a run is a reason to hold the buy for weeks rather than days, since the move is still growing at four times the tested horizon.
Why:
- F1: The break's rise keeps building well past the tested 5 days. Over the same 2,744 to 2,444 cases, the unusual move per case is +0.71 % at 5 days (t 2.55, strength 0.052), +1.33 % at 10 days (t 3.24, strength 0.070) and +2.30 % at 20 days (t 3.53, strength 0.080). The 20-day unusual move alone is about 10 times the 0.22 % cost of a trade.
- F2: Almost all of the edge sits in the strongest breaks. In the top quartile of entry forecast (679 cases), the strength is 0.139 at 5 days, 0.154 at 10 days and 0.182 at 20 days. The three lower quartiles sit between 0.007 and 0.096, with no order among them.
- F3: Much of the break's move happens on the signal's own bar, before a trader acting at its close can capture it. That bar's unusual return is +5.6 % (t 31.5 over 2,693 cases), against +0.71 % over the 5 days after it.
