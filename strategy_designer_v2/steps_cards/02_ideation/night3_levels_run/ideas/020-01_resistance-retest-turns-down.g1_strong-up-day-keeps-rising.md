Idea: After a coin's daily rise, the larger that rise compared with its typical daily move over the past 20 days, the more the coin rises over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader leans long for about a week after an unusually strong up day, more so the stronger the day, and refuses shorts after one.
Why:
- F2: A large daily rise, measured against the coin's 20-day typical daily move, is followed by a rise over the next 5 days that grows with its size: +0.59 % after a rise of 0 to 1 typical move (n 985, t 1.7), +2.14 % after 1 to 2 (n 286, t 3.3), +3.19 % above 2 (n 94, t 2.2), against a normal 5-day drift of about 0.6 %, so an unusual move of about 1.5 to 2.6 %, 8 to 13 times the 0.19 % cost of a round trip.
- F3: Falls are not mirrored: after daily falls of 1 to 2 and of more than 2 typical moves the next 5 days return +0.60 % (t 1.2, n 319) and +0.71 % (t 0.7, n 72), about the drift, so the continuation in F2 belongs to rises only; the quintiles of the day's return agree only at the top (top fifth +1.0 % over drift, t 2.1; slope t 1.74 across all five).
