Idea: The larger a coin's 30-day return, measured against its own recent daily volatility, the more it rises over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A coin whose 30-day run stands high against its own volatility is a reason to be long for the following week, and one with a weak 30-day return is a reason to refuse a long.
Why:
- F3: Grading by the past 30-day return pays on every grid bar, not only at breaks. The slope of 5-day strength on the volatility-scaled 30-day return is 0.040 (t 2.24, 2555 cases) and is positive in both halves of the sample (t 1.96 up to 2021, t 1.13 after). It is positive in 5 of 6 coins (XMR −0.72 is the exception).
- F4: The top 30-day-return quintile earns +1.18 % unusual over 5 days (t 1.99, 510 cases, strength 0.10), against −1.07 % for the second-lowest quintile (t −2.76, 510 cases). The top quintile's move is about 6 times the cost.
