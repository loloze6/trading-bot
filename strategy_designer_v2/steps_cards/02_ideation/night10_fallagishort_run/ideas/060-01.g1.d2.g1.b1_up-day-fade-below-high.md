Idea: In a falling, agitated market, a coin that closes in the upper part of its day's high-low range on a day it closed higher than the day before falls over the next day. The further that close sits below the day's high, the larger the next-day fall.
Consequence: A trader shorts an up day's upper-half close in a falling, agitated coin for one day, and shorts it hardest when the close has already slipped back from the day's high rather than finishing on it.
Why:
- F1: The test's grading points the wrong way. In folds B and C the 389 cases fall 1.08 % more than drift (t 2.34, 181 days). By close-location quartile the fall shrinks as the close nears the high: 1.87 % (t 3.04) in the lowest quartile, then 1.00 %, 0.80 %, and 0.62 % (t 0.57) in the highest. The lower half minus the upper half is +0.73 % (Welch t 1.39), the Spearman correlation of location with the fall is -0.088, and step 5's upper-minus-lower is -0.51 %. That reversed grading, not a missing fall, is why the result is inconclusive.
- F2: The grading shows again in the distance from the close up to the day's high, as a share of price. Cases in the largest-gap quartile fall 2.05 % more than drift (t 2.46, 59 days), against 0.40 % (t 0.51) in the smallest-gap quartile, with a Spearman correlation of +0.113 over 389 cases.
- F3: The fall lives in the falling agitated profile. All in-profile up days fall 0.98 % more than drift (t 2.54, 613 cases, 258 days); the 0.5–0.75 location band falls 1.22 % (t 2.76) and the 0.75–1 band 0.85 % (t 1.58). Up days closing in the upper half outside the profile fall only 0.18 % (t 1.16, 3,090 cases).
- F4: The effect lasts one day only. In-profile up days with a close in the upper half show 0.31 % over 2 days (t 0.36, 260 cases) and -0.07 % over 3 days (t -0.06, 167 cases), against about 1 % over the next day.
