Idea: In a falling, agitated market, when a coin closes up on the day, the further that close sits below its 20-day high, measured in units of its 20-day daily swing, the larger its fall over the next day.
Consequence: A trader keeps the short fade of deep up-day closes for coins that have fallen over the past two months and swing more than usual, and refuses it after a rise, where the giveback did not appear.
Why:
- F1: In the agitated bars (regime not "chop") of folds B and C, up days were followed by a next-day excess return of -0.62 % (t -2.38, day-clustered, 1589 cases), while calm up days were followed by only -0.12 % (t -0.71, 2095 cases), so the giveback lives in agitated markets.
- F2: Inside agitated up days, the giveback sits almost entirely in falling markets: up days after a 30-day fall (below -7 %) were followed by -1.16 % (t -2.50, 510 cases), up days in a choppy 30-day trend by -0.70 % (t -1.54, 158 cases), and up days after a 30-day rise by +0.00 % (t 0.01, 630 cases). This is a proxy; the profile's own 60-day ±10 % trend was not measured.
- F3: The agitated up-day giveback points the same way in every year (2018 -1.28 %, 2019 -0.44 %, 2020 -0.45 %, 2021 -0.60 %), in 9 of the 12 windows, and on all six coins (BTC -0.32 % to ZEC -1.11 %; ETH, XMR and ZEC each with t below -2).
- F5: The book's short positions held after agitated up days earned their side of the move: on those 1494 cases the next day's excess was -0.70 % (t -2.60). The 95 up days the book did not hold short were followed by +0.58 % (t 0.67), so the book is not losing its edge by missing cases.
