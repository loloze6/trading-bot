Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's 3-day rise is against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger the fall in its price over the next 3 days.
Consequence: A trader would treat a large scaled few-day rally in a falling, agitated market as a reason to short or refuse longs for the next few days only, not for two weeks.
Why:
- F1: Inside the falling-agitated profile, a scaled 3-day rise is given back mostly over the next 3 days. On the 3-day grid, the excess return falls as the rise grows: slope -0.0318, t -2.2, 180 rise cases, mean excess -1.02 %. The top quintile of the scaled 3-day move (mean 1.32 usual moves) is followed by -3.00 % excess, t -3.01, 58 cases. The slope on all cases is negative on every one of the 3 grid offsets, with t from -1.59 to -0.86.
- F2: At the tested 15-day horizon, rises in the profile are followed by lower prices, but the size of the rise does not grade the outcome. Rise cases show a mean excess of -12.69 %, yet the slope among rises only is +0.0141, t 0.4, 42 cases. Across grid offsets the 15-day slope ranges from t -2.71 to +2.11, and only 67 % of offsets are negative. This is why the tested slope only reached t 1.21.
- F6: The book stays short long after the signal has faded, which is why it gives back the 3-day give-back of F1. In the profile the forecast is below 0 on only 39.3 % of bars, but the position is short on 83.9 %. Its 245 short trades lost 5.09 % on average after fees, with a median of -1.46 %, and in 2020 and 2021 the book lost 0.078 % and 0.006 % a day.
