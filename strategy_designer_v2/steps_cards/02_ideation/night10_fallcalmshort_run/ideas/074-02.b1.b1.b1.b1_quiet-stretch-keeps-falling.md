Idea: In a falling, calm market, when a coin's close sits below its 20-day average, measured in units of the standard deviation of its daily returns over the same 20 days, it keeps falling over the next 5 days, the more the quieter its daily returns have been over the last 5 days against their 20-day standard deviation.
Consequence: A trader would short a stretch below the average in a falling calm market when the slide has been slow and quiet, sizing the short larger the quieter the last days, and would stay out when the last days have been swinging hard.
Why:
- F1: The result points the way the idea predicts, but it is uneven across periods. In folds B and C, the 276 short cases have a mean unusual 5-day move of +2.13 % (day-clustered t 2.86), and +3.00 % over 10 days (t 2.40).
