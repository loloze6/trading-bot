Idea: In a falling, calm market, the first up day after a run of down days is followed by a fall in the coin's close over the next five days, and the deeper the run's fall against the coin's usual daily swing over the last 30 days, the larger that five-day fall.
Consequence: A trader who shorts the relief day after a deep slide keeps the short for about a week, so the move is many times what the trade costs.
Why:
- F1: The book's gain comes almost entirely from the deepest runs, and the shallower runs add risk but no gain. Among the signal cases on folds B and C, runs whose fall exceeds 2.85 of the coin's 30-day daily swing gave a short-signed next-day move of +3.17 % (t 5.68, 56 cases). The other 161 cases gave -0.03 % (t -0.09). Weighted by the position held, the deep cases sum to +1.82 and the shallow ones to +0.05. Yet the shallow cases hold the worst single day (-15.3 % against -4.3 % for the deep ones). So the sizing, which caps typical and deep runs alike at -20, spreads the book's risk over cases that earn nothing. That fails Sharpe minus holding's (-0.10) and Calmar (0.23).
- F2: The grading the idea predicts is steep, and it lives in its top quarter. By depth quarter, the next-day short-signed move is -0.33 %, +0.44 %, -0.15 % and +2.95 % (t 5.96, 65 cases). By the 5-day return before the signal, the most fallen quarter gives +3.15 % (t 4.63) and the other three quarters give between -0.48 % and +0.16 %.
- F4: The fall of a deep case keeps going beyond one day. Deep signal cases give a short-signed move of +4.16 % over 3 days (t 3.92) and +6.66 % over 5 days (t 4.74). On non-overlapping 5-day cells, the 5-day move is +6.89 % (t 4.74, 54 cases on 34 days), 36 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade. Shallow cases over 5 days give +0.49 % (t 0.59).
