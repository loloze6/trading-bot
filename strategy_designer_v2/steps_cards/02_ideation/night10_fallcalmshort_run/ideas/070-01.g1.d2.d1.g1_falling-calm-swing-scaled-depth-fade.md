Idea: In a falling, calm market, an up day that ends a run of down days is followed by a fall over the next day, and that fall is larger the more the price dropped over the run, measured against the coin's daily swing over the past 30 days.
Consequence: A trader should fade the first up day of a quiet downtrend in proportion to how deep the run was against the coin's own recent swing, and should not grade it by how many days the run lasted.
Why:
- F1: Over runs of at least 2 down days, the depth of the run in percent grades the next-day short excess in a steady way: from the shallowest to the deepest quartile it is +0.37 % (t 1.28, 177 days), -0.08 %, +1.41 % (t 2.52) and +2.43 % (t 3.42, 120 days), with a Spearman correlation of 0.16. The run's length in days does not grade it (Spearman -0.009). Scaling the loss by the 30-day swing gives the same picture, from +0.00 % (t 0.01) to +1.94 % (t 3.13, 99 days). Step 5 found no grading through the book's 60-value standardised forecast, so the grading exists in the raw depth and is lost in that transform.
- F4: The fade depends on the 60-day trend. With a proxy for agitation, it is +1.57 % (t 2.62, 65 days) in falling calm markets, +1.20 % (t 1.56) in falling agitated ones and +1.26 % (t 1.50) in choppy calm ones. It is +0.25 % (t 0.37) in rising agitated markets and -1.02 % (t -1.60, 61 days) in rising calm ones.
