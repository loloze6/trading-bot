Idea: In a falling, calm market, when a coin closes up on the day, the lower that close sits in its 20-day high-low range, the larger its fall over the next day.
Consequence: A trader fades up days that close near the bottom of the recent range while the coin trends down quietly, and leaves alone those that close near the range's top.
Why:
- F4: The tested idea's own grading, the close's place in its 20-day high-low range on up days, shows only in the falling calm market. There, a higher place ranks with a higher next-day return, Spearman +0.168 (n 533, p 1e-4), so a lower close falls more. In the falling agitated market the same reading has no grading: Spearman +0.031 (n 776, p 0.39).
- F5: Over all falling up days the depth below the 20-day high does not grade monotonically. By quintile, deepest first, the next-day excess is -1.01 %, -0.99 %, -0.13 %, -0.83 % and +0.11 %. By place in the 20-day range it is -0.82 %, -1.01 %, -0.52 %, -0.46 % and -0.04 %. The effect is a giveback of up days that weakens only near the top.
