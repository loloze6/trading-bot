Idea: The larger a coin's one-day rise against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the further it keeps rising over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader follows large up days on the long side only, sized by the day's size in swing units, and does not short large down days.
Why:
- F1: The book's loss comes from its short side. Short trades lost at every holding length: 214 trades of up to 5 days summed -387.8 %, 56 of 6 to 10 days -122.9 %, and 52 of 10 to 30 days -199.9 %. Long trades held longer than 5 days gained: 11 trades at 5 to 6 days +90.6 %, 57 at 6 to 10 days +148.7 %, 58 at 10 to 30 days +106.4 %. Per bar in a position, short positions earned -0.019 % on 688 bars and long positions +0.021 % on 830 bars.
- F2: Over 5 days, only up days follow through, and the follow-through grows with the size of the day. On all bars, the 5-day excess return in the day's direction rises with the day's size in 30-day swing units (|z|) after up days, with slope +0.0162 (t 2.18, 610 non-overlapping cases), but not after down days, with slope -0.0002 (t -0.03, 754 cases). Up days with |z| > 1.5 were followed by a mean of +3.94 % (t 2.26, 72 cases), down days with |z| > 1.5 by -0.33 % (t -0.29, 75 cases).
- F5: In choppy markets (60-day return between -10 % and +10 %), up days continue clearly over 5 days and down days do not. The graded slope was +0.0612 after up days (t 2.67, 64 cases) and -0.0008 after down days (t -0.02, 61 cases). In the tested profile, up days with |z| > 1.5 in a choppy calm market were followed by +6.16 % (t 2.57, 7 cases).
