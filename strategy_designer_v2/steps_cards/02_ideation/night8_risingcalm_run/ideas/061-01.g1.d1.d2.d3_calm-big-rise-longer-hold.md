Idea: In a choppy, calm market, the larger a coin's one-day rise against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, and the closer that rise takes its close to its highest close of the past 20 days, the further it keeps rising over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A trader holds calm-market large up days near their highs for ten days rather than five, keeping the move while paying for half as many trades.
Why:
- F6: The effect of large rises still shows over 10 days inside the profile but fades in strength. Rises above one swing earn +7.02 % in excess over 10 days (n 11, t 1.60) against +6.22 % over 5 days. At the 20-day high, the excess over 20 days is +12.99 % (n 4). The 5-day horizon is right in order of size, and holding to 10 days keeps most of the move with half the trades.
- F2: The book trades mostly the losing small rises. The forecast is each component standardised over its past 60 values and clipped at 0, so any ordinary up day gets a position. The median entry day was a rise of only 0.76 swing units, and just 32 % of the 98 entries rose more than one swing. On the grid, the bottom half of positive forecasts loses -1.80 % in excess over 5 days (n 48, t -1.50) and -5.36 % over 10 days. The top half gains +2.80 % (t 1.61) and +5.18 % over 10 days (t 1.92). The book's position-day mean is therefore 0.030 % a day against the 0.05 % required, and its Sharpe is 0.40 below holding.
