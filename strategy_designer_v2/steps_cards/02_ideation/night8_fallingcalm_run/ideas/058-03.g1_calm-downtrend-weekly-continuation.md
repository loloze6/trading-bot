Idea: In a falling, calm market, a coin's last 5 days carry on over the next 5 days, and the further it has fallen over its last 5 days, the more it falls over the next 5 days. The further it has risen over those 5 days, the more it rises.
Consequence: In a calm downtrend, a trader would trade the coin in the direction of its past week rather than the depth of its past month, shorting the sharpest recent drops.
Why:
- F1: In falling calm bars, the coin's past 5-day return predicts its next 5-day return in the same direction (slope 0.17, t 2.62, 342 non-overlapping cases). The lowest third of past 5-day returns is followed by -1.6 %, the middle third by -0.3 % and the highest third by +2.2 %. Inside the tested situation (30-day return below 0) the slope is 0.22 (t 2.68, 254 cases), with thirds at -2.1 %, +0.0 % and +1.9 %.
- F2: Outside falling calm, the same 5-day continuation is absent (slope -0.001, t -0.05, 1207 cases), so this short-horizon continuation lives in the falling calm profile.
- F3: The brief's own situation does not grade the move: in falling calm, the depth of the 30-day fall has no link with the next 5 days (slope 0.017, t 0.48, 281 cases), nor does the 60-day fall (t -0.26, 209 cases).
