Idea: In a falling, calm market, the deeper a coin's 30-day fall and the more its volume over those 30 days has shrunk against the 30 days before, the more it keeps falling over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A deep calm decline made on thinning volume is a reason to stay short, and the same depth on swelling volume is a reason to refuse the short.
Why:
- F1: Among the 5-day grid cases where the coin was below its close of 30 days earlier, the continuation is graded by how much volume has thinned. The quintiles of last-30-day volume over the 30 days before run from thinnest to heaviest at -4.73 % (t -2.40, 82 cases, 40 days), -2.16 % (t -1.57), -2.71 % (t -1.69), -0.44 % (t -0.26) and +0.84 % (t 0.29), as 5-day excess returns. Their correlation is +0.153, against -0.009 over all cases.
- F2: Depth only predicts more falling when volume has thinned. Splitting the falls into thirds by depth and by volume ratio, the thinnest-volume third goes from -1.86 % for the shallowest falls to -4.76 % and then -5.89 % for the deepest. The heaviest-volume third goes the other way, with the deepest falls at +2.25 % (69 cases). This is the brief's contrast between quiet and heavy volume.
- F4: The thin-volume falls continue mainly in a falling market.
- F5: The thin-volume fall effect points the same way across the data.
