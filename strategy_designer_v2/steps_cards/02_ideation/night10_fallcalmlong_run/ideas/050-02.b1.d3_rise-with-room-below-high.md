Idea: In a falling, calm market, a coin that has risen over the last 7 days while still below its 30-day high keeps rising over the next 7 days, and rises further the larger its scaled 7-day rise and the further below its 30-day high it still stands.
Consequence: A trader buys the weekly rises that still leave room below the month's high, and gives the least weight to those that have already reached it.
Why:
- F6: A rise that leaves the coin still well below its 30-day high carries further. Over 7 days, signal cases more than 5 % below the high had an unusual move of +3.2 % (22 cases), against -4.5 % for those within 5 % of the high (9 cases), Welch t 2.10. Trades entered near the high averaged -0.58 % net (52 trades), against +5.63 % for those entered furthest below it (52 trades).
- F7: The distance from the high is not an effect on its own. Without the signal, bars more than 15 % below the high had an unusual 3-day move of -2.5 % (91 cases), and bars 5 to 15 % below had +0.1 % (32). With the signal, the same middle bucket had +2.1 % (24). Signal cases both far below the high and with a scaled rise above 0.4 had +3.3 % (t 2.56, 26 cases).
- F4: The signal is the effect, not the profile. In the falling calm profile, grid bars with no signal had an unusual 3-day move of -1.37 % (149 cases), against +1.24 % with the signal (106 cases, strength 0.134, t 1.96). So staying in the profile without the rise loses.
