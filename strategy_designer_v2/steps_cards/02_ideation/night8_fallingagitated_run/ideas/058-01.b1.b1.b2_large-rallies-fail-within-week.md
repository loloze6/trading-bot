Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's 3-day rise is against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger the fall in its price over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader would short a large scaled rally in a falling, agitated market at once and expect it to start giving back within the week, while small rallies give no edge over that span.
Why:
- F4: The grading also shows at 5 days: slope 0.0043, t 2.19, 58 cases. The 5-day unusual fall is 6.7 % and 5.7 % in the two largest forecast quarters, against 0.5 % and 0.4 % in the two smallest. So the large rises start giving back at once and the small ones do not.
- F2: Across forecast quartiles at 15 days, the unusual fall is 13.0 % for the largest quarter, 12.2 %, 11.0 % and 9.1 % for the smallest (12 to 16 cases each). On the scaled rise itself the four quarters give 9.3 %, 12.1 %, 16.0 % and 12.7 %, with a slope t of 0.41 on 42 cases. The small rises give back nearly as much as the large ones, which is why the 15-day slope is unclear.
