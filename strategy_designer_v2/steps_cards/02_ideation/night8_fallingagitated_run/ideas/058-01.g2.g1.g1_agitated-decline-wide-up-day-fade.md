Idea: In a falling, agitated market, an up day is followed by a fall over the next day, and the wider the day's range from low to high, measured against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger that next-day fall.
Consequence: In an agitated decline a trader would short up days for one day, and short more the wider the up day's swing against the coin's usual swing, standing aside on narrow up days where the squeezes that cost the book most happen.
Why:
- F2: On in-profile up days the whole range predicts the fall, not just the part above the close. In a regression of the next day's return on the upper wick, the rest of the range and the day's return (all scaled by the 30-day swing), the part of the range below the close has beta -0.0215 (t -3.38). That is as large as the upper wick's -0.0213 (t -3.00). The day's own scaled return has beta +0.020 (t +3.02).
- F3: Sorted by range alone, in-profile up days show the same grading. The scaled range has a Spearman rho of -0.137 (t about -3.05, 500 cases) with the next day's unusual return. Its quintiles run from -0.42 % and +0.10 % in the bottom two to -1.26 %, -1.59 % and -1.80 % (t -2.28, -2.75, -2.89) in the top three. Out of the profile the range on up days carries nothing (rho -0.008, t -0.44).
- F5: The book's losses come from short squeezes after narrow up days, and its gains from wide ones.
- F1: The idea's effect lives in the falling agitated profile only. On in-profile up days, the scaled close-to-high distance has a Spearman rho of -0.147 with the next day's unusual return (t about -3.3, 500 cases). The top quartile is followed by -2.24 % (day-clustered t -2.59, 63 days) against -0.47 % for the bottom quartile. Out of the profile the same measure on up days has a rho of +0.019 (t 0.98, 2706 cases). In falling but not agitated markets the top quartile is followed by only -0.70 % (t -0.98).
