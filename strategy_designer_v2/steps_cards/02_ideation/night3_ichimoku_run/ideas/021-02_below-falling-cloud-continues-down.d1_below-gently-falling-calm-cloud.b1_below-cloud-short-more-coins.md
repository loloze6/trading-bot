Idea: When a coin closes below its Ichimoku cloud and the cloud is falling, its return over the next 26 days is negative, and more negative the slower the cloud has fallen over the past 26 days and the calmer the coin's daily moves have been over the past 30 days.
Consequence: A trader would short a coin below a falling cloud mainly when the cloud's decline and the coin's swings are mild, and size down as either grows.
Why:
- F1: The tested short is on the side the idea predicts but too weak and too scattered to clear the bar. On the plan's 156 non-overlapping 26-day cases, my unweighted drift-adjusted short gain was +1.5 % (t 0.69, 59 % of cases positive), against step 5's weighted +2.8 % (t 1.15). Four of six coins were positive (BTC +3.1 %, ETH +3.2 %, LTC +5.6 %, ZEC +3.4 %) and two negative (XMR −4.4 %, XRP −4.3 %), with no coin reaching t 1.3 on 20 to 32 cases.
- F2: The market's own year drowns the effect. The drift-adjusted short gain was +20.0 % in 2018 (t 4.86, 34 cases), +5.5 % in 2019 (t 2.44, 30 cases) and +9.1 % in 2025 (t 1.92, 14 cases). It was −32.8 % in 2020 (t −3.00, 7 cases), −35.3 % in 2021 (t −2.91, 12 cases) and −10.9 % in 2023 (t −2.15, 11 cases).
- F5: The combined forecast still grades in the idea's direction. Its smallest quarter gained −3.7 % and its largest +7.4 % (t 1.94), with a Spearman correlation of |forecast| with gain of +0.20. This grading rests on the cloud's half (F3).
- F7: More data is available than the test used. Thirteen more coins have daily bars: in folds A and D, and partly in B and C. The test ran on the six default coins only.
