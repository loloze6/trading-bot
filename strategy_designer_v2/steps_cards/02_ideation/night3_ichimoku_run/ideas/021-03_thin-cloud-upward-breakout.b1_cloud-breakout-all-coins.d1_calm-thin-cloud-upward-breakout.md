Idea: After a daily close that crosses up from below a coin's Ichimoku cloud to above it, the coin rises over the next 26 days, and the thinner the cloud at the cross, measured as its width relative to price, the larger the rise, and the calmer the coin's daily moves over the 30 days before the cross, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader would weight an upward cloud breakout by how quiet the coin was before it and refuse breakouts that come out of turbulent, falling markets, where this book took its deepest fall.
Why:
- F1: The Calmar of 0.60 fails because of one fall of 31.8 %, which runs from w1 (day 87) through all of w2 to w3 (day 68), in a stretch where holding the coins lost 31.7 %, 38.5 % and 19.6 % in those three windows. The book lost only 7.9 %, 6.3 % and 11.5 % there, so it falls less than holding, but its yearly return of about 19 % is too small to carry that fall.
- F2: During the fall every coin's long bets lost: the 12 worst coins lost between 0.24 and 0.64 in summed bar gains, while holding only 6 to 40 % of the time. On days with the book more than 15 % below its peak, the book averaged −0.02 % a day against −0.09 % for holding. The loss came from bets opened inside a falling market, not from staying long through it.

- F4: The same holds per trade. By quartile of the coin's 30-day volatility at entry (median 41 %, 62 %, 77 % and 107 % a year), mean net trade returns were 17.3 %, 12.1 %, 1.2 % and 3.5 % over 1,130 trades. The bets held through the deep fall had a higher entry volatility (median 79 %) than all held bets (70 %).
- F6: The idea's move is far above the cost of a trade: the total move per case is 7.91 %, 35 times the 0.22 % round trip, and the unusual move is 2.32 %, 10 times it. The book fails on its falls, not on its costs, which agrees with findings F10 and F11 from earlier runs for horizons of several days.
