Idea: After a daily close that crosses down from above a coin's Ichimoku cloud to below it, the coin falls over the next 26 days, only while its 26-day return is already a fall, and the deeper that 26-day fall at the cross, the larger the fall that follows.
Consequence: A trader would short a downward cloud break only when it confirms an ongoing slide, and would refuse the shallow breaks that tend to rebound with the coin's drift.
Why:
- F1: The book loses mainly because it is short while the coins rise. Its per-bar position gain is -0.0283 %, and -0.0241 % of that comes from the market's own rise against the short; timing adds only -0.0042 %. Over its 213 first-in-cell cases the short's total move is -3.97 % per 26 days against an unusual move of +0.46 %.
- F2: The cloud's thinness carries nothing. The step-5 check gives a thin-minus-thick Welch t of -0.36 over 198 cases, with an unusual move of -0.19 % (strength -0.020), so neither the main measure nor the grading the forecast is sized by is in the data.
- F3: Among the book's cases, the short's unusual move depends on how far the coin had already fallen. After a 26-day fall deeper than about -20 % it is +4.4 % (t 1.26, 50 cases), and the total move is +0.3 %. After a 26-day return above about -6 % it is -4.9 % (t -1.04), and the total move is -9.0 %. The 10-day return grades the same way: below about -13 %, +6.0 % (t 1.64, 58 cases); above about -6 %, -3.4 %.
- F8: Below the cloud, the coin falls further when it is already falling. With a 26-day return under about -5.6 %, the unusual move is -8.9 % (t -2.08, 20 cases) and -16.7 % (t -2.54, 19 cases). With a 26-day return above that, it is +4.0 % (total +11.2 %, 20 cases).
