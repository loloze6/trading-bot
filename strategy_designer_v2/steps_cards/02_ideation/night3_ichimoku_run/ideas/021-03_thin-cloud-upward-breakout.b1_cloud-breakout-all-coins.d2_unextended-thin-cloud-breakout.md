Idea: After a daily close that crosses up from below a coin's Ichimoku cloud to above it, the coin rises over the next 26 days, and the thinner the cloud at the cross, measured as its width relative to price, the larger the rise, and the smaller the coin's 26-day return into the cross, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader would favour cloud breakouts that start from a flat base over those that come at the end of a sharp run-up, which give back most of their move.
Why:
- F5: Crosses that come after a long run-up pay less:
- F6: The idea's move is far above the cost of a trade: the total move per case is 7.91 %, 35 times the 0.22 % round trip, and the unusual move is 2.32 %, 10 times it. The book fails on its falls, not on its costs, which agrees with findings F10 and F11 from earlier runs for horizons of several days.
