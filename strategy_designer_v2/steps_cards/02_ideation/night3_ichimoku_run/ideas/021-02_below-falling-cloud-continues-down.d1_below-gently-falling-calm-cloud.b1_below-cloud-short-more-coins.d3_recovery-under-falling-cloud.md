Idea: When a coin closes below its falling Ichimoku cloud, its return over the next 26 days is higher the higher its own 26-day return.
Consequence: A trader would buy a coin that is climbing back toward a falling cloud from below, sizing up with the strength of its 26-day rise.
Why:
- F1: The book's loss comes from shorting coins that kept rising. On the 26-day grid, coins below a falling cloud gained +3.90 % over the next 26 days (t 2.49, 353 cases), and the 995 short trades lost a mean of -4.49 % net of fees, with only 37 % winners and a book mean daily return of -0.000067 against the coins' +0.001409.
- F3: The book earns only in windows where the coins fell and loses where they rose. Summed daily returns show w14 at book +0.121 against coin -0.522, but w16 at -0.093 against +0.561, w4 at -0.079 against +0.510, and w11 at -0.031 against +2.265. The shorts' forward return was -16.0 % in 2018 and -9.9 % in 2025, but +23.9 % in 2020, +36.4 % in 2021 and +25.9 % in 2023.
- F6: A coin below a falling cloud that has itself risen over the past 26 days rises strongly next. The top quartile of past 26-day return (mean +3.3 %) gained +14.3 % over the next 26 days, against -0.5 % to -6.0 % in the other quartiles; the Spearman correlation of past 26-day return with the forward return is +0.198.
