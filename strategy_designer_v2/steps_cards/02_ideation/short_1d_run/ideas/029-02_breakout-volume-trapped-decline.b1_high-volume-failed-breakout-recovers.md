Idea: After a daily close above the prior 20-day high is followed within 3 days by a close back below that high, while the coin's 60-day return is positive, the coin rises over the next 10 days. The higher the breakout day's volume against its 20-day average volume, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A high-volume breakout that slips back under the old high in a rising coin is a pause before a second break higher, a reason to refuse the short and to look for a long; the move (about 4 % to 7 % over 10 days) is many times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
Why:
- F1: The main measure falls on the side opposite the idea: after failed breakouts the short loses 0.77 % per case against the coin's drift over 5 days (t -1.03, 335 cases), and 1.36 % in total with the drift included (t -1.81), so the coin rises more than usual rather than falling.
- F2: The volume grading is reversed and is the clearest effect in the data: the slope of the unusual short move on the breakout-volume ratio is -0.0227 over 5 days (t -2.84, 335 cases) and -0.0405 over 10 days (t -3.38, 321 cases). The high-volume tercile loses 6.39 % on the short over 10 days (t -2.31, 108 cases), while the low tercile gains 0.47 % (t 0.37).
- F3: The opposite move grows with the horizon past the plan's 5 days: the unusual short move is -0.47 % at 1 day (t -1.74), -0.77 % at 5 days (t -1.03), -2.32 % at 10 days (t -2.04, 321 cases) and -3.17 % at 20 days (t -1.56, 290 cases).
- F4: The rise after failed breakouts sits mostly in coins whose 60-day return is positive: their unusual short move over 10 days is -4.27 % (t -2.94, 123 cases) against -1.11 % (t -0.69, 198 cases) in falling coins, and -7.08 % (t -3.02, 52 cases) when the breakout volume ratio is also in its high tercile.
- F5: Failures that close back below the old high but stay above the breakout bar's low are followed by the rise: the short loses 4.05 % over 10 days (t -2.50, 193 cases). Failures that break the breakout bar's low show nothing (-0.17 %, t -0.08, 58 cases).
