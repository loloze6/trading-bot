Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, when a coin closes near the top of that day's high-low range on an up day that follows a fall over the previous three days, its price falls over the next five days, and the smaller the day's gain against its usual daily swing, the larger the fall.
Consequence: A trader shorts a quiet strong close that comes after a short pullback in a choppy, agitated market, where the fade is largest.
Why:
- F4: A strong close after a short pullback is a stronger case of the same situation, and the pullback alone does not earn. In the profile, signals after a 3-day fall gave +7.8 % over 5 days (t 4.16, 29 cases), while 3-day falls without the signal gave +0.9 % (t 0.50, 126 cases). Signals after a 5-day fall gave +6.1 % (t 3.64), against +1.0 % after a 5-day rise (t 1.00), and 5-day falls without the signal gave +0.5 % (t 0.27).
- F2: The effect sits almost entirely in the smaller gains, which is the idea's own grading. Signals whose gain was under one usual daily swing gave an unusual 5-day fall of +5.5 % (t 3.84, 39 cases on 37 days), against +0.8 % for gains of one usual swing or more (t 0.46, 36 cases). The book's 44 trades on smaller gains averaged +1.92 % net (sum +84.6 %), while its 5 trades on larger gains averaged -3.08 % (sum -15.4 %).
- F5: Agitation carries the effect, as the brief's theory says. The same signal in choppy calm markets gave -1.4 % over 5 days (t -0.99, 105 cases), and outside the profile it gave +0.1 % (t 0.45, 1265 cases). Outside the profile, only the subset after a 5-day fall with a gain under one usual swing still shows +1.9 % (t 2.17, 286 cases on 163 days).
