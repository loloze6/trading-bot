Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, when a coin's daily close sits in the upper half of that day's high-low range after its 5-day return has been negative, its price falls over the next five days for as long as its 60-day trend stays choppy, the fall growing with the depth of that 5-day fall.
Consequence: A trader holds the short only while the coin stays in a choppy market and covers once its two-month trend turns to falling, avoiding the post-crash rebounds that made the book's deepest falls.
Why:
- F1: The book's losses come from shorts held after the situation and the profile had ended, not from the signal itself. Held bars in the `mean_reversion` regime earned +1.64 % a bar (121 bars, summed about +1.99). Held bars in `chop` (172 bars, −0.12 % a bar) and `unknown` (95 bars, −0.15 % a bar) gave back about −0.35 of that.
- F2: The deepest falls behind the failed Calmar (0.43) and the Sharpe below holding's (−0.25) are rebound days after crashes. The worst book days were −5.5 % in w16 (coins +18.4 % that day), −4.1 % in w15 (coins +8.3 %) and −2.2 % in w18 (coins +22.7 %). The worst held bars were mostly `chop` bars 1 to 3 days into a short, after a 5-day fall of 42 to 53 %, a 20-day fall of 50 to 60 %, a day's fall of 14 to 41 % and a close near the low (location 0.02 to 0.54). The next day the coin rose 16 to 31 %: by then the coin had left the choppy market for a falling one, yet the 5-bar minimum holding kept the short open.
