Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, when a coin's daily close sits in the upper half of that day's high-low range after its 5-day return has been negative, its price falls over the next five days until it gives back the push in one sharp down day, and the fall grows with the depth of that 5-day fall.
Consequence: A trader covers the short after the first sharp one-day fall inside the five days, taking the given-back push and leaving the rebound that follows.
Why:
- F2: The deepest falls behind the failed Calmar (0.43) and the Sharpe below holding's (−0.25) are rebound days after crashes. The worst book days were −5.5 % in w16 (coins +18.4 % that day), −4.1 % in w15 (coins +8.3 %) and −2.2 % in w18 (coins +22.7 %). The worst held bars were mostly `chop` bars 1 to 3 days into a short, after a 5-day fall of 42 to 53 %, a 20-day fall of 50 to 60 %, a day's fall of 14 to 41 % and a close near the low (location 0.02 to 0.54). The next day the coin rose 16 to 31 %: by then the coin had left the choppy market for a falling one, yet the 5-bar minimum holding kept the short open.
- F3: Held bars on the day after a one-day fall of more than 5 % lost −0.86 % a bar (65 bars). Held bars after a day between −5 % and 0 earned +0.78 % (126), and after a rise +0.36 % to +1.54 %. Once the push has been given back sharply, the rest of the short loses. This condition was not measured on bars without the signal.
