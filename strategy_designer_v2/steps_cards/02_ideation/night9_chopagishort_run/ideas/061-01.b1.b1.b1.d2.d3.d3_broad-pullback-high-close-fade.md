Idea: When a coin closes near the top of that day's high-low range on an up day that follows a fall over the previous five days, its price falls over the next five days, and the smaller the day's gain against its usual daily swing, the larger the fall.
Consequence: A trader takes the quiet late-buying fade after a pullback in every market, which gives the short many more occasions to trade.
Why:
- F5: Agitation carries the effect, as the brief's theory says. The same signal in choppy calm markets gave -1.4 % over 5 days (t -0.99, 105 cases), and outside the profile it gave +0.1 % (t 0.45, 1265 cases). Outside the profile, only the subset after a 5-day fall with a gain under one usual swing still shows +1.9 % (t 2.17, 286 cases on 163 days).
- F4: A strong close after a short pullback is a stronger case of the same situation, and the pullback alone does not earn. In the profile, signals after a 3-day fall gave +7.8 % over 5 days (t 4.16, 29 cases), while 3-day falls without the signal gave +0.9 % (t 0.50, 126 cases). Signals after a 5-day fall gave +6.1 % (t 3.64), against +1.0 % after a 5-day rise (t 1.00), and 5-day falls without the signal gave +0.5 % (t 0.27).
- F1: The book trades too rarely because its situation is rare. The choppy agitated profile covers 4.6 % of the coins' bars, a close in the top fifth of the range on an up day occurs on 20 % of those bars, and so the book made 49 trades in folds B and C (0.006 trades per day against the 0.01 required) and was in a position on 2 % of coin-days.
