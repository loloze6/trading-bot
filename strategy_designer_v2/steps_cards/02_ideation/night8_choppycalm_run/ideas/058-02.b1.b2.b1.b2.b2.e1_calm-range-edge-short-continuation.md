Idea: In a calm market, the nearer a coin's close sits to the top of its 20-day high-low range, the larger its rise over the next 2 days, and the nearer to the bottom, the larger its fall.
Consequence: A trader in a calm coin can lean with a close near its 20-day edge on a short hold, where the move per day is as reliable as over a week and the cases are more numerous.
Why:
- F3: In the calm profile, the next 5 days follow the close's place in its 20-day range on the continuation side, rising and graded at the edges: the mean excess 5-day return is −2.41 % (t −3.14, 163 cases) for the bottom fifth of the range, −2.38 % (t −3.78, 193) for the next, −0.17 % (t −0.24, 173) at the middle, +0.68 % (t 1.13, 198) and +4.40 % (t 4.40, 177) at the top edge, so the brief's reversion is not there and its mirror, continuation, is.
- F4: The effect is as clear at shorter horizons as at the plan's 5 days, with more independent cases: the forecast-signed excess return is +0.30 % over 1 day (t 4.80, 5416 non-overlapping cases), +0.91 % over 3 days (t 4.77, 1776), +1.44 % over 5 days (t 4.24, 1056), +2.29 % over 10 days (t 3.48, 518) and then flattens at +2.44 % over 20 days (t 1.72, 250); these t are not clustered by day, unlike step 5's 1.71.
- F5: The edge continuation is stronger in calm bars than in the other bars of the same coins: the top-edge excess 5-day return is +4.40 % in calm against +1.78 % otherwise, and the bottom edge −2.41 % in calm against +0.28 % otherwise.
- F7: The signed 5-day effect points the same way in every year seen: 2018 +2.72 % (t 3.70, 203), 2019 +1.80 % (t 2.96, 227), 2020 +2.66 % (t 3.75, 208), 2021 +1.35 % (t 2.08, 266).
