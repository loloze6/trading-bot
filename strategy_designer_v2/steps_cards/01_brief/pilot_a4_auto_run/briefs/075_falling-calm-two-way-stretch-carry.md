In a falling, calm market, a coin whose close stands far from its recent average keeps moving the same way over the following days. The distance is measured against the coin's usual daily swing. A close well above the average is followed by more rise, and one well below it by more fall. The further the stretch, the larger the carry.

The behaviour lives in a falling, calm market because there the flow that moves price is mostly unforced. With swings quiet, few holders are being liquidated or squeezed, so a large stretch is not an overshoot that liquidity providers will be repaid for.

The reason is ours; the source gives none. A large stretch in a quiet decline more likely reflects deliberate repositioning that is worked in pieces over days. Below the average, that is underwater holders exiting slowly. Above it, it is shorts and underweight holders rebuilding exposure as the decline loses hold, while trend followers and attention-led buyers arrive only after the move is visible. The upside half runs against the common view that bounces in a downtrend are sold into. It may also be the weaker half.

The theory is wrong if, in falling, calm markets, closes far above their recent average are followed by flat or falling prices over the next days. It is also wrong if closes far below are followed by flat or rising prices. It is wrong too if the later move does not grow with the size of the stretch, or if it reverses the stretch instead of extending it.
