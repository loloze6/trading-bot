In any market, rising, choppy or falling, a day that closes up but still sits far below the coin's recent high is followed by a fall the next day. The fall grows with how deep the close sits below that high, measured in the coin's own usual daily swing. The effect belongs to up days only. After a down day, the same depth says nothing about the next day.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. Holders who bought near the recent high are under water. Many of them wait for relief to cut part of their loss, and an up day gives it to them. Measuring the depth in swings gauges how unusual that loss is for this coin. A deeper loss means more holders past their pain point and more supply waiting above the price.

The up day's buying is often short covering or bargain hunting. That flow is spent within the day. The waiting supply then meets the next session without that support. A down day offers these holders no relief, so it releases no such selling, and depth alone carries nothing.

The theory is wrong if any of these holds:
- Up days closing deep below the recent high, in swing units, do no worse the next day than shallow up days.
- The fade does not grow with depth.
- It appears only in one trend rather than across trends.
- Down days at equal depth fade just as much, which would point to depth itself rather than relief selling.
