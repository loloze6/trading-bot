A large one-day fall is followed by a next-day rise only when the day's high-to-low range was unusually wide against the coin's own recent ranges. The rebound grows with that width. A fall of the same size on a narrow day is followed by no rebound at all.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. A wide range on a falling day shows that price travelled far within the day, beyond what the closing loss alone shows. That points to a book being swept: stops triggered, leveraged longs liquidated, and market sell orders walking down through thin bids. Sellers forced to trade at any price push the price below where willing holders value the coin. Once that forced flow ends, liquidity providers and patient buyers who absorbed it are paid by a partial recovery the next day.

A fall of the same size within a narrow range came down in an orderly way, without sweeping the book. That is more like a steady repricing by sellers acting on information than a forced overshoot, so there is nothing excess to give back. On this view, width is the mark that separates forced selling from informed selling. The size of the fall alone does not.

The theory is wrong if large falls on narrow days rebound as much as large falls on wide days. It is also wrong if the next-day rise after large falls does not grow with the day's width against recent ranges. Finally, it is wrong if large falls on wide days are followed by further decline rather than a rise.
