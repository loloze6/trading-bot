In a calm market, a single day whose move is large against the coin's own recent daily swing marks the start of a move, not noise. The coin keeps moving the same way over the following days, up or down, and more so the larger that day was in swing units. The effect lives in calm markets only. In an agitated market the same day carries no such follow-through.

The source states the pattern and its restriction to calm markets but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. When swings are quiet, little forced flow is moving the price: few liquidations, few margin calls, few stops. A day that stands out against that quiet is therefore more likely driven by new information or a deliberate repositioning by a large holder. Such flow is worked over several days rather than finished in one, and other participants who size by volatility or wait for confirmation join only after the move shows. So the price keeps adjusting in the direction of the first day.

In an agitated market, large days are common and are mostly forced, two-sided flow. Their overshoot is repaid rather than extended, so any continuation is drowned out.

The theory is wrong if, in calm markets, days large against the coin's usual swing are followed by no move, or by a reversal, over the next days. It is also wrong if the follow-through does not grow with the size of the day, or if it is just as strong in agitated markets as in calm ones.
