In a choppy, calm market, a coin that has fallen over several days by a lot against its own usual daily swing keeps falling over the following days. The larger the fall in swing units, the larger the further fall.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In a calm market, holders who size positions to recent volatility carry their largest exposure, because the swings they measure are small. With no 60-day trend, there is no momentum crowd waiting to buy dips. Range traders see no stretched extreme worth fading either, since the fall starts from the middle of a flat range rather than from a trend's end.

A fall that is large for the coin's quiet swing does two things. It pushes the swing these holders measure upward, and it breaches the loss limits they set against small expected moves. Both force them to cut exposure. They do it in pieces over the following days, because their measures update gradually and large holders split their orders. Nothing forced happened in a single day to draw liquidity providers, as a panic would. So the selling is not met by buyers paid to absorb it, and it carries on rather than rebounding.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy calm markets, larger swing-scaled several-day falls are followed by rebounds or by no further fall. It is also wrong if the further fall does not grow with the size of the prior fall.
