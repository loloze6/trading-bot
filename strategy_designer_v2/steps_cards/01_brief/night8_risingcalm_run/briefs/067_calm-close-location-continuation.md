In a calm market, where a coin's daily close sits within that day's high-low range points the way of its next several days. A close near the low is followed by weaker returns, a close near the high by stronger ones, and the effect is graded across the range rather than confined to the extremes. It adds to what the day's net move already says. This is the reverse of the next-day snap-back seen after low closes in agitated markets.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In a calm market few traders are forced to act: there are no liquidation cascades, no margin calls and no stretched liquidity providers waiting to be repaid. So a close pinned at one end of the range is more likely to show a patient party, informed or rebalancing, that kept pressing into the close. That party has more to do, and it works its order over following days instead of all at once. Liquidity providers are not overloaded and offer no discount to buy back, so there is no overshoot to revert. What remains is the slow drift of an order still being filled. In agitated markets the same close location instead marks forced, information-free flow that overshoots, which is why the sign flips there.

The theory is wrong if, in calm markets, a close's place in its range carries no graded relation to the following days, if low closes are followed by rebounds as in agitated markets, or if the relation disappears once the day's net move is known.
