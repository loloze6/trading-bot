In a choppy, agitated market, a coin's move over the past week tends to be partly given back over the following week. The operator's reason is that with no trend to carry it, a large swing is noise rather than news. I add a fuller account of who trades and why.

When a coin has gone nowhere over two months but swings more than usual for it, its large weekly moves come mostly from flows that demand liquidity without carrying lasting information. These include leveraged positions being forced out, stops clustered near recent extremes, and short-lived chasing after headlines. In a trending market, such a move is joined by trend followers and by holders who change their view, so it can persist. In a trendless market few traders follow the move. Range traders and liquidity providers take the other side, and they are paid as the price drifts back once the forced or impatient flow has passed. High agitation matters because it makes these noisy swings larger and more frequent. That gives the reversal more room than in a calm trendless market, where weekly moves are small and closer to random drift.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy, agitated conditions, a coin's past-week move is followed by a next-week move in the same direction, or by no move related to it. It is also wrong if the giving back is no stronger there than in rising or falling markets or in calm choppy ones. That would mean the reversal does not live in this profile.
