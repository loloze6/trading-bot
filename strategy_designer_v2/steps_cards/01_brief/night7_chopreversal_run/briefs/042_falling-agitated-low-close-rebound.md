In a falling, agitated market, where a coin closes within its own day's high-low range tells something about the next day. The lower the close sits in the range, the larger the coin's rise over the following day. A close at the very bottom of the range points to the strongest rebound. The effect lives in this profile only. Outside it, the place of the close says little.

The source reports the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In a coin that has fallen for two months and is swinging more than usual, many holders are leveraged longs or holders near their pain limit. A close pinned at the day's low marks selling that kept pressing into the end of the day. That selling comes from margin calls, stop orders and holders who give up. It is driven by need, not by news. The buyers who absorb it are few and stretched in such a market. They take the other side only at a discount, and they are repaid when the forced flow stops and price drifts back up the next day.

In a calm market, or one without a falling trend, a low close is more often plain information or ordinary flow. So it carries no such discount.

The theory is wrong if, in falling, agitated markets, next-day returns do not rise as the close moves lower in the day's range. It is also wrong if low closes outside this profile rebound just as much as low closes inside it.
