Where a coin closes in its day's high-low range points the opposite way for the next day, and the wider the day is against the coin's usual daily range, the stronger that reversal. A close near the low of a wide day is followed by a rise next day. A close near the high of a wide day is followed by a fall. On a day of ordinary width, the close's place in the range says little. This holds in any kind of market. The source states the pattern but gives no reason for it.

My reason is about who is stretched. A day far wider than usual is a day when one side's flow overwhelmed the liquidity on offer. That flow comes from stops, liquidations and urgent orders. A close pinned at the day's extreme shows that this pressure was still running into the close, and that market makers and absorbing traders ended the day holding inventory taken at a concession. On a normal-width day, the same close location is mostly noise. On a wide day, it marks the stretched side. The next day, the forced flow is over and fresh capital arrives. Those holding the inventory are repaid as the price moves back.

The theory is wrong if, across a coin's days, the next-day move does not lean against the close location. It is also wrong if that lean does not grow with the day's width against usual, or if it is as strong on narrow days as on wide ones. Continuation in the direction of a wide day's extreme close would also prove it wrong.
