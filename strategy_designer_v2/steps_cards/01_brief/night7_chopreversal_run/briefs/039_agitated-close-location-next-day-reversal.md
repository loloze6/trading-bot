In an agitated market, where a coin closes within its day's high-low range points the next day's move the other way, and the effect is graded. The lower the close sits in the range, the stronger the coin's next day. The higher the close sits, the weaker the next day. The close location carries this information beyond the day's net change. The effect lives in agitated markets and is weak or absent in calm ones.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason below is mine.

When swings are larger than usual for the coin, much of the trading near the end of the day is forced or impatient rather than informed:
- Margin calls, stop-outs and de-risking push the price down into the close.
- Short covering and chasing push it up into the close.

A close pinned at the day's low is the mark of selling that ran out of buyers at the last moment. A close pinned at the high is the mark of the same thing on the buying side. Liquidity providers who took the other side are already stretched in agitated markets. They demand a larger concession, and they are paid when the pressure stops and the price settles back the next day.

In calm markets, the flow is more patient and less forced, so the close location says less about pressure that will undo itself.

The theory would be shown wrong if any of these held:
- In agitated markets, the next-day return does not fall as the close moves up the range.
- The next-day return rises as the close moves up the range, meaning late-day pressure continues instead of reverting.
- The relation is just as strong in calm markets as in agitated ones.
