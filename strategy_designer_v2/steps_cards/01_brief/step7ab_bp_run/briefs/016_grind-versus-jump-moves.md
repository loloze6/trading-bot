A multi-day price move tells you more about the coming days through the way it was made than through its size. Some moves are a grind: many small hourly steps in the same direction, with modest volume spread evenly across the hours. These tend to keep going for several more days. Other moves of the same size come from one or a few violent hours with a burst of volume. These tend to give back part of the move over the following days.

The reason is my own and is not from a source. A grind is what a large, patient trader leaves behind when splitting a big order across many hours and days to hide it and limit its price impact. Such an order is rarely finished when the drift first becomes visible, so the pressure that made the move is still there. A jump is usually forced flow: stop runs, liquidations of leveraged positions, or a crowd reacting to a headline all at once. Forced flow pushes price past where willing holders value the coin. Once it is spent, nobody is left to keep pushing, and liquidity providers who absorbed it lean against the move.

Because both effects play out over days, they are large next to a round-trip cost.

The theory is wrong if grinding moves do not continue more than jump-made moves of similar size over the following days. It is also wrong if jump-made moves do not partly reverse, or if the way a move was built adds nothing beyond its size and direction.
