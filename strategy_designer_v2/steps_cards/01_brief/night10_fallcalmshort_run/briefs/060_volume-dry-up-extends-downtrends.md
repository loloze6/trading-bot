In a falling market, calm or agitated, a coin keeps falling over the following days, and it falls further the more its trading volume over the past month has shrunk against the month before. Growing volume in the same falling market does not bring this continuation; it may mark the point where the fall stalls.

The pattern and its place come from the source. The source gives no reason, so the reason here is mine. A downtrend that goes on while volume dries up is one in which the buyers have stepped away. Dip-buyers and liquidity providers have stopped absorbing, so the holders who are still underwater can sell in small pieces, and each piece moves the price down a little in a thin market. Nothing in that selling is forced or sudden enough to exhaust it, so the supply is not cleared and keeps coming. When volume grows in a fall, someone is taking the other side. That can be capitulation that clears the sellers, or new buyers who find the price attractive. Either way, the slow one-sided drift is broken.

The theory is wrong if, in falling markets, shrinking volume is not followed by further decline. It is also wrong if the decline does not grow with the degree of shrinkage. Finally, it is wrong if growing volume is followed by the same continuation as shrinking volume, which would mean the fall alone carries the effect and not the dry-up.
