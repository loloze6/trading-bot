In a falling market, calm or agitated alike, the first up day after a run of down days is followed by a fall the next day. The longer the run of down days that the up day ends, the larger that fall. The effect is not expected in a rising market.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In a coin whose trend has been down for weeks, each down day of a run leaves another group of holders underwater. These holders have watched the price slip day after day without a chance to get out. The first up day is the first exit they are offered, and many of them sell into it. That pushes the price back down the next day. A longer run creates more such groups and a deeper sense of being trapped, so more supply is waiting at the first relief.

The selling does not depend on whether the market is calm or agitated. It comes from holders choosing to exit, not from forced liquidation, so it should appear in both. In a rising market, the coin's holders mostly sit on gains. Dip-buyers there treat an up day after a short slide as the trend resuming, so no waiting supply meets the rise.

The theory is wrong if, in falling markets, the day after a run-ending up day is no weaker than other days. It is also wrong if that weakness does not grow with the length of the run. Finally, it is wrong if the same fall appears just as strongly in rising markets, since that would point away from trapped holders in a downtrend.
