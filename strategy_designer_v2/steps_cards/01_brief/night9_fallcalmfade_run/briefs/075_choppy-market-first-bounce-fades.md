In a choppy market, the first up day after a run of down days tends to be followed by a fall in the coin's price over the next several days. The deeper the run's fall, measured against the coin's usual daily swing of the past month, the larger that later fall.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason, so the reason here is mine. In a market with no trend for two months, many holders bought somewhere inside the range. A deep slide leaves them underwater with no trend to promise a recovery. The first up day is their first chance to cut the loss at a better price, so they sell into it. The deeper the slide, the more holders are caught and the more supply waits above. Trend followers, who in a rising market would buy the bounce as the uptrend resuming, have no trend to follow here. Range traders who bought the low may also take quick profits on the bounce. Neither group absorbs the supply, so the bounce is sold back over the following days.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy markets, the days after the first up day following a down run fall no more than ordinary choppy days do. It is also wrong if that later fall does not grow with the depth of the run in usual swings, or if the bounce holds or extends.
