In a falling, calm market, a coin's one-day rise that is large against its own recent daily swing is given back over the next few days. The larger the rise in those swing units, the more is given back.

The source shows the fade at both agitation levels of a falling market. The general falling-market version is already indexed (034_squeezed-rallies-fade-in-falling-markets), so this brief keeps the calm case, which the source states on its own.

The reason is mine, not the source's, and I am not sure of it. In a calm decline, sellers are not forced. Holders who want out work their orders slowly and step aside while nothing happens. A sudden large up day is then not short covering or a squeeze, since a calm market has little crowded leverage to squeeze. It is more likely one burst of buying flow, from a single large buyer or a short-lived piece of news, that liquidity providers fill from their own inventory at a rising price. Nothing in a calm downtrend draws a second wave of buyers after it. The patient sellers who paused see a better price and resume. The liquidity providers rebuild their inventory by leaning against the move. So price drifts back toward where the steady selling had it, and the bigger the burst, the more there is to give back.

The theory is wrong if, in falling calm markets, large scaled up days are followed by flat or further rising prices over the next days. It is also wrong if the giveback does not grow with the rise's size in swing units.
