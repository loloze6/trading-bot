In a choppy, agitated market, where a coin closes within its day's range points the other way for the next day. A close high in the range is followed by a weaker day, a close low in the range by a stronger one, and the further the close sits from the middle, the larger the move back. Both sides belong to one effect, graded by close location.

The reason is mine; the source gives none. A choppy, agitated coin swings widely but has gone nowhere for two months. The late-day pushes that pin a close near an extreme are mostly urgent flow: stop runs, liquidations, squeezes and hurried hedges. They carry little lasting information. In a rising or falling market, trend followers and holders who are already positioned may take the push as confirmation and carry it on. In a trendless market few traders do that. The liquidity providers and range traders who took the other side of the late push are paid when price drifts back the next day. Because swings are larger than usual for the coin, these pushes overshoot further, so the reversal shows more clearly here than in calm or trending markets.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy, agitated markets, close location shows no next-day relation to the move. It is also wrong if high closes are followed by strength and low closes by weakness, if the next-day move does not grow as the close nears the extreme, or if the effect is no clearer in this profile than in the others.
