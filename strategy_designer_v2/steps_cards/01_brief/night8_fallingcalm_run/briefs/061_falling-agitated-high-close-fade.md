In a falling, agitated market, the higher a coin closes within its day's high-low range, the more it falls back over the next day. A close pressed at the day's high there is a false sign of recovery, not the start of one. Outside that market, in calm or rising conditions, the same high close does not fade and may carry on.

The source gives no reason; this one is mine. In a falling, agitated coin, many traders are short and heavily levered, and the swings are wide enough to push them past their limits. A rise late in the day squeezes them: stops fire and margin calls force them to buy, and that forced buying pins the close near the high. It is buying by people who must cover, not by new holders who want the coin. Once the covering is done, the downtrend's sellers return. Underwater holders also sell into the strength, and nothing is left to hold the price, so it falls back.

In a calm or rising market, fewer shorts are stretched. A high close there more often shows real demand that lasts.

The theory is wrong if, in falling agitated markets, closes near the day's high are followed by no fall or by a rise the next day. It is also wrong if the fall does not grow as the close moves toward the high, or if the fade is no stronger there than in calm or rising markets.
