A coin's one-day fall, measured against its own usual daily move, is followed by a rise the next day only when the fall came on volume heavy for that coin. The heavier the volume and the deeper the fall, the larger the rise, and the two reinforce each other. A fall of the same size on ordinary volume is followed by no rise.

The source gives the pattern without a reason. The reason below is mine. Heavy volume on a deep down day marks sellers who had to sell now: stop-losses firing, margin calls, liquidations, holders cutting risk all at once. Their urgency lets them push price below what patient buyers think the coin is worth. Market makers and dip-buyers take the other side and are paid a discount, which they recover once the forced selling is spent. A deep fall on ordinary volume is different. It points to fewer, unhurried sellers or to a real change in what the coin is worth, so there is no overshoot to repay.

For the same reason I expect the rebound to be weak in a calm downtrend. There, heavy volume is more likely to be orderly selling by holders leaving than a forced flush. The theory names no profile.

The theory is wrong if any of these hold:
- Deep falls on heavy volume are followed by next-day returns no better than equally deep falls on ordinary volume.
- The next-day rise does not grow with volume against the coin's usual volume.
- Heavy-volume falls keep falling the next day.
