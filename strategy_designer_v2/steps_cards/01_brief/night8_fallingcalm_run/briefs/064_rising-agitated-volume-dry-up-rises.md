In a rising, agitated market, the more a coin's trading volume over the past month has shrunk against the month before, the more it rises over the following days. When that volume has grown instead, the coin rises less.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason below is mine. In a rising market that swings more than usual, growing volume is likely to mark a crowd arriving: late chasers and leveraged buyers, drawn in by the swings, who have already paid for their position. Their buying is spent, and their stops and margin make the coin fragile, so the rise that follows is smaller. Shrinking volume in the same market means the trend and its wide swings are carried by few participants. Positioning stays light, sellers who wanted out have mostly left, and the buyers who usually join a rising coin have not yet come. That leaves room for more rise.

The behaviour should live in the agitated rising market. There, swings attract leverage and attention, so volume tells crowding apart from quiet holding. In a calm rising market, growing volume may simply be healthy new participation.

The theory is wrong if, in rising agitated markets, shrunk volume is followed by rises no larger than grown volume. It is also wrong if the relation is no different in rising agitated markets than in rising calm ones, since then it is not this mechanism but the rising-market effect already indexed.
