In a falling, calm market, a coin's decline carries on, and the more it has fallen over the past weeks, the more it keeps falling over the next days. This holds mainly when the fall came on ordinary or thin volume. When the same fall came on unusually heavy volume, it carries on much less, or not at all.

The reason is my own, not the operator's. In a calm downtrend nobody is forced to sell all at once. Holders who have lost faith exit a little at a time, and a deep fall made on quiet volume shows that most of them have not yet sold. Their remaining supply still hangs over the price, and each pause meets the next slice of it. Buyers have little cause to step in early, because nothing in the bars says the selling is done.

A deep fall on heavy volume tells a different story. Many holders have already sold in a short span, and patient buyers have taken the other side. The supply that drove the decline has been largely cleared, so the depth of the fall says less about the days ahead.

The theory is wrong if any of these show in falling, calm markets:
- deeper past-weeks declines are not followed by more falling;
- declines on heavy volume continue as much as, or more than, equally deep declines on quiet volume;
- the continuation also appears unchanged in the other market profiles, which would mean the calm downtrend is not where it lives.
