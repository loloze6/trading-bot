In an agitated market of any trend, not only a falling one, a day whose high-low range is wide against the coin's recent ranges and which closes near its high tends to be followed by a fall the next day. The fall grows both with how wide the day was and with how high in its range it closed, and the two compound: a high close on an ordinary-width day carries little of it.

The reason is mine; the source gives none. When swings are larger than usual for the coin, positions are fragile, and a wide up-push late in the day is mostly forced or reflexive buying. Shorts hit by the swing cover, stops above recent prices fire, and chasers who read the strong close as a breakout buy at the top of the range. None of this flow carries news, and it ends once it is spent. Liquidity providers who sold into it then hold short inventory at a stretched price and are repaid as price settles back. A wider day means more of this one-off flow. A higher close means more of it came in late, so the reversal falls on the next day rather than inside the same one.

The theory is wrong if wide, high-closing agitated days in rising or choppy markets do not fall the next day. It is also wrong if they fall no more than narrow high-closing days, or if the same pattern is as strong in calm markets, where forced flow should be scarce.
