When a coin has gone nowhere for weeks, moves away from the middle of its recent range tend to come back toward it over the following days. A coin far above the middle of a flat month tends to fall back, and one far below it tends to rise back.

The reason is mine. With no trend, no group of traders is building a position: buyers and sellers agree on a band of value, and a push toward one edge of the band is usually an order or a piece of news without follow-through. Traders who have watched the band hold sell near its top and buy near its bottom, which brings the price back. This only works while there is no trend; once a trend starts, the same move is the beginning of a breakout.

The theory is wrong if, in coins without a trend over the past weeks, moves away from the middle of the range are not followed by moves back toward it, or if the reversion is no larger than it is in trending coins.
