instruments: BTC, ETH, BNB

Does a divergence between price and RSI tell anything about what BTC, ETH and BNB do next? A divergence is when price makes a new high (or low) but RSI makes a weaker one. The new extreme was reached with smaller gains (or losses) per bar than the last one. This brief asks whether that fading push predicts a turn, or carries no information beyond the price path itself. It does not ask whether RSI levels alone predict anything.

The answer could be yes, for a reason I propose myself. A move that keeps going with ever smaller steps may be running out of new buyers (or sellers). The urgent participants, such as breakout chasers, stopped-out shorts and liquidated leveraged traders, have already traded, and the remaining flow is thinner. Once that one-sided demand is used up, nothing holds price at the extreme, and part of the move gives back.

The answer could also be no. RSI is bounded and smooths recent bars, so in a long, steady trend it tends to flatten while price keeps rising. Divergence may then just describe a calm trend, not a tired one. If information about these coins spreads slowly, that trend can keep going regardless of what the indicator shows. Divergences are also judged by eye on chosen swing points. What looks like a signal may be ordinary noise in bar-to-bar step size.

The theory is wrong if price after a divergence behaves no differently from price after a new high or low of similar size without one. That means no more frequent or larger give-back.
