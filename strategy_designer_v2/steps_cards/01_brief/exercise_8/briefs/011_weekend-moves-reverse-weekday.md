Price moves made over the weekend are partly undone once the working week begins. Spot crypto trades without pause, but the people who supply depth do not. Market-making desks, arbitrage funds and institutional flow run thinner on Saturday and Sunday, and banking rails that move fiat onto exchanges are closed. Order books are shallower and trading is left more to retail and automated flow. In a thin book a given amount of one-sided buying or selling moves price further than it would on a weekday, and part of that move is the price of getting liquidity, not new information about the coin's value.

When deeper liquidity returns on Monday, arbitrageurs and market makers lean against prices that drifted on little volume, and the part of the move that was only a liquidity cost fades. The reason is my own reasoning. It follows from how liquidity providers are paid for absorbing imbalance when few others are present.

The theory says weekend moves should reverse more than weekday moves of similar size. That reversal should also be stronger when weekend volume was low relative to the coin's usual volume.

The theory is wrong if weekend moves continue into the following days as much as weekday moves do, or more. It is also wrong if weekend moves reverse no more than weekday moves, or if any reversal is spread evenly over time and not concentrated around the return of weekday trading.
