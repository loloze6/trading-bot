Question: in BTC, ETH and BNB, does the side of its midpoint on which RSI has been sitting tell us the direction of the coin's next moves? The idea is that RSI stays mostly above the midpoint in an uptrend and mostly below it in a downtrend. If so, it would work as a measure of which side has been in control, and that control may carry forward.

There are reasons to expect RSI to carry information. A long stretch on one side means buyers or sellers have dominated the coin's own price changes for some time. That can happen when information reaches traders slowly. It can also happen when trend followers and chart watchers add to positions as the move becomes visible, or when leveraged traders on the losing side are forced out step by step. Each of these makes net order flow persist, so direction would continue.

There are also reasons to expect no edge, or the opposite. RSI is only a smoothed summary of recent gains and losses. It holds no information beyond past returns. In the most liquid coins, any persistent drift in past returns may be traded away quickly. Because the indicator is so widely watched, crowded positioning could make moves reverse once the crowd has bought or sold. Market makers paid to absorb one-sided flow would also push toward reversal. I do not know which force dominates for these coins.

The idea that RSI predicts direction would be shown wrong if the coin's later price changes look the same no matter which side of the midpoint RSI has held. It would also be shown wrong if those changes tend to run against that side.
