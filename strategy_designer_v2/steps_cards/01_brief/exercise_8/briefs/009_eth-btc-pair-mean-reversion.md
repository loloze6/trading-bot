Question: does the ETH/BTC spot pair, read on its own bars as a single traded market, tend to drift back toward its own recent average after moving away from it, or do its departures persist or wander with no pull back?

Reasons it could return to its average (my own reasoning, not from a source): both coins are held by much the same investors and priced off the same broad crypto flows. A large share of ETH/BTC trading is rebalancing and relative-value positioning, so desks that hold a target mix of the two sell whichever side has run ahead. Market makers quoting the pair also lean against moves that come from one-sided order flow rather than news. That would make stretches away from the average partly temporary.

Reasons it might not: ETH and BTC have different supply schedules, uses and narratives, such as staking yield, network upgrades, fund flows and changes in how each is regulated. Shifts in these are real changes in relative value, not noise, so the pair could trend for long spans and its "average" would just trail behind it. Momentum traders who rotate between the two in "alt season" stories could also extend moves rather than fade them.

What would answer it: if, after the pair moves well away from its own recent average, later moves are on balance toward that average, the reversion view holds. If later moves are as likely to extend the gap as close it, or tend to extend it, the reversion view is wrong for this pair.
