In a rising market, a coin's rise over the past few weeks tends to continue over the following couple of weeks. The continuation is stronger the larger that rise is when measured in the coin's own recent daily swings. A rise that is big for how much the coin usually moves keeps going further than a rise that is ordinary for it. The source states the pattern only in the rising profile. It reports no such grading in a falling market.

The source gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In an uptrend, attention and buying capital arrive in stages. Early buyers act on the first signs. Trend followers, momentum funds and retail chasers enter only once the rise is clear against the coin's usual noise. Holders who sold too early also buy back late. A rise that is large in swing terms is the kind these late groups notice and act on, so their buying lands after the move has already shown, and the move extends. In a falling market this staged uptake may not appear. Selling there is driven more by forced exits and bounce-hunters who trade against the move, which could explain why the source finds no grading there. I do not know which of these effects carries the most weight.

The theory is wrong if, in a rising market, the size of a coin's past few weeks' rise in its own swing units does not line up with a larger later rise. It is also wrong if larger swing-scaled rises are followed by smaller later moves or by giving part of the rise back.
