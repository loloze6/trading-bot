In a choppy market, a single day whose move is large against the coin's own recent daily swings is followed by further movement the same way over about the next week. The larger that day is in swing units, the further the coin carries. The carry is not there in the first day or two after the large day. It builds over the following days. The source states the pattern, that it belongs to the choppy market, and that it builds with the horizon. It gives no reason. The reason below is mine.

In a market with no trend, most participants expect moves to fade. Range traders sell strength and buy weakness, so a large day is first met by fading flow, which delays any follow-through. When a single day stands far outside the coin's usual swing in such a market, it often reflects real new information or a large holder repositioning. It is not noise. Traders who wait for confirmation that the range has broken, or who review positions weekly, act on it only over the following days. Their late flow pushes the price on in the same direction, and range faders who are proven wrong close out and add to it. In a rising or falling market a large day is part of flow that is already under way and already watched, so it carries less news.

The theory is wrong if, in a choppy market, large one-day moves are followed over the next week by no further move their way or by reversal. It is also wrong if the follow-through does not grow with the day's size, or if it is just as strong outside the choppy market.
