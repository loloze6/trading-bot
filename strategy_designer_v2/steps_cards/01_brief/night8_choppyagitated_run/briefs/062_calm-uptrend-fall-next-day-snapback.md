In a rising, calm market, a one-day fall that is large against the coin's usual daily swing is mostly repaid on the very next day. The larger the fall in units of that swing, the larger the next-day rebound. The effect lives in this profile and is weaker or absent in calm markets that are choppy or falling.

The source gives the pattern but no reason. The reason here is mine. In a quiet uptrend, a sharp down day rarely carries news. It is more often one hurried seller, a cluster of stops, or a leveraged long being cut, and that flow pushes through a book that is briefly thin.

The buyers who absorb it are liquidity providers and holders waiting to add to a trend they trust. Because swings are calm, their risk limits are not stretched. They can refill the book at once rather than over several days, so the discount the urgent seller paid is taken back quickly and in proportion to how far the price was pushed.

In a choppy or falling calm market, the same fall is more likely to be holders leaving for a reason. No waiting buyer treats it as a gift, so it does not snap back.

The theory is wrong if, in a rising, calm market:
- the next-day move after such falls does not grow with the fall's size in swing units;
- the rebound there is no larger than in choppy or falling calm markets;
- the recovery comes mainly after the first day rather than on it.
