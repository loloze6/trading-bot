In a falling market, the nearer a coin closes to the lowest close of its recent days, the more it keeps falling over the following days. A close in the upper part of that short range is followed by a better path than a close pinned at its low. The source states this pattern but gives no reason for it beyond saying the fall continues.

The reason here is mine. After two months of decline, many holders are underwater and have been waiting for a chance to get out. A close at a fresh short-term low shows that no buyer stepped in during the day to defend price. Each new low pushes another layer of holders past the loss they can bear. That layer includes those with stops, those on margin and those whose patience runs out, and they sell over the next days rather than all at once. Dip-buyers who would absorb this in a rising market stay away while the trend is down, so the selling meets thin demand and price keeps sliding. A close well off the low shows that demand appeared and the supply at that level is spent for now. The effect should therefore live in falling markets, where the waiting supply of underwater holders is largest.

The theory is wrong if, in falling markets, closes near the recent low are followed by moves no worse than those after closes high in that range, or by rebounds.
