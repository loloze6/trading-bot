The Ichimoku cloud, built from the midpoints of a coin's highest and lowest prices over the past weeks and months, marks a zone of equilibrium. A coin trading above a rising cloud tends to keep rising, a coin trading below a falling cloud tends to keep falling, and a close through a thin cloud is followed by a larger move than a close through a thick one.

The reason is mine. The midpoint of a range is a robust summary of where buyers and sellers have recently agreed, less sensitive than an average to a single spike. Ichimoku is also one of the most watched indicators among crypto traders, so its levels attract orders and decisions, which makes its signals partly self-fulfilling. A thin cloud means that equilibrium is weak, so less resistance stands in the way of a move.

The theory is wrong if the cloud's signals predict nothing about the following days, or if they add nothing beyond a plain moving-average trend of similar length.
