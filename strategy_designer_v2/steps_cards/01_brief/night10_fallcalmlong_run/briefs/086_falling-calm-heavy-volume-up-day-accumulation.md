In a falling, calm market, an up day made on volume heavy against the coin's recent average leads to further rise over the following days. The heavier that day's volume, the larger the later rise. An up day on ordinary or light volume carries no such follow-through and tends to be sold into.

The reason is my own, not from a source. In a calm decline the selling is unforced. Underwater holders leave in pieces, and no liquidation cascade is under way, because the swings are no larger than usual. A sudden burst of volume on a rising day is therefore unlikely to be forced flow such as short squeezes or margin calls, which belong to agitated markets. It more likely marks a new, deliberate buyer absorbing the steady supply. A large buyer of that kind rarely finishes in one day. They work the order over several sessions, so the demand that showed up in the volume is still present afterwards. Other holders who watch volume also read the day as a sign that the decline is being bought, and they join late. A light-volume up day shows only that sellers paused. The same underwater supply returns, and the rise fades.

The theory is wrong if, in falling calm markets, heavy-volume up days are followed by no more rise than light-volume up days. It is also wrong if heavy-volume up days are given back over the next days, or if the later rise does not grow with the volume.
