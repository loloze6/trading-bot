In a falling, calm market, a coin's return over the past several days, measured against its usual daily swing, grades how much it rises over the next few days. The larger that push upward against the trend, the more the coin keeps rising. A coin with a weak or negative recent return keeps lagging.

The source states the pattern and that the push "carries on rather than being sold". It gives no reason; the reason below is mine.

In a calm decline, nothing forces anyone to trade. There are no liquidation cascades and no squeezes. Supply comes from underwater holders who sell steadily into every pause. A rise of several days that is large for the coin's usual swing therefore cannot be explained by forced short covering. It means a buyer has been paying through that steady supply, day after day.

A buyer like that is usually working a large order in pieces, so its demand is not finished when the push becomes visible. Holders who were selling into strength also step back once price stops behaving as the decline taught them. Both keep the rise going for a few more days.

The behaviour belongs to the falling, calm market for two reasons:
- In an agitated market, the same push is more likely to be a squeeze or a forced flow, which reverses.
- In a rising market, a push upward carries no news about demand.

The theory is wrong in any of these cases:
- Inside falling, calm markets, larger scaled multi-day rises are followed by no rise, or by giving the rise back.
- The later move does not grow with the push's size.
- The effect is just as strong outside this profile, which would contradict the reason given.
