In a choppy, agitated market, a coin whose daily close sits near the top of that day's high-low range tends to fall over the next two days. The nearer the close is to the high, the larger the fall.

The source reports the pattern but gives no reason for it. The reason here is mine. In a market that has gone nowhere for two months but swings more than usual, the swings come mostly from flow that carries no information: short squeezes, stop runs, and levered traders chasing intraday breaks of a range that has not held.

A close pinned at the day's high usually means buying that was urgent and late in the session. Liquidity providers absorb it by selling into it, and they ask to be paid for carrying that inventory. Two things would normally carry such a push further, and neither is present here. Without a trend, no trend followers are waiting to add to it. Without calm, patient buyers are not settling in at the new level. So the squeezed and chasing buyers run out within a day or two, and the price gives back the push as the absorbing sellers unwind.

The theory would be shown wrong in either of two cases:
- In choppy, agitated markets, closes near the day's high are followed by flat or rising prices over the next two days.
- The reversal is no stronger in choppy, agitated markets than in choppy, calm ones, which would mean the agitation is not what carries it.
