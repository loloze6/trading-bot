In an agitated market, the next-day reversal of a coin's daily move is one-sided. A day's fall, large against the coin's usual daily swing, is partly made up the next day, and more so the larger the fall. A day's rise of the same scaled size is not given back. The source states this asymmetry.

The reason is mine; the source gives none. In crypto, leverage and stretched positions sit mostly on the long side, and in an agitated market they are close to their limits. A sharp fall therefore sets off selling that carries no information: liquidations, margin calls and risk cuts. These sellers must trade now at any price. Buyers who absorb that flow ask for a discount, and the price comes back once the forced selling is done. A sharp rise has no matching pool of forced buyers. It is driven more by new information, or by buyers who chose to buy, so it holds or keeps going instead of reverting.

The behaviour is placed in an agitated market because forced selling clusters when swings are larger than usual for the coin. When the market is calm, a large fall is more likely to carry information.

The theory is wrong if, in an agitated market, scaled rises are followed by a next-day giveback as large as the rebound after equal scaled falls. It is also wrong if large scaled falls are not followed by a next-day rise that grows with the size of the fall.
