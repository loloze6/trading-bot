In a rising, agitated market, a coin's rise over the last few days tends to continue over the next few days. The larger that rise is against the coin's own usual daily swing, the larger the further rise. In this kind of market, it pays to follow a strong short push rather than wait for a pullback to end. The source adds that dips in this market do not recover more than usual.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In an uptrend that is also swinging more than usual, two groups are forced to buy after a sharp rise, and they do not all buy at once:
- Sellers who faded the move, and holders who were underweight, are squeezed out in stages as the rise grows.
- Traders who size positions by their recent swing come in late and in steps, once a move stands out against that swing.

A rise that is large in the coin's own swing units draws more of this delayed buying than a small rise that ordinary noise could explain. That is why the further rise should be graded by size. In a calm uptrend, buyers are patient and unforced, so less of this staged chasing would be expected. That last point is a contrast I suggest, not a claim of the source.

The theory is wrong if, in rising agitated markets, few-day rises that are large against the coin's usual swing are followed by no further rise or by a giveback. It is also wrong if larger scaled rises are not followed by larger further rises than smaller ones.
