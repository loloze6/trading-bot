A day whose high-to-low range is unusually wide for the coin's recent swing is partly undone the next day. The coin moves against the direction that day took, and the wider the range in the coin's own swing units, the larger the move back. The claim is about the width of the day's range, measured against the coin's usual daily swing, and not about the size of its close-to-close return. It holds for wide up days and wide down days alike.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. A range far wider than the coin's usual swing is a sign that urgent orders hit a book too thin to hold them. These orders come from stop runs, leveraged positions being liquidated, or a large holder who must trade now. Such orders push the price past where patient holders would trade it. The traders who take the other side do so only at a discount. When the urgent flow ends, their quotes and the returning ordinary flow pull the price partly back, and that move back is their payment. A wider range means a larger overshoot relative to normal liquidity, and so a larger repayment. The source also finds the effect weak in a falling, calm market. I do not know a reason for that and do not claim one.

The theory is wrong if, after wide-range days, the next day's move is zero or goes the same way as the wide day. It is also wrong if the move back does not grow as the range gets wider relative to the coin's swing.
