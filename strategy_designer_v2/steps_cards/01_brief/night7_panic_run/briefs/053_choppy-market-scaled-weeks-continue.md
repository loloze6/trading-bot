In a choppy market, a coin's move over the last five days, measured against its usual daily swing, carries on the same way over the next few days. The larger that scaled five-day move, the larger the move that follows. The source states the pattern and says it is strongest in a choppy market, but gives no reason. The reason below is mine.

In a coin with no trend for two months, most holders and market makers have settled on a range. They treat moves as noise to be faded. A five-day move that is large for the coin's swing is more likely news or a shift in demand than noise. The range-anchored crowd is slow to accept it. They reprice in steps rather than at once, so the price keeps drifting toward its new level.

At the same time, trend followers, whose signals were flat while the market chopped, start to pick up the new direction only as the move builds. Their buying or selling arrives after the move and extends it.

In a rising or falling market, this late flow is already in place or meets an established crowd on the other side. So the effect should be weaker there than in the choppy market.

The theory is wrong if, in a choppy market, a scaled five-day move is followed over the next days by no move or by a reversal. It is also wrong if the follow-on move does not grow with the size of the scaled move, or if the continuation is no stronger in a choppy market than in rising or falling ones.
