In a falling, agitated market, when a coin trades below its lowest price of the past month during a day but closes back above that low, what comes next? Does the coin rise over the following days, or keep falling?

It could rebound. Many stop orders and breakdown entries sit just below a well-watched monthly low. A pierce that fails to hold shows that this supply was triggered and absorbed within the day. The sellers who were forced or tempted to act have acted, and buyers took all of it. Short-sellers who sold the break are now under water. In a market swinging more than usual, their tolerance is low, so their covering adds buying. This is the downside mirror of a failed breakout trapping buyers. It belongs here because a falling, agitated market is where such stop clusters below lows are densest and leverage is highest.

It could also keep falling. In an agitated market, wide daily ranges make a pierce-and-reclaim ordinary noise rather than a sign of exhaustion. Holders under water from the downtrend sell into any recovery. The reclaim may only delay the break, and the next attempt then cascades.

The rebound view is wrong if the days after a reclaimed monthly-low pierce do no better than the days after an ordinary day in the same market. The continuation view is wrong if they do no worse. The reasons above are mine; the request gave none for this case.
