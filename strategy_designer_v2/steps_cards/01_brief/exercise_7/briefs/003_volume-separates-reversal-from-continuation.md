A large price move behaves differently depending on how much volume came with it. When a coin moves sharply on thin volume, the move tends to be partly given back afterwards. When a similar move comes on heavy volume, it tends to hold or carry on.

The reason is my own and draws on the general idea that volume shows what kind of trader is behind a move. A sharp move on little volume usually means one impatient trader hit a thin order book. That trader might be a forced liquidation, a fund rebalancing, or a large holder who had to sell. Nothing new is known about the coin's value, so liquidity providers and patient traders who step in afterwards push the price back, and the impatient trader pays them for the immediacy. A sharp move on heavy volume more likely means many traders are acting on the same new information, or on a shift in demand that is still spreading. Others learn of it slowly or are slow to act, so the move continues as they arrive. Crypto spot books are often thin outside the largest coins and at quiet hours, so moves of the first kind should be common.

The theory is wrong if what follows a large move does not depend on the volume that came with it. It is also wrong if thin-volume moves persist at least as much as heavy-volume moves, or if heavy-volume moves reverse at least as much as thin-volume ones.
