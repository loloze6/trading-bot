Question: when a coin is already falling and trading with unusually wide daily swings, does a fresh low over the past month lead to more falling over the following days, or to a rebound?

The case for more falling: in an agitated decline, much of the selling is forced, not chosen. A new monthly low breaks the levels where many recent buyers placed stops and where leveraged longs set their margin. Their exits push price lower, which triggers the next layer, so selling feeds itself. Holders who bought during the month also now see a loss on every purchase, and some sell to cut it. This reason is mine, not from a source.

The case for a rebound: forced sellers are a limited pool. By the time a wide-swinging decline prints a new monthly low, much of that pool may already have been liquidated. With few sellers left, patient buyers can move price back up, and short sellers who pressed the fall take profit by buying. This reason is also mine.

How it could be shown wrong: if coins that make a new monthly low during a volatile fall then drift no differently from other days in that same kind of falling, volatile market, there is neither a self-feeding cascade nor exhaustion. Either side's claim also fails if the move after the low runs against its sign.
