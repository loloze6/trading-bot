When a short moving average of price crosses a long moving average, or price itself crosses the long average, a new trend in that direction has begun and tends to persist. The market then keeps drifting the way of the cross more often than chance would allow. A cross the other way marks the end of that trend.

The source names this trend-following and calls the filled zone between the averages a trend signal. It gives no reason why trends should persist.

The reason here is my own. New information and changes in demand spread slowly across crypto spot traders. Some participants act late: slower discretionary traders, funds that rebalance on schedules, and followers who chase moves once they are visible. Their late buying or selling extends a move that has already started.

The theory is wrong if price after such crosses moves in the direction of the cross no more often or no further than after random points. It is also wrong if moves after crosses tend to reverse, which would mean the crossing average only lags noise and marks exhaustion rather than the start of a trend.
