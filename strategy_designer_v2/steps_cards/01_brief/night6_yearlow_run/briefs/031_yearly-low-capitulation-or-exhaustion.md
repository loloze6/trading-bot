Question: when a coin trades near its lowest price of the past year, does it tend to keep falling or to bounce? And when a daily close goes below that low, does the decline carry on or reverse?

Reasons it could keep falling: holders who bought higher give up near the low. Many sell once the yearly low looks likely to break, and others sell after it breaks as their last hope of getting out near cost fades. Stops placed just under the obvious low fire together. Trend followers and momentum screens add shorts or drop the coin. In that case selling feeds on itself and a break of the low marks a new leg down.

Reasons it could bounce: by the time price nears a yearly low, most willing sellers may already have sold. Value buyers and patient bids gather at a level everyone can see. A break below the low may be a stop run that clears out the last weak holders, after which nobody is left to sell and price climbs back above the old low.

These reasons are my own reading of who trades near the low. The theme gives no source.

Two results would each show one side wrong. If coins near their yearly low then do no worse than coins away from it, and closes below the low are followed by recovery, the giving-up story is wrong. If declines near the low persist and breaks below it extend, the exhaustion story is wrong. If behaviour near the low and after a break looks like any other day, neither story holds.
