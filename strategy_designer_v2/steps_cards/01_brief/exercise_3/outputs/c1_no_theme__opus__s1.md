Large coins lead small coins. When the market-wide price moves, the biggest and most liquid coins absorb new information first. Smaller coins then catch up over the following bars. The reason is from me. Fast traders and market makers watch and quote the major coins most closely. Thin coins are repriced later by slower participants: retail traders, fewer arbitrageurs, and market makers who widen spreads or pull quotes rather than chase. So a move in the leaders should partly predict a same-direction move in the laggards.

The theory is wrong if small coins' moves are not predicted by the leaders' earlier moves. It is also wrong if small coins move at the same time as the leaders, or if they lead.
