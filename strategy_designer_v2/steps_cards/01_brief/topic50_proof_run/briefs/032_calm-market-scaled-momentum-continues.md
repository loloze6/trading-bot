In a calm market, a coin's move over the past few weeks tends to carry on over the following days in the same direction. The larger that move is against the coin's own usual daily swing, the stronger the carry: a move that is big for a quiet coin continues more than the same move in a coin that always swings widely. It holds for rises and falls alike.

The source gives no reason. The reason here is my own. In calm conditions, prices are moved mostly by slow, steady flows rather than forced ones: news reaching holders gradually, allocators adding or cutting exposure in steps, and trend and volatility-targeting traders who size up when swings are small and the direction is clear. Each of these trades in the direction the coin has already gone and spreads its orders over days, so the move under-reacts at first and continues. A move that is large against a quiet coin's usual swing is unlikely to be noise, so it more likely reflects this slow flow. In an agitated market, forced liquidations, stop runs and panic buying make moves that overshoot and partly reverse, and this cancels the slow drift. That is why the effect should live only in calm markets.

The theory is wrong if, in calm markets, a coin's recent move scaled by its swing carries no information about its next days, or is followed by a partial reversal. It is also wrong if larger scaled moves do not continue more than smaller ones, or if the carry is just as strong in agitated markets.
