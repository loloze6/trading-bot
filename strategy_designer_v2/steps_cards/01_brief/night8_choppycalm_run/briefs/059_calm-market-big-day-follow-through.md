In a calm market, a coin's one-day move that is large against its usual daily swing over the past month carries on in the same direction over the following week. The larger the day is in those swing units, the larger the follow-through. In an agitated market the same day does not carry on.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. When a coin has been swinging less than usual, few holders are being forced out by margin calls or risk limits, and liquidity providers are not stretched. An unusually large day in that setting is then less likely to be urgent, information-free flow that gets repaid. It is more likely a deliberate repositioning or a response to news. That kind of flow spreads slowly: the first movers trade on the day, and slower holders, trend followers and traders who wait for confirmation join over the next days.

In an agitated market, large days are more often made by forced liquidations and squeezes. Their overshoot offsets this drift. The source also suggests the follow-through is stronger when the coin has already been rising for weeks, where the large day confirms a move that late buyers are already chasing.

The theory is wrong if, in calm markets, large scaled one-day moves are followed by no move or by a partial reversal over the next week. It is also wrong if the follow-through does not grow with the size of the day, or if it is as strong in agitated markets as in calm ones.
