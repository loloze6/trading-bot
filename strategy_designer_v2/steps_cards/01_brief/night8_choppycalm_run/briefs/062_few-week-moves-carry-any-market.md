In any market, whatever its trend or agitation, a coin's move over the past few weeks keeps going its own way over the following week or two, and the larger that move, the larger the move that follows. This holds on both sides: a large few-week rise is followed by more rise, a large few-week fall by more fall. A small move carries little.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. A few-week move often reflects news or a change in demand that most holders take in slowly. Many spot holders check their coins rarely and act only after a move has lasted. Trend followers whose signals look back over weeks or months buy or sell only once the move is old enough to register, and they add size as it grows. Funds and treasuries rebalance on a schedule, so they trade late. Each group arrives after the first move, in the same direction, so the price keeps drifting the way it started. The drift ends once these late traders have finished.

The theory is wrong if a coin's move over the next week or two does not grow with the size of its past few weeks' move. It is also wrong if large few-week moves are partly given back instead. A third way it fails is if the carry shows on one side only, rises but not falls or the reverse.
