Does the relative strength index, which measures how much of a coin's recent price change came from up closes rather than down closes, carry information about where the price goes next? The question is whether a stretched reading tells anything about the following move, and in which direction.

It could predict reversal. A run of one-sided closes often comes from traders who must trade now: leveraged positions being liquidated, stops being hit, latecomers chasing. They pay for immediacy. Market makers and patient buyers or sellers absorb that flow at a discount and are rewarded when price drifts back. On this view, a high reading should be followed by weaker-than-usual returns and a low reading by stronger ones.

It could predict continuation. Crypto news and flows spread slowly across a scattered base of participants. Trend followers add to positions as a move persists, and investors underreact before catching up. On this view, a one-sided run reflects a move that is still unfolding, so returns after a high reading stay higher than usual.

It could predict nothing. The indicator is free, simple and watched by almost everyone, so any reliable edge in the most liquid coins may have been traded away. The reading may also only repackage recent returns, adding nothing beyond them.

The idea would be shown wrong if returns and volatility after extreme readings were no different from those after neutral readings. The direction of any difference between them would show which of the first two answers holds.
