In a rising, calm market, a day whose range is wide against the coin's usual daily swing and that closes near its low is followed by a rise the next day. The wider the day against that swing, the larger the rise. This holds on any one coin's own bars.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason, so the reason here is mine. In a rising coin whose swings are quieter than usual, a wide day that ends at its low is rarely news that changes the trend. It is more likely a burst of hurried selling late in the day: stops below recent lows firing, a leveraged holder trimming, or one large seller dumping. That selling pushes price past what holders would accept without being rushed. Buyers who have been waiting to add to a rising, orderly coin see the low close as a discount. With no wider panic to make them wait, they step in, and the overshoot is repaid. A wider day means a larger overshoot and so a larger repayment. In agitated markets the same selling may be part of a cascade. In falling markets dip-buyers are scarce. The calm, rising market is where the buyers are most ready and the selling is most likely to carry no information.

The theory is wrong if, in rising calm markets, wide days that close near their low are not followed by a next-day rise. It is also wrong if the next-day move does not grow with the day's width, or if such days lead to further falls.
