In any market, a coin's daily volume, measured against its own recent average and taken on its own, without the day's direction, tells which way the next day leans. After a day of unusually heavy volume the coin tends to rise. After a day of unusually light volume it tends to drift lower. The lean grows the further volume sits from its usual level, on either side. The source states this pattern across all days. It also shows that the heavy side comes mostly from down days, while up days show no grading by volume.

The reason is mine, not the source's. A day far above usual volume marks urgent, crowded flow that someone had to absorb. In crypto that crowding is more often forced selling than forced buying: liquidations, stop cascades, margin calls. So whatever the day's net move, the absorbers took inventory at a concession, and they are repaid the next day as the urgency is gone.

For the light-volume side I have no firm reason. One possibility is that thin participation leaves resting sellers unmet by buyers, so price sags. That is a guess.

The theory is wrong if the next day's return does not rise with the day's volume against its usual level. It is also wrong if heavy-volume days are not followed by stronger next days than ordinary ones, or if light-volume days are not followed by weaker ones.
