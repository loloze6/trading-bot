After a run of rising price far larger than the coin's usual move, made of many bars in a row in the same direction, and while the volume behind the run fades, the coin tends to give back part of the run over the next one to three days. The same holds for falls.

The reason is mine. A long run attracts late buyers who chase it; when volume fades while the price is still rising, the buyers who drove the run have stopped and only the late ones remain. Their positions are fragile: the first pause makes them sell, and the traders who bought early take their profits at the same time. Four-hour bars show the run, its length and the fading volume more precisely than daily bars, with less noise than hourly ones.

The theory is wrong if long runs with fading volume are not followed by a partial give-back, or if the give-back is no larger than after runs of the same size with rising volume.
