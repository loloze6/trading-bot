In a falling, calm market, a few-day move that is large against the coin's usual daily swing keeps going in its own direction for about two weeks. The larger the move is in swing units, the further it goes. The follow-through is not spent in the first days. It builds over the weeks after.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. A calm downtrend is a quiet, low-attention market. Few forced traders are in it, and the price drifts as holders sell slowly. In that setting a sharp move against the small usual swing is rare, so it is more likely to carry news than noise.

The traders who act on such news are slow:
- Trend followers on longer horizons change their positions gradually as their signals turn.
- Volatility-sized holders add or cut in steps.
- Attention returns to the coin only over days.

So a large rise in a calm downtrend can mark the start of a turn that these slow traders join over the following weeks. A large fall can mark the decline resuming, with the same slow traders selling in turn. No forced flow is there to overshoot and snap back, which is what would make the move reverse.

The theory is wrong if, in falling, calm markets, large scaled few-day moves show no later continuation over the following two weeks. It is also wrong if the continuation is spent within the first days rather than building, or if the moves reverse.
