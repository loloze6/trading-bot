profile: calm

In a calm market, a coin's move over the past several days carries on in the same direction over the next few days. The move counts by its size against the coin's own usual daily swing. The larger that scaled move, the larger the follow-through, for rises and falls alike. In an agitated market this continuation is absent or much weaker.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. When a coin is calm, its prices are moved less by forced, urgent trades such as liquidations, margin calls and panic exits. Those trades push a price away from its value and are paid back when liquidity providers revert them. A move that stands out against a quiet background is more likely to come from new information or from patient capital. That capital splits large orders over many days, and slower holders read the change late and follow it. So the price adjusts in steps rather than at once, and the move persists.

In an agitated market, the same price moves are mostly forced flow and noise. Those tend to reverse, which hides or cancels any slow adjustment.

The theory is wrong if, in calm markets, large scaled moves over the past days are followed by no continuation or by a partial reversal. It is also wrong if the follow-through does not grow with the size of the scaled move, or if it is just as strong in agitated markets as in calm ones.
