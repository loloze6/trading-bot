In a falling, calm market, the further a coin has climbed above its lowest close of the past few weeks, the more it rises over the following days. A coin still sitting at that low does not share in the rise.

The source states the pattern. The reason is mine. In a calm decline nobody is forced to trade. Prices fall because underwater holders sell in pieces and buyers wait. A climb well off the recent low therefore cannot be a squeeze or the repayment of a forced-selling discount. It shows that an unforced buyer has started to take that supply, and that the sellers' flow has thinned near the low.

That buyer works its order slowly. Others notice the change only late: holders who would have sold more, range traders measuring from the low, and trend followers waiting for confirmation. So the lift goes on. A coin still at its low shows no such buyer, and its orderly selling continues.

In an agitated decline the same climb would more often be short covering or relief buying, which is spent quickly. That is why the effect should live in the calm profile.

The theory is wrong if, in falling calm markets, the climb off the recent low carries no information about the following days. It is also wrong if larger climbs are followed by weaker days, as trapped holders sell into the lift. Finally, it is wrong if the same link appears just as strongly outside the falling calm profile.
