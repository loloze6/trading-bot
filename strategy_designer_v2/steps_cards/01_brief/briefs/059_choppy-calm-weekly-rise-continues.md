In a choppy, calm market, a coin that has risen over the past week keeps rising over the following week. The larger that week's rise is against the coin's usual daily swing of the past month, the larger the further rise. The source states the pattern only, not its cause. The reason below is mine.

A coin with no trend and quiet swings draws little attention and few new buyers. Its price is set mostly by range traders, who sell rallies and buy dips. A week's rise that is large for such a quiet coin is unusual, and it is unlikely to come from noise. It suggests a buyer with a reason, building a position over days rather than at once. Range sellers who faded the early rise end up short or underweight. Once the move leaves the range they must buy back. Traders who ignore quiet coins notice the move only after it shows on charts and scanners, and they arrive late. Both groups buy after the first week, so the rise carries on.

The bigger the rise against the coin's usual swing, the clearer the signal and the more faders are caught, so the further rise grows with it. A fall need not mirror this, because quiet coins attract no comparable late selling crowd.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy calm markets, large scaled weekly rises are followed by no further rise or by a giveback. It is also wrong if the further rise does not grow with the scaled size of the week's rise.
