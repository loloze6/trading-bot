In a falling market, a day whose high-low range is unusually narrow for the coin's recent swing is followed by a rise over the next few days. The narrower the day is against that swing, the larger the rise.

The source reads the quiet day as a sign of selling exhaustion, and that reason comes from the source. The rest of the account is mine. A downtrend is carried by sellers who must or want to get out, such as holders cutting losses, lenders' collateral being sold, and funds reducing risk. They keep pressing price lower and keep the days wide. When a day in that trend barely moves, it suggests that this supply has largely gone. The sellers who needed to sell have sold, and new sellers are not willing to hit bids at these prices. Short sellers who rode the decline now see the pressure stop. Some of them take profits, and buyers waiting for the fall to end step in. With little selling left to meet that buying, price lifts for a short while. This lives in a falling market because only there is a quiet day a break from heavy, one-sided selling. In a rising or choppy market, a narrow day carries no such message.

The theory is wrong if, in falling markets, narrow days are followed by no rise or by further decline. It is also wrong if the next days' move does not grow as the day's range shrinks against the coin's usual swing.
