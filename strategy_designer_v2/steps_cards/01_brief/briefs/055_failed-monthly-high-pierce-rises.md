When a coin trades above its highest price of the past month during a day but closes back below that high, it tends to rise over the following days, up to about two weeks. This is the opposite of the usual reading that such a day is a failed breakout and a trap for buyers. The source states the behaviour and says the rise grows with how far the day's high went above the monthly high. Its own evidence supports that grading only weakly, so the core claim is the rise itself, not its scaling.

The reason is mine, not the source's. A pierce of the monthly high shows buyers strong enough to reach new ground. The close back below it shows that sellers at the old high supplied a large block that day: profit-takers, holders who had waited to sell at break-even, and new shorts reading the day as a trap. That supply is spent in one day. In the days after, the remaining demand meets thinner overhead supply. The shorts who sold the failed break must buy back as the price returns toward the high. Breakout and trend buyers who waited for confirmation also arrive late. Together these lift the price.

The theory is wrong if, after such days, coins fall or do no better than after other closes near their monthly high. It is also wrong if the move turns into the decline that a trapped-buyer story would predict.
