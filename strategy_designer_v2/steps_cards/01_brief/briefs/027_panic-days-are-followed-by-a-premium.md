After a panic, a stretch of hours where the coin's range is several times its usual size and the price falls hard, the coin tends to earn more than usual over the following one to two weeks, whatever its trend before.

The reason is mine. In a panic, holders who must sell (margin calls, liquidations, funds meeting withdrawals) sell at any price, and buyers ask for a large discount to take the risk on. Once the forced selling is over, the price no longer needs that discount, so it drifts back up over days as calmer buyers come back. The premium is a payment for providing liquidity when nobody else wants to.

The theory is wrong if returns over the one to two weeks after panic stretches are no higher than usual, or if they are higher only in coins that were already rising before the panic.
