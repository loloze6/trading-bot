In a choppy market, a daily close near the top of the day's range does not carry a direction of its own. The coin's path over the past few weeks gives it one. After a few-week fall, a high close is followed by days of decline. After a few-week rise, it is followed by days of further rise. The larger that few-week move in either direction, the larger the later move.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In a coin without a two-month trend, holders anchor to the range. After a few-week fall, many recent buyers sit underwater inside the range. A strong close looks to them like the exit they were waiting for, and they sell into the following days. The urgent buying that pinned the close is relief or short covering with no lasting buyer behind it. After a few-week rise, the same strong close shows buyers pressing toward the top of the range. Range-faders who sold the rise are caught and cover late, and attention returns slowly, so the push continues. In rising or falling markets, trend followers and dip-buyers swamp this anchored crowd, so the effect should belong to the choppy market.

The theory is wrong in either of these cases. In choppy markets, the days after a high close do not depend on the prior few-week move, or go against it. Or the same dependence shows just as strongly after closes that are not high in the range.
