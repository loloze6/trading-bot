In a choppy, agitated market, where a coin closes within its day's range points the opposite way for the next day. The signal grows with how wide that day was against the coin's recent swing. A wide day closing near its low is followed by a rise. A wide day closing near its high is followed by a fall. The effect is larger the wider the day and the further the close sits from the middle of the range, and it is spent within the next day. It works on both sides, more strongly after low closes.

The pattern comes from the source. The reason is mine. A choppy market has no trend for informed or trend-following flow to extend. When such a market is also agitated, much of the urgent trading is forced: stops, liquidations, and holders who size positions by volatility cutting or adding late in the day. That flow pushes the close toward one end of a wide range without carrying information. Liquidity providers and range traders absorb it at a discount and are repaid the next day as price returns toward the range's middle. A wider day means more forced flow and a larger discount. In a trending market, the same close could instead mark information that continues. In a calm one, there is little forced flow to overshoot.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy, agitated markets, wide days closing near an end of their range are followed the next day by moves the same way or by no move. It is also wrong if the next-day reversal does not grow with the day's width and the close's distance from the middle.
