A coin that has fallen over the past few weeks tends to keep falling over the next few days, in any market profile. The deeper that fall is when measured in the coin's own usual daily swing, the larger the further fall. The claim is about falls. It does not say that a rise of the same size carries on as well.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason below is mine. A few-week decline, deep for the coin's usual swing, leaves many holders who bought in the past weeks under water and many risk budgets breached. These sellers do not all act at once. Some cut exposure in steps as their risk measures catch up. Some wait for a bounce and sell into any pause. Some only give up once the loss has lasted long enough to look lasting. Meanwhile, buyers who would absorb the selling hold back, because a fall that is large for the coin's own swing reads as news rather than noise. They wait for the decline to steady before stepping in. Supply keeps arriving with too little demand to meet it, and the remaining supply grows with the depth of the fall.

The theory is wrong if a few-week fall that is deep for the coin's swing is followed in the next days by a rise or by no move. It is also wrong if the further fall does not grow with the depth of the decline. If it holds only in one trend or one level of agitation, the claim that it lives in every market is wrong.
