In a calm market, a coin's single-day move, up or down, that is large against its own recent daily swing tends to carry on the same way over the following week. The larger the day stands out against the coin's quiet noise, the further it carries. The source states this pattern but gives no reason, so the reason below is mine.

When a coin has been swinging less than usual, few traders are being forced to trade. There are no liquidation cascades and little volatility-driven de-risking, and two-sided liquidity is ample. A day that still stands far out from that quiet noise is therefore unlikely to be an overshoot that liquidity providers are paid to undo. It is more likely the first visible trace of new information or of a deliberate repositioning by a large, patient holder.

The move then spreads slowly for two reasons:
- The holder works the rest of the order over the following days.
- Others confirm the news, revise their view and follow in turn, while trend followers who need the move to persist arrive later still.

In an agitated market the same-sized day would more often be forced flow, which reverses. Calm is where the information share of a big day is highest.

The theory is wrong if, in calm markets, large single-day moves against the coin's swing are followed over the next week by no same-way drift, or by drift that does not grow with the day's size. It is also wrong if they are followed by a reversal. It would be weakened if the carry were no stronger in calm markets than in agitated ones.
