In a calm market, a coin's one-day rise that is large against its own recent daily swing is followed by more rise over the next week. The larger the rise is in those swing units, the larger the further rise. The effect runs one way only. An equally large one-day fall in a calm market is not reliably followed by more falling. In an agitated market, large up days do not carry on.

The pattern and its one-sidedness come from the source. The reason is mine, and the source gives none.

When a coin has been quiet, a big up day stands out against weeks of small moves. It draws attention and buying from people who were not watching: momentum and breakout followers, holders who were waiting for a sign, and new money. These buyers arrive over days, not at once. On spot markets, few traders sell short to lean against them.

A large fall in the same quiet setting gets no such follow-on flow. Holders in a calm market are not forced to sell. Patient buyers treat the fall as a discount, so it stalls.

In an agitated market, big up days are more often squeezes and two-sided forced flow. They carry no news to spread slowly, so they fade rather than continue.

The theory is wrong if any of these holds:
- In calm markets, large scaled one-day rises are not followed by further rise, or the further rise does not grow with the rise's size.
- Large calm-market falls continue just as reliably, so the effect is symmetric.
- The same continuation holds just as well in agitated markets.
