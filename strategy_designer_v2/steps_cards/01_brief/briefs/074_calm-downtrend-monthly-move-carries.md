In a falling, calm market, a coin keeps moving in the direction of its past month for the next week or two. The larger the past month's move is against the coin's own recent daily swing, the further it carries. This holds whichever way the month went. The source states the pattern only. The reason below is mine.

Much of the money in a coin reviews its holdings on a monthly or slower cycle. This includes allocators, funds that rebalance, and holders who judge a position by how it did over the month. When a month's move is large for how quietly the coin has been trading, that move stands out to these reviewers as a real change, not noise. They then act on it, adding to rising coins and cutting falling ones, but they do so in pieces over the following days.

In a calm downtrend nothing forces the adjustment to finish quickly. There is no liquidation flush that clears sellers at once. There are no squeezes that pull buyers in at once. Dip-buyers are scarce because the trend gives them no reason to step in early. So the repricing begun over the month is still incomplete at its end, and it keeps going. In an agitated market, forced two-sided flow would instead overshoot and be repaid, which is why the effect should live in calm conditions.

The theory is wrong if, in falling calm markets, a large swing-scaled monthly move is followed by no further move its way. It is also wrong if the move partly reverses, or if the follow-through does not grow with the move's size.
