An hour in which a coin moves far more than usual on volume several times its usual hourly volume tends to be followed by a move in the same direction over the next two to six hours. A large hourly move on ordinary volume does not carry on in the same way.

The reason is mine, from how information reaches a market. News and large orders do not hit the price at once: the first traders to act move it in the hour they learn it, and the volume surge marks that real information or a large participant is behind the move; the rest of the market follows over the next hours as the news spreads and large orders are worked in slices. A move without volume is more often noise from thin liquidity, which fades.

Trading only the two most liquid coins keeps the cost of a buy and a sell near 0.10 % of the value traded, which a move over a few hours can cover; on less liquid coins the cost is two to three times higher.

The theory is wrong if hourly moves on surging volume are not followed by moves in the same direction over the next two to six hours, or if they are followed no more than large hourly moves on ordinary volume.
