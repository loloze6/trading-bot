Question: in crypto spot markets, does a coin's volatility respond unevenly to the direction of its own past moves, and if so, which way? In stock markets, falls tend to raise later volatility more than rises of the same size. Crypto may break that pattern, or even reverse it.

There are reasons the answer could go either way.

Rallies might raise later volatility more. Rising prices draw in new, often leveraged buyers who chase the move. Short sellers get squeezed and must buy back at any price. Attention, news coverage and trading activity all jump after strong up moves. Each of these adds disorderly, one-sided flow, so the bars that follow swing more widely.

Falls might raise later volatility more, as in stocks. Losses force leveraged longs to sell, and margin calls cascade. Market makers widen their quotes or step away when prices drop, which thins the book. Fear spreads faster than greed. Most crypto leverage sits on the long side, which would make the downside path the more violent one.

There is also a reason for no asymmetry at all. If volatility just clusters, large moves in either direction would be followed by similar turbulence, and direction would carry no extra information.

These reasons are mine, not taken from any source, and I do not know which one dominates. Any one-sided answer would be shown wrong if later volatility, measured as the range or size of the following bars, came out the same after up moves and down moves of equal size. It would also be shown wrong if the gap ran the opposite way to the side claimed.
