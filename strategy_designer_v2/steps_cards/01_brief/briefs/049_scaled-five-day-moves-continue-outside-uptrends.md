When a coin that is not in a rising trend makes a five-day move that is large for its own recent daily swing, it keeps moving the same way over the following days, up or down. The larger the move in units of that swing, the larger the move that follows. In a rising market the same move carries on only weakly. The behaviour and its weakness in rising markets come from the source. The reasons are mine.

The first reason is that participants adjust slowly. After a move that is large for the coin's normal swing, holders who size by risk, slower funds and traders who wait for confirmation trade in pieces over several days. Their late flow pushes the price further in the direction already taken.

The second reason explains why rising markets differ. In a rising market there is a standing crowd on both sides: dip-buyers wait to absorb falls, and holders sitting on gains sell into rallies. Both lean against a sharp move, so the late flow is soaked up rather than extending the move. In choppy and falling markets that crowd is thin, so nothing stops the late flow.

The theory is wrong if, outside rising trends, large scaled five-day moves are followed by no further move or by a reversal. It is also wrong if the follow-through does not grow with the size of the move. Finally, it is wrong if the carry-on is as strong in rising markets as in choppy and falling ones.
