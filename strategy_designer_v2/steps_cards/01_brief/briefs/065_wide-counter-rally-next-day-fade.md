In any market, not only in falling ones, a coin that has an up day while it is still down over the past few days tends to fall back the next day. The wider that up day's high-to-low range is against the coin's usual daily swing, the larger the fall. Up days that come after a short rise, and down days of either kind, show no such next-day move.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. A bounce inside a short fall is mostly relief flow, not news. Shorts cover, dip-buyers step in early, and holders caught by the fall wait for a better price. A wide range shows this flow was hurried and pushed through thin offers, so the day closes above where patient sellers will trade. The next day the hurried buyers are done. Holders who wanted out during the fall then sell into the higher price, and liquidity providers who sold the bounce are repaid as it settles back. A wider range means a bigger overshoot and more stranded supply above, so the giveback is larger. This would work in any trend because it rests on a short fall, which happens in every profile, not on the long trend.

The theory is wrong if any of these hold:
- such up days are not followed by a next-day fall against the coin's drift;
- the fall does not grow with the day's range in usual swings;
- up days after a short rise fade just as much, which would mean wide up days fade in general, not counter-moves inside a fall.
