In a falling, calm market, the further a coin closes below its high of the past few weeks, measured in its own usual daily swings, the more it keeps falling over the next few days. The pattern comes from the source. The reason is mine.

Many holders bought in recent weeks, near that high. A close many usual swings below it puts them clearly underwater. Some of them measure their loss from the recent peak, through a loss limit, a trailing stop or a mandate. The deeper the close sits below the peak, the more of them cross their own line and sell.

In a calm market this selling is not forced in one burst. There is no liquidation cascade that clears sellers at once and draws bargain buyers with the discount. So the supply comes out in pieces over the following days. In a downtrend, buyers wait for a flush that does not come, and price drifts lower as the sellers take turns.

In an agitated falling market the same depth comes more often from forced selling that overshoots and is repaid. That is why the effect should belong to the calm, falling profile and fade or reverse in the agitated one.

The theory is wrong if, in falling, calm markets, deeper closes below the recent high are followed by no larger falls than shallow ones, or by rebounds. It is also wrong if the continuation is just as strong in falling, agitated markets, which would undercut the reason.
