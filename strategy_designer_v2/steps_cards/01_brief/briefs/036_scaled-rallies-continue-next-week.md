A coin that has risen sharply over the last few days keeps rising over the following week. The rise is measured against the coin's own usual daily swing, and the larger that scaled rally, the larger the further gain. The source says this holds whatever the coin's trend, so the theory names no market profile. Rallies in falling markets continue too and are not faded.

The source gives no reason; the reasons below are mine. Information and attention spread slowly. A rally that is large for this coin draws notice from traders who were not watching, and they buy over the following days rather than at once. Breakout and trend-following traders act only once a move has proven itself, so they buy after the rally. Short sellers caught by a rally of unusual size cut their positions in stages over several days, and their buying extends the move. Holders who sold early and then saw the rally re-enter at higher prices. In each case the buyers are late or forced, so the push outlasts the first days. Scaling by the usual swing matters because what counts is how unusual the move is for this coin, not its raw size.

The theory is wrong if sharp multi-day rallies, measured in the coin's usual swing, are followed over the next week by flat or falling prices. It is also wrong if larger scaled rallies are not followed by larger gains, or if the continuation appears in only one trend and reverses in another.
