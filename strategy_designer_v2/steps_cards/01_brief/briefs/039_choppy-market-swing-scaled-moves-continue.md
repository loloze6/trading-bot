In a choppy market, a coin keeps moving over the following days in the direction of its move over the past few weeks, rising after a rise and falling after a fall. The larger that move is against the coin's own daily swings, the further it carries. This is the opposite of what a range trader expects. It also differs from the falls-only claim already indexed, because it holds on both sides and depends on size measured in swing units.

The claim comes from the source. The reason is mine; the source gives none, and it may be wrong. When a coin has gone nowhere for two months, many participants read it as a range. They fade moves away from the middle, so a move that is large against its own swings meets selling into rises and buying into falls. That slows the move but does not stop it.

In a range market, a move of that size more likely reflects a real change in demand for that one coin than noise. Examples are news, a shift of holdings, or a new buyer or seller working an order over weeks. Attention is low when nothing is trending, so the market absorbs this slowly and the move keeps going. In a trending market, the same move is mostly the trend itself, which is already watched and chased. So the extra carry would belong to the choppy market.

The theory is wrong if, in a choppy market, large moves of a few weeks show no further drift their way in the following days. It is also wrong if they partly reverse, or if the continuation does not grow with the move's size in swing units.
