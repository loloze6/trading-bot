A daily situation that traders act on at the daily close often becomes true hours earlier, during the day. A coin that breaks above its range of recent weeks, or makes a move several times its usual daily size, usually does so in one or a few hours, and much of the move is already made by the close. A trader who watches the same daily situation on hourly closes, and acts at the hour it first holds, catches part of the move that a daily trader misses, then holds the position for the days the move lasts.

The reason is mine. Most systematic and discretionary crypto traders read daily candles and act at or after the close. The first hours after a daily situation forms are therefore traded by fewer people, and the larger flow that follows the close pushes the price further in the same direction.

Because the position is held for days, the move it captures is large next to the cost of a trade.

The theory is wrong if acting at the hour the daily situation first holds earns no more than acting at the following daily close, or if the move after the hour of entry is not larger than the coin's usual move over the same days.
