In a rising, calm market, a one-day fall that is large against the coin's usual daily swing over the past month is followed by a rebound over the next few days. The larger the fall in those swing units, the larger the rebound. The same fall in a calm choppy or calm falling market brings no such rebound.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In a calm uptrend, holders are mostly patient and in profit, and the low swing keeps risk-sized holders at full exposure. A fall that is large against that small swing is therefore unlikely to be news that changes what the coin is worth. More likely it is one hurried seller, a cluster of stops, or a thin moment in the book. The many buyers waiting for a pullback in a trend they believe in absorb it. One day of selling does not disturb the calm reading that sized their positions, so they keep buying, and the discount is repaid over the following days rather than at once. In a calm choppy or calm falling market there is no crowd waiting to buy weakness. There, a large fall more often starts orderly selling by underwater holders.

The theory is wrong if, in rising calm markets, large scaled one-day falls are followed by no rise over the next few days. It is also wrong if bigger falls rebound no more than small ones, or if calm choppy and calm falling markets rebound just as much.
