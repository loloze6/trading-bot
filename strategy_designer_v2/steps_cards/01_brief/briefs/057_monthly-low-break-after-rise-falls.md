When a coin closes below its lowest price of the past month, it keeps falling over the following days. The more the coin rose over the two months before the break, the larger that later fall. After a long fall, the same break carries little further decline.

The source states this pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. After a two-month rise, many holders bought recently and at higher prices. Many of them hold on leverage or on the belief that dips get bought, and their stops and margin levels sit below the recent lows. A close below the monthly low is the first sign that dip-buying failed. Those holders then exit in turn: stops fire, levered longs are cut, and profit-takers who waited for a top start selling. Trend followers who were long flip or step aside, so no one absorbs the supply. That exit is spread over days, not finished on the break day. After a two-month fall, the weak and levered holders are already gone, so a new low releases little forced or late selling.

The theory is wrong if closes below the monthly low that follow a two-month rise fall no more over the next days than those that follow a fall or a flat stretch. It is also wrong if they rebound, or if the later move shows no grading by the prior two-month return.
