Prices where a coin turned several times before act as barriers. When price comes back to a level where it reversed repeatedly, it tends to turn again; when it finally closes clearly beyond such a level, it tends to keep going for several days.

The reason is mine, from how traders place orders. Many traders mark the prices where the market turned before and leave limit orders there, which defends the level when price returns. Others place stop orders just beyond it, expecting it to hold. When price breaks through, the defending orders are used up and the stops become market orders in the direction of the break, so the move accelerates. The more often a level held, the more orders sit at it, and the stronger both effects.

The theory is wrong if price near a level that held several times turns no more often than price at any other level, or if closes beyond such a level are followed by no larger continuation than other closes of the same size.
