In a falling, calm market, meaning a coin down over the past two months whose swings are no larger than usual for it, the decline tends to carry on. The more the coin has fallen over the past weeks, the more it keeps falling over the next days.

The reason, as the operator states it, is that holders sell into every pause. In an orderly decline there is no single panic that forces weak holders out at once. Selling is spread out instead. Holders who bought higher wait for a bounce to cut their loss and sell on each flat or up day. Funds and slower holders reduce exposure gradually rather than in one go.

I add, as my own reasoning, why this would live in the calm profile. A calm decline has no liquidation cascade or capitulation day that empties the supply of sellers. With no such event, the overshoot-and-rebound pattern of agitated falls does not appear, and the remaining supply keeps pressing on price. Buyers also see no washout that would signal a bottom, so they stay patient and bid lower. A deeper fall over the past weeks means more holders sitting on losses, so more supply waits to be released into later pauses. That is why the size of the past fall should grade the size of the continuation.

The theory is wrong if, in falling calm markets, larger past falls are followed by next-day returns no lower than smaller ones. It is also wrong if they are followed by rebounds, or if the continuation is no stronger than in other profiles.
