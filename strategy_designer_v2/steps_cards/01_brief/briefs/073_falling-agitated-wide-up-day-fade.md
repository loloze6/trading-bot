In a falling, agitated market, a day that closes above the day before tends to be followed by a lower next day. The size of that fall is set by how wide the up day's high-low range was against the coin's recent typical range. It is not set by where the close sat in the range, and not by how much the day gained. A narrow up day is followed by little or nothing. A wide one is followed by a clear fall. The effect belongs to this profile. In other markets, up days of any width show neither the fall nor the grading.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason, so the reason here is mine and uncertain. In a falling, agitated market, many holders are underwater and many traders are short with leverage. A wide day shows that urgent flow passed through thin books: short covering forced by stops or margin, and quick bargain buying. That flow carries no lasting information and pushes the price further than it would go otherwise. Once it is spent, liquidity providers who sold into it and holders who used the swing to get out press the price back. The wider the day, the more of this flow and the larger the overshoot to be repaid. Close location tells little because the forced buying can end anywhere in the day.

The theory is wrong if, in this profile, wide up days are not followed by larger next-day falls than narrow ones. It is also wrong if close location or the size of the gain grades the fall better than width does, or if the same grading appears just as strongly outside the profile.
