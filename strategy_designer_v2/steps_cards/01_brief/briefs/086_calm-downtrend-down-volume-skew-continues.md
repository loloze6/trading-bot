In a falling, calm market, the more a coin's recent trading volume sits on its down days rather than its up days, the further it keeps falling over the following days. The decline is driven by sellers who have reasons to leave but are not forced. These are underwater holders, funds trimming a losing position and early buyers taking what is left. Because nothing forces them, they spread their selling over many sessions. They sell harder when price slips, which lets them place size into trailing stops and into weak bids, and they hold back on up days.

The footprint of this is a lopsided volume pattern. Down days are heavy and up days are thin. While that lopsidedness lasts, the supply is not finished, and price keeps drifting lower as each new day brings more of it.

The calm profile matters for two reasons:
- In an agitated decline, heavy down-day volume more often marks forced liquidation. That kind of selling is quickly exhausted and then repaid by a rebound, so the same volume pattern would mean the opposite there.
- In a calm decline, no flush clears the sellers, so their remaining supply keeps arriving.

When the up days carry as much volume as the down days, the theory says buyers are absorbing the supply and the drift should weaken.

This reason is my own, not taken from a source. The theory is wrong if, in falling, calm markets:
- a heavier down-day share of recent volume is not followed by weaker days than a balanced or up-heavy share, or
- it is followed by stronger days, which would mean the heavy selling marks sellers running out rather than sellers still at work.
