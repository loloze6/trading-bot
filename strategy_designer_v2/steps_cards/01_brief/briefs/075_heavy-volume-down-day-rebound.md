After a down day, a coin's rise over the next two days grows with how heavy that day's volume was against its own recent volume. The size of the day's fall is not what matters here. Volume alone tells a down day that partly reverses from one that does not, and this holds in any market. Heavy volume on an up day carries no such follow-through, so the effect lives on the down side only. It is spent within about two days.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. Unusually heavy volume on a down day marks sellers who had to sell at once: margin calls, liquidations, stop-loss cascades, risk limits being hit. They accept a discount to get out quickly. The buyers who absorb that flow, such as market makers and patient dip-buyers, take on inventory they did not want. They are paid by a price that recovers once the forced flow stops, and it stops within a day or two. A down day on thin volume is instead ordinary, unforced selling with no discount to repay, so nothing pulls the price back. Forced flow in spot crypto is mostly one-sided, from levered longs, which would explain why heavy-volume up days show no matching reversal.

The theory is wrong if heavy-volume down days are followed by no rise, or by further falls, over the next two days. It is also wrong if the rise does not grow with volume, or if heavy-volume up days reverse just as much. That last case would point to a general liquidity effect, not to forced selling.
