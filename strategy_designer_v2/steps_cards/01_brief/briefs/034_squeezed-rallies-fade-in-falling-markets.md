In a falling market, a coin's up day is followed by a decline over the next few days. The larger the up day is compared with the coin's usual daily swing over the past month, the larger that decline. So a sharp rally inside a downtrend is a temporary displacement, not a turn.

The source reports the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In a falling market, much of a sudden large up day comes from buying that is forced or one-off rather than from new demand. Short sellers and leveraged shorts are squeezed and cover. Stops above recent prices fire. Thin order books let that burst move the price far. Once the covering is done, nobody is left to keep buying. Holders who wanted out, and sellers who were waiting, meet the higher price, and the downtrend's selling resumes. The bigger the jump compared with the coin's normal swing, the larger the share of it that was forced and unsupported, so the more of it is given back.

This lives in a falling market because that is where shorts pile up, waiting sellers are many and real buyers are scarce. In a rising market a large up day draws real buyers in, so it need not reverse.

The theory is wrong if, in a falling market, up days are followed by no decline or by further rises over the next few days. It is also wrong if the decline does not grow with the size of the up day measured in the coin's own swing. It is wrong as a falling-market theory if large up days reverse just as much in rising markets.
