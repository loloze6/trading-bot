In a calm market, a coin that has moved a lot over the last several days, measured against its own recent daily swings, keeps moving the same way over the following days. The larger the move in those swing units, the further it continues, and this holds for rises and falls alike.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason for it. The reason here is mine. When a coin is swinging less than usual, few traders are being forced out, so a move that is large for that coin is unlikely to be noise or a liquidation flush. It more likely reflects deliberate repositioning on new information. That repositioning spreads slowly. Holders who anchor on the quiet range react late. Traders who size by recent volatility, and see the move as large, add to it with a delay. Large holders spread their orders over days. All of these flows push the same way after the move has begun.

In an agitated market, large moves are more often forced buying or selling that overshoots and is partly reversed. That reversal offsets any slow drift, which would explain why the effect lives in a calm market only.

The theory is wrong if, in a calm market, the days after a move that is large in the coin's own swings show no further drift its way or a give-back. It is also wrong if the size of the move does not order what follows. The claim that it lives only in calm markets is wrong if the same continuation appears just as strongly in an agitated market.
