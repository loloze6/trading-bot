In a rising market, the day that follows a Thursday or Friday close, that is, Friday and Saturday, gains more than the coin's usual day. The gain is larger the stronger the coin's rise over the past two months. On other days of the week no such extra gain appears, and outside rising markets these same days are ordinary.

The source reports the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine and is offered as a guess. At the end of the week, professional market makers and arbitrage desks thin their books and step back. Individual traders, who trade more as the weekend nears, make up a larger share of the flow. In a rising market, that flow leans toward buying: retail traders chase coins that have been going up and are drawn in by the attention a steep trend gets. Short sellers and hedgers may also cut their positions before a weekend they cannot watch, which adds buying. With fewer liquidity providers to absorb it, this one-sided demand moves price further than it would midweek. A steeper trend draws more attention and more chasing, so the effect scales with the trend. When no trend exists, there is nothing to chase and the late-week flow has no common direction.

The theory is wrong if, in rising markets, Friday and Saturday returns are no larger than the coin's usual day, if they do not grow with the strength of the two-month rise, or if other weekdays show the same extra gain.
