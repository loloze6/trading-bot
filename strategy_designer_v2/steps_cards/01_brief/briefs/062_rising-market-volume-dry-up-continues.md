In a rising market, a coin whose trading volume over the past month has shrunk against the month before goes on to rise more over the following days than one whose volume has held up or grown. The more the volume has shrunk, the larger the later rise. This holds whether the market is calm or agitated.

The source gives the reason: an uptrend that keeps going while volume dries up has no sellers left to stop it. I add the detail of who those sellers are. Holders who wanted to take profit or cut their position have already done so. The coins still held are in patient hands that do not sell into strength. The few buyers who keep arriving therefore move price easily through a thin offer.

Common chart reading says the opposite: falling volume in an uptrend is a warning that buying interest is fading and the rise is about to end. If that reading were right, shrinking volume would come before weaker rises or before declines.

The theory is shown wrong if, in rising markets:
- the coin's later rise does not grow as its volume shrinks; or
- coins on shrinking volume rise less than coins on steady or growing volume.
