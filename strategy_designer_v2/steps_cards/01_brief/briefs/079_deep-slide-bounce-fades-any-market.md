In any market, an up day that ends a run of down days is followed by a fall over the next few days. The size of that fall is set by how deep the preceding slide was, measured in the coin's usual daily swing. It is not set by how many days the slide lasted, and not by how large the up day itself was.

The claim comes from the source. The reason is mine, because the source gives none. A deep slide leaves many holders who bought during it, or just before it, sitting on a loss they did not plan for. Many of them do not sell into the falling market. They wait for the first relief to get out nearer their entry. The first up day is that relief, and their selling then takes back the rise and more. Depth matters more than length because the loss in swing units is what such a holder feels: a short, sharp slide can trap as many holders as a long, gentle one. The up day's own size matters little because the waiting supply is fixed by the slide, not by the bounce.

The theory is wrong if any of these hold:
- Up days ending deep slides are not followed by larger falls than up days ending shallow ones.
- The fall tracks the run's length or the up day's size rather than the slide's depth.
- Run-ending up days are followed by rises as often as by falls.
