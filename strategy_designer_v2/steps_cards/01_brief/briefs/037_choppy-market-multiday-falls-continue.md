In a choppy market, a coin that has fallen over the past several days keeps falling over the following days. The continuation is larger the more of the coin's own recent daily swings that fall spans. A small drift lower carries little forward. A fall of many swings carries more. The source states this only for choppy markets and finds it weaker when the coin is clearly rising or falling.

The source gives no reason; the one here is mine and uncertain. In a sideways market no strong trend gives holders a reason to buy weakness. Few dip buyers trust a rally that has not come, and few short sellers are crowded enough to be squeezed. A multi-day fall that is large for the coin may then come from holders leaving on their own news or loss of conviction. They sell in pieces over days, so the fall is not over when it first shows. In a rising market, dip buyers absorb such selling. In a falling market, much of it may already be in the price. In both cases the carry-over would be weaker.

This sits against the view that hurried, one-day overshoots rebound. Here the claim is about falls built over several days, not single sharp days.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy markets, multi-day falls large in swing units are followed on average by flat or rising prices. It is also wrong if what follows does not grow worse as the fall grows larger in swing units.
