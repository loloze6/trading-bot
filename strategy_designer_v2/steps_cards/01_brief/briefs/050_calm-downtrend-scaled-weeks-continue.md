In a falling, calm market, a coin's move over about a week that is large against its own recent daily swing keeps going the same way over the following days. The larger the move is in units of the coin's usual swing, the further it carries. This holds for unusual falls and for unusual rises alike.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason below is mine. A calm downtrend is a market without forced flow. No liquidation cascade overshoots the book, and no squeeze spends itself in a day. A large move there is therefore more likely news or a shift in holders' views than a temporary imbalance that liquidity providers are paid to undo.

Holders in a calm market adjust slowly. Those who size positions by risk see no swing spike that would make them act at once. Discretionary holders reassess in pieces. Trend followers need several days of confirmation before they act.

As a result, a fall that is unusual for the coin is followed by more orderly selling from holders who are late to give up. An unusual rise is followed by more buying from holders who are late to accept that the selling is over. In an agitated downtrend the same move is more often forced flow, which reverses. That is why the effect would live in the calm profile only.

The theory is wrong if, in falling, calm markets, large scaled moves are followed by no further move in their direction, or by a reversal. It is also wrong if the carry-on does not grow with the move's size.
