In a choppy market, the further a coin closes below its 20-day high, measured in units of its own recent daily swing, the more it tends to fall the next day. This should hold whether that day closed up or down. A deep close below the high is therefore a sign that the breakdown will continue, not that a rebound is due.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason; the reason here is mine. In a sideways market, many holders bought within the recent range, near its top, while expecting the range to hold. A close far below that top, large against the coin's usual swing, leaves them clearly underwater, and the market gives them no trend to wait for. Range traders who bought near the middle of the range see their reason to hold fail, and they sell. Stops placed below the range's middle and lower prices fire in turn. Because this selling comes from many separate holders reaching their own limits, it is spread over the following sessions. An up day does not change the picture. Holders who missed the earlier exit use the bounce to sell, so the pressure continues the next day.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy markets, closes deep below the 20-day high are followed by next-day rises, or by moves no worse than those after closes near the high. It is also wrong if the next-day fall does not grow with depth, or if it appears only after down days or only after up days.
