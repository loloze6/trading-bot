In a falling market, an up day is followed by a next-day fall that grows as the up day's rise shrinks against the coin's usual daily move. A timid bounce is the one that gives way most. A large bounce relative to the coin's swing is not followed by a fall of the same kind. The day's volume does not drive this: the fade appears whether the up day traded heavily or lightly.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason beyond ruling out volume. The reason that follows is mine. In a coin that has fallen for weeks, many holders are underwater and sell into any rise, and new buyers are scarce. A small rise shows a bid that could only nudge price while that supply stayed in place. The buying was spent without clearing the sellers, so the decline resumes the next day. A large rise more often comes from a short squeeze or real new demand that has, at least for a day, absorbed the waiting supply. Its next day is therefore not weak in the same way. I do not know why the behaviour would be confined to falling markets beyond the lack of buyers there.

The theory is wrong if any of these hold in a falling market:
- small up days are not followed by next-day falls beyond the coin's normal drift;
- the next-day fall does not grow as the rise gets smaller;
- large up days fade just as much as small ones.
