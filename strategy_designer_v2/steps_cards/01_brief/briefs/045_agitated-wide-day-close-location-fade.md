In an agitated market, where a coin closes within its day's high-to-low range points the opposite way to its next day. A close near the high is followed by a weaker next day, and a close near the low by a stronger one. The wider that day's range is against the coin's usual daily range of the past month, the stronger this reversal. On narrow days the close's place says little.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. In agitated markets much of a day's late push comes from flow that must trade now and does not care about price: stop-outs, margin calls, liquidations, and traders cutting or chasing risk after a big swing. That flow pins the close toward one end of the range, and the liquidity providers who take the other side want a discount for doing so. A wide day marks heavy forced flow and thin books, so the pin goes further from fair value. When the pressure is spent the next day, that discount is repaid, and more of it is repaid after a wide day. In calm markets the late push more often carries real information, so it is not given back.

The theory is wrong if, in agitated markets, a coin's next-day return does not fall as its close moves up its range. It is also wrong if this reversal does not grow with the day's range width, or if it is just as strong in calm markets.
