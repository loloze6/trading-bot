Question: in a falling, agitated market, when a coin closes at its lowest level of the past month, does it keep falling over the following days, or does it rebound?

The case for more falling: in a coin that has dropped for two months and swings more than usual, a fresh monthly low breaks the floor that recent dip buyers and leveraged longs leaned on. Their stops and margin calls sit just below it. The forced sales push price lower and set off the next layer of stops. Holders who have been underwater for weeks may also give up once the last visible floor fails. On this view, a new low in this market is a cascade that feeds itself.

The case for a rebound: by the time a falling, agitated coin makes a new monthly low, most weak holders and over-leveraged longs may already have been forced out. Sellers who chose to sell have mostly done so. The new low may be the last of the forced selling, not the start of more. When that pressure stops, buyers who stood aside in the high swings face a thinner order book, and price snaps back. On this view, high agitation marks a market near exhaustion.

The calm or rising markets do not carry the same forced selling. That is why the question is asked only in the falling, agitated profile.

Either answer would be shown wrong if, in this profile, coins after a new monthly low moved no differently from coins on other days in the same profile.
