Where a coin closes within its own day's high-low range predicts the next day in the opposite direction. A close near the day's low is followed by a rise over the next day. A close near the high is followed by a weaker or falling next day. The effect is graded with the close's position in the range, and it fades within a day or two.

The source reports the pattern but gives no reason for it. The reason below is mine. A close pinned near the low means sellers who had to trade were still hitting bids into the end of the day. These could be stop orders, liquidations, or holders cutting risk before the day ends. Their selling pushes the price below the level patient buyers would pay. Market makers and patient buyers absorb that flow at a discount. They are paid when the pressure stops and the price drifts back the next day. A close near the high is the mirror case: late chasing buyers pay up, and the next day gives part of it back. Because the push is a short-lived imbalance and not news, the correction is quick and does not last for days.

The theory is wrong if next-day returns after closes near the low are no higher than after closes near the high. It is also wrong if a close near the low is followed by further falls, which would mean the late selling carried information and not just pressure.
