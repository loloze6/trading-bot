In a coin that has been falling for months, sharp rises do not last: a bounce of several usual daily moves tends to be given back over the next days, and the coin resumes its fall.

The reason is mine. In a long fall, many holders are under water and want to get out at a better price; every bounce is the chance they wait for, and they sell into it. Short sellers who were squeezed by the bounce come back once it stalls. So the supply that meets a bounce in a falling market is much larger than the demand that made it.

The theory is wrong if, in coins falling over the past months, sharp rises are not followed by falls over the next days, or if they are followed by the same moves as sharp rises in rising markets.
