In a choppy, calm market, a one-day rise that is large against the coin's own recent daily swing keeps rising over the following days, about a week. The larger the rise is in units of that swing, the larger the further rise.

The source states the behaviour but gives no reason; the reason here is mine. In a coin that is quiet and going nowhere, few traders hold a view and little is being forced through the book. No leverage is being flushed, and range traders sell the top of a calm range. A rise that is large for such a coin is therefore unlikely to be forced flow, which would be repaid. It more likely reflects a buyer with information or conviction. Others take it in slowly. Range sellers first lean against it and then step aside. Holders who ignore a dormant coin notice it only once it has moved and been talked about. Trend followers need more than one day before they act.

In a rising, calm market the same day carries less news, because buyers are already positioned and the rise was expected. That is why the effect should be weak or absent there. In an agitated market, large days are more often forced and tend to reverse.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy calm markets, large scaled one-day rises are followed by no further rise or by a give-back. It is also wrong if the further rise does not grow with the size of the rise. Finding the same behaviour just as strong in rising calm markets would contradict the reason given.
