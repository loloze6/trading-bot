In a falling, agitated market, the deeper a coin's fall over the past several days, the more it rises the next day. The depth of the multi-day slide, not the size of the last day alone, sets the size of the rebound. A small down day that ends a deep slide still rebounds, while a large down day after a shallow slide rebounds little.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason for it. The reason here is mine. In an agitated downtrend, leveraged and swing-sized holders are pushed out over several days, not in one session. Each further down day triggers more margin calls, stops and risk cuts. The buyers who absorb this selling take on more inventory each day and ask a bigger discount as the pile grows. The longer and deeper the slide, the more of the forced supply has already been sold and the more stretched the absorbers are. Once the selling pauses, price springs back toward where unforced buyers value the coin, and it springs back more the more was forced out. In a calm or rising market the selling is mostly voluntary, so no overshoot builds and there is nothing to repay.

The theory is wrong if, in a falling, agitated market, the next-day rise does not grow with the depth of the multi-day fall. It is also wrong if the single day's fall explains the rebound and the multi-day depth adds nothing. It is wrong as well if the same relation appears just as strongly in the other profiles.
