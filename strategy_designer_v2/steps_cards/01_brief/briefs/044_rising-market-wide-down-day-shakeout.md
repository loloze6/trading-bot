In a rising market, a day that closes down after a high-low range much wider than the coin's usual daily swing is a shakeout, not the start of a fall. Over the next few days the coin rises, and the wider that day's range against its usual swing, the larger the rise.

The source gives the shakeout reading. The reason below is mine. In an uptrend, many holders are long on leverage or have stops packed below recent prices. A sudden push down sets off those stops and margin calls in a cascade. The cascade drives price well below what willing sellers would accept. The day's range grows wide because this selling is forced, not informed. Underneath, the uptrend's buyers are still there: holders who missed earlier entries and traders waiting for a dip. They take the forced supply at a discount. Once the forced sellers are gone, nothing is left to hold price down, so it recovers what the cascade pushed out. A wider range means a deeper cascade and a larger overshoot to repay. A wide day that closes up has had no forced long selling, so it should carry no such rebound. In a falling market, dip-buyers are scarce, so the same flush is not absorbed.

The theory is wrong if, in a rising market, wide down-closing days are followed by no rise or by further decline. It is also wrong if the rise does not grow with the range's width against the usual swing, or if wide up-closing days are followed by the same rise.
