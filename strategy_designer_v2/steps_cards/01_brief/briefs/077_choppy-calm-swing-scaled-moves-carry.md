In a choppy, calm market, a coin's move over the past couple of weeks, measured against its own recent daily swing, carries on the same way over the following week. It works up and down, and it is graded: the larger the move in swing units, the further it carries. Moves of ordinary size carry nothing.

The pattern comes from the source. The reason is mine and is a conjecture; the source gives none.

Many systematic holders size their positions by volatility. When a coin's daily swing is small, a given move becomes a strong signal for them and their positions are large. These traders do not all act at once. Their signals cross their thresholds on different days and they spread their orders out, so a move that is large against a small swing draws buying or selling that is still arriving days later.

In a calm market there is little forced flow, such as liquidations or margin calls. A large move is therefore more likely to reflect deliberate repositioning than an overshoot that liquidity providers must be repaid for, so nothing pulls it back. In an agitated market the same move is swollen by forced, information-free trading that reverts, and volatility-sized holders shrink their positions, so the carry should vanish there. A choppy market has no prevailing trend whose followers are already positioned, which leaves a fresh move to be adopted.

The theory is wrong if, in choppy, calm markets, the coin does not keep moving the same way after large swing-scaled moves. It is also wrong if it reverses, if the carry does not grow with the move's size, or if it holds in only one direction.
