Question: on a day of very heavy volume but little net price change, does the close's position within the day's high-low range show which side absorbed a large order, and so which way the coin moves next?

The case for yes: heavy volume with a flat result means a large one-sided order met an equally large patient counterparty. If price was pushed down during the day but closed near the high, buyers soaked up the selling and held the level. A seller who has finished is a source of pressure that is gone, and a buyer who has built a position tends to defend it. A close near the low suggests the reverse. That favours a later move toward the side of the close. Reason from me, not a source.

The case for reversal: a close at one end of the range may be the last push of the aggressive side, for example a large seller finishing near the close. Liquidity providers who took the other side then unwind, and price drifts back the other way.

The case for no information: daily ranges in crypto are often set by brief wicks from stop runs or thin overnight books. The close may be a near-random point inside a range made by noise, and what was absorbed may be invisible in where the day happened to end.

The theory is wrong in either direction if, after such days, the coin's following moves show no link to where the close sat in the range. It is also wrong if that link is no stronger than after ordinary-volume days with the same close position.
