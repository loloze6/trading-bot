In a calm market, a coin that has fallen a long way over the past month keeps falling over the following week. The deeper the monthly fall, the deeper the further fall. This should hold whatever the trend, as long as the coin's recent swings are no larger than usual for it.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason. The reason here is mine. A deep fall with quiet swings is not a panic. It looks like orderly distribution: holders who decided to leave and are selling in pieces on a schedule. These may be funds trimming, project or early holders selling unlocked supply, or underwater holders selling into every pause. Because nobody is forced out, there is no liquidation flush that clears the supply at once, and the sellers still have inventory left after a month. Dip-buyers who wait for a capitulation day to buy never get one, so they stay out. The selling then meets thin demand for days more.

In an agitated market, the same monthly fall more often comes from forced selling. That selling ends abruptly and is repaid, so continuation should be weaker or absent there.

The theory is wrong if, in calm markets, coins after deep monthly falls do no worse over the next week than coins after shallow falls or flat months. It is also wrong if they do better. It is weakened if the continuation is as strong when the coin is agitated as when it is calm.
