Question: when a coin closes in the same direction several days in a row, does it then give back part of that run over the following days, or keep going? And does the follow-on move, whichever way it goes, grow with how unusually long the streak is for that coin?

Reversal could come from crowding. A long streak in one direction draws in late buyers or sellers who pay for immediacy. Holders take profit, and leveraged positions pile up on one side. Once that one-sided flow runs out, the price drifts back as market makers and contrarians are paid for having taken the other side. On this view, a longer streak means more stretched positioning and a larger giveback.

Continuation could come from slow information and herding. Each extra close in the same direction is a visible signal. It pulls in trend followers and attention-driven traders who act late. It can also force those positioned against the run to cover. On this view, a longer streak means news is still being absorbed and more flow is still to come.

Both reasons are mine, not from a source. Which force wins may differ between up and down streaks, and between coins, and I do not know which dominates.

The claim that size grows with streak length would be shown wrong if the moves after streaks far longer than a coin's usual are no larger, in either direction, than the moves after ordinary streaks. The question as a whole would be answered "neither" if what follows a streak, of any length, looks no different from what follows any other day.
