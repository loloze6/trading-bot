In a rising, agitated market, an up day whose high-to-low range is wide against the coin's usual daily swing is followed by further rise over the next few days. The wider the range, the larger the rise. Within the source, the effect is tied to up days and to this profile only. Wide down days in the same market do not carry the effect, and wide days in other markets show none.

The source states the pattern but gives no reason for it. The reason here is mine. In a market already rising but swinging more than usual, a wide up day shows forceful buying that swept through resting offers and stops. That buying brings in people who act in stages: short sellers caught by the sweep cover over the following days, and trend followers and chasers notice the outsized day only after it closes. Holders whose position size depends on recent swings can also add once the push confirms the trend.

A rising trend gives these late buyers a direction to join. Agitation means more leverage and wider stops are in play, so a sweep forces more of them out. In a calm or falling market the same wide day draws no such crowd, or it meets supply.

The theory is wrong if, in rising agitated markets, any of these holds:
- wide up days are followed by no rise, or by a fall;
- the later rise does not grow with the day's width;
- wide down days predict the next days just as well, which would point to plain volatility rather than demand arriving in stages.
