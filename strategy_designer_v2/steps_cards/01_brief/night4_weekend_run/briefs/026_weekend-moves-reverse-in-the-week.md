Price moves made during the weekend, when trading is thin, tend to be partly reversed once the week's liquidity returns, over Monday and Tuesday.

The reason is mine. On weekends, the large desks and the market makers who provide most of the liquidity trade less, so a modest order moves the price further than it would during the week. When they return, they price the coin again with full depth and lean against the moves that were made without them.

Because the reversal plays out over a day or two, it is held long enough for its move to be large next to the cost of a trade.

The theory is wrong if weekend moves are not followed by moves in the opposite direction early in the week, or if moves of the same size made during the week are reversed just as much.
