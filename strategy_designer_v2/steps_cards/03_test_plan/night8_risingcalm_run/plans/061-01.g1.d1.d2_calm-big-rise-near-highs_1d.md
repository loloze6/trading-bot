Fidelity:
- Situation: a one-day rise, measured against the standard deviation of the past 30 days' daily returns, that takes the close near its highest close of the past 20 days, in a choppy, calm market -> kept: the profile is added by code; the rise is the 1-day return over the 30-day standard deviation, and closeness weights the rise through proximity to the 20-day closing high.
- Comparison: larger rises and closer-to-high rises versus smaller ones and bounces far below the high -> kept: the forecast grows with the rise size and with closeness to the high.
- Outcome: the coin keeps rising over the next 5 days -> kept: 5-day forward return, long side, held for 5 bars.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is zero off rise days and grows with the rise in volatility units and with nearness to the 20-day high, so a 5-day forward return that grows with it is the idea's own claim.
Forecast: The forecast is the equal-weight mean of two graded, long-only components. Each is standardised over its past 60 values (min 20), then clipped at 0. - (a) Rise in volatility units: VolatilityScaledReturnComponent (period 1, vol_period 30), the day's return divided by the standard deviation of the last 30 daily returns. - (b) Rise weighted by closeness: HighProximityMomentumComponent (period 20, return_period 1, proximity "convex", power 2), the day's percent return times (1/(1+D))², where D is the percent distance below the highest close of the last 20 days. The weight is 1 at a new 20-day high and shrinks for bounces well below the high. The forecast is exactly 0 on down and flat days. It is positive on up days, larger the bigger the rise against the usual 30-day swing and the closer the close sits to its 20-day high. It is never negative. Code keeps the book flat outside the choppy-calm profile.
