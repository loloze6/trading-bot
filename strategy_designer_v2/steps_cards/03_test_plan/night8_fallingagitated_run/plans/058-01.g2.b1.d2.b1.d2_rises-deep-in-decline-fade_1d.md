Forecast: The forecast has two components with equal weight. Each is set to zero on down days. Both are short, so the forecast is never above 0. - Component (a) is the day's return divided by the 30-day standard deviation of daily returns. It is standardised over 60 bars, clipped at 0 (rises only) and negated. The short grows with the rise in usual moves, up to the cap of 20. - Component (b) multiplies the day's rise in usual moves, capped at one usual move, by the percent depth of the close below its 20-day closing high. It is standardised over 60 bars and negated. The short grows with the depth for rises up to one usual swing and stays flat in the rise beyond that. The forecast is the mean of (a) and (b). It is 0 on down and flat days. On up days it is negative and grows with the size of the rise and with the depth below the high. Code adds the falling-agitated profile around it, so the book is flat outside that profile.

- Main measure: The unusual move over the cases, before costs. - A case is a short bet: a fall of the forecast of at least 2 points. - For each case, take the coin's next-day return minus the coin's mean daily return over all windows, with its sign flipped so that a fall counts as positive. - Cases on the same day are summed into one day, and t is computed over days.
- Supporting check: Compare the cases whose |forecast| is above the median with those below it. The unusual move of the larger cases minus that of the smaller cases must be above 0 (Welch t).
