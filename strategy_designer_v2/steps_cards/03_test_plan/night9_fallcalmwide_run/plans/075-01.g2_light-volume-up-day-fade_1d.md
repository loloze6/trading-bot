Fidelity:
- Situation: a day the coin rises, graded by how light its volume is -> adapted: the up day (close above the prior close) is a regime; inside it the size grows as the day's volume falls below its average.
- Comparison: the day's volume against its own average daily volume over the past 20 days -> kept: the day's volume over the mean of the prior 20 days' volumes.
- Outcome: a larger fall over the next day, short -> kept: a short held 1 daily bar, larger when the volume is lighter.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the test bets exactly the idea's graded short on thin-volume up days over its one-day horizon, so its confirm or reject is the idea's.
Forecast: A regime rule marks up days, where the 1-day return is above 0. Every other day is flat. On an up day, the volume ratio is the day's volume over the mean volume of the prior 20 days, minus 1. It is standardised against its last 60 values and clipped at 0 from above. The forecast is 0 when the volume is at or above its 20-day average. It is negative (short) when the volume is below that average, and the short grows as the volume gets lighter. It is never positive.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: The cases are bars with forecast at or below -2. For each case, take the coin's next-bar return minus its normal daily drift (the mean over all windows), in the short direction. The measure is the mean of these values, with a t that counts the cases of one day together.
- Supporting check: Split the cases into thirds by abs(forecast). The mean short-direction unusual move of the top third, minus that of the bottom third, must be above 0.
- Confirm: The main measure's mean is positive with t ≥ 2, and the supporting check passes.
- Reject: The main measure's mean is negative with t ≤ -2.
- Inconclusive: Any other result is inconclusive: a t between -2 and 2, or a confirming t with a failed supporting check. The test needs at least 300 distinct case days across all coins and windows; with fewer, it is inconclusive.
