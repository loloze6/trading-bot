- Situation: in a falling market, a coin's one-day rise or fall against its usual daily swing over the last 20 days -> kept: the 1-bar return over the std of the last 20 daily returns, with the falling profile added by code.
- Comparison: the move against its usual daily swing over the last 20 days -> kept
- Outcome: over the next day, a fall after a rise and a rise after a fall, larger the larger the move -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast fades each day's move in proportion to its size in usual swings, inside the falling profile, for one day.
Forecast: The forecast uses `VolatilityScaledReturnComponent` (`period` 1, `vol_period` 20): today's return divided by the standard deviation of the last 20 daily returns. It is standardised against its own past 60 values, then negated. The forecast is short after an up day and long after a down day, and it is zero only on a flat day. Its size grows linearly with the move measured in usual daily swings, capped at ±20. Code keeps the book flat outside the falling profile.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: On every recorded bar with a nonzero forecast, compute the forecast (post_pipeline_value) x (next-day return minus the coin's mean daily return over all windows). Sum these per calendar day across coins, then take the t of the mean of those daily sums.
- Supporting check: The book's mean daily return before costs, minus its gain from the market's own rise (the timing part), is above 0.
- Confirm: The main measure's t is 2 or more, and the timing part is above 0.
- Reject: The main measure's t is -2 or less.
- Inconclusive: A t between -2 and 2, or a confirming t whose timing part is not above 0. The test also needs at least 200 distinct days carrying a nonzero forecast; with fewer, the result is inconclusive.
