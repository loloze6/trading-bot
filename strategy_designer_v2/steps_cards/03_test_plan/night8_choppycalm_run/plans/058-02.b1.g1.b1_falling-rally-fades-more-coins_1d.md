Fidelity:
- Situation: a coin's one-day rise, against its usual daily swing over the last 20 days, in a falling market -> kept (the profile "falling" is added by code)
- Comparison: the rise's size against the 20-day standard deviation of daily returns -> kept
- Outcome: a fall over the next day, larger the sharper the rise -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the idea's own scaled rise, shorted for its own one-day horizon, inside its own profile.
Forecast: The forecast uses `VolatilityScaledReturnComponent` with `period` 1 and `vol_period` 20. Its raw value is today's return divided by the standard deviation of the last 20 daily returns. It is standardised against its own past 60 values (`min_periods` 20). It is then clipped at 0 from below, so only rises count, and negated. The forecast is zero on down and flat days. On up days it is negative, and it grows in size with the rise measured in usual daily moves, up to -20. It is never positive. The code keeps the book flat outside the falling profile.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: Take the bars with forecast < 0, each a case. For each case, take the coin's next-bar return minus that coin's mean daily return over all its windows. Average these excess returns weighted by abs(forecast). The t uses deviations summed per day.
- Supporting check: The least-squares slope of that excess next-bar return on abs(forecast), over the same cases, is below 0.
- Confirm: The weighted mean excess return is negative with t ≤ -2, on at least 100 cases over at least 60 distinct days, and the slope is below 0.
- Reject: The weighted mean excess return is positive with t ≥ +2, on at least 100 cases over at least 60 distinct days.
- Inconclusive: Any t between -2 and +2 is inconclusive. A confirming t with a slope at or above 0 is inconclusive. Fewer than 100 cases or fewer than 60 days is inconclusive.
