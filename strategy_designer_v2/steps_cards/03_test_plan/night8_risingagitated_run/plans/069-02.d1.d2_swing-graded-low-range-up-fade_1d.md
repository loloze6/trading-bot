- Situation: in a falling market, a coin closes up on the day well below its 20-day high -> kept: the falling market is the profile; "up on the day" and "low in its 20-day high-low range" (lower fifth, the stochastic 20 convention, from the brief's "near the bottom of its range") form a regime.
- Comparison: the 30-day daily swing against its usual swing over the past year -> kept: the 30-day volatility over its 365-day mean sizes the short.
- Outcome: a fall over the next day, larger the more agitated the coin -> kept: short only, 1-day horizon, graded by the volatility ratio.

Forecast: The regime detector selects `mean_reversion` only on bars that meet two conditions. The 1-day return must be above 0. The close must sit in the lower fifth of its 20-day high-low range, which is a Donchian position (sf 1, high_low, bar included) below -0.6. Every other bar is flat. Inside the regime, the forecast is built from the 30-day standard deviation of daily returns divided by its mean over the past 365 days (`VolatilityRatioToAverageComponent`, 30/365). This ratio is standardised over 365 bars (min_periods 91), so it reads about 10 when the ratio is at its usual level. It is then damped with `signed_power` exponent 2, so quiet coins get small forecasts and agitated ones large forecasts. Last, it is negated. The forecast is therefore always zero or negative, which keeps the book short only. A ratio of 0.7 gives about -5 and a ratio of 1.4 gives about -20.

- Main measure: Over the case bars (forecast < 0), the least-squares slope of the next-day excess return on the size of the forecast (abs(forecast)). The excess return is close[t+1]/close[t] - 1 minus the coin's mean daily return over all its windows, taken in the short direction (negated), and it is measured inside each window.
- Supporting check: The mean short-direction next-day excess return over all cases is above 0.
- Confirm: Both must hold: the slope is positive with t ≥ 2, and the supporting check passes (mean above 0).
- Reject: The slope is negative with t ≤ -2: larger forecasts are followed by smaller falls or by rises.
- Inconclusive: Any slope with t between -2 and 2 is inconclusive. So is a slope with t ≥ 2 whose mean excess return is at or below 0. Fewer than 100 cases is inconclusive whatever the result.
