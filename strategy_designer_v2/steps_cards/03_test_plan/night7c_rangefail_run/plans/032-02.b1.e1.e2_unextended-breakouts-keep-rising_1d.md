Fidelity:
- Situation: a daily close above the prior 60-day closing high -> kept: the "trending" regime fires only on a bar whose close is above every one of its prior 60 closes.
- Comparison: the smaller the return over the 20 days before the break -> adapted: graded inside the break, the position is larger the smaller the prior 20-day return, and zero once that run-up reaches one usual move ("refusing" strong climbs).
- Outcome: a larger rise over the next 20 days -> kept: each bet is held 20 bars, long only.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the book holds only upside breaks, sized by how little the coin ran up before them, so the size-to-gain relation over 20 bars is the idea itself.
Forecast: On a bar that closes above its prior 60 daily closes, the forecast is (10 + max(-10, -x)) / 2. Here x is the 20-day return that ended the day before the break, counted in usual moves (20-day standard deviation, square-root-of-time scaled) and then standardised. A flat run-up gives about +5. A prior fall gives up to +15. A run-up of about one usual standardised move or more gives 0. The forecast is never negative. On every other bar it is 0, and the 20-bar holding period keeps each break's bet.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 20 bars
- Main measure: Cases are the break bars (regime "trending"), counted once per coin and window, skipping any break within 20 bars of the last counted one. The measure is Welch's t of the 20-bar return from the break's close minus the coin's mean 20-bar return over all windows. It compares cases with forecast above the cases' median against cases at or below it, in that order.
- Supporting check: The forecast-weighted mean of the cases' 20-bar excess returns is above 0.
- Confirm: The main t is 2 or more, and the forecast-weighted mean excess return is above 0.
- Reject: The main t is -2 or less (low-run-up breaks rise less than high-run-up breaks).
- Inconclusive: A t between -2 and 2, or a confirming t with a supporting check that fails. The test needs at least 60 non-overlapping break cases, with at least 25 in each half; with fewer it is inconclusive.
