Fidelity:
- Situation: a coin's one-day fall, measured in its own usual daily move (30-day volatility) -> kept
- Comparison: the fall against the coin's 30-day volatility of daily returns -> kept
- Outcome: the coin rises back over the next day, more after a bigger fall; no short into the drop -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the fall in 30-day usual moves, long only, held one day, so its next-day payoff is exactly the idea's claim.
Forecast: The forecast uses one indicator, VolatilityScaledReturnComponent with period 1, vol_period 30 and lag 0. It is today's return divided by the standard deviation of the last 30 daily returns. The forecast is standardised over 60 bars (min_periods 20), then negated, then clipped at 0. It is positive only on a down day, and it grows linearly with the size of the fall in usual daily moves: a fall of about one usual move gives roughly +12, and the cap is +20. It is exactly 0 on up and flat days, and it never goes short.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: Use the bars with forecast > 0, every bar of each window (1-bar grid). Take each bar's next-bar excess return: close[t+1]/close[t] − 1 minus the coin's mean daily return over all its windows. Regress this excess return on the bar's forecast and take the slope. Its t uses standard errors clustered by day, so cases on several coins on the same day count as one.
- Supporting check: On the same bars, the forecast-weighted mean of the next-bar excess return is above 0, with a day-clustered t ≥ 2.
- Confirm: The slope is positive with t ≥ 2 and the supporting check passes.
- Reject: The slope is negative with t ≤ −2, or the forecast-weighted mean excess return is negative with t ≤ −2.
- Inconclusive: Any other result, including t between −2 and 2 on either measure. At least 300 bars with forecast > 0, spread over at least 200 distinct days, are needed; fewer is inconclusive.
