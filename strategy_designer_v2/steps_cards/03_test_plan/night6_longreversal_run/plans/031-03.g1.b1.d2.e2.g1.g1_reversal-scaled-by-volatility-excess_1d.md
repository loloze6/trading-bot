- Situation: XMR's one-day return, measured against its 30-day daily volatility, the larger the stronger -> kept: a 1-bar return divided by the 30-bar standard deviation of returns, graded.
- Comparison: 30-day volatility against its usual level over the past six months -> kept: the 30-bar volatility over its 180-bar mean multiplies the scaled move, graded.
- Outcome: price moves back the other way over the next day, more when volatility is elevated -> kept: a contrarian position held 1 bar, sized by the product.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the idea's graded fade, so a confirmed or rejected next-day gain per unit of forecast confirms or rejects the idea.
Forecast: `VolatilityScaledReturnComponent` uses `period` 1, `vol_period` 30 and `vol_lookback` 180. Its value is the day's return divided by the mean 30-day volatility over the last 180 days. That equals the return in usual 30-day moves times the ratio of current volatility to its six-month mean. It is standardised over 180 bars (min_periods 45) and then negated. The forecast is short after an up day and long after a down day, and it is 0 for a flat day. Its size grows with the move in usual moves and with how far volatility sits above its six-month norm. It shrinks when volatility is subdued. The forecast applies on every bar, with no regime.
