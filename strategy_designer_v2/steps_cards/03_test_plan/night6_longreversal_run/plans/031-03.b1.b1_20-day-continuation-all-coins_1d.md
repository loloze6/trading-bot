Fidelity:
- Situation: a coin's 20-day return, larger or smaller, up or down -> kept
- Comparison: a larger 20-day return against a smaller one -> adapted: graded, so the position grows with the size of the return
- Outcome: the price moves further the same way over the next 10 days -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the 20-day return itself, held 10 days, so its forward gain tests the claim directly.
Forecast: The forecast is the coin's 20-bar percent return (`PriceEvolutionComponent`, period 20), standardised against its own last 60 values, so a usual-sized move gives about ±10, capped at ±20. It is positive after a rise and negative after a fall, and grows in step with the size of the move. It is zero only when the 20-day return is zero. There is no regime gate, because the idea is a steady relation on every bar.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 10 bars
- Main measure: The measure uses the non-overlapping 10-bar grid of each window. For each case, multiply forecast/10 by the coin's excess 10-bar return: close[t+10]/close[t]−1 minus the coin's mean 10-bar return over all its windows. Take the mean of that product over all cases. Its t counts all cases of one day as one piece of evidence.
- Supporting check: The cases in the top third by |forecast| must have a mean directional excess return above that of the bottom third (Welch difference, top minus bottom, estimate > 0). The directional excess return is the excess 10-bar return × sign(forecast).
- Confirm: The mean is positive with t ≥ 2, and the supporting check passes.
- Reject: The mean is negative with t ≤ −2: the coins move back against their 20-day move.
- Inconclusive: Any t between −2 and 2 is inconclusive. So is a confirming t whose supporting check fails. The minimum is 300 cases with a nonzero forecast, falling on at least 100 distinct days.
