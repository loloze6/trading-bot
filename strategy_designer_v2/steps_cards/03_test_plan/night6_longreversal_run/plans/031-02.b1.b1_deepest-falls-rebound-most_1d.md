Fidelity:
- Situation: a coin's 90-day return stands below its own usual 90-day move -> adapted: a regime holds the book only while the 90-day return is at least one usual 90-day move down, graded inside by depth.
- Comparison: the fall measured in usual 90-day moves, from the coin's own past -> kept: the 90-day return divided by the mean absolute 90-day return over the past 365 days.
- Outcome: price rebounds over the next 30 days, most strongly after the deepest falls -> kept: long held 30 bars, sized convexly by depth.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the test buys exactly these falls, sized by depth in usual moves, and judges the 30-day rebound against holding the coin.
Forecast: The forecast uses one indicator, the 90-day return in usual 90-day moves. The usual move is the mean absolute 90-day return over the last 365 days. The book is flat unless that reading is -1 or lower. Inside that regime, the reading is standardised against its own past 455 values and then negated, so it is positive and grows with the depth of the fall. It is then squared with sign kept (signed_power, exponent 2, pivot 10), so the deepest falls get much the largest positions. The forecast is never negative.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 30 bars
- Main measure: The unusual move per case: the coin's 30-bar return after each bet (a forecast rise of at least 2 points), minus the coin's normal 30-bar drift, before costs. Bets are read on a 30-bar grid within each window. The t is clustered by day.
- Supporting check: The cases are split at the median forecast. The mean unusual move of the upper half minus that of the lower half must be above 0.
- Confirm: The mean unusual move is positive with t ≥ 2, and the deeper half's mean unusual move exceeds the shallower half's.
- Reject: The mean unusual move is negative with t ≤ -2, which means the falls keep falling relative to holding the coin.
- Inconclusive: Any t between -2 and 2, or a confirming t whose depth check fails. At least 30 cases on at least 20 distinct days are required; with fewer, the result is inconclusive.
