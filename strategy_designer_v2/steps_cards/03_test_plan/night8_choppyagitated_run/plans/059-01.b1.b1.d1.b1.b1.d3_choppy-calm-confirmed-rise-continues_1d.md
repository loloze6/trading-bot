Fidelity:
- Situation: a coin's rise over 7 days, in multiples of its usual 7-day swing over the past 30 days, in a choppy calm market -> kept: the 7-day return over (30-day daily std x sqrt 7) is the brief's usual-swing scaling; only rises open a position (a regime); the profile is added by code.
- Comparison: the further rise is larger the more the coin kept rising in the first week after the signal -> adapted: graded, the first-week rise is added to the forecast, and a first-week fall adds nothing.
- Outcome: the coin rises in the second week after the signal, more the larger the scaled rise -> kept: the forecast is made at the first week's end, reading the signal week 7 bars back, and is held 7 bars.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the position exists only after a scaled weekly rise and is sized by it and by the first-week rise, so second-week gains that grow with it confirm the idea and losses that grow with it reject it.
Forecast: The forecast is computed at bar t, the end of the first week after the signal week. The signal is the 7-day return ending 7 bars ago (t-14 to t-7), divided by the daily-return std of the 30 days ending at t-7 times sqrt(7). The forecast exists only when that lagged scaled rise is above 0; otherwise the regime is null and the book is flat. Inside, the forecast is the equal-weight mean of two parts. The first is the lagged scaled rise, standardised (60/20) and clipped at 0. The second is the first-week 7-day return (t-7 to t), standardised (60/20) and clipped at 0. It is long only. It grows with the size of the scaled signal rise and with how much the coin kept rising in the first week. A first-week fall leaves only the signal part, which halves the position.
