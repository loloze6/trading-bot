Fidelity:
- Situation: in a choppy calm market, a coin's 7-day rise against its usual daily swing over 30 days -> kept: the profile comes from code, and the 7-day return is divided by the 30-day daily return std (the √7 constant cancels under standardise).
- Comparison: the larger the scaled rise, the larger the further rise -> adapted: the forecast grows with the scaled rise, and the measure is the slope of the return on the forecast.
- Outcome: further rise over the next 14 days, above the coin's drift -> kept: the 14-bar forward return minus the coin's mean 14-bar return.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the idea's scaled weekly rise, long only, inside its profile, held for its 14-day horizon.
Forecast: VolatilityScaledReturnComponent (period 7, vol_period 30) is the 7-day return divided by the std of the last 30 daily returns times √7. It is standardised over its past 60 values (min 20) to a typical size of 10, capped at 20, then clipped at 0. The forecast is positive and grows linearly with the scaled weekly rise. It is 0 after a flat or falling week. It is never short. Code keeps the book flat outside the choppy calm profile.

Profile: choppy calm (the idea): no position outside it, by step 3's code (common/market_profile.md)
Criterion:
- Horizon: 14 bars
- Main measure: the slope of the coin's 14-bar forward return minus its mean 14-bar return (over all its windows) on the forecast. It is measured on the non-overlapping 14-bar grid of each window, on cells with forecast > 0, with all coins pooled. Its t uses cases of one day taken together.
- Supporting check: the mean of that excess 14-bar return over the same cells is above 0.
- Confirm: the slope's t is 2 or more, and the mean excess return is above 0.
- Reject: the slope's t is -2 or less, or the mean excess return over the cells has t of -2 or less (a giveback).
- Inconclusive: any other result, for example a slope t between -2 and 2, or a positive slope with a mean excess return at or below 0. At least 100 grid cells with forecast > 0 are needed; with fewer the result is inconclusive.
