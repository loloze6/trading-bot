Fidelity:
- Situation: at Friday's close (UTC), a coin's 20-day return is a gain, and the larger the gain the stronger the situation -> kept: a Saturday-bar regime, with a long position graded by the 20-day gain clipped at 0.
- Comparison: a 20-day fall says nothing about Saturday, so there is no short -> kept: the forecast is exactly 0 when the 20-day return is not positive.
- Outcome: the coin rises over the next day, Saturday, more the larger the gain -> kept: the long is held from Friday's close to Saturday's close.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the book is long only on Friday closes after a 20-day gain, sized by that gain, and is judged on Saturday's return, which is exactly the claim.
Forecast: On the bar whose coming bar is a Saturday (UTC), the forecast is the 20-day percent return, standardised against its own past 60 daily values (target 10, capped at 20) and clipped at 0. It is positive and grows with the size of the 20-day gain. It is 0 when the coin fell over those 20 days, and it is 0 on every other weekday.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: The case is a Friday-close bar with a positive forecast. On each case, take Saturday's return minus the coin's mean daily return over all windows. The measure is the forecast-weighted mean of that excess, with its t, where cases on the same day are summed as one piece of evidence.
- Supporting check: Among the cases, the least-squares slope of Saturday's excess return on the forecast (`post_pipeline_value`) is above 0.
- Confirm: The weighted mean excess is positive with t ≥ 2, and the slope is positive.
- Reject: The weighted mean excess is negative with t ≤ -2.
- Inconclusive: Any t between -2 and 2 is inconclusive. A t of 2 or more with a slope at or below 0 is also inconclusive. The test needs at least 100 cases (Friday closes after a 20-day gain, all coins together), or it is inconclusive.
