Fidelity:
- Situation: a coin's 14-day rise in usual daily moves, at Friday's close (UTC) -> adapted: Friday's close is a regime (the coming bar is Saturday); the rise is graded, long only, as the idea's consequence says.
- Comparison: higher rises against smaller ones ("the higher ... the more") -> kept: the position grows with the volatility-scaled 14-day rise.
- Outcome: the coin rises over Saturday and Sunday together -> kept: the long is held 2 daily bars from Friday's close.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the test measures whether larger Friday 14-day rises are followed by larger weekend returns above the coin's drift, which is the idea itself.
Forecast: The forecast is computed only on the bar that closes at Friday's close UTC, the bar before a Saturday bar. Every other bar is flat. On that bar the forecast is the coin's 14-bar return divided by its usual move (VolatilityScaledReturnComponent, period 14, vol_period 20). It is standardised against its own past 60 values and clipped at 0. So it is zero after a 14-day fall and positive after a 14-day rise. It grows in proportion to the size of the rise in usual moves, up to the cap of 20. The book holds the bet through Saturday and Sunday (2 bars).

Criterion:
- Horizon: 2 bars
- Main measure: Use Friday-close bars (regime trending) with forecast > 0. Regress the coin's 2-bar return from that close, minus the coin's mean 2-bar return over all windows, on the forecast. Read the slope's t, with cases on the same day grouped.
- Supporting check: The forecast-weighted mean of the same excess 2-bar return is above 0.
- Confirm: The slope's t is 2 or more and the forecast-weighted mean excess return is above 0.
- Reject: The slope's t is -2 or less.
- Inconclusive: Any slope t between -2 and 2, or a confirming slope with a weighted mean excess return at or below 0. The test needs at least 100 cases with forecast > 0, spread over at least 50 distinct Fridays; with fewer it is inconclusive.
