- Situation: the daily close's place in that day's high-low range, on a day wider than usual, the effect growing with the width -> kept: a regime holds only on days wider than the 20-day average, and inside it the forecast is the signed close location times the range multiple.
- Comparison: the day's range against the coin's average daily range over the previous 20 days -> kept: the regime rule tests exactly this, and the forecast divides by the mean range of bars t-20 to t-1.
- Outcome: the coin rises over the next 1 day after a low close, and falls after a high close -> kept: the position is long after a low close and short after a high close, and the book holds it for 1 bar.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the test bets only on wide days, sized by close location times width, so its next-day result answers the idea directly.
Forecast: The regime detector computes MeanBarRangeRatioComponent with short_period 1, long_period 21 and form "inverse". This is the day's range over the mean range of bars t-20 to t. That ratio is above 1 exactly when the day's range exceeds the mean of the previous 20 days. Those bars are "mean_reversion"; every other bar is "unknown", which is flat. Inside the regime, RangeMultipleWeightedCloseLocationComponent with period 20 gives (2·close − high − low) / M, where M is the mean range of bars t-20 to t-1. It is positive for a close in the upper half and negative for one in the lower half, and its size grows with the distance from mid-range and with the day's width. It is standardised (60/20) and then negated. The forecast is therefore positive (long) after a low close, negative (short) after a high close, near 0 at mid-range, and larger the more extreme the close and the wider the day. It is 0 on ordinary and quiet days.
