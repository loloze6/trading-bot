Fidelity:
- Situation: where the daily close sits within that day's high-low range -> kept
- Comparison: lower in the range against higher in the range -> kept
- Outcome: the lower the close, the more the coin rises over the next 1 day; the higher, the more it falls -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the close's place in the day's range, inverted, and it is tested against next-day returns.
Forecast: The forecast uses `CloseLocationInRangeComponent` with `reference` "high". Its raw value is (high − close) / (high − low): 0 when the close is at the day's high and 1 when it is at the low. `standardise` (60/20) scales it against its own past mean size, about 0.5. That puts it near 20 × location, capped at 20. A standardised `BuyAndHoldStrategy` offset negated to −10 is added, at equal weight. The forecast is therefore (x − 10) / 2. It is graded from about −5 (close at the day's high: short) through 0 (close at mid-range) to about +5 (close at the day's low: long), and its size grows linearly with how far the close sits from the middle of the range. No regime is used: the idea is a steady relation on every bar.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: The pooled least-squares slope of each coin's next-day return, minus that coin's mean daily return over all windows, on the bar's forecast. It uses every bar of every coin and window, with no cross-window returns, and is read with its t.
- Supporting check: The mean before-cost position gain, minus its market-drift part (the position held × the coin's mean daily return), is above 0 on the book's daily returns.
- Confirm: The slope is positive with t ≥ 2 and the supporting check passes (timing gain above 0).
- Reject: The slope is negative with t ≤ −2.
- Inconclusive: Any slope with |t| < 2, or a slope with t ≥ 2 whose timing gain is not above 0. At least 500 bars with a non-zero forecast are needed; with fewer, the test is inconclusive.
