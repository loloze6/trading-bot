Forecast: Take the one-bar hourly return of BTC (PriceEvolutionComponent, period 1). Standardise it by the mean absolute one-bar return over the previous 60 bars (min 20), so a typical move scores ±10, capped at ±20. Then apply signed_power with exponent 2 and pivot 10, so sharp drops weigh far more than small ones (−5 becomes −2.5, −15 becomes −22.5). The bar is labelled thin when its volume is below its own 20-bar mean, and heavy otherwise. - On a thin bar, the forecast is the negated value with negative results clipped to 0. That is a long position that grows with the square of the drop's size, betting that the drop reverts. - On a heavy bar, the forecast is the value with positive results clipped to 0. That is a short position that grows with the square of the drop's size, betting that the drop holds or continues. - On any up bar the forecast is 0. - Each position lasts one bar and is re-set at every close. The portfolio therefore earns the reversion of thin drops minus the reversion of heavy drops, weighted by drop size.

Confirm: Horizon: 1 bar. Main measure, before costs: the sum over bars of position × (next-bar close-to-close return − that window's mean hourly return). Subtracting the mean return removes the coin's own drift, so the result is judged against holding the coin. Each bar is counted once, so returns do not overlap. The idea is confirmed if this excess P&L is positive in at least 5 of the 6 windows and the pooled per-bar t-statistic is at least 2.0. A supporting check guards against differences in drop size between the two groups: split the drop bars into terciles of |forecast|. In at least 2 of the 3 terciles, the mean next-bar excess return on thin drops (positive forecast) should exceed the mean on heavy drops (negative forecast, sign-flipped to the reversion direction).
Reject: The idea is rejected in either of two cases: - The pooled excess P&L is zero or negative, with a t-statistic of 0 or below, and positive in at most 3 of the 6 windows. - In the tercile check, heavy drops revert as much as or more than thin drops in at least 2 of the 3 terciles.
Inconclusive: Any of the following neither confirms nor rejects the idea: - A pooled t-statistic between 0 and 2.0. - A positive t-statistic of 2.0 or more but positive in only 4 of the 6 windows. - A main measure and tercile check that disagree.
Deviation: The test differs from the idea as written in five ways: - A "sharp downward move" is a single hourly bar's drop, scaled to its recent typical size, not a multi-bar move. - "Low or high volume" is a yes/no split of that bar's volume against its 20-bar mean, not a graded measure of volume. - "Moves of similar size" are handled by sizing positions on drop strength plus a tercile check, not by exact matching. - "Give back a larger share afterwards" is measured only over the next single bar. - "Heavy moves hold more" is expressed as a continuation short, so that the portfolio measures the difference between thin and heavy drops.
