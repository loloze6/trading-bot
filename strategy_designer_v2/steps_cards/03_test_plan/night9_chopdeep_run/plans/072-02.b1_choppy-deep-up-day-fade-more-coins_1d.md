- Situation: a choppy-market day that closed up, with the close far below the 20-day high in 20-day return-std units -> kept: the profile is added by code, the up close is a regime rule, and the depth is graded by the 20-day high distance in usual moves.
- Comparison: deep closes against closes near the high -> kept: the forecast is near zero at the high and grows more negative with depth.
- Outcome: the price falls over the next day -> kept: a short position held for 1 daily bar.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the test measures the idea's exact quantity, under its exact conditions, over its exact horizon.
Forecast: The forecast exists only on bars where the close is above the prior close (1-bar return above 0); on other bars the regime is null and the book is flat, and code adds the choppy profile on top. On up days the forecast is the distance of the close below the highest of the last 20 closes (bar t included), divided by the high and by the std of the last 20 daily returns. It is standardised over 60/20 and clipped at 0. It is therefore never positive: 0 when the close is at its 20-day high, about -10 at a typical depth, and down to -20 at depths twice the usual or more. The short grows linearly with depth.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: On bars with a nonzero forecast, take the next-day return minus the coin's mean daily return over all its windows. Pool all coins and split the bars into thirds of the forecast. Compute Welch's t of the deepest third (most negative forecast) minus the shallowest third.
- Supporting check: The deepest third's mean next-day excess return is below 0.
- Confirm: The difference has Welch t ≤ -2 and the supporting check passes (the deepest third's mean excess return is below 0).
- Reject: The difference has Welch t ≥ +2, meaning deep up-day closes are followed by better next days than shallow ones.
- Inconclusive: Any t between -2 and +2, or t ≤ -2 with the deepest third's mean at or above 0. The test needs at least 100 bars in each third (300 active bars).
