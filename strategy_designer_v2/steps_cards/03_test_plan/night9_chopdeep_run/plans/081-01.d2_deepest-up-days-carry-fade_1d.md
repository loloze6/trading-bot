- Situation: a day that closes up, deep below the 20-day high in 20-day swing units -> kept: the up day is a regime (bar's 1-day return above 0), and the depth is the close's distance below the 20-day closing high over the 20-day return std.
- Comparison: deeper versus shallower up days, while down days at equal depth say nothing -> kept: the graded depth sets the position size, and down days are flat (null regime).
- Outcome: a larger fall over the next day, growing faster than the depth -> kept: a short held for 1 bar, sized convexly in the depth (signed_power exponent 2).
- Verdict transfer: yes: the book shorts only up days, more than proportionally to the depth in swings, for one day, which is exactly the claim.
Forecast: The forecast exists only on up days, where the close is above the prior close. On every other bar it is 0. - The measure is VolatilityScaledDistanceFromHigh (period 20, vol_period 20, source close, high including today, base level). It is the close's distance below the 20-day closing high, in 20-day standard deviations of daily returns. It is 0 at the high and negative below it. - The measure is standardised over its past 60 values (min 20). Standardise only scales it, so the sign stays negative: the forecast is short. - signed_power (exponent 2, pivot 10) then makes the short convex. Shallow depths are damped towards 0 and deep ones are amplified, so the short grows faster than the depth. - The forecast is never positive.
