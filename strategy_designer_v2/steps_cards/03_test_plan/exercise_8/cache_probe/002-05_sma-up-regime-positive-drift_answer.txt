forecast: The percent spread between the 50-day and 200-day simple moving averages of the close, (SMA50 - SMA200) / SMA200 x 100. It is divided by the mean absolute spread of the previous 200 daily values and scaled so that an average spread gives a forecast of 10, capped at 20. The forecast is then clipped at 0 from below. It is positive on every bar where SMA50 is above SMA200, which is the stretch from an upward cross to the next downward cross, and it grows as the short average pulls further above the long one. It is zero on every bar where SMA50 is at or below SMA200, and it is never negative.

confirm: Horizon 1 bar, counted once per bar, before costs, pooled over all 6 windows. Main measure: mean next-day close-to-close return on bars with forecast > 0, minus the mean next-day return on all bars (buy-and-hold). The idea is confirmed if this difference is positive with a Welch t-statistic of at least 2.0, with at least 100 in-cross bars. Supporting check: the share of up days on in-cross bars is higher than the share on all bars.
reject: The idea is rejected if the difference in mean next-day return is zero or negative. It is also rejected, as the brief's reversal case, if the difference is negative with a t-statistic of -2.0 or below.
inconclusive: A positive difference with a t-statistic below 2.0. Also any result with fewer than 100 in-cross bars, or with every in-cross bar falling in one or two windows, so that one trend episode decides the outcome.
deviation: The position size follows the width of the SMA50-SMA200 spread rather than being held equally over the whole stretch, though the test reads only the sign. "From an upward cross until the next downward cross" is tested as "while SMA50 is above SMA200", which is the same set of bars. Only the long stretches are traded; down-cross stretches stay flat. The 50/200 settings are a convention because the brief gives none.
