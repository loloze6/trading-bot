- Situation: the coin has risen over the past 28 days -> kept: a regime rule requires the 28-day return to be above 0, and the book is flat outside that regime.
- Comparison: how close the close is to the highest close of those 28 days -> kept: 28-bar closing-high proximity, graded, larger nearer the high.
- Outcome: a larger further rise over the next 5 days -> kept: 5-bar forward return, long side, held 5 bars.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the test measures the same condition, nearness and horizon as the idea, so a confirm or a reject carries over directly.
Forecast: The regime is trending when the coin's 28-day close-to-close return is above 0. On every other bar the forecast is 0. Inside the trending regime the forecast is the 28-day closing-high proximity, 1/(1+D), where D is the percent distance of today's close below the highest of the last 28 closes, today included. This is standardised over its past 60 values. It is always positive: about +10 at the usual nearness, up to +20 at the high, and smaller the further the close has pulled back from the high. There are no shorts.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 5 bars
- Main measure: Use the bars of the trending regime on a non-overlapping 5-bar grid of each window. Regress each case's 5-bar forward excess return on its forecast. The excess return is the coin's 5-bar return minus that coin's mean 5-bar return over all windows. The measure is the slope's t, with cases from all coins pooled.
- Supporting check: In-regime grid cases whose forecast is in the top third have a mean 5-bar excess return above that of the bottom third (Welch t > 0).
- Confirm: The slope t is 2 or more and the supporting check passes.
- Reject: The slope t is -2 or less.
- Inconclusive: Any slope t between -2 and 2, or a slope t of 2 or more with the check failing. A test with fewer than 150 in-regime grid cases is also inconclusive.
