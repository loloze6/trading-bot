Fidelity:
- Situation: a coin that has risen over the past 28 days, with its close near its 28-day highest close -> kept: the 28-day rise is a regime, and the closeness is graded inside it.
- Comparison: closer to the 28-day high against further pulled back, among rising coins -> kept: the 28-day closing-high proximity sets the position's size.
- Outcome: a larger further rise over the next 5 days -> kept: the position is held 5 bars, long only.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the test reads the 5-day return against the 28-day high proximity, only inside 28-day uptrends, which is exactly what the idea claims.
Forecast: The forecast exists only when the coin's 28-day close-to-close return is above 0, which is the regime. On every other bar it is 0. Inside the regime it is the proximity to the 28-day highest close: 1 / (1 + D), where D is the percent distance of the close below that high. This proximity is standardised against its own past 60 values. The forecast is therefore always positive (long only). It is largest, up to 20, when the close sits at the high. It shrinks smoothly toward 0 as the close pulls back.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 5 bars
- Main measure: Use the bars in the trending regime on the non-overlapping 5-bar grid of each window. For each case, take the coin's 5-day return minus its normal 5-day drift (its mean over all windows). Compare the mean of these excess returns for the top third of forecast values against the bottom third, as a Welch t of top minus bottom.
- Supporting check: The top third's mean excess 5-day return is above 0.
- Confirm: The difference (top third minus bottom third) is positive with Welch t >= 2, and the top third's mean excess return is above 0.
- Reject: The difference is negative with Welch t <= -2.
- Inconclusive: Any t between -2 and 2. Also inconclusive: t >= 2 with the top third's mean excess return at or below 0. A result needs at least 30 cases in each third, otherwise it is inconclusive.
