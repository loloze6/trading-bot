Fidelity:
- Situation: the coin's fall on its latest day -> kept
- Comparison: that fall against the size of its own daily moves over the past 60 days -> kept
- Outcome: it rises over the next 2 days, more the larger the fall -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the latest day's fall in units of its 60-day daily volatility, zero on up days, so a 2-day rise that grows with it is the idea, and its absence refutes it.
Forecast: The forecast uses VolatilityScaledReturnComponent with period 1 and vol_period 60. This is the latest day's return divided by the standard deviation of the last 60 daily returns. Standardise then scales it against its own last 60 values (an average move gives about 10). It is negated and clipped at 0. The forecast is 0 on up days and flat days. On down days it is positive and grows linearly with the size of the fall in usual daily moves, up to the cap of 20. It is long-only by construction, because the idea says nothing about up days.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 2 bars
- Main measure: The sample is a non-overlapping 2-bar grid in each window, using bars where the forecast is above 0. For each bar, the coin's 2-day return from the close is reduced by the coin's normal 2-day drift (its mean over all its windows). This excess return is regressed on the forecast. The test is the slope's t, with cases on the same day taken together.
- Supporting check: The mean excess 2-day return over the same grid bars with forecast above 0 is greater than 0.
- Confirm: The slope's t is 2 or more, and the supporting check's mean excess return is positive.
- Reject: The slope's t is -2 or less.
- Inconclusive: A slope t between -2 and 2 is inconclusive. A t of 2 or more with a non-positive mean excess return is also inconclusive. At least 200 grid bars with forecast above 0 are required, spread over at least 100 distinct days; fewer is inconclusive.
