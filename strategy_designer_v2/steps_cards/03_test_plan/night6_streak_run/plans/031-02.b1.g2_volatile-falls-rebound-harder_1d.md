Fidelity:
- Situation: the coin's percentage fall on its latest day -> kept: the 1-day fall in percent; the forecast is 0 on up or flat days.
- Comparison: the rebound is larger when the past 60 days' volatility is higher -> kept: the fall is multiplied by the standard deviation of the last 60 daily returns.
- Outcome: the coin rises over the next 2 days, more after larger falls -> kept: 2-bar return, long only, holding 2 bars.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is exactly the fall times the 60-day volatility, so its link to the 2-day rebound confirms or rejects the idea as stated.
Forecast: One component, `VolatilityWeightedFallComponent` with `vol_period` 60. It is computed as max(0, minus the day's return) × 100 × the standard deviation of the last 60 daily returns. It is standardised over its own past 61 values, with `min_periods` 20. The forecast is 0 on up and flat days. On falling days it is positive and grows with the fall and with the 60-day volatility, capped at +20. It is long only and never negative. No regime is used, because the component is already 0 outside the falling-day situation.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 2 bars
- Main measure: Use non-overlapping 2-bar grid points, one grid per window, on every coin. At each point take the coin's 2-bar return minus its mean 2-bar return over all windows. Regress this excess return on the forecast at that bar, pooling all coins, and report the slope's t. Its standard error sums the deviations per day.
- Supporting check: Take the grid points with a forecast above 0 and split them into two groups by 60-day volatility, using each coin's median. The volatility is raw value / fall % / 100. Compute the mean excess 2-bar return of the high-volatility group minus that of the low-volatility group. The check passes if this difference is above 0.
- Confirm: The slope's t is 2 or more, and the supporting difference is above 0.
- Reject: The slope's t is -2 or less, meaning larger volatility-weighted falls are followed by weaker 2-day returns than usual.
- Inconclusive: Any slope t between -2 and 2 is inconclusive. So is a slope t of 2 or more with a supporting difference at or below 0. At least 100 grid points with a forecast above 0 are needed, spread over at least 50 distinct days; with fewer, the result is inconclusive.
