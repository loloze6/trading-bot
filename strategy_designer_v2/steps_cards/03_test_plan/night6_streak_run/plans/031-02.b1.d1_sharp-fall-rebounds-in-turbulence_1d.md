Fidelity:
- Situation: the latest day's fall, measured against the coin's own daily moves over the past 60 days -> kept
- Comparison: only while daily moves over the past 10 days are larger than usual moves over the past 60 days -> kept
- Outcome: the larger the fall, the more the coin rises over the next 2 days -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the test measures this fall size, inside this turbulence condition, against the 2-day rise.
Forecast: Each day, the config checks the turbulence condition with RecentToPriorVolatilityRatioComponent (vol_period 10, base_period 60, base_lag 0). This is the standard deviation of the last 10 daily returns over that of the last 60. Only when the ratio is above 1 is the regime "mean_reversion" active. On every other day the forecast is 0 (flat). Inside the regime, the forecast is MeanAbsScaledReturnComponent (period 1, window 60): today's return divided by the mean absolute daily return over the last 60 days. It is standardised (window 61, min_periods 20), then negated, then clipped at 0. So the forecast is 0 on up days and positive on down days. It grows in a straight line with the size of the fall counted in usual daily moves: a fall of about one usual move gives about +10. It is never negative.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 2 bars
- Main measure: Sample a non-overlapping 2-bar grid in each window. Keep the grid bars where the forecast is above 0. On those bars, take the coin's 2-bar forward return minus its mean 2-bar return over all windows (the hold-the-coin drift). Regress that excess return on the forecast, pooling all coins, and read the t of the slope.
- Supporting check: On the same bars, the mean excess 2-bar return is positive.
- Confirm: The slope t is 2 or more and the mean excess return is above 0.
- Reject: The slope t is -2 or less.
- Inconclusive: A slope t between -2 and 2 is inconclusive. So is a slope t of 2 or more with a mean excess return at or below 0. At least 30 independent grid cases with a positive forecast are needed; with fewer, the result is inconclusive.
