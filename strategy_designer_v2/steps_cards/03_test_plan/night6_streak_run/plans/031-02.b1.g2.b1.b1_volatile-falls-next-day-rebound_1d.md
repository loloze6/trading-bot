Fidelity:
- Situation: the coin's percentage fall on its latest day, the larger the stronger -> kept
- Comparison: the coin's volatility over the past 60 days, higher meaning a larger rebound -> kept
- Outcome: the coin rises over the next 1 day, more after a larger fall in higher volatility -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the day's fall times the 60-day volatility, held for one day, so its link to the next day's return is the idea's claim.
Forecast: The forecast uses one component, VolatilityWeightedFallComponent with vol_period 60. Its raw value is the latest day's fall in percent times the standard deviation of the last 60 daily returns, and it is 0 on up days and flat days. It is standardised against its own past 61 values (min_periods 20), so a usual reading is about +10, capped at +20. The forecast is that value. It is positive after a down day and grows with both the depth of the fall and the 60-day volatility. It is 0 after an up or flat day, and never negative, because the idea is long only.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: The slope of the next-day return (close[t+1]/close[t]-1, minus the coin's mean daily return over all windows) on the forecast, over the bars with forecast > 0, pooled across coins and windows. Its t is computed with the cases of one day taken together.
- Supporting check: On those bars, the mean excess next-day return of the top fifth of forecasts is above the mean of the bottom fifth.
- Confirm: The slope is positive with t >= 2, and the supporting check passes.
- Reject: The slope is negative with t <= -2.
- Inconclusive: A slope with t between -2 and 2 is inconclusive, as is a positive slope with t >= 2 whose check fails. At least 300 cases with forecast > 0, spread over at least 100 distinct days.
