Fidelity:
- Situation: the coin's percentage fall on its latest day, measured against the standard deviation of its last 60 daily returns -> kept
- Comparison: the larger the fall in 60-day standard deviations, the larger the rise -> kept
- Outcome: the coin rises over the next 1 day -> kept
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the latest day's fall in 60-day standard deviations, zero on up days, so a confirm or reject of its next-day gain is a confirm or reject of the idea.
Forecast: The forecast uses VolatilityScaledReturnComponent with period 1 and vol_period 60. This is the latest day's return divided by the standard deviation of the last 60 daily returns. It is standardised over 60 values, then negated, then clipped at 0. The forecast is therefore positive (long) only on a down day and 0 on up or flat days. It grows in proportion to the depth of the fall in usual daily moves: a fall of an average size reads about +10, and the cap is +20. It is never short, because the idea names only falls. There is no regime: every bar is active, and a day without a fall gives 0.

Criterion:
- Horizon: 1 bar
- Main measure: The timing gain before costs, measured on bars with forecast > 0. For each such bar, take the position held times (the next day's return minus the coin's mean daily return over all its windows). Sum this per day across coins, and compute the t of its mean over days.
- Supporting check: On bars with forecast > 0, compare the top third by forecast with the bottom third. The check passes if the mean next-day excess return of the top third minus that of the bottom third is above 0.
- Confirm: The main measure has a mean above 0 with t ≥ 2, and the supporting check passes.
- Reject: The main measure has a mean below 0 with t ≤ -2, and the top-minus-bottom-third difference is below 0.
- Inconclusive: Everything else is inconclusive, including any t between -2 and 2 and a main measure that disagrees with the check. The test needs at least 300 distinct days with forecast > 0 across the coins; fewer is inconclusive.
