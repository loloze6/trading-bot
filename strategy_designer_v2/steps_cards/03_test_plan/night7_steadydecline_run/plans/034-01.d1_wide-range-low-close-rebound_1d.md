Fidelity:
- Situation: in an agitated market, the close sits near the day's low and the day's range is wide against its past-60-day usual range -> kept: "agitated" (30-day swing above its past-year median) is a regime, and the location times range width is one graded component.
- Comparison: the day's range against the coin's mean daily high-low range over the past 60 days -> kept
- Outcome: a larger rise over the next day, growing with both readings -> kept: horizon 1 bar (1 day on 1d bars).
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the idea's product, read only in agitated markets, and judged on the next day's move.
Forecast: The forecast exists only on agitated bars. A bar is agitated when the 30-day standard deviation of daily returns ranks above the median of the past 365 days (`VolatilityPercentileRegimeComponent`, vol_period 30, lookback_period 365, smooth_period 1, rule value above 0.5). On every other bar the book is flat. Inside the regime the forecast is `BreakoutInMeanRangesComponent` (period 1, base_period 60, side "below", high_current 1, base_exclude_current 1). That is (high − close) / the mean high−low range of the prior 60 days, which equals the close's place below the high (0 at the high, 1 at the low) times the day's range in usual daily ranges. It is standardised over its past 60 values (min_periods 20). It is long only and never negative. It is 0 when the close is at the high and grows as the close nears the low and as the day's range widens, capped at +20.

- Main measure: On agitated bars with forecast > 0, the slope of the next-day return in excess of the coin's mean daily return (over all windows) on the bar's forecast. The t uses errors clustered by day, so cases on several coins the same day count as one.
