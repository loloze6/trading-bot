- Situation: in a falling, agitated market, the close sits near the day's low within its high-low range -> kept: the profile line keeps the market; `CloseLocationInRangeComponent` ("high") measures where the close sits.
- Comparison: none, beyond "the nearer to the low, the larger" -> kept: the forecast is graded by the close's place in the range.
- Outcome: a larger rise over the next day -> kept: next-day return, horizon 1 bar.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the forecast is the close location itself, held one day inside the named profile, so a confirm or reject speaks for the idea.
Forecast: The forecast uses one measure: how near the day's close is to the day's low, from 0 at the high to 1 at the low. It is standardised against its last 60 values, which gives about 10 at its usual level. A constant of -10 is added at equal weight. The forecast is therefore (x − 10) / 2, where x is the standardised close location. It is positive when the close sits nearer the low than usual, up to about +5 at the low. It is negative when the close sits nearer the high, down to about −5 at the high. It is near 0 at the usual mid-range close. The size grows linearly with how near the low the close is. The profile (falling agitated) is added by code, so the book is flat outside it.

- Main measure: Use the in-profile bars where the forecast is not 0. On each, take (forecast / 10) × (the coin's next-day return minus its mean daily return over all windows). Sum these per day, then take the mean over days and its t.
- Supporting check: none
- Confirm: The mean is above 0 with t ≥ 2, over at least 100 days.
