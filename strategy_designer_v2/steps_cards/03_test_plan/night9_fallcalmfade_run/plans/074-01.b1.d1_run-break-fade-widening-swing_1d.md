Fidelity:
- Situation: in a falling market, the first up day after a run of consecutive down days -> kept: a regime selects only up closes that end a down run while the 60-day return is below -10 %, the profile convention for "falling".
- Comparison: the run's fall in usual 30-day daily swings, and the 10-day swing against the 60-day swing -> kept: both are graded in the forecast and added, so the short grows with each.
- Outcome: the close falls over the next day -> kept: short only, horizon 1 bar.
- Verdict transfer: yes: the short is taken only on the idea's run-ending up days in falling markets, sized by the idea's two strengths, and held for its one-day horizon.
Forecast: The forecast is 0 on every bar except the selected ones. A bar is selected when two things hold: the bar closes up after a run of down closes, and the coin's 60-day return is below -10 %. On a selected bar the forecast is negative (short) and is the mean of two parts. The first part is the run's total fall in standard deviations of the last 30 daily returns. It is standardised over 60 bars with target 2.5, because only about a quarter of all bars carry a nonzero value, then negated. The second part is the 10-day over the 60-day standard deviation of daily returns. It is always positive, standardised over 60 bars, then negated. The forecast is never positive. Its size grows with the depth of the run's fall and with how far the 10-day swing exceeds the 60-day swing. When the coin has gone quiet and the run was shallow, the short is small.
