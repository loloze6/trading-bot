Idea: In a rising, agitated market, on the first up day after a run of consecutive down days, the coin rises over the next 5 days, and the larger that up day's return, measured against the standard deviation of the coin's daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger that rise.
Consequence: A trader buying the break of a down run in a rising agitated market should size by how forceful the turning day is, not by how deep the fall before it was, and should treat a weak turning day after a deep run as no buy.
Why:
- F3: The size of the break day itself carries the effect: the larger the up day against the coin's 30-day swing, the larger the 5-day excess return. The slope has t 3.23 at 5 days, and t 3.01 and 2.92 at 3 and 10 days. By tercile of the up day (0.22, 0.63 and 1.77 swings), the 5-day excess return is -0.7 %, +3.3 % and +4.1 % (34 cases each).
- F4: The same up-day grading appears, weaker, after a single down day (slope t 3.11 at 1 day, 1.82 at 5 days). It also appears on all rising agitated days (slope t 2.25 at 5 days, 3.31 at 3 days), while the mean of all profile days is 0 (+0.05 %, t 0.05, 340 cases). The sign therefore lives in the strength of the up close, not in the run before it.
- F2: The rise shrinks as the run gets deeper, the opposite of the theory: the depth slope has t -2.15 at 5 days and -1.89 / -1.75 / -1.74 at 2, 3 and 10 days. The deepest quartile of runs (3.5 usual swings) is followed by -6.7 % over 5 days, against +5.0 %, +4.1 % and +6.7 % for the three shallower quartiles. Runs of 5 or more days are followed by -8.0 % (8 cases).
- F1: In rising agitated markets, the first up day after a run of at least 2 down days is followed by a 5-day excess return of -0.81 % (t -0.50, 160 cases on 110 days); the 1, 2, 3 and 10-day horizons are also negative (t -1.06, -0.74, -0.74, -0.11), so no horizon shows the claimed rise.
