Your line: the idea was rejected. Its test found clear evidence against it.

Objective: find why the idea was rejected, what the market did instead and where, and what other idea could be
confirmed.

Look at two things.
- What the market did instead: its direction and size; where it holds and where it does not (coins, periods,
  regimes, the size of the forecast, the long and the short side).
- Whether the test measured the idea elsewhere than where it shows: another horizon than the plan's, another
  comparison, another side.

Propose ideas that could be confirmed, each from a finding:
- what the market does instead (often the opposite effect): an idea of its own, written like any idea;
- or, if the findings show the tested idea holds in one part of the data, the tested idea amended to where it holds
  (a condition, one side, its horizon), written in full, as one sentence that keeps the tested idea's own situation in
  its own words and adds the change ("... , only while ...", "... , over the next N days ...").
If the findings show nothing a trader could act on, say so: no ideas, with that finding.
