Idea: In a falling, calm market, a down day made on volume heavy against the coin's 20-day average is followed by a rebound over the next 5 days, and the heavier that day's volume, the larger the rebound.
Consequence: In a calm decline, a trader would buy after a heavy-volume selling day, read as supply being absorbed, and stay out after a light-volume down day, which tends to keep falling.
Why:
- F3: On down days in a falling calm market, heavier volume is followed by a larger later move up, not down. The rank correlation of the volume ratio with the 5-day unusual move is +0.303 (67 cases). Down days in the heaviest volume quartile returned +2.18 % (t 1.24, 15 cases; total +3.33 %), against -4.07 % (t -2.86, 19 cases) in the lightest quartile. Heavy-volume days in the lowest third of day returns returned +4.31 % (7 cases), against -5.34 % for the middle third.
- F4: The rest of the falling calm market kept falling, and that is where the losses sit. All falling calm days returned -1.58 % over 5 days (t -1.77, 121 cases) and -4.03 % over 10 days (t -2.16, 57 cases). Light-volume down days returned -4.74 % over 5 days (t -3.21, 48 cases) and -7.25 % over 10 days (t -3.31). The weakest third by 5-day past return returned -3.91 %, against +0.04 % for the strongest third. That continuation is a short-side effect, outside this research's long box.
