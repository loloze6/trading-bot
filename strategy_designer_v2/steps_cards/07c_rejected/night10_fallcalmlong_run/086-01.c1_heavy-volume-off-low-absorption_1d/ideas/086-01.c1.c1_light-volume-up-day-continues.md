Idea: In a falling, calm market, an up day made on volume light against the coin's 20-day average is followed by a further rise over the next 5 days, and the lighter that day's volume, the larger the rise.
Consequence: In a calm decline, a trader would buy a quiet up day rather than a heavy-volume one, and would refuse a heavy-volume up day as one that tends to be given back.
Why:
- F2: The market did the opposite of the brief on up days: the lighter the volume, the larger the later rise. Among falling calm up days, the rank correlation of the volume ratio with the 5-day unusual move is -0.194 (54 cases). By volume tercile, up days returned +3.71 % on the lightest volume, -2.63 % on middle volume and -1.92 % on the heaviest. Up days in the lightest volume quartile returned +5.36 % over 5 days (t 1.73, 12 cases; total +6.52 %) and +1.19 % over 2 days (t 1.48, 36 cases). Up days on volume under the 20-day mean returned +2.27 % over 5 days (t 1.38, 29 cases).
- F1: The tested situation shows no rise. On the 5-day grid of the explore half, falling calm days with volume at least 1.5 times the prior 20-day mean were followed by an unusual move of -0.13 % (t -0.07, 21 cases), and heavy-volume up days by -2.32 % (t -0.78, 11 cases). The step 5 verdict on all windows agrees: -0.41 %, t -0.71, 340 cases on 268 days, a total move of only +0.19 % against a 0.31 % trade cost.
