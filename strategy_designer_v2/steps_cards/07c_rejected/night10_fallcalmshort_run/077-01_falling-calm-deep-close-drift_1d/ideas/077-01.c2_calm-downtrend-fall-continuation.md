Idea: In a falling, calm market, the larger a coin's 3-day fall, measured in its standard deviation of daily returns over the past 20 days, the larger its fall over the next five days.
Consequence: In a calm downtrend, a trader sizes a short by how hard the coin fell in the last three days, rather than by how far it sits below its peak of recent weeks.
Why:
- F3: What carried the continuation was the last few days' fall, not the distance from the 20-day peak. In the profile, the 3-day return in usual swings graded the next 5 days with a clustered t of 2.09 (91 cases, 65 days), and the next 3 days with t 1.56 (162 cases). The 20-day depth reached only t 0.44 and 0.77 on the same cases.
