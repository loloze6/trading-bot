Idea: In a falling, calm market, a down day that closes near its low is followed by a further fall over the next 5 days, and the nearer the close sits to the day's low, the larger that fall.
Consequence: In a calm slide, a down day that closes at its low is a reason to sell for the next several days, not to buy the dip.
Why:
- F7: Where a day closes inside its range also carries a continuation over 5 days in falling calm markets, though not on every coin: the unusual 5-day move is -1.88 % after the third of days that close nearest their low and +2.18 % after the third that close nearest their high (110 cases, t 2.02), driven by XMR (-4.39 % after low closes) and XRP (-2.41 %), not by BTC (+0.47 %).
- F8: Outside the falling calm profile, large one-day falls rebound the next day (+2.01 % unusual, 62 cases, t 2.35), while inside the profile falls of the same size do not rebound (-0.27 %, 17 cases). The brief's claim that falls are not repaid in this market fits that difference, but the rise fade that the brief pairs with it is not in the data.
- F3: The continuation after a decline shows at the 3 to 5 day horizon, not at the plan's 1 day: after a negative 3-day scaled return the unusual move is -0.36 % over 2 days (t -0.80), -1.45 % over 3 days (95 cases, t -2.45) and -2.63 % over 5 days (49 cases, t -2.34).
