Idea: In a falling, calm market, a coin whose return over the last 5 days is negative against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the last 30 days keeps falling over the next 3 days, and the larger that scaled 5-day decline, the larger the fall that follows.
Consequence: In a calm slide, a trader would sell after a few down days rather than wait for the rebound that usually follows such days in other markets, since in this market the decline carries on for several days.
Why:
- F2: What the market did instead is continue its last few days in either direction over the next 3 days, not reverse them: on the 3-day grid, after a negative 5-day scaled return the unusual 3-day move is -1.68 % (89 cases, t -2.74), and after a positive one it is +1.21 % (73 cases, t 1.65).
- F3: The continuation after a decline shows at the 3 to 5 day horizon, not at the plan's 1 day: after a negative 3-day scaled return the unusual move is -0.36 % over 2 days (t -0.80), -1.45 % over 3 days (95 cases, t -2.45) and -2.63 % over 5 days (49 cases, t -2.34).
- F4: The continuation is graded over the whole range: on the 3-day grid, from the lowest to the highest tercile of the 5-day scaled return the unusual 3-day move goes from -1.98 % to +0.96 % (high minus low t 2.50, Spearman 0.19, 162 cases), but the very largest declines below -1.5 scaled do not continue (-0.37 %, 16 cases, t -0.19).
- F5: The continuation after a decline holds on every coin and in most years: over 3 days the unusual move is -1.25 % on BTC (34 cases), -1.82 % on XMR (26) and -2.05 % on XRP (29, t -2.14); by year it is -3.35 % in 2018 (15), -1.66 % in 2019 (42) and -1.53 % in 2021 (23), with only 2020 positive (+0.75 %, 7 cases).
- F6: Its size is larger than a trade's cost: the -1.68 % unusual 3-day move after a 5-day decline is about 5.8 times the 0.29 % cost of a trade and about 0.26 of the 3-day move's spread in this profile (6.2 %), whereas the tested 1-day idea's total move was -1.0 times the cost.
