Idea: After a day that closes near its low, the coin rises over the next 1 day by more the wider that day's high-low range is against its average daily range over the past 20 days, growing steadily from ordinary-range days to the widest ones.
Consequence: A trader buying low-close days should weight them by how wide the day's range was rather than by a single width cut, and expect the strongest rebounds from the most extreme ranges.
Why:
- F3: Across all low-close days, the next-day excess return grows with the day's range against its 20-day mean:
- F2: Inside that high-volatility third, the rebound does not need the plan's 1.5× range. Low-close days with a range of only 1.0 to 1.5 times the 20-day mean range gain +1.75 % (208 cases, t 3.99), and those beyond 2.5 times gain +8.50 % (27 cases, t 2.41). The same range bands in the low-volatility third give +0.07 % to +0.50 % (all t < 0.9).
