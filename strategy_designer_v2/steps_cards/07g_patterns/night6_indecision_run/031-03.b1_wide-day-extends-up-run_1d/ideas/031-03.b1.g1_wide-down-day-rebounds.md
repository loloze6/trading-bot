Idea: On a day when a coin falls, the wider that day's high-low range compared with its 20-day average daily range, the larger the coin's rise over the next 3 days.
Consequence: A wide-range down day is a reason to buy the coin for the next 3 days rather than sell into it, and the wider the day, the stronger that reason.
Why:
- F1: On down days, the coin's unusual 3-day move (in units of its usual 3-day move) rises with the log of the day's range over its 20-day mean range, with slope 0.134 (t 3.23, 2245 non-overlapping cases); on up days the slope is -0.035 (t -1.00, 2470 cases), so the widening effect belongs to falling days.
- F2: Across down days, the unusual 3-day move climbs with the range ratio: -0.09 % at a ratio of 0.8 or less (414 days), -0.21 % at 0.8–1.2, +0.38 % at 1.2–1.6, +2.20 % at 1.6–2.2 (strength 0.24, t 2.14, 96 days) and +2.28 % above 2.2 (strength 0.27, t 1.49, 39 days).
- F4: Wide down days (ratio 1.6 or more) are followed by a positive unusual 3-day move in all 6 coins, from +0.37 % (ETH, 46 days) to +4.34 % (ZEC, 41 days; LTC +2.64 % t 1.98, XMR +2.74 % t 2.16), and in 7 of 8 years, the exception being 2018 (-1.95 %, 10 days).
- F5: The days with the widest ranges (ratio above 2.0) split by the day's direction: after a down day the unusual 3-day move is +3.35 % (t 2.23, 60 days), after an up day it is -0.58 % (t -0.51, 99 days). The tested idea's rise after wide days is therefore carried more by wide falling days than by up-runs.
