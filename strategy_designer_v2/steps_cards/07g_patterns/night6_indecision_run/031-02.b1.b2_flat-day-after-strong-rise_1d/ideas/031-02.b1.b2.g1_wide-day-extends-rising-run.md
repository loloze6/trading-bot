Idea: After a coin has risen over the past 5 days, the wider a day's high-low range is against its mean daily range over the past 20 days, the larger the coin's rise over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A trader in a coin that is rising should add to a long when a day's range widens sharply, and more so the wider the range, rather than read the wide day as the end of the run.
Why:
- F1: After a 5-day rise, the coin's unusual 10-day move grows step by step with the day's range over its 20-day mean range: −0.34 % (t −0.52, 970 cases) below 0.7, +0.53 % (t 0.83) at 0.7–1, +0.85 % (t 1.20) at 1–1.5, +2.00 % (t 1.86, 432 cases) at 1.5–2, and +4.80 % (t 3.61, 302 cases on 224 days, strength 0.28) at 2 or more.
- F2: After a 5-day fall, the same range bins show no rise: the unusual 10-day move is −0.39 %, −0.27 %, +0.29 %, −0.56 % and −0.64 % (t −0.48 at 2 or more, 266 cases), so the effect of a wide day only appears after a rise.
- F3: After a 5-day rise, a wide day (range at least its 20-day mean) is followed by +1.07 % unusual move when its close is not flat (958 cases, t 1.49) and +3.78 % when it is flat (177 cases, t 2.59), and a flat day after a rise with a narrow range gives −0.24 % (489 cases, t −0.31); the flat body is part of the gain, but a wide range is needed for it to show.
- F4: The continuous evidence is weaker than the bins: on the 10-day grid after a 5-day rise, the slope of strength on the log range ratio is 0.17 with t 1.55 (730 cases, 199 days), so the gain sits mostly on the widest days and does not spread evenly across the range.
