Idea: The deeper a coin's one-day fall in units of its 20-day daily volatility, the larger its rise over the next day. This rebound grows as the coin's 20-day volatility rises above its own average over the past 60 days.
Consequence: A trader would lean long after sharp falls mostly when the coin is in an elevated-volatility stretch, and would refuse the trade after falls in calm stretches.
Why:
- F1: Falls of more than one 20-day standard deviation rebound the next day mainly when volatility is high against its recent past. Ranked by the 20-day volatility over its own 60-day mean, the high third is followed by +1.18 % above drift (t 2.49, 504 cases, 234 days), the middle third by +0.47 % (t 1.82) and the low third by -0.30 % (t -0.95). The high-third rebound is about 6 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade, against the idea's average unusual move of +0.19 %, which only equals that cost.
- F2: How low the day closes in its own range predicts the next day's return, and more so when volatility is high against its 60-day mean. Over 11764 coin-days, regressing next-day return minus drift on the centred close location gives a close-location slope of 0.0070 (t 2.38) and an interaction with log volatility ratio of 0.0266 (t 2.93, 1962 days).
- F7: The high-volatility fall rebound is not steady over time. Its yearly slope is strong in 2019 (t 3.11) and 2023 (t 3.13) and near zero or negative in 2018, 2024 and 2025. The book's mean daily return is likewise about zero or below in 2018, 2019, 2022 and 2025.
