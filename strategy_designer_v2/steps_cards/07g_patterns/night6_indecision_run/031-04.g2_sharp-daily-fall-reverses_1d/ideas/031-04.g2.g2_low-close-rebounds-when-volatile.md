Idea: The nearer a coin's daily close sits to the day's low, the larger its rise over the next day. This rebound is larger the higher the coin's 20-day volatility stands against its own average over the past 60 days, and the wider the day's range in units of that volatility.
Consequence: A trader would lean long the day after a day that closed near its low in a volatile stretch, and would lean less or not at all after closes near the high.
Why:
- F2: How low the day closes in its own range predicts the next day's return, and more so when volatility is high against its 60-day mean. Over 11764 coin-days, regressing next-day return minus drift on the centred close location gives a close-location slope of 0.0070 (t 2.38) and an interaction with log volatility ratio of 0.0266 (t 2.93, 1962 days).
- F3: Over all days, closes in the lowest fifth of the day's range are followed by +0.38 % above drift the next day (t 2.19, 2655 cases). Closes in the top fifth are followed by -0.21 % (t -1.71), with the middle steps falling in order.
- F4: Among falls of more than one standard deviation, those closing in the lowest third of the day's range are followed by +0.66 % (t 2.64, 1476 cases, 546 days). Those closing mid-range are followed by +0.37 % (t 0.79).
- F5: The close-near-low effect also grows with the day's range in volatility units: the interaction slope is 0.244 (t 2.13, 14500 coin-days) on volatility-scaled next-day returns. On its own and without conditioning, close location is weak (t 0.91) and is significant in only one coin (XMR, t 3.31) and one year (2020, t 2.68).
