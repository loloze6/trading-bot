Idea: In a choppy, calm market, on a day that closed up, the further the close sits below the coin's 20-day high, in units of its 20-day daily-return standard deviation, the larger its fall over the next 10 days.
Consequence: An up day in a quiet sideways market that still closes deep below the range top is a day to sell into, with a larger short the deeper it sits, and none in an agitated sideways market.
Why:
- F2: The depth gradient appears only in calm markets: the 5-day unusual move per standard deviation of depth below the 20-day high is -3.23 % (t -3.54, 160 cases) in choppy calm, -1.88 % (t -4.18, 298 cases) in falling calm and -6.27 % (t -5.16, 178 cases) in rising calm, against -1.66 % (t -1.17), +0.29 % (t 0.51) and -1.25 % (t -0.71) in the agitated versions; over 10 days, choppy calm is -5.56 % (t -2.96, 79 cases) and choppy agitated -2.01 % (t -0.65).
- F3: In a choppy market the depth gradient comes from up days. Over 10 days it is -7.07 % per depth sd after up days (t -3.45, 64 cases) against -2.40 % after down days (t -1.25, 62 cases). In choppy calm it is -9.23 % after up days (t -3.59, 47 cases on 27 days) against -0.70 % after down days (t -0.25). The 10-day choppy-calm slope over all days is not stable by year: 2021 t -3.07, 2020 t -1.29, 2019 t 0.10, 2018 t +2.15.
