Idea: In a choppy market, a coin's fall over the next five days grows faster than the depth of its close below its 20-day high, measured in units of its 20-day daily-return standard deviation. Closes far below the high are followed by much larger falls than closes moderately below it.
Consequence: In a sideways market, a trader shorts only lightly on ordinary closes below the range top and much more heavily on the rare very deep closes, holding about a week, where the move (about 6 % beyond normal drift) far exceeds the 0.22 % trade cost.
Why:
- F1: In the choppy profile, on the non-overlapping 5-day grid, the deepest fifth of closes below the 20-day high (mean depth -6.4 vol units) was followed by an excess 5-day return of -6.46 % (t -2.30 with one day's cases taken together, 70 cases on 43 days). The next three fifths were near zero: -0.21 % (t -0.17), -1.16 % (t -1.28) and -1.83 % (t -1.44). The shallowest fifth was +6.55 % (t 1.10). The fall is therefore concentrated in the deepest closes rather than rising evenly with depth, which fits the tested linear slope's weak t of 0.69.
- F2: The deepest choppy fifth's 5-day excess return was negative in every year seen: 2018 -5.4 % (t -1.83), 2019 -4.3 % (t -1.92), 2020 -13.6 % (t -1.35) and 2021 -4.7 % (t -1.16). It was also negative on all seven coins with enough cases, for example XRP -10.5 % (t -3.14, 9 cases) and BTC -6.4 % (t -2.00, 16 cases).
- F5: The tested book lost money (Sharpe -0.21, -4.8 % a year). Its daily returns had a correlation of -0.58 with its coins. It earned only in 2018 (+0.059 % a day while the coins fell 0.35 % a day) and lost in 2020 and 2021 (-0.03 % and -0.06 % a day while the coins rose 0.47 % and 0.64 % a day). The linear short over all depths mostly carried the market's direction rather than the deep-close tail.
