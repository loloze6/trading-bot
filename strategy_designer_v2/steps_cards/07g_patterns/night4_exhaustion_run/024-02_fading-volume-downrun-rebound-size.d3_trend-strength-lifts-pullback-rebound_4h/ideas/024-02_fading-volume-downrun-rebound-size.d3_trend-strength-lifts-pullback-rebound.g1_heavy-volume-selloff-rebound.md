Idea: When a coin has fallen sharply over the past day on volume heavy against its own 30-day average, it rises over the next three days, and the heavier that day's volume, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader buys high-volume sell-offs, with conviction growing with how unusual the volume was, and refuses to buy sell-offs on quiet volume.
Why:
- F1: When a coin's past-day volume was in its top fifth against its 30-day average and its 1-day move was in its lowest third (the sharpest falls), the next 3 days ended 2.16 % above the coin's normal drift (t 4.67, about 340 non-overlapping cases). That is the strongest cell of every grouping looked at. The same top-volume fifth after mid or rising days gave −0.74 % (t −1.03) and +0.79 % (t 1.59).
- F2: The excess 3-day move rises with the past day's volume against its 30-day average. The top fifth gave +1.13 % (t 3.64) and the bottom fifth −0.73 % (t −3.01), on about 1030 cases each. This survives on the two other non-overlapping grids: top fifth +0.54 % (t 1.74) and +0.94 % (t 3.15); bottom fifth −0.27 % (t −1.15) and −0.56 % (t −2.42).
- F3: The top-volume effect is positive in 5 of 6 coins (XRP +3.12 %, t 3.08; LTC −0.16 %) and in 7 of 8 years (2018 −0.32 %; 2020, 2021 and 2024 each above +1.6 % with t above 2), so no single coin or year carries it.
- F4: The brief's volume contrast does not show. Down runs of 2 or more bars on fading volume were followed by +0.15 % excess (t 0.43, 625 cases), and the same runs on rising volume by +0.38 % (t 1.11, 592 cases). Up runs gave +0.19 % with fading and +0.14 % with rising volume. The give-back follows heavy volume, not fading volume.
