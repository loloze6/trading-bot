A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a rising market, a coin rises more over the next 3 days the larger its return over the last 3 days, measured against its usual daily swing over the last 30 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In an uptrend, a trader would lean into a coin that has just made a large rise for its own volatility, and would not buy one whose last days were flat or down.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a rising market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 035-01_rising-market-quieting-swings-rise_1d (idea 035-01_rising-market-quieting-swings-rise, brief 035_calming-swings-lift-rising-coins).
What it rests on:
- F1: Inside the rising profile, the coin's 3-day excess return rises with its past 3-day return scaled by its 30-day daily swing. The top fifth of that scaled return is followed by +2.75 % over the next 3 days (t 2.45, 173 non-overlapping cases on 79 days), against +0.69 %, −0.29 %, −0.71 % and +0.88 % for the lower four fifths. The unscaled 3-day return gives the same top fifth: +1.97 %, t 1.96, 241 cases on 112 days.
- F2: The pattern in F1 seems to live in the rising profile. Outside it, the top fifth of the scaled 3-day return is followed by −0.25 % (t −0.34, 256 cases on 115 days). Inside the rising profile, the link is positive in 5 of 6 coins (correlation from +0.02 to +0.31, LTC −0.05), and the top fifth's excess return is positive in 8 of 12 windows.
- F3: Even F1 is only borderline once the number of angles is counted. I looked at about 15 groupings of 5 bins each, plus weekday, and no other cell reached a clustered t above 2.45. Its grading is also not monotone across the lower four fifths.
