Idea: After a down day, a coin rises over the next day, more so the further the day's close sits below its high, measured as a share of the close, so that a wide day closing at its low is followed by the largest rise.
Consequence: A trader buys a down day's close in proportion to how far price fell from the day's high, and leaves small, narrow down days alone because their rebound does not cover the cost of a trade.
Why:
- F1: Among down days in folds B and C, the move after the day depends mostly on how wide the day was. Down days in each coin's widest high-low range quintile were followed by a next-day return +1.02 % above the coin's normal drift (t 4.36, 1003 cases). The four narrower quintiles gave between -0.16 % and +0.25 % (all t under 1.2).
- F2: The tested close-near-low effect is only present on wide days. Among down days in the top close-near-low quintile, the next-day excess return was +1.12 % (t 2.97, 223 cases) and +1.38 % (t 3.42, 239 cases) in the two widest range quintiles. In the three narrowest it was between -0.77 % and +0.05 % (all t under 1.9 in absolute value).
- F6: The tested idea's per-case unusual move, +0.19 % (t 1.70), only equals the 0.19 % trade cost. The wide-range and high-volatility down days of F1 to F3 carry 1.0 % to 1.8 % per case, five to nine times that cost.
