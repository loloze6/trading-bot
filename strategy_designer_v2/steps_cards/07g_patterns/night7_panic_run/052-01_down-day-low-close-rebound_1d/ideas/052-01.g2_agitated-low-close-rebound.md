Idea: In an agitated market, after a down day, a coin rises over the next day, more so the nearer that day's close sits to its low, as a share of the day's high-low range.
Consequence: The low-close rebound is worth trading when the coin's 30-day swing is above its usual level, and not when the market is calm.
Why:
- F3: The effect also sits in high-volatility periods. With volatility measured as each coin's 20-day swing, down days closing nearest the low were followed by +1.79 % (t 4.85, 266 cases) in the top volatility tercile, against +0.08 % (t 0.28) and -0.12 % (t -0.34) in the middle and bottom terciles. The book's gain per bar in a position follows the same order: 0.49 % (t 5.55), 0.28 %, 0.12 % from high to low volatility.
- F6: The tested idea's per-case unusual move, +0.19 % (t 1.70), only equals the 0.19 % trade cost. The wide-range and high-volatility down days of F1 to F3 carry 1.0 % to 1.8 % per case, five to nine times that cost.
