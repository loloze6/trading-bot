A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: A coin whose one-day fall is large against its usual daily swing (the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days) rises over the next day, and the larger that swing-scaled fall, the larger the rebound.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader can buy a coin after a day that falls far beyond its usual swing and expect a graded rebound over the following day, while not fading large up days.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 036-02_range-scaled-rally-continuation_1d (idea 036-02_range-scaled-rally-continuation, brief 036_scaled-rallies-continue-next-week).

The size of the fall, not the shape of the day, carries the rebound. Per case, a 1 to 2 standard deviation fall rebounds +0.75 %, about 3.9 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
