Idea: After a one-day fall measured in units of its 30-day daily-return standard deviation, a coin rises over the next day, more so the heavier that day's volume against its 30-day average volume.
Consequence: A large scaled fall on heavy volume, the mark of forced selling, is a stronger reason to buy for the next day than the same fall on quiet volume.
Why:
- F4: Among falls beyond 1 usual swing, the rebound is largest when the day's volume is heavy for the coin. The top volume quartile (1.9 to 9 times its 30-day mean) gives +1.99 % next day (t_day 2.61, 216 cases, 103 days), the third quartile +1.01 % (t_day 2.04) and the two lower ones +0.64 % and +0.17 % (t_day 1.19 and 0.27). The top-volume subset is positive in every year 2018 to 2021, from +0.85 % to +2.33 %, but each year's t_day is under 2, and the fall × log-volume interaction is only t 1.18.
- F3: The tested effect holds in folds B, C on day-clustered errors: on all down days, the next-day excess return rises with the fall in usual swings (slope 0.0070 per swing, t 1.99, 3574 cases on 877 days). Adding the 5-day return scaled by the usual swing leaves the fall's slope at t 2.24 and gives the 5-day return no effect of its own (t -1.01).
