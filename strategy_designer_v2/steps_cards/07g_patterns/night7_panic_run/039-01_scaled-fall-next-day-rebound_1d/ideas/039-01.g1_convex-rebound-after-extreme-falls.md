Idea: After a one-day fall, a coin rises over the next three days, and the rise grows faster than the fall: a fall of several times its 30-day daily-return standard deviation is followed by a rebound many times larger than a fall of one such swing.
Consequence: The rebound worth trading after costs sits in the coin's rare, very large falls for its own swing, held for a few days, not in ordinary down days.
Why:
- F1: The next-day rebound after a down day is convex in the fall's size rather than linear. In folds B, C the mean next-day excess return is +0.10 % for scaled moves of -0.5 to 0 (1681 cases), +0.16 % for -1 to -0.5 (1033), +0.83 % for -1.5 to -1 (t 2.95, 463), +1.48 % for -3 to -2 (t 3.20, 132) and +3.55 % below -3 (t 2.07, 34). The tested forecast caps at 20 and trades every down day (71 % of coin-days in a position), so its per-case unusual move is only +0.18 %, under the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F2: The rebound after a large scaled fall lasts beyond one day. On the non-overlapping 3-day grid, falls beyond 2 usual swings are followed by a +2.33 % excess 3-day return (t 2.62, 46 cases), against +0.66 % (t 0.91, 218) for falls of 1 to 2 swings and +0.10 % (t 0.30, 879) for falls under 1 swing.
- F3: The tested effect holds in folds B, C on day-clustered errors: on all down days, the next-day excess return rises with the fall in usual swings (slope 0.0070 per swing, t 1.99, 3574 cases on 877 days). Adding the 5-day return scaled by the usual swing leaves the fall's slope at t 2.24 and gives the 5-day return no effect of its own (t -1.01).
