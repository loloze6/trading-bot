Idea: In a rising market, a day whose high-low range is wide against the coin's 30-day average daily range is followed by a further rise over the next 3 days. The wider that day's range relative to this average, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader in a rising coin reads a wide day as a reason to be long for the next 3 days, not as a sign of a top.
Why:
- F1: The wider the day's range against its 30-day average, the lower the next 3 days in a falling market (60-day return below -10 %) and the higher in a rising one (above +10 %). On the 3-day grid the slope of the 3-day excess return, in units of the coin's usual 3-day move, on the range multiple is -0.189 (t -3.09, 968 cases) when falling and +0.176 (t 3.50, 531 cases) when rising. These t are ordinary OLS t, not clustered by day.
- F2: In a rising market, wide days (range at least 1.5 times the 30-day mean) are followed by a 3-day excess return of +3.0 %, strength +0.31, t 1.86 with one day's cases taken together, 135 cases on 63 days. In a falling market the same days are followed by -1.69 %, strength -0.17, t -1.25, 92 cases on 44 days, so the idea's short side is the weaker of the two.
