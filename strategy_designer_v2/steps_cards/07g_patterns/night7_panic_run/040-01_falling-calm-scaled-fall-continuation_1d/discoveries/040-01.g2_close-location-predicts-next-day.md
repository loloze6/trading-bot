A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: The lower within its day's high-low range a coin closes, the more it rises over the next day relative to its normal drift, and the higher it closes, the more it lags.
What it would mean for a trader: A close near the day's low is a reason to lean long for one day, and a close near the high is a reason not to buy.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 040-01_falling-calm-scaled-fall-continuation_1d (idea 040-01_falling-calm-scaled-fall-continuation, brief 040_calm-downtrend-falls-keep-falling).
What it rests on:
- F3: Where the day closes inside its range grades the next day's drift-removed return. A close in the lowest fifth of the range is followed by +0.52 % (t 1.94, 503 days) and a close in the highest fifth by -0.38 % (t -1.76, 523 days). The slope of the next day's return on close location is negative in all 12 windows (w10: -3.14 %, t -3.11).
- F4: A large one-day fall, scaled by the 30-day swing, rebounds the next day. The lowest decile of scaled daily returns is followed by +1.01 % (t 2.33, 696 cases, 224 days), and among down days the slope is -1.05 % per unit outside the falling calm profile (t -2.39) against -0.29 % inside it (t -0.66).
