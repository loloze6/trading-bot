A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: Over the next day, a coin moves against the direction of a day whose high-low range is wide relative to its 30-day daily-return swing, and the wider that scaled range, the larger the move back.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader should fade unusually wide days in the coin's own swing units over the following day, and refuse to chase them.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 040-01_falling-calm-scaled-fall-continuation_1d (idea 040-01_falling-calm-scaled-fall-continuation, brief 040_calm-downtrend-falls-keep-falling).
What it rests on:
- F1: Over the next day, a coin moves against the direction of a day whose high-low range is wide for its 30-day swing; the widest-range quintile is followed by a signed drift-removed move of -0.82 % (day-clustered t -3.03, 1,390 cases on 532 days), against -0.07 % to -0.28 % in the other quintiles. The slope of the next day's move on the signed range is negative in 11 of 12 windows (t -3.41 in w10). The 0.82 % is 4.3 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F2: The wide-day reversal lives outside the falling calm profile; the slope of the next day's move on the signed scaled range is -0.29 % per unit (t -3.14, 5,287 bars, 1,146 days) outside it, against -0.09 % (t -0.88, 1,664 bars) inside it.
