Idea: In a rising market, a day that closes down after a high-low range far beyond the coin's 30-day standard deviation of daily returns rises over the next 3 days, and the rise grows steeply with that range-to-swing ratio, so the widest such days carry almost all of it.
Consequence: In an uptrend, only an exceptionally wide down day is a shakeout worth buying for the next three days, and an ordinary red day is not.
Why:
- F1: In a rising market, down-closing days sorted by raw range in units of the 30-day standard deviation show the rebound only in the widest days, and it is not graded below them: the top sixth gained +4.34 % excess over 3 days (t 2.07, 74 cases, 34 days) and the fifth sixth +2.69 % (t 1.90, 72 cases), while the lowest three sixths ran from -1.02 % to +0.71 % (all |t| < 0.7); by quartile, the top one gained +3.93 % (t 2.12, 110 cases). The test averaged all down days at +0.21 % (t 0.61, 1813 cases).
- F2: The widest quarter of rising-market down days beat the rest of them in three of four years: 2019 +3.88 % against +0.76 %, 2020 +4.39 % against +0.20 %, 2021 +6.11 % against +1.28 % (each t 1.3 to 1.5). The exception is 2018, -2.63 % against -2.56 %, on 13 cases.
- F3: In a rising market, wide up-closing days are not followed by a rise: the widest quarter gained +0.34 % excess over 3 days (t 0.29, 136 cases), against +3.93 % for the widest quarter of down-closing days. This matches the brief's claim that only the down close carries the rebound.
- F5: The book's gain sits in its tails: its best 1 % of coin-days carry 116 % of its total daily gain and its worst 1 % take back 87 % (8689 coin-days). That fits a rebound that lives only in rare very wide days.
