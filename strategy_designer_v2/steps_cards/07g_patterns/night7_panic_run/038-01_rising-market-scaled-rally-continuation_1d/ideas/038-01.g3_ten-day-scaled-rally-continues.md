Idea: In a rising market, a coin keeps rising over the next 3 days, more so the larger its 10-day return measured in units of the standard deviation of its daily returns over the last 30 days.
Consequence: A trader holds or adds to coins whose rise over the last ten days is large for their usual swing while the uptrend lasts.
Why:
- F6: A longer scaled run carries the same lift in rising markets. The top quintile of the 10-day return over the 30-day swing is followed by a 3-day strength of +0.178, an excess return of +1.66 %, with t 3.02 on 318 cases. The other quintiles give -0.02 to +0.08. Outside rising markets the top quintile gives +0.018 (t 0.24).
- F1: Inside the rising profile the continuation sits in the largest scaled rises only. The top sextile of the 3-day return in units of the 30-day swing (z3 from 0.86 to 4.30) is followed by a 3-day strength of +0.236, an excess return of +2.33 %, with t 3.44 on 223 grid cases. The next sextile (0.30 to 0.86) gives +0.138 (t 2.16, 196 cases). The near-flat sextile (-0.03 to 0.30) gives -0.144 (t -2.04, 147 cases). Outside rising markets the top sextile gives -0.086 (t -1.52, 170 cases). This agrees with the confirmed slope of 0.00109 (t 3.88), but the size measured per case is weak: +0.30 % unusual move with t 0.90 over 766 days.
