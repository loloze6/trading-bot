A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: A coin that falls sharply in one day, measured in units of the standard deviation of its daily returns over the last 30 days, rises over the next day, more so the larger that scaled one-day fall.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader buys a coin's unusually large one-day drop for a one-day rebound and avoids selling into it.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 038-01_rising-market-scaled-rally-continuation_1d (idea 038-01_rising-market-scaled-rally-continuation, brief 038_scaled-rallies-continue-in-rising-markets).
What it rests on:
- F3: A one-day fall that is large against the coin's 30-day swing is followed by a rise the next day, larger the deeper the fall, in every profile. Outside rising markets the lowest two deciles of the scaled one-day return are followed by +1.26 % (t 4.30, 454 cases) and +0.60 % (t 2.85, 456 cases), against -0.15 % to -0.47 % in the middle deciles. Inside rising markets the lowest decile is followed by +0.93 % (t 2.41, 271 cases).
