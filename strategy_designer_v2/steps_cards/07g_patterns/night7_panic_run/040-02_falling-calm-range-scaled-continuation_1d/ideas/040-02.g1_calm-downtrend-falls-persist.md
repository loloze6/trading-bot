Idea: In a falling, calm market, a coin whose 3-day return is negative keeps falling over the next 3 days, by more the larger its 3-day fall in percent.
Consequence: In calm downtrends a trader should short the coin after any 3-day decline and hold for three days, rather than wait for an unusually large fall.
Why:
- F1: Inside the falling calm profile, the 3-day grid points where the coin had fallen over the last 3 days were followed by a 3-day unusual move of -2.02 % (day-clustered t -2.82, 139 days); the falls held in both halves of the shown windows (w7–w12 -1.60 %, t -3.36, 200 cases; w13–w18 -2.72 %, t -3.27, 119 cases) and on all six coins (-0.74 % for BTC to -3.17 % for ETH).
- F2: Grading the size of the fall does not add to this: among in-profile falls, the slope of the unusual 3-day move on the range-scaled 3-day return is 0.0030 (clustered t 0.31, 319 cases); the three lowest in-profile quintiles give a similar -1.84 %, -2.41 % and -1.62 % (t -2.28, -3.46, -2.19), against +0.16 % and +1.15 % for the two highest.
- F3: Across all in-profile grid points, the unusual 3-day move rises with the range-scaled 3-day return (slope 0.0092, clustered t 1.68, 537 cases, 194 days; t 2.30 in w13–w18 but 0.17 in w7–w12); so the tested idea's main measure, read on its standardised forecast, came out only +0.64 % (t 1.59).
- F4: Out of profile there is no such continuation: the bottom quintile of the scaled 3-day return gives +0.61 % (t 1.10), the top quintile +1.03 % (t 2.03), and the slope is 0.0044 (clustered t 1.03, 1,724 cases).
- F8: The book's best fifth of active days came when the coin fell 5.17 % on the day after a 3-day fall of 8.05 %, and its worst fifth when the coin rose 4.77 %. The yearly return was +2.2 %: per coin, LTC and ETH gained (+0.44 and +0.46 summed) while BTC lost (-0.40), so the shorts paid in falls but the forecast picked too few of them.
