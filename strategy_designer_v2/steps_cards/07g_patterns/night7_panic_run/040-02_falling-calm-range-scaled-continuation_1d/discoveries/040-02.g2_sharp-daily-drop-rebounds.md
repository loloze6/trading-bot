A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: A coin whose one-day return is a large fall in units of its 30-day daily return standard deviation rises over the next day, by more the larger that scaled fall.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader can buy a coin at the close of a day of unusually sharp decline for a one-day rebound, whatever its trend, and should refuse to short into such a close.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 040-02_falling-calm-range-scaled-continuation_1d (idea 040-02_falling-calm-range-scaled-continuation, brief 040_calm-downtrend-falls-keep-falling).
What it rests on:
- F5: After the largest one-day falls (bottom seventh of the day's return over the 30-day daily standard deviation), the next day's unusual return is +0.82 % (day-clustered t 2.34, 319 days, 993 cases).
- F6: This rebound held in both halves of the shown windows (+0.83 %, t 3.87; +0.82 %, t 2.69), in falling, rising and choppy 60-day trends alike (+0.83 %, +0.95 %, +0.76 %, t 2.4 to 2.7) and on all six coins (+0.23 % for BTC to +1.21 % for XMR).
- F7: The rebound is graded: on down days, the slope of the next day's unusual return on the scaled one-day move is -0.0071 (clustered t -2.00, 3,417 cases, 874 days), larger falls being followed by larger rebounds, while up days show no slope (0.0014, t 0.75). It fades over 3 days (+0.94 %, t 1.67 on the 3-day grid).
