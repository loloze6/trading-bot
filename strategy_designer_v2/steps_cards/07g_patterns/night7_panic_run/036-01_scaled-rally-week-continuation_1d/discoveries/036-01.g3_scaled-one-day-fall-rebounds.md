A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: A coin whose 1-day return is a large fall against its usual daily swing (the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days) rises over the next day, and the larger that scaled one-day fall, the larger the rebound.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader should refuse to sell into, and may buy, a one-day drop that is unusually large for the coin, expecting part of it back the next day.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 036-01_scaled-rally-week-continuation_1d (idea 036-01_scaled-rally-week-continuation, brief 036_scaled-rallies-continue-next-week).
What it rests on:
- F5: A coin that falls unusually hard in one day rebounds the next day, and the rebound is graded. Over all bars, cases of one day taken together, the bottom decile of the 1-day return over the usual swing (median -1.57 swings) is followed by a next-day return of +1.14 % (t 2.57, 224 days). The second decile (median -0.84) is followed by +0.59 % (t 2.16, 363 days), and deciles 3 to 10 by between -0.24 % and +0.18 % (all |t| < 1). The rebound is several times the 0.19 % cost of a trade. It stands out moderately given the many angles I looked at.
