Idea: A coin whose 14-day return is large against its usual daily swing (the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days) keeps rising over the next 7 days, and the larger that scaled 14-day rise, the larger the further gain.
Consequence: A trader should hold a coin's longer rally that is unusual for that coin for the following week, rather than buying on a short 3-day burst.
Why:
- F1: The idea's effect is in the coin's longer scaled rise, not its 3-day rise. On every 7-day grid offset (7 non-overlapping grids, about 974 cases each, folds B and C), I regressed the 7-day unusual move on the prior return over the usual swing. The 14-day scaled return has a mean slope of 0.0151 with mean t 3.53, and its t is positive on all 7 offsets (1.62 to 4.56). The 3-day version has a mean t of 2.62, ranges from 0.88 to 4.72, and its first grid shows no clear slope in deciles. The 1-day version has a mean t of 1.39 and changes sign (from -3.11 to +5.21).
- F3: The book earns where the 7-day scaled rise is high. Its mean bar return is +0.382 % with a mean allocation of 0.80 in the top tercile of the 7-day scaled return, +0.082 % (allocation 0.47) in the middle tercile, and -0.004 % (allocation 0.19) in the bottom one. Its Sharpe of 0.72 comes from the bars where the longer rise is large, not from the 3-day signal: the 3-day cases' mean unusual move is -0.30 %, t -0.67.
- F4: The continuation of the 7-day scaled rise is present in every trend, so it names no profile. The mean slope over offsets is 0.039 (t 2.50, about 87 cases per grid) in choppy markets, 0.020 (t 2.45, about 264) in falling markets and 0.017 (t 1.54, about 212) in rising markets. By year it is weak: mean t of 1.00, -0.02, 1.61 and 0.25 for 2018 to 2021.
