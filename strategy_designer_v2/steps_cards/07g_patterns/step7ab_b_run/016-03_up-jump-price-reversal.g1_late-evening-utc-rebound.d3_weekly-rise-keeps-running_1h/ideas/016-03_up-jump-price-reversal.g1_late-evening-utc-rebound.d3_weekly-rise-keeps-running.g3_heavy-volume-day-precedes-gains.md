Idea: The higher a coin's volume over its last 24 hours compared with its average daily volume over the last 7 days, the more the coin rises over the next 24 hours.
Consequence: A day of unusually heavy trading is a reason to lean long over the next day rather than treat the volume burst as exhausted flow.
Why:
- F7: A volume burst points the other way from the brief. With the 24-hour return held fixed, a higher ratio of last-24-hour volume to the 7-day average is followed by +17 bps per sd of next-day excess (t 4.2, 15950 cases), not by a reversal.
- F1: The past day's return reverses over the next day, and this is the strongest pattern in the record. On the daily grid of non-overlapping 24-hour cells, one standard deviation of the coin's 24-hour return is followed by -45 bps of excess return over the next 24 hours (t -11.3, 15950 cases). It holds on every coin (t -3.5 to -6.6) and in both halves of the windows (t -6.1 and -9.3). It holds in 6 of 8 years with t below -2.
