Idea: The larger a coin's fall over the past day, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: A trader should refuse to short a coin the day after a sharp one-day drop, and the sharper the drop the more it favours a short-lived long.
Why:
- F1: After a coin's largest one-day falls (bottom fifth of daily returns, -3.4 % or worse), the next day's return beats the coin's mean by +0.96 % (t 3.89, 1988 daily cases). The other four fifths sit between -0.18 % and -0.30 %, and the slope of next-day excess return on the day's return has a t of -4.92 (9940 cases). This rebound is several times the 0.22 % cost of a trade.
