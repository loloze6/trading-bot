Idea: Among coins that have fallen over the past 28 days with daily high-low ranges narrower than their own usual range, the larger the 28-day fall, the larger the further fall over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader should refuse to buy a quiet, deep decline and may short it, more so the deeper it is.
Why:
- F2: In calm 28-day declines, the deeper the fall, the weaker the next 5 days. Here "calm" means a 28-day mean high-low range below the coin's mean range since the window began, on the non-overlapping 5-day grid. The deepest third (-18 % to -55 %) is followed by an excess return of -2.98 % (t -3.10, 143 cases), the middle third by -1.92 % (t -2.03, 142), and the shallowest third by +0.88 % (t 1.17, 143). The slope of excess return on the 28-day return is positive with a t of 2.44 (428 cases), so larger calm falls lead to larger further falls.
- F3: In wide-range 28-day declines, the tested idea's situation, the rebound appears only in the deepest third. Those cases (-34 % to -61 %) average +0.64 % (t 0.37, 94 cases), while the middle and shallowest thirds fall further: -3.39 % (t -3.16) and -1.88 % (t -2.08). The bars the backtest marked in-regime average -1.47 % excess over 5 days (t -2.21, 375 grid cases), which goes against the rebound the idea expected.
