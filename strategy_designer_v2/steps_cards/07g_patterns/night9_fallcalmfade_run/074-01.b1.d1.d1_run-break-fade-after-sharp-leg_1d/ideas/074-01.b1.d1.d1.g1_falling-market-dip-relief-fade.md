Idea: In a falling market, an up day that follows one or more consecutive down days is followed by a fall in the coin's close over the next day, and the more down days in a row the up day ends, the larger that fall.
Consequence: A trader fades relief days in a downtrend from the first down day on, leaning harder the longer the slide they interrupt, instead of waiting for a run of two.
Why:
- F1: In falling markets the up day that ends a run of down closes is followed by a weaker next day for every run length from one day. The next-day unusual move is -0.93 % (t -3.04, 281 cases) after a 1-day run, -1.53 % (t -3.71, 142) after 2 days, -0.33 % (t -0.61, 79) after 3 days and -2.45 % (t -3.54, 54) after 4 or more. An up day after an up day does not fade: +0.16 % (t 0.63, 417). These t's are not day-pooled.
- F2: The fade belongs to falling markets, not rising ones. On up days ending a run of 2 or more down closes, the next-day unusual move is -1.59 % in falling calm markets (day-pooled t -2.75, 115 cases, 64 days) and -1.19 % in falling agitated ones (t -1.60, 160 cases, 77 days). In rising markets it is +0.89 % when calm (t 1.35) and -0.08 % when agitated (t -0.11). This matches the tested idea and its brief; the falling short side is already held by this book (074-01.b1.d1.d1 in the map).
