Idea: The first down day that ends a run of up days is followed by a rise in the coin's close over the next day. The larger the run's gain against the coin's usual daily swing over the last 30 days, the larger that rise.
Consequence: A trader buys the first dip after a strong multi-day climb rather than reading it as the climb's end, and stays small after a mild one.
Why:
- F2: The first down day after a run of at least 2 up days, outside rising markets, is followed by a rise the next day that grows with the run's gain in 30-day swings. The slope of the next-day excess, in swings, on that gain is +0.120 (t 2.63, 314 cases). By the gain's size the mean next-day excess goes -0.18 % (gain up to 1.5 swings, 156 cases), +0.81 % (1.5 to 2.5), +2.07 % (2.5 to 4, t 1.75) and +1.70 % (above 4, t 1.79).
- F3: The same graded rebound shows weaker in rising markets: the slope is +0.084 (t 1.84, 229 cases), and only the largest gains carry it (above 4 swings: +1.51 %, 45 cases). Over the next two days the largest-gain bin rebounds by about the same amount in both groups: +3.52 % outside rising markets (t 1.92, 22 cases) and +3.24 % in rising markets (t 1.83, 45 cases).
