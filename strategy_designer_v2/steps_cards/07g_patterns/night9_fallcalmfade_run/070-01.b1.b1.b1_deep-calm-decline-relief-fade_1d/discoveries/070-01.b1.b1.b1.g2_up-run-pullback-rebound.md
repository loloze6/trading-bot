A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: A day that closes down after an unbroken run of up days is followed by a rise over the next day. The more usual daily moves that run of up days added to the price, the larger the next day's rise.
What it would mean for a trader: Buy the first down day after a strong run of up days, sized by how far the run carried the price in usual moves, instead of reading it as the start of a reversal.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 070-01.b1.b1.b1_deep-calm-decline-relief-fade_1d (idea 070-01.b1.b1.b1_deep-calm-decline-relief-fade, brief 070_falling-calm-run-break-fade).
What it rests on:
- F6: Mirror pattern outside falling calm: a down day that breaks a run of up days is followed by a next-day excess rise that grows with the run's gain in usual moves (slope +0.18 % per move, t 2.67, 1728 cases). After runs of 2 or more moves the rise is +0.81 % to +1.73 % in every year 2018–2021 (2020 t 2.56), and +2.94 % in a falling trend (t 2.40, 30 days).
- F7: Falling calm pulls the other way for short up runs: a down day after an up run of under 1 usual move is followed by −0.88 % (t −2.44, 134 days). After up runs of 3 to 5 moves it is +0.26 % (t 0.44), and after 5 or more +1.23 % (t 1.44, 19 days), so the rebound after long up runs points the same way there but cannot be told from 0.
