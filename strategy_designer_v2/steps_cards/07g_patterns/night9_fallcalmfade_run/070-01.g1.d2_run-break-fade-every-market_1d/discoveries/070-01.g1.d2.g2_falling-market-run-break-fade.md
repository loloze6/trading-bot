A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling market, an up day that ends a run of down days is followed by a fall over the next day. The fall is larger the more down days that run held beyond the first.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader can fade the breaks of multi-day down runs whenever the coin's 60-day trend is down, calm or agitated, and refuse them in rising markets, where the fade disappears.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 070-01.g1.d2_run-break-fade-every-market_1d (idea 070-01.g1.d2_run-break-fade-every-market, brief 070_falling-calm-run-break-fade).
What it rests on:
- F2: The 1-day fade after runs of 2 or more down days depends on the 60-day trend. It is +1.57 % in falling calm (t 2.75, 117 cases, 65 days), +1.20 % in falling agitated (t 1.59, 158 cases), +1.26 % in choppy calm (t 1.49, 56 cases) and +0.92 % in choppy agitated (t 0.86, 26 cases). In rising markets it is gone or reversed: +0.25 % in rising agitated (t 0.37, 85 cases) and −1.02 % in rising calm (t −1.59, 91 cases).
- F3: After a single down day the break is not followed by a fall in any size bucket: +0.41 %, +0.35 %, +0.42 % and −0.18 % from the smallest to the largest up-day quartile, all with |t| < 1.3. After runs of 2 or more, the fade holds at +0.73 % to +1.01 % whatever the up day's size.
