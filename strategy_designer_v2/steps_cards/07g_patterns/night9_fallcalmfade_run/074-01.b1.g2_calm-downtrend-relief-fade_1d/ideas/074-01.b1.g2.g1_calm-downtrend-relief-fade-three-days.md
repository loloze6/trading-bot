Idea: In a falling, calm market, the first up day after a run of down days is followed by a fall in the coin's close over the next three days. The deeper the run's fall against the coin's usual daily swing over the last 30 days, the larger that three-day fall.
Consequence: A trader shorting relief days in a quiet downtrend holds the short for about three days rather than one, so each trade captures several times its cost.
Why:
- F1: In the falling calm profile the run-break fade lasts beyond the tested day. After an up close ending 2 or more down closes, the short-signed excess return is +0.92 % at 1 day (t 2.22, 258 cases, 152 days), +1.35 % at 2 days (t 2.23, 150 days) and +1.38 % at 3 days (t 1.73). On a non-overlapping 3-day grid the 3-day reading rises to +2.56 % (t 2.15, 103 cases, 58 days). That is about 2.5 times the 1-day move, against the same 0.19 % cost per trade.
- F2: Among all run-breaks with 2 or more down closes, the fade is convex in the run's depth scaled by the 30-day swing. The bottom three depth quartiles give next-day short excess of −0.05 %, +0.88 % and +0.35 % (t ≤ 1.7). The deepest quartile gives +2.11 % (t 4.16, 216 cases, 99 days). The convex deep-run fade in falling markets is already held (074-01.b1.g1).
- F3: The fade is absent in rising markets, as the brief predicts. There the 1-, 2-, 3- and 5-day short excess is −0.39 %, −0.67 %, −0.31 % and −0.33 % (t from −0.24 to −0.80, 176 cases, 98 days), and the deep half is −0.40 % at 1 day.
- F8: The tested book's cases are concentrated in one year. 2018 gives +1.79 % next-day short excess (t 2.46, 120 cases), while 2019, 2020 and 2021 give +0.23 %, +0.18 % and +0.11 % (all t < 0.5). This fits its Calmar of 0.23 and its Sharpe falling short of holding's.
