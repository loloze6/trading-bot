A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a choppy market, the first up day after a run of down days is followed by a fall in the coin's close over the next five days. The deeper the run's fall against the coin's usual daily swing over the last 30 days, the larger that fall.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader in a sideways market fades the first bounce after a deep slide, in the choppy short cells where the research holds almost no short strategy.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a choppy market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 074-01.b1.g2_calm-downtrend-relief-fade_1d (idea 074-01.b1.g2_calm-downtrend-relief-fade, brief 074_falling-market-run-break-fade).
What it rests on:
- F4: The fade also appears in choppy markets (60-day return between −10 % and +10 %), and grows with the horizon. The short excess is +1.26 % at 1 day (t 1.78), +2.36 % at 3 days (t 2.37) and +3.42 % at 5 days (t 2.78), over 77 to 81 cases on 61 to 65 days.
- F5: The choppy fade is graded by depth. The deep half gives +3.49 % over 3 days (t 2.11, 40 cases, 35 days) against +1.16 % for the shallow half (t 1.17), and the 3-day slope on depth is +0.0133 (Spearman 0.12).
- F6: Part of the choppy fade is drift. Every choppy day taken short on a 3-day grid already gives +1.34 % excess (t 1.70, 212 cases). The choppy run-break on the 3-day grid is +1.79 % (t 1.32, 31 cases), so the gain over the profile's base rate is only about 1 point on few independent cases.
- F3: The fade is absent in rising markets, as the brief predicts. There the 1-, 2-, 3- and 5-day short excess is −0.39 %, −0.67 %, −0.31 % and −0.33 % (t from −0.24 to −0.80, 176 cases, 98 days), and the deep half is −0.40 % at 1 day.
