Idea: An up day that ends a run of down days is followed by a fall over the next day, and that fall is larger the more the up day's volume exceeds the coin's average daily volume over the past 20 days.
Consequence: A trader should treat a heavy-volume first bounce after a down run as trapped holders selling into it, and fade it harder than a quiet bounce.
Why:
- F2: The up day's volume against its own past 20-day mean also grades the fade: from the lowest to the highest quartile the next-day short excess is +0.45 % (t 0.96), +0.84 % (t 2.21), +1.10 % (t 2.32) and +1.51 % (t 3.19, 146 days), with a Spearman correlation of 0.10.
