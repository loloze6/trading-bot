Idea: An up day that ends a run of down days is followed by a fall over the next day, and that fall is larger the wider the up day's high-low range is against the coin's daily swing over the past 30 days.
Consequence: A trader should fade a wide, contested first bounce after a down run more than a narrow one.
Why:
- F3: The width of the up day's high-low range grades the fade too: from the narrowest to the widest quartile it is +0.44 % (t 1.23), +0.46 % (t 1.24), +1.34 % (t 3.07) and +1.90 % (t 3.01, 141 days). The size of the up day's close-to-close gain does not grade it (Spearman 0.03), and neither does the share of the run it took back (-0.04).
- F5: The deep half of runs fades more when the coin is swinging a lot: +1.77 % (t 2.88, 120 days) in the high-volatility half against +0.82 % (t 1.67) in the low-volatility half. The shallow halves give +0.63 % and +0.32 %, neither distinguishable from 0.
