Idea: The more extreme a coin's 10-day return in units of its own 60-day daily volatility, the larger its rise beyond its normal drift over the next 10 days, with the largest moves following the coin's most extreme rises.
Consequence: A trader would keep a long position on a coin's most extreme volatility-scaled rises for about ten days rather than five, and refuse to hold modest rises that long.
Why:
- F1: The tested idea's gain sits almost entirely in the coin's most extreme rises, not across the range: on the 5-day grid, a 10-day return of 3 or more 60-day volatility units is followed by +5.53 % beyond drift (t 2.64, 54 days, 79 cases, strength 0.42). The 2-to-3 band gives +1.12 % (t 1.06, 99 days), and the 1-to-2 band gives +0.07 % (t 0.08, 233 days).
- F2: The same extreme rises keep going past the plan's 5-day horizon. On a 10-day grid, a volatility-scaled 10-day return of 3 or more is followed by +13.35 % beyond drift over 10 days (t 2.41, 27 days, 36 cases, strength 0.69), against +3.25 % for the 2-to-3 band (t 1.21) and +1.26 % for the 1-to-2 band (t 0.85). On a 20-day grid it fades to +12.4 % with t 1.47 over 15 days, so the evidence weakens beyond about 10 days.
- F3: The book earns from the trades that stay open well past the 5-day minimum. Over 756 trades, the top 10 % carry 93 % of the summed net trade return, and the median trade nets only +0.26 % with a 52 % win share. Trades held up to 5.5 days net a median of −1.72 % (478 trades), those held 10 to 20 days a mean of +56.0 % (59 trades), and those held over 20 days a mean of +80.7 % (7 trades).
