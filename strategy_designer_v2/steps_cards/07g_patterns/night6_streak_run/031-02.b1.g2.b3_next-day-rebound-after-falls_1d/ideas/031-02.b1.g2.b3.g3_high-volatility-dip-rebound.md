Idea: The larger a coin's fall on its latest day, the more it rises over the next 1 day, and this rebound is larger the higher the coin's 60-day standard deviation of daily returns stands against its own past values.
Consequence: A trader should size dip-buying up in the coin's high-volatility periods, where the next-day rebound is several times the cost of a trade, and treat dips in calm periods as no edge.
Why:
- F3: Falls taken when the coin's 60-day volatility is in its own highest third rebound +0.861 % the next day (t 2.53, 521 days), against +0.152 % and +0.199 % in the lower two thirds. The rebound is positive in all six coins (+0.58 % BTC to +1.37 % XMR) and in every year with data except 2022 (−0.14 %).
- F4: Sorting down days by their raw percentage fall, with no volatility scaling, gives the same grading: +0.690 % (t 2.42, 614 days) after the largest fifth of falls and +0.292 % after the next, down to −0.223 % and −0.077 % after the smallest. The days with the largest falls also cluster on fewer calendar days (614 against about 1100), which points to market-wide volatile episodes.
- F6: The tested signal's edge (+0.19 % unusual move per case, about 1.0 times the 0.19 % cost) is concentrated: falls on wide-range days, on volatility-heating days or in high-volatility thirds carry an unusual next-day move of about +0.86 % to +0.95 %, roughly 4.5 to 5 times the cost of a trade.
