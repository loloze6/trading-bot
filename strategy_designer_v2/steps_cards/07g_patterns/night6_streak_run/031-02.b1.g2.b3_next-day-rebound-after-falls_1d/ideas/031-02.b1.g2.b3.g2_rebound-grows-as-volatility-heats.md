Idea: The larger a coin's fall on its latest day, the more it rises over the next 1 day, and this rebound is larger the higher the standard deviation of the coin's daily returns over the past 10 days stands against that over the past 60 days.
Consequence: A trader should expect a dip to rebound strongly when the coin's volatility is heating up, and expect no rebound when the coin has recently been calmer than usual.
Why:
- F2: The rebound after falls (latest day below −0.5 standard deviations) is graded by the coin's 10-day volatility against its 60-day volatility: −0.167 % (t −0.79, 580 days) when the last 10 days are calmer, +0.389 % (t 1.96, 668 days) when they are about the same, and +0.947 % (t 3.28, 578 days) when they are hotter.
- F6: The tested signal's edge (+0.19 % unusual move per case, about 1.0 times the 0.19 % cost) is concentrated: falls on wide-range days, on volatility-heating days or in high-volatility thirds carry an unusual next-day move of about +0.86 % to +0.95 %, roughly 4.5 to 5 times the cost of a trade.
