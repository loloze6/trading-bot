Idea: The wider a coin's high-low range on a day it closes down, measured against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 60 days, the more the coin rises over the next 1 day.
Consequence: A trader buying a fall should favour falls made on wide, violent days and refuse the rebound trade after narrow, quiet down days.
Why:
- F1: On down days the next-day excess return grows with the day's high-low range measured in 60-day standard deviations of daily returns: −0.184 % (t −1.08, 706 days) in the narrowest fifth, rising through +0.069 % and +0.140 % to +0.882 % (t 3.41, day-clustered, 1648 cases on 596 days) in the widest fifth. On up days the same widest fifth gives −0.324 % (t −1.43), so the effect belongs to falls.
- F5: Down days that close in the lowest fifth of their high-low range rise +0.349 % the next day (t 2.24, 1252 days), against −0.217 % and −0.785 % for down days closing in the upper fifths. This is weaker than F1 to F3 given the number of angles looked at.
- F6: The tested signal's edge (+0.19 % unusual move per case, about 1.0 times the 0.19 % cost) is concentrated: falls on wide-range days, on volatility-heating days or in high-volatility thirds carry an unusual next-day move of about +0.86 % to +0.95 %, roughly 4.5 to 5 times the cost of a trade.
