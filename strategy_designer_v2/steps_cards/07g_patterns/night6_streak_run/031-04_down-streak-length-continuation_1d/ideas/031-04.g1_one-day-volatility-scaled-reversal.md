Idea: The larger a coin's return on its last day, measured against its own daily volatility over the past 30 days, the more the coin moves the opposite way over the next 1 day. A large down day is followed by a rise above its drift, and a large up day by a weaker day.
Consequence: A trader should lean against an unusually large daily move for the following day only, sized with how unusual the move was. The same move is no reason to bet on a reversal lasting 5 days. The effect of about 0.08 of a usual daily move is only somewhat larger than the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
Why:
- F3: Next-day moves run against the last day's move, measured in the coin's own 30-day daily volatility, and the effect is graded.
- F4: The reversal fades with the horizon. Over 3 days the correlation is weaker: -0.048 (p 0.002) for the last day's return and -0.034 (p 0.03) for the run's return, on 4,026 cases. Over 5 days it is gone or turned: +0.023 (p 0.27) and +0.018 (p 0.38), on 2,346 cases.
