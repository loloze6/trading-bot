Idea: The larger a coin's one-day fall, measured against its own daily volatility over the past 60 days, the more it rises above its normal drift over the next 3 days.
Consequence: A sharp, volatility-adjusted down close is a reason to buy the coin for a few days, and a reason to refuse a fresh short on it, more firmly the deeper the fall.
Why:
- F3: The size of a down day, scaled by the coin's 60-day daily volatility, grades the rebound that follows. The Spearman correlation between the fall's size and the next unusual move is +0.075 over 1 day (4190 cases, p 1e-6), +0.069 over 2 days (2022 cases, p 0.002) and +0.084 over 3 days (1293 cases, p 0.002). It is positive on all six coins, from +0.033 (ZEC) to +0.137 (XMR, p 0.0004) and +0.128 (XRP, p 0.0009).
- F4: The unusual move falls steadily across the deciles of the volatility-scaled daily return. Over 3 days the lowest decile is followed by +1.28 % (280 cases, 105 days, day-clustered t 1.29), the next by +0.71 %, and the 7th to 9th deciles by -0.60 %, -0.57 % and -0.89 %. Over 1 day the bottom decile shows +0.58 % (t 1.64, 308 days) and the bottom quintile +0.39 % (t 3.14, 1703 cases), positive on all six coins (XMR +0.97 %, t 3.72) and in 7 of 8 years.
- F5: Large up days are not followed by a giveback. The top quintile of the volatility-scaled daily return shows -0.09 % over the next day (1703 cases, 678 days, t -0.52). Over 5 days the top quintile shows +1.52 % (t 2.31), which is the opposite of the tested reversal.
- F2: The tested situation, the up streak, predicts nothing. By coin, the Spearman correlation between streak length and the unusual 5-day move runs only from -0.050 (BTC) to +0.043 (ZEC). Streaks of +3, +4 and +5 days show unusual moves of +1.41 % (t 1.70), -0.25 % (t -0.23) and +0.28 % (t 0.28), with no grading and no reversal.
