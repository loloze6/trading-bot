Idea: The larger a coin's one-day fall, measured against its own daily volatility over the past 60 days, the more it rises above its normal drift over the next day.
Consequence: The rebound after a deep down close is largest in the first day, so a trader buying the fall gains most by acting at that close rather than waiting.
Why:
- F3: The size of a down day, scaled by the coin's 60-day daily volatility, grades the rebound that follows. The Spearman correlation between the fall's size and the next unusual move is +0.075 over 1 day (4190 cases, p 1e-6), +0.069 over 2 days (2022 cases, p 0.002) and +0.084 over 3 days (1293 cases, p 0.002). It is positive on all six coins, from +0.033 (ZEC) to +0.137 (XMR, p 0.0004) and +0.128 (XRP, p 0.0009).
- F4: The unusual move falls steadily across the deciles of the volatility-scaled daily return. Over 3 days the lowest decile is followed by +1.28 % (280 cases, 105 days, day-clustered t 1.29), the next by +0.71 %, and the 7th to 9th deciles by -0.60 %, -0.57 % and -0.89 %. Over 1 day the bottom decile shows +0.58 % (t 1.64, 308 days) and the bottom quintile +0.39 % (t 3.14, 1703 cases), positive on all six coins (XMR +0.97 %, t 3.72) and in 7 of 8 years.
- F1: The book lost because it was short almost all the time, not because the timing was wrong: its mean position ran from -0.70 (ZEC) to -1.14 (BTC). Its daily returns correlated -0.91 with the coins'. Of its mean daily return of -0.105 %, -0.111 % came from the coins' own rise and +0.011 % from timing.
