Idea: After a 1-day fall in a coin, the rise over the next day is larger the higher the coin's volatility over the past 7 days, and larger again the deeper the fall.
Consequence: A trader buying sharp one-day falls should commit most when the coin has been volatile over the past week and least when it has been calm.
Why:
- F3: The rebound after a sharp 1-day fall was concentrated where the coin's 7-day volatility was high. In the two deepest deciles, the top volatility tercile gave an unusual 24-hour move of +1.58 % (strength 0.31, t 6.6, 1084 cases), about 8 times the cost. The middle tercile gave +0.11 % (t 0.7) and the low tercile -0.14 % (t -1.2).
- F1: A coin's 1-day fall that is large against its usual daily move was followed by an unusual rise over the next 24 hours. In the deepest decile of the 1-day return scaled by recent volatility, the unusual move was +0.76 % (strength 0.145, t 4.6, 1626 non-overlapping cases), and in the next decile +0.27 % (strength 0.060, t 2.4). That is about 4 times and 1.4 times the 0.19 % cost of a buy and a sell, while deciles 2 to 9 were flat or slightly negative.
