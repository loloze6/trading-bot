Idea: A coin whose 1-day return is a large fall against its usual daily move rises over the next 1 to 3 days by more than its usual drift, and the deeper that fall against its usual daily move, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A sharp one-day fall is a reason to go long for the next day or few days, in proportion to how unusual the fall was, rather than to sell it.
Why:
- F1: A coin's 1-day fall that is large against its usual daily move was followed by an unusual rise over the next 24 hours. In the deepest decile of the 1-day return scaled by recent volatility, the unusual move was +0.76 % (strength 0.145, t 4.6, 1626 non-overlapping cases), and in the next decile +0.27 % (strength 0.060, t 2.4). That is about 4 times and 1.4 times the 0.19 % cost of a buy and a sell, while deciles 2 to 9 were flat or slightly negative.
- F2: The rebound after a sharp 1-day fall was graded and held up across samples. On the falling side, the slope of strength on the scaled 1-day return was -0.14 (the deeper the fall, the larger the rebound). The two deepest deciles were positive on all 6 coins (strength 0.077 on BTC to 0.128 on XRP, t 1.6 to 2.8), in 7 of 8 years (all but 2022, at -0.006), and in 69 % of coin-windows.
- F4: The rebound after the deepest 1-day falls lasted beyond one day. In the deepest decile the unusual move was +0.59 % over 48 hours (t 2.2, 803 cases) and +1.29 % over 72 hours (strength 0.14, t 3.2, 529 cases), so the move per case keeps growing past the first day.
