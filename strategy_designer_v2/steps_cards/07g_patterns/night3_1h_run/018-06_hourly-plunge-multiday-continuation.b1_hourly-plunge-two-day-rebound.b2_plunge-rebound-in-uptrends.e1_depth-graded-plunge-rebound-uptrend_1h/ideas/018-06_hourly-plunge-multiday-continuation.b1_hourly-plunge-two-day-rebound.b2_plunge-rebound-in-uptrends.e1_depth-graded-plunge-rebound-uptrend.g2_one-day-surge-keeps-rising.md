Idea: The larger a coin's 24-hour rise in units of its 30-day mean absolute daily return, the more its price rises over the next 10 days above its usual 10-day drift.
Consequence: A one-day surge several times the coin's usual daily move, caught at the hour it forms, is a reason to buy for about 10 days rather than to fade it.
Why:
- F4: A 24-hour rise larger than 2 usual daily moves is followed by an unusual move of +3.43 % over the next 10 days (t 2.28, 248 cases) and +1.23 % over 4 days (t 1.64, 258 cases). This matches the brief's breakout, but on the long side rather than after a plunge.
- F1: A coin's 7-day return measured in units of its 30-day mean absolute daily return predicts its next 10 days, and the effect is graded at the top: on non-overlapping 240-hour cells, the top fifth of that measure is followed by an unusual move of +4.49 % (t 3.44, 278 cases, strength 0.27), the fourth fifth by +0.20 % (t 0.25), and the bottom three fifths by -1.23 %, -1.92 % and -1.57 % (t -1.5 to -2.2). Top minus bottom is positive in all 6 coins (Spearman 0.03 to 0.15, BTC and ETH p 0.02) and in 6 of 8 years (2022 and 2023 negative).
- F5: The book is carried by trades held far beyond the 4-day horizon: the median trade lasts 219 hours. The longest quarter of trades (median 822 hours) earns +23.8 % mean and +11.7 % median after fees, against +0.10 % for the shortest quarter (median 5 hours) and -3.2 % for the second quarter (median 130 hours).
