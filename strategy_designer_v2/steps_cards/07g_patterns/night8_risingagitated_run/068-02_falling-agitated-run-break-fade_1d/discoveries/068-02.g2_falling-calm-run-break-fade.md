A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, calm market, on a day that closes up, the longer the unbroken run of down days just before it, the larger the coin's fall over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: In a quiet downtrend, a trader shorts the first up day after a run of falls, more heavily the longer the run, for the next day.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 068-02_falling-agitated-run-break-fade_1d (idea 068-02_falling-agitated-run-break-fade, brief 068_falling-agitated-first-bounce-fades).
What it rests on:
- F3: The same graded fade is present in the falling calm profile, which the brief's profile leaves out (60-day trend below -10 %, calm, so outside the book). The slope of an up day's next-day excess return on the prior down run is -0.52 % per day (t -2.85, 541 cases, 220 days). For up days after 2 or more falls, the mean is negative on 5 of 6 coins (XMR -1.51 % t -2.02, ZEC -1.78 % t -2.18, BTC -2.26 %, ETH -1.69 %, XRP -1.05 %; LTC -0.11 %) and on 6 of the 8 windows with more than 3 cases.
- F4: The fade does not appear when the trend is rising: up days after 1, 2 and 3+ down days are followed by +0.31 %, +0.58 % and +0.08 % (all |t| < 1). Over all falling-trend days, up days after 1 and 2+ down days give -1.07 % (t -1.92) and -1.34 % (t -2.54), against +0.13 % after an up day.
