A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, a coin that rises on a day after falling on the days before falls over the next day, and the more of its previous two days were falls, the larger that next-day fall.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader in a falling, agitated coin shorts the first up day after a string of down days for one day, rather than shorting every strong close.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 060-01.g1.d2.d1_own-range-close-height-fade_1d (idea 060-01.g1.d2.d1_own-range-close-height-fade, brief 060_falling-agitated-high-close-fades).
What it rests on:
- F1: Inside the falling agitated profile, close location does not separate up days: up days closing in the upper half of their range are followed by a -0.97 % unusual next day (t -2.07, 171 days, 412 cases) and those closing in the lower half by -1.05 % (t -1.11, 50 days, 88 cases); across all in-profile up days the figure is -0.97 % (t -2.52, 258 days, 613 cases). The fade follows a rise, not a close near the high.
- F3: Inside the profile, the lone up day fades most. When it is the only rise in the last three days, the next day's unusual move is -2.10 % (t -2.95, 87 days, 159 cases), against -0.48 % (t -0.99, 172 days) with two rises and -0.70 % (t -1.03, 58 days) with three.
