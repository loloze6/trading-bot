A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a rising market, the larger a coin's one-day fall measured in its own usual daily swings, the more it rises over the next 3 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In a coin whose 60-day trend is up, a trader buys a sharp down day rather than reading it as the end of the rise, sizing up with the size of the fall.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO, in a rising market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 058-03.e2.b1_choppy-distance-continuation-more-coins_1d (idea 058-03.e2.b1_choppy-distance-continuation-more-coins, brief 058_rising-agitated-overextension-gives-back).
What it rests on:
- F4: In a rising trend (60-day return above +10 %), a sharp down day is followed by a rebound over the next 3 days in both agitation states. After a fall of 1 to 2 of the coin's daily swings, the excess 3-day return is +6.1 % (t 3.65, 41 cases) when calm and +5.5 % (t 2.14, 34 cases) when agitated. It is positive in every year, 2019 to 2021 (calm +2.8 %, +9.3 %, +4.4 %; agitated +1.7 %, +1.7 %, +8.0 %), while falls of under one swing give only +0.9 % (calm, t 0.99) and -0.5 % (agitated).
- F5: Across all coin-days, the next day after the largest daily falls is strongly up. The lowest decile of the coin-standardised daily return is followed by +1.47 % (t 6.93, 1222 days) and the second decile by +0.64 % (t 3.64), against about 0 % for the middle deciles.
