A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day rise measured in its own usual daily swings, the more it gives back over the next 3 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In a coin that has been falling and swinging hard, a trader sells into a sharp up day instead of chasing it, more heavily the larger the rise.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 058-03.e2.b1_choppy-distance-continuation-more-coins_1d (idea 058-03.e2.b1_choppy-distance-continuation-more-coins, brief 058_rising-agitated-overextension-gives-back).
What it rests on:
- F6: In a falling, agitated market, the larger an up day, the weaker the next 3 days. The Spearman rho of the day's standardised rise against the excess 3-day return is -0.183 (p 0.0024, 272 cases), and the excess return falls from +2.0 % to -2.6 % (t -2.22) from the lowest to the highest quartile of the rise. Three of four years point that way (2018 -2.6 %, 2019 -5.0 %, 2020 -5.5 %, 2021 +2.5 %). Taken per day it is weaker (t 1.34 over 77 days); it is the only pattern seen in the empty falling-agitated short cell.
