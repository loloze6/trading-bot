A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a choppy, agitated market, a coin that closes in the upper part of its day's high-low range falls over the next day, and the nearer the close sits to the day's high, the larger that next-day fall.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader in a sideways but swinging market sells strength at the close for one day, sized by how near the close is to the high, a place where no strategy now trades short.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a choppy agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 060-01.g1.d2.d1.g3_wide-high-close-fade_1d (idea 060-01.g1.d2.d1.g3_wide-high-close-fade, brief 060_falling-agitated-high-close-fades).
What it rests on:
- F4: In a choppy market (60-day return between -10 % and +10 %) with 30-day swing above the coin's median (a proxy, since the 365-day lookback cannot be rebuilt in a window), high closes were followed by -1.69 % excess the next day (t -2.28, 145 cases over 112 days). Low closes there were followed by +0.22 % (t 0.53). In the calm half of choppy markets, high closes gave only -0.23 % (t -0.38). The choppy agitated short cell of the profile map is empty.
- F5: Outside the tested profile, the weakness in falling markets is trend drift, not close location. Every close-location quintile in falling or choppy non-tested bars had a negative next-day excess, from -0.14 % to -0.84 %. The middle quintile was strongest (t -2.73, 224 days) and the top quintile gave -0.42 % (t -1.61), so there is no grading by how near the close sits to the high.
