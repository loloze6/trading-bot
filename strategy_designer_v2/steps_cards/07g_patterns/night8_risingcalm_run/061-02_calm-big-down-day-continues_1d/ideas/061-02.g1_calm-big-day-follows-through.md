Idea: In a calm market, the larger a coin's one-day return against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger its return in the same direction over the next 5 days.
Consequence: In calm markets, a trader follows a day that stands out against the coin's recent swing, long after a large up day and short after a large down day, with more size the larger the day.
Why:
- F1: In calm markets the brief's effect is present on both sides together. A day's move in units of its 30-day swing is followed by a 5-day excess return the same way of +1.52 % (t 2.75 with same-day cases summed; 771 cases on 195 days), and the slope is +1.74 % per swing unit (t 4.82). In agitated markets the same measure is +0.03 % (t 0.03; 587 cases), with a slope of -0.01 % per unit.
- F2: In calm markets the follow-through grows with the day's size. By quartile of |day's return / 30-day std| it runs from +0.80 % (t 1.12) in the lowest to +2.66 % (t 2.57, 86 days) in the highest. Measured from the next day's close, the highest quartile gains +4.08 % over 5 days (t 3.47) against +0.87 % in the lowest.
- F3: The tested idea took only down days, and that is why it fell short. Calm down days follow through by +1.56 % (t 1.97, 141 days) and calm up days by +1.47 % (t 1.58, 125 days, slope t 2.68). The verdict's down-only main measure had t 1.59, and its median split failed (-0.25), because the forecast is standardised over 60 values rather than read in raw swing units.
