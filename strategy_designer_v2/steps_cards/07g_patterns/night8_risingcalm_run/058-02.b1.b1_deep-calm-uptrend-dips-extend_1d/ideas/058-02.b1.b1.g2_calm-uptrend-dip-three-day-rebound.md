Idea: In a rising, calm market, the larger a coin's one-day fall against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the larger its rise over the next 3 days.
Consequence: A trader can hold a calm-uptrend dip purchase for about three days rather than one, but not as long as five.
Why:
- F3: The rebound persists past one day. On non-overlapping grids, the graded product in rising calm is +1.07 % over 2 days (t 2.24, 377 cases, 161 days) and +1.96 % over 3 days (t 2.30, 234 cases, 104 days). Falls of more than one swing gain +4.39 % excess over 3 days (t 2.79, 67 cases).
- F4: The 5-day horizon the tested idea used shows no effect. The verdict found an unusual move of +0.04 % per case (t 0.09, 1159 cases). Short trades lost −5.33 % on average net (1098 trades), and the shorts paid the profile's drift of about +0.34 % a day. The rebound is concentrated in the first 1–3 days.
- F2: The rebound is graded across bins in the rising calm profile. Days falling more than one usual swing gain +1.62 % excess the next day (t 2.97, 173 cases, 91 days). Smaller falls gain +0.47 % (t 1.81, 560 cases), and up days gain −0.02 % (t −0.05, 624 cases).
