Idea: In a rising, calm market, the larger a coin's one-day fall against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: A trader in a calm uptrend can buy a sharp one-day drop for a one-day hold, sized by how large the drop was against the coin's usual swing.
Why:
- F1: In the rising calm profile, a one-day fall is followed by a next-day rebound that grows with the fall's size, measured against the coin's 30-day daily swing. The product of the scaled fall and the next day's excess return over all down days averages +0.81 % (t 3.10 with day-clustered errors, 731 cases on 324 days). Outside the profile the same product is weaker: +0.50 %, t 1.81, 3304 cases.
- F2: The rebound is graded across bins in the rising calm profile. Days falling more than one usual swing gain +1.62 % excess the next day (t 2.97, 173 cases, 91 days). Smaller falls gain +0.47 % (t 1.81, 560 cases), and up days gain −0.02 % (t −0.05, 624 cases).
- F5: The next-day graded product is positive in all 4 years: 2018 +0.50 % (t 0.50), 2019 +0.14 % (t 0.23), 2020 +1.23 % (t 4.73) and 2021 +0.83 % (t 1.47). It is positive in 9 of 10 coins; DOGE is the exception with 8 cases. It is positive in 9 of 12 windows (w15 −0.48 %, t −0.33).
- F8: The size of the rebound clears the cost of a trade. Next-day excess after falls of more than one swing is +1.62 % (F2), against a cost of 0.22 % per trade, about 7 times the cost before the drift is added.
