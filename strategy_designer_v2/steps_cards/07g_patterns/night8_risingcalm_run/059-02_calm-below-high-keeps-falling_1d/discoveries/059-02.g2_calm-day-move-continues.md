A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a calm market, the larger a coin's one-day return measured in units of its own 30-day daily swing, the further it moves the same way over the next 5 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In quiet markets a trader follows an unusually large day for a week, long after a large up-day and short after a large down-day, instead of fading it.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 059-02_calm-below-high-keeps-falling_1d (idea 059-02_calm-below-high-keeps-falling, brief 059_calm-market-steady-declines-continue).
What it rests on:
- F6: In calm markets a single day's swing-scaled return carries on over the next 5 days. The largest-up fifth (+1.40 swings) is followed by +3.11 % (t 3.25) and the two lowest fifths by −1.55 % (t −2.24) and −2.30 % (t −2.88). In agitated markets this is absent: every fifth has |t| ≤ 0.82.
- F7: The calm one-day continuation keeps its sign every year: slope t 0.68 in 2018, 3.99 in 2019, 1.57 in 2020 and 2.52 in 2021. It is strongest in rising calm (t 3.54, 178 cases) and choppy calm (t 2.67, 101 cases).
- F2: The same graded effect is absent in agitated markets. Depth fifths there give −1.52 % to +1.68 % with no order and no |t| above 1.74, and the depth slope in falling agitated is the other way (t −1.93, 150 cases). The effect lives in calm markets only.
