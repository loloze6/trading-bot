A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling market, the larger a coin's one-day rise against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the more it falls back over the next 3 days.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader sells, or refuses to buy, large up days in a falling coin, sized by the rise's size in swing units, instead of chasing them.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 061-01.g1.g3_calm-big-fall-rebounds_1d (idea 061-01.g1.g3_calm-big-fall-rebounds, brief 061_calm-market-big-day-continues).
What it rests on:
- F1: Outside rising markets (60-day return at or below +10 %), the larger a coin's up day in 30-day swing units, the lower its excess return over the next 3 days. The day-summed slope is -0.028 (t -3.23, 134 days). In falling markets alone it is -0.033 (t -2.97, 103 days). The sign holds in 2018 (t -2.30), 2019 (t -2.09) and 2020 (t -3.23), turns in 2021 (t +2.67), and is negative on all six coins (t from -3.10 on LTC to -0.02 on ZEC).
- F2: In falling markets, an up day of more than one swing is followed by a 3-day excess return of -4.84 % when agitated (t -3.06, 29 cases) and -2.79 % when calm (t -2.10, 35 cases). The fade is present at both agitation levels. The profile map holds no strategy on the short side of falling agitated and only one on falling calm.
