Idea: In a calm market, the larger a coin's one-day fall against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the more it rises back over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A trader buys large down days in quiet markets, sized by the fall's size in swing units, instead of following them.
Why:
- F4: In calm markets, a down day that is large against the coin's swing is followed by a rebound that grows with the size of the day. The graded slope of the move in the day's direction was -0.0077 over 2 days (t -2.60, 1391 cases), -0.0100 over 5 days (t -1.36, 616 cases) and -0.0298 over 10 days (t -2.09, 314 cases). After calm up days the slopes were +0.0017 over 2 days (t 0.60) and +0.0135 over 10 days (t 0.86), neither distinguishable from zero.
- F1: The book's loss comes from its short side. Short trades lost at every holding length: 214 trades of up to 5 days summed -387.8 %, 56 of 6 to 10 days -122.9 %, and 52 of 10 to 30 days -199.9 %. Long trades held longer than 5 days gained: 11 trades at 5 to 6 days +90.6 %, 57 at 6 to 10 days +148.7 %, 58 at 10 to 30 days +106.4 %. Per bar in a position, short positions earned -0.019 % on 688 bars and long positions +0.021 % on 830 bars.
