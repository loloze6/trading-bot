Idea: In a calm market, the nearer a coin's close sits to the low of a day whose range is small against its 30-day daily swing, the larger its fall over the next 5 days.
Consequence: In calm markets a quiet day that ends near its low is a reason to expect further decline and to refuse buying it as a dip.
Why:
- F6: In calm markets a narrow day (range below the median in units of the 30-day swing) that closes in the lower third of its range is followed by a drift-removed 5-day return of -3.60 % (t -3.00, 109 cases, 58 days). The same day in agitated markets gives -1.59 % (t -1.01), and calm narrow days closing elsewhere give +1.56 %. It is negative in 8 of the 12 visible windows but positive in w11 (+8.8 %) and w17 (+5.7 %). Given the many angles examined, it is weaker evidence than F1.
- F2: The same continuation is absent in agitated markets. There the signed product averages +0.11 % (t 0.12, 718 cases, 186 days), and the deepest-decline quintile is followed by a rebound of +1.62 % (t 1.24, 137 cases). The calm condition carries the effect.
