A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a calm market, the larger a coin's one-day return in units of its own 30-day daily swing, the larger its move in the same direction over the next 5 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In low-swing markets a single unusually large day is the start of a move, not noise to fade, and a trader can follow it for about a week.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 059-01_calm-decline-continues-return_1d (idea 059-01_calm-decline-continues-return, brief 059_calm-market-steady-declines-continue).
What it rests on:
- F5: A one-day move scaled by the 30-day swing also carries on in calm markets. The top quintile (mean +1.40 swing units) is followed by +3.11 % excess over 5 days (t 3.3, n 155), and the bottom two quintiles by -1.55 % and -2.30 %. In agitated markets every quintile has |t| < 0.9.
- F2: The same measure in agitated markets shows nothing. The slope has t 0.13 on 591 grid bars, and the deepest-decline quintile is followed by +1.63 %, not a fall. The continuation lives in calm markets only.
