Idea: In a choppy, calm market, a coin whose 20-day return is more negative, measured against its 30-day daily swing, falls further over the next 10 days. The larger the swing-scaled 20-day fall, the larger the further fall.
Consequence: In a flat, quiet market, a trader should short coins that have slid steadily over several weeks by a lot for their quiet swing, rather than after a single sharp week, and should refuse to buy them as range bargains.
Why:
- F1: In choppy markets (60-day return between -10 % and +10 %, folds B and C), the coin's 20-day return in units of its 30-day daily swing predicts its next 5-day excess return in the same direction: slope +0.051 with t 3.67 (days pooled) on 126 calm cases over 58 days, against slope +0.084 with t 1.34 on only 26 agitated cases. Over all profiles the slope is only t 1.84, and in rising and falling markets t is 1.34 and 0.79.
- F2: The effect is larger at 10 days. In choppy markets the 20-day swing-scaled return predicts the next 10-day excess return with slope +0.080 and t 5.66 (77 non-overlapping cases, 33 days). By choppy quartile of that return, the most fallen quarter (mean -0.95 swing units) is followed by -9.06 % over 5 days (t -2.18, 21 days) and the next quarter by -2.65 % (t -1.73).
- F3: This continuation in choppy markets is not steady across time or coins. By year its slope t is -1.39 (2018), -0.10 (2019), 2.55 (2020) and 4.97 (2021). By coin it is positive on all 6 coins with enough cases, with t from 0.45 (BTC) to 4.67 (XRP). On the falling half alone (r20 < 0) it gives t 1.30 on 78 cases.
- F4: The tested 5-day swing-scaled fall is weaker than the 20-day one inside the profile. Its slope there has t 1.72 (211 grid cases), and only its deepest quintile stands out (-3.37 % excess over 5 days, t -2.29, 43 cases). Its yearly slopes on falls alone run from t -0.83 to 0.80. This matches step 5's inconclusive slope t of 0.87 and its size of 0.030 of a usual move, below the 0.22 % cost.
