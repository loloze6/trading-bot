Idea: In a rising, agitated market, a coin's 3-day return measured in units of its own usual daily swing (the 30-day standard deviation of its daily returns), read directly and not ranked against its recent values, is followed by a rise over the next 3 days that grows with the number of swing units, most strongly for the largest pushes.
Consequence: A trader follows a short push by its absolute size against the coin's swing, scaling up most on the rare very large pushes, and does not rank it against the coin's own recent pushes.
Why:
- F1: Across all in-profile bars on the 3-day grid, the 3-day return scaled by the 30-day daily swing ranks the excess 3-day return that follows (Spearman rho +0.168, p 0.003, 314 cases); the quintile means rise from −2.43 % to +4.88 %, and the top quintile has a day-pooled t of 2.02.
- F2: Grouped by raw swing units, the grading is monotone and steepest at the top: (−1, 0] gives −1.52 % (120 cases, t −1.36), (0, 1] gives +0.49 % (113, t 0.41), (1, 1.155] gives +2.61 % (9), (1.155, 2] gives +3.50 % (22, t 1.34), and above 2 gives +11.14 % (20, t 1.91).
- F3: The tested forecast re-ranks this value against its own last 60 values, and that ranking does not grade the outcome: step 5's slope of the excess return on the forecast is −0.0025, and trades by entry-forecast tercile earn +7.31 %, +1.16 % and +6.33 % net (52 trades each).
- F4: The effect is a 3-day one: at a 5-day horizon the same scaled 3-day return ranks the excess return only weakly (rho +0.089, p 0.23, 182 cases).
