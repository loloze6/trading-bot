Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is negative, a one-day fall that is large relative to its 20-day standard deviation of daily returns is followed by a rise over the next day, and the larger the fall in standard deviations, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader would buy the day after sharp drops in falling coins for a one-day rebound, and refuse to short into them.
Why:
- F4: In falling coins, the next day's move runs against the day's move. The slope of the 1-day unusual move on the day's move in standard deviations is −0.065 (t −6.05, n 7335). On up days alone it is −0.031 (t −1.34, n 3519), so the effect is carried mostly by down days that rebound. In rising coins the same slope is −0.019 (t −1.65, n 7885).
