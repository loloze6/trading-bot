Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is negative, the larger a one-day rise in usual daily moves (the day's return over the standard deviation of its last 20 daily returns), the higher the coin's return over the next 20 days.
Consequence: A trader would refuse to short a sharp bounce in a coin down over 90 days, since larger bounces were followed by gains rather than by a resumed fall.
Why:
- F4: In 90-day fallers, the brief's fade does not appear; the reverse does. Among up days in fallers, the 20-day unusual move rises with the size of the day's rise (slope +3.57 % per usual move, t 2.34, 379 cases). Rises of 2.5 to 3 and above 3 usual moves are followed by +5.9 % and +4.2 % over 20 days (t 0.96 and 1.14).
