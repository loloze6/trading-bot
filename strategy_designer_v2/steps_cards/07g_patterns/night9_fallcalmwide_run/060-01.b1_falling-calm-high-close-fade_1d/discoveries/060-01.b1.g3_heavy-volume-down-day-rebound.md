A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: A down day traded on heavier volume than the coin's 20-day average is followed by a rise over the next day, larger the heavier the volume against that average.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader buys the day after a heavy-volume down day in any market, more the heavier the volume.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 060-01.b1_falling-calm-high-close-fade_1d (idea 060-01.b1_falling-calm-high-close-fade, brief 060_falling-calm-wide-high-close-fade).
What it rests on:
- F5: Down days (z below −1) are followed by a rebound that grows with the day's volume against its 20-day mean, by volume tercile within each coin: low +0.36 % (73 cases), middle +0.94 % (248 cases, t 1.50), high +1.22 % (641 cases, 212 days, t 2.61). Up days show no such grading (+0.24 %, −0.20 %, −0.23 %). Over all bars, the top volume-ratio quintile is followed by +0.56 % (t 1.95) and the bottom quintile by −0.43 % (t −2.11).
