Idea: In a rising, calm market, a day whose high-to-low range is wide against the coin's 30-day swing of daily returns is followed by a rise over the next three days, whichever way it closed; the wider the day against that swing, the larger the rise.
Consequence: In an orderly uptrend, an unusually wide day is a reason to lean long for a few days, more so the wider it was, rather than to read it as the trend breaking.
Why:
- F2: At the 3-day horizon in rising calm, across days at least 1.5 usual moves wide, the move grows with the day's width more than with where it closed. The slope of the 3-day excess return on range was +1.28 % per usual move (t 1.98, 248 cases), against +3.93 % per unit of close location (t 1.54). This is the brief's own "the wider the day, the larger the rise", which the tested idea (graded on close location only, 1 day) did not test.
- F4: In rising calm, wide days closing in the upper half also rose over the next 3 days (+2.06 %, t 1.39, 79 cases) against +4.14 % for low closes. Over 1 day the 2–3 usual-move high-close days showed +0.59 % (t 1.21). In this profile a wide day leans to a rise whichever way it closed, and the low close adds to it.
