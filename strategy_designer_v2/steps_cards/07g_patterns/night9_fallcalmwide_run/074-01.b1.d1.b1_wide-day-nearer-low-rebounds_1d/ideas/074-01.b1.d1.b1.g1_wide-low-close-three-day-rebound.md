Idea: In a rising, calm market, a day whose high-to-low range is wide against the coin's 30-day swing of daily returns and that closes in the lower part of its range is followed by a rise over the next three days; the wider the day against that swing, the larger the rise.
Consequence: In an orderly uptrend, a wide low-close day is a reason to buy for a few days rather than one, sized by how wide the day was against the coin's usual swing.
Why:
- F1: In the rising calm profile (the bars the regime did not send to "chop"), wide days closing in the lower half of their range (range of at least 2 usual moves, close location of at least 0.5) were followed by a larger move the longer the horizon. The coin's excess return was +1.44 % over 1 day (t 2.53, 161 cases, 85 days), +2.25 % over 2 days (t 2.19, 90 cases, 47 days) and +4.14 % over 3 days (t 2.46, 64 non-overlapping cases, 32 days). The 3-day move is about 19 times the 0.22 % cost of a trade.
- F2: At the 3-day horizon in rising calm, across days at least 1.5 usual moves wide, the move grows with the day's width more than with where it closed. The slope of the 3-day excess return on range was +1.28 % per usual move (t 1.98, 248 cases), against +3.93 % per unit of close location (t 1.54). This is the brief's own "the wider the day, the larger the rise", which the tested idea (graded on close location only, 1 day) did not test.
- F3: The 3-day rebound after rising-calm wide low-close days was positive in every window with cases: w8 +3.4 %, w10 +3.1 %, w11 +2.5 %, w12 +2.7 %, w15 +0.8 %, w17 +13.6 %, w18 +7.8 %. Each window has few cases (4 to 10).
