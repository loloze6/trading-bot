Idea: In a rising, calm market, a day whose high-to-low range is wide against the coin's 30-day swing of daily returns and that closes near its low is followed by a rise over the next day. The wider the day's range against that swing, the larger the next-day rise.
Consequence: In an orderly uptrend, a trader buys the close of a wide low-closing day for a one-day rebound, and buys more the wider the day was rather than the nearer it closed to the low.
Why:
- F1: In folds B and C, rising-calm days that close in the bottom quarter of their range rebound the next day only when the range is wide against the 30-day swing, and the rebound grows with the width. The unusual next-day move by width band is:
- F2: The tested grading, nearness to the low, does not hold among the wide rising-calm days. The 0.75–0.9 band gives +2.77 % next day (t 1.88, 33 cases). The 0.9–1 band gives +1.86 % (t 2.65, 24 cases). Over 3 days the two bands give +3.80 % and +5.42 % on only 15 and 12 cases. This fits step 5's inconclusive verdict on the close-location forecast (+0.73 %, t 1.14, 164 days).
- F3: The rising-calm wide low-close rebound is already there after one day.
- F4: The effect is uneven across years. In 2019 the next-day move is +0.02 % (9 cases). In 2020 it is +4.72 % (t 2.83, 9 cases) and in 2021 +2.45 % (t 1.78, 38 cases). The pattern carries no reliable weight outside the 2020–2021 rise, so it may be weaker in folds A and D.
