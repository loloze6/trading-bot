A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a calm market, after a coin has fallen over its last 20 days, a day closing above the middle of its range is followed by a fall over the next day. The fall is larger the nearer the close sits to the day's high and the deeper the 20-day fall.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader sells the strong close inside a calm downtrend for one day, a short-side position the map holds almost nowhere.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO, in a calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 077-03.d2_low-close-rebound-three-days_1d (idea 077-03.d2_low-close-rebound-three-days, brief 077_choppy-agitated-wide-close-fade).
What it rests on:
- F3: The mirror case also appears. After a 20-day fall, a day closing above the middle of its range is followed by a fall the next day: −0.61 % against drift (t 2.13, 1,974 cases on 587 days). The fall grows with how near the high the close sits: 0.41 %, 0.51 % and 0.90 % across the terciles of the close's distance from the middle (t of the top tercile 2.65).
- F4: This down-trend fade lives in calm markets, at 0.78 % (t 3.42, 378 days), against 0.47 % (t 1.10) in agitated ones. It is positive on 8 of 10 coins. By year it is 0.87 %, 0.85 % and 1.22 % in 2018 to 2020, and 0.00 % in 2021.
- F8: The pullback moves in F1 and F3 are 0.6 % per case in one day, about three times the 0.22 % cost of a trade. A next-day hold could therefore pay, unlike the tested idea, whose total move was 0.1 times the cost.
