A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, calm market, the further a coin's close has risen above its lowest close of the last 20 days, the larger its rise over the next 3 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In a calm decline, a trader would buy coins that have already lifted well off their recent low and avoid those still sitting on it, which keep falling.
Seen on: 1d bars of BCH, ETH, ZEC, AAVE, FIL, DOGE, ALGO, XMR, in a falling calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 086-02.c2_quiet-three-day-rise-continuation_1d (idea 086-02.c2_quiet-three-day-rise-continuation, brief 086_falling-calm-heavy-volume-up-day-accumulation).
What it rests on:
- F1: In falling calm markets, a coin's 5-day excess return grows with how far its close stands above its lowest close of the last 20 days (log distance): day-clustered t 2.75 on 121 grid cases over 83 days. Measured from the 10-day low, t is 3.04 on 129 cases over 87 days. At a 3-day horizon, t is 3.29 on 216 cases over 148 days.
- F2: The same measure outside falling calm carries nothing: t 0.05 at 5 days on 572 cases and t -0.16 at 3 days on 932 cases. The pattern lives in this profile only.

- F4: The effect points the same way on every coin, with Spearman 0.12 (BCH, 18 cases), 0.24 (ETH, 44), 0.28 (XMR, 27) and 0.37 (ZEC, 32). It also points the same way in 3 of 4 years, at 0.21 (2018), 0.17 (2019) and 0.37 (2021). The exception is 2020 at -0.004 on 15 cases.
