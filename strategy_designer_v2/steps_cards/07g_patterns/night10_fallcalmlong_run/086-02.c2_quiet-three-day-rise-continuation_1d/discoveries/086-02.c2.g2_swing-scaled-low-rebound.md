A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, calm market, the larger a coin's rise above its lowest close of the last 20 days measured in units of its own 20-day swing, the larger its rise over the next 5 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In a calm decline, a trader would size longs by how many usual swings the coin has already climbed off its recent low.
Seen on: 1d bars of BCH, ETH, ZEC, AAVE, FIL, DOGE, ALGO, XMR, in a falling calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 086-02.c2_quiet-three-day-rise-continuation_1d (idea 086-02.c2_quiet-three-day-rise-continuation, brief 086_falling-calm-heavy-volume-up-day-accumulation).
What it rests on:
- F3: The effect is large and graded across the 20-day distance terciles at 5 days:
- F4: The effect points the same way on every coin, with Spearman 0.12 (BCH, 18 cases), 0.24 (ETH, 44), 0.28 (XMR, 27) and 0.37 (ZEC, 32). It also points the same way in 3 of 4 years, at 0.21 (2018), 0.17 (2019) and 0.37 (2021). The exception is 2020 at -0.004 on 15 cases.
- F5: Scaling the distance from the 20-day low by the coin's 20-day swing keeps the effect, at t 3.25 (3 days, 216 cases) and t 2.87 (5 days, 121 cases). The effect is gone by 10 days, at t 0.93 on 57 cases, so it is a short follow-through.
