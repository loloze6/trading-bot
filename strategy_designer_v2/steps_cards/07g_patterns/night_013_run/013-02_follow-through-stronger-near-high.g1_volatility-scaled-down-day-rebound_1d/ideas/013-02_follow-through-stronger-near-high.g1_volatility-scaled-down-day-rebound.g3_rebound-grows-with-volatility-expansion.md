Idea: A coin's fall on a day is followed by a rise over the next 1 day that grows with how high its 20-day volatility stands against its 60-day volatility. The more volatility has expanded, the larger the next day's rise.
Consequence: A trader has a reason to buy down days mainly when volatility is expanding, and to refuse rebound trades in calm, contracting markets.
Why:
- F4: The down-day rebound sits in high-volatility conditions in every coin and almost every year.
- F5: Volatility rising relative to its own longer level strengthens the rebound. Down days where 20-day volatility is high relative to 60-day volatility (top tercile) give +0.0054 (t = 3.77, n = 1417). The bottom tercile gives −0.0025 (t = −1.84).
