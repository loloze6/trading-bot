Idea: When a coin has fallen over the past 30 days and its daily swings over the past 5 days are wider than usual, the larger today's return is against the coin's usual daily move, the more the coin moves the opposite way over the next 1 day.
Consequence: In an agitated decline, a trader would refuse to chase the day's move and would favour fading the largest up and down days for one day.
Why:
- F1: When the coin was falling and agitated (30-day return below 0, and the 5-day spread of daily returns above the coin's median ratio to its 60-day spread), a larger day's return measured in units of its usual daily move was followed by a move the other way the next day. The day-clustered slope t was -2.45 over 334 days, and -2.87 with the range ratio added. Outside that regime the same slope had a t of -0.10 over 671 days.
- F2: The reversal in F1 is graded and holds across coins and years. Inside the falling, agitated regime, the next day's unusual move was +2.34 % after a day below -2 usual moves (n 83, t 2.75), +0.74 % after -2 to -1 (n 151), and -1.59 % and -1.32 % after days of 0 to +1 usual moves (t -3.30 and -3.26). The per-coin slope t was negative for all six coins (from -1.23 to -3.78, five beyond -2). The per-year slope t was negative in three of four years (2018 -1.64, 2019 -2.51, 2020 -5.70, 2021 -0.49).
- F3: The F1 effect is large enough to cover the cost of a trade, but it is a one-day effect. Fading the 40 % largest absolute days in that regime earned +1.07 % per case over the next day (t 3.78, 412 cases), which is 0.19 of the usual 1-day move (5.57 %) and about 5.6 times the 0.19 % cost. On a non-overlapping 2-day grid the slope t fell to -0.66 (168 days).
