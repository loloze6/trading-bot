Idea: When a coin's daily ranges over the past 30 days are wide compared with the past 90 days, the larger its 1-day fall measured against its 20-day volatility, the larger its rise over the next 1 day.
Consequence: A trader would buy a sharp down day in a volatile month for a one-day rebound, more heavily the larger the fall, giving many more trades than the monthly-low setup.
Why:
- F5: After a down day of 1.5 or more of its 20-day standard deviation, the next-day rebound appears only when the month's ranges are wide against the past 90 days.
- F6: The tested situation itself is rare, and in these folds its slices do not stand out from chance.
