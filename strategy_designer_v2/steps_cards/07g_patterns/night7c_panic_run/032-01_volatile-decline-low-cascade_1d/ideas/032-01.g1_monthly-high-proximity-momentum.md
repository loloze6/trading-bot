Idea: The closer a coin's close sits to its highest high of the prior 30 days, the more it rises over the next 5 days beyond its normal drift, and closes above that high are followed by the largest rises.
Consequence: A coin pressing on its monthly high is a reason to hold it long for the following days, and a coin far below that high is a reason to refuse a long.
Why:
- F1: How close a coin closes to its highest high of the prior 30 days sorts the next 5 days, on a non-overlapping 5-day grid across folds B and C. The top decile, closing about 0.3 % above that high, then beats the coin's drift by +3.89 % (t 2.50 with same-day cases taken together, 126 cases on 50 days). The next decile, about 5 % below the high, beats it by +3.29 % (t 2.50, 121 cases on 67 days). The four deciles 10 % to 35 % below the high all trail the drift, by -1.9 % to -3.4 % (decile 3: -3.39 %, t -2.69, 67 days).
- F2: The gap between the top fifth of closes and the rest is consistent. It holds in all 6 coins (from +2.3 % for LTC to +11.6 % for XRP) and in 10 of 12 windows (w7 and w9 are the exceptions, at -1.5 % and -1.1 %). The Spearman correlation of distance-from-high with the next 5-day unusual move is 0.13 (p 3e-6) over all cases. It is only 0.01 (p 0.85) among closes within 5 % of the high, so the effect grades across the whole range, not within the top.
- F3: A coin up over 30 days that closes above its prior 30-day high then beats its drift by +5.05 % over 5 days (t 2.37, 54 cases).
