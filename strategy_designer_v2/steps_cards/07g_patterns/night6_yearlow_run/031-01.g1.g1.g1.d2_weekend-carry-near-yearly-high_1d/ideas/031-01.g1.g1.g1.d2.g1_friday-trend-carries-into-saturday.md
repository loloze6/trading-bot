Idea: The larger a coin's 14-day return relative to its own recent daily volatility at Friday's close (UTC), the more the coin rises over the next day, Saturday.
Consequence: A trader would lean long into Saturday in coins with the strongest volatility-scaled two-week trend at Friday's close, and drop the yearly-high condition and the Sunday hold.
Why:
- F1: The weekend gain belongs to the coin's recent trend, not to its nearness to the yearly high. On Fridays, the 2-day excess return rises with the volatility-scaled 14-day return (slope 0.0062, t 2.78, 359 days, 2094 cases). Its slope on the distance below the 365-day high is flat (−0.0074, t −0.42, 101 days, 590 cases), which matches the tested slope's own t of 0.99.
- F2: The whole effect is on Saturday. From Friday's close, the next day's excess return rises with the volatility-scaled 14-day return (slope 0.0061, t 3.11, 363 days, 2118 cases) and with the 7-day return (t 3.18). Sunday's leg shows nothing (14-day t 0.22; 30-day t 0.08).
- F3: The trend-then-next-day link belongs to Friday alone. The next-day slope on the volatility-scaled 14-day return is −0.0012 to +0.0023 on every other weekday, with t between −0.72 and 1.13. Pooled over the days other than Thursday and Friday it is −0.00056 (t −0.61, 1811 days).
- F4: The effect is graded and concentrated in the strongest trends. On Thursday and Friday bars, the top quintile of the volatility-scaled 14-day return (mean 1.64) is followed by a +0.96 % next-day excess, strength 0.19 of a usual day's move (t 3.47, 334 days). The middle quintile gets −0.01 % and the bottom quintile +0.004 %. That +0.96 % is about five times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F5: The trend effect holds in both halves of the period and in every coin. On Thursday and Friday bars, the next-day slope is t 2.31 up to 2021 and t 1.83 from 2022. Every coin's slope is positive: XRP t 2.74, ZEC t 2.49, LTC t 1.83, BTC t 1.66, ETH t 1.45, XMR t 0.65.
- F6: The book's largest gains and losses both come on its strongest-trend Fridays. The book's best 2-day decile follows Fridays with a mean volatility-scaled 14-day return of 1.31, and its worst decile follows 1.22, against 0.38–0.58 in the middle deciles. Both sit nearest the high (distance −8 %). Strong trends carry both the payoff and the risk of the position.
