Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, a coin whose up day closes near the top of that day's high-low range falls over the next five days, and the nearer the close is to the day's high, the larger the fall.
Consequence: A trader shorts strong closes on up days in a choppy, agitated market, whether or not a pullback came before, and sizes the short by how near the close sits to the high.
Why:
- F1: In the choppy, agitated profile, an up day closing in the top third of its range is followed by a 5-day return 7.10 % below the coin's drift (t -2.85, 26 cases on 19 days, non-overlapping 5-day grid, folds B and C). This is the brief's fade without the tested idea's prior-fall condition.
- F2: The prior three-day fall that the tested idea requires adds little. In the profile, up days closing in the top quarter of the range fade by -7.19 % over 5 days after a prior fall (13 cases, t -1.95) and by -5.48 % after a prior rise (10 cases, t -2.20). Keeping only the pullbacks roughly halves the cases, which helps explain why the book fails on 0.0046 trades per day.
- F3: In the profile, the fade grows with how near the close sits to the high. By thirds of the close's place in the range, the 5-day excess is -1.46 % (19 cases), -2.03 % (38) and -5.96 % (29 cases on 20 days, t -2.09), with a Spearman correlation of -0.215 between close location and excess return.
- F4: The tested grading, a smaller gain against the usual swing giving a larger fall, does not show. Among in-profile high closes, gains under 0.7 usual moves give -9.08 % (11 cases, t -1.77) and larger gains give -7.14 % (13 cases, t -3.02).
