A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: After a coin's 5-day return has been a deep fall, a daily close near the top of that day's high-low range is followed by a further fall over the next five days, and the nearer the close sits to the day's high, the larger that fall.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader who shorts such a high-closing bounce keeps the short for about five days instead of covering after one or two.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 061-01.b1.b1.b1.d1.d1.d2_upper-close-after-sharp-week-fall_1d (idea 061-01.b1.b1.b1.d1.d1.d2_upper-close-after-sharp-week-fall, brief 061_choppy-agitated-high-close-fades).
What it rests on:
- F1: After a deep 5-day fall (5-day return of -1 usual move or less), the close's place in the day's range splits what follows in two. Closes in the top quarter of the range were followed by a further fall: -5.1 % against the coin's normal 3-day drift (t -2.5, 55 cases on 22 days). Closes in the 0.5–0.75 band showed no fall: +0.2 % (t 0.09, 84 cases). Closes in the lower half showed none either: -0.5 % (t -0.31, 227 cases).
- F4: The fall after a high close following a deep 5-day fall grows with the horizon. It is -5.1 % over 3 days (t -2.5) and -9.3 % over 5 days (t -1.78, 23 cases on 11 days). The book's trades show the same: trades held 4–6 days earned +7.4 % on average (97 trades) and trades held 6–10 days +12.5 % (20 trades). Trades closed within 3 days lost -1.8 % (465 trades).
