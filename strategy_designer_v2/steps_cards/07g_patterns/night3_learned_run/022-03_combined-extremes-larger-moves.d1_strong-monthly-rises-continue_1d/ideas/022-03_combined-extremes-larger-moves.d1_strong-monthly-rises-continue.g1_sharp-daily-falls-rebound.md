Idea: The larger a coin's fall over its last day, measured against its usual daily move over the past 20 days, the more the coin rises over the next day.
Consequence: A trader would buy a coin after its sharpest one-day falls, sized by how unusual the fall was, and would refuse to sell into them.
Why:
- F1: The day after a coin's largest falls (lowest tenth of the previous day's return over its 20-day volatility), the coin rose 0.56 % more than its normal daily drift (t 3.98, 1596 days), and 0.34 % more after the next tenth (t 2.82). Past the middle tenths the next day turns lower, down to -0.31 % in the eighth tenth (t -2.76). The next-day return falls as the previous day's standardised return grows in five of six coins: XMR t -5.23, ETH t -2.44, LTC t -2.25, BTC t -1.56. The move after the deepest falls is about 3 times the 0.19 % cost of a round trip.
