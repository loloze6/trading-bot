Idea: The larger a coin's fall over the past day, measured against its own daily volatility over the past 20 days, the higher its return over the next day.
Consequence: A trader would lean long a coin for one day after an unusually large daily fall, and would refuse to short it then, because the bounce per case is several times the cost of a trade.
Why:
- F1: Large one-day falls in a coin are followed by a next-day rebound that grows with the fall's size. Days are sorted into deciles of the day's return divided by the coin's 20-day daily volatility. The deepest decile (mean z -1.81, 1,522 days) is followed by a next-day unusual move of +0.59 % (t 4.0) and a total move of +0.69 %, which is 3.6 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade. The next decile (z -0.91) is followed by +0.26 % (t 2.1), and the third (z -0.55) by +0.07 % (t 0.6).
- F2: The next-day reversal is graded within each coin. The slope of the next day's unusual return on that z is negative for all 6 coins: XMR t -5.3, ETH -2.6, LTC -2.2, BTC -1.7, ZEC -1.0, XRP -0.2. On the two deepest deciles, the next-day unusual move is positive for every coin, from +0.16 % on BTC to +0.85 % on XMR (t 3.9).
- F3: The rebound is mainly a one-day move and is uneven across years.
- F4: Large up days are not followed by a symmetric fall. The top decile (z +1.89) is followed by +0.00 % the next day (t 0.0). The negative next-day moves sit in the middle-upper deciles: decile 7 gives -0.32 % (t -2.9).
- F5: The book's own tails show the same reversal.
