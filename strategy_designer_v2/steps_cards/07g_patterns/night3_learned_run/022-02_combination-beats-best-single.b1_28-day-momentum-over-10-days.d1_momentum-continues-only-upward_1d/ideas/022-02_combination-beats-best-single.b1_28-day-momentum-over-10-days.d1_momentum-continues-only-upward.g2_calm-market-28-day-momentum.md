Idea: While a coin's 20-day volatility is low compared with its own past, the larger its 28-day rise, the higher its return over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A trader would trust a 28-day rise as a reason to go long mainly in calm markets, and refuse to size up on it when volatility is high.
Why:
- F4: Inside the rise side, the 28-day rise is graded only when volatility is low. In the lowest 20-day-volatility tercile the slope has t 2.30 (215 cases); in the middle and high terciles it is -0.31 and -0.11 (212 and 217 cases). The mean 10-day strength is still 0.002 in the low tercile against 0.063 in the other two.
- F2: The fall side carries nothing, which supports refusing shorts. On the 615 grid cases with a 28-day fall, the slope of the 10-day unusual move on the size of the fall has t 0.21, and their mean strength is -0.032 (t -0.88).
- F1: The confirmed 28-day momentum rests mostly on one coin. Coin by coin, the t of the 10-day unusual return on the 28-day return is 2.86 for ZEC and between -0.95 and 1.26 for the other five (about 210 grid cases each). Pooled over both sides, the 28-day return reaches only t 1.66 on 1259 cases.
