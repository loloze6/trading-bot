Idea: When a coin's four-hour bar closes above the high of its preceding two days while its 30-day return is positive, the coin rises more than usual over the next 6 to 24 days, and the stronger its 30-day rise relative to its volatility, the larger the further rise.
Consequence: A trader should take, and hold for days, upward breakouts in a coin that is already rising over the month, rather than fade them, the more so the stronger the month's rise.
Why:
- F5: An upward breakout in a coin whose 30-day return is positive is followed by a larger rise than usual, at every horizon from 6 to 24 days:
- F6: On the 12-day grid, the coin's next 12 days rise with its volatility-scaled 30-day return (Spearman 0.058, p 0.009, about 2070 cases).
- F4: Shorting fights the coins' normal rise. Per case the unusual move is +2.55 %, but the normal drift in the short direction is -2.21 %, so a short earns only +0.34 % before costs. Trades held 13 to 20 days lost 10.5 % net on average (452 trades).
