A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a rising market, the more a coin's trading volume over the past 30 days has shrunk against the 30 days before, the larger its rise over the next 5 days.
What it would mean for a trader: An uptrend that keeps going on drying volume has no sellers left to stop it, so a trader would hold or add to longs in it rather than read the thin volume as a warning.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a rising market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 060-01_volume-shrink-fall-continues_1d (idea 060-01_volume-shrink-fall-continues, brief 060_volume-dry-up-extends-downtrends).
What it rests on:
- F2: In rising markets (60-day return above +10 %), volume shrinkage is followed by the opposite of the tested effect: the coin rises more.
- F3: The rising-market effect shows in both agitation states, at each end of the volume ratio.
- F4: Read across both trends, volume shrinkage goes with continuation of the coin's 60-day direction. The trend-signed excess 5-day return on the volume ratio has a slope of −0.029 (t −1.70, 719 cases). Over all bars, with no trend split, the slope is −0.0005 (t −0.03, 846 cases): the effect only appears once it is signed by the trend.
