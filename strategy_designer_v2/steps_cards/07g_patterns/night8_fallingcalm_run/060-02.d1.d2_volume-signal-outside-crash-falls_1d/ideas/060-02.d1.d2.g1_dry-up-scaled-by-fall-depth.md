Idea: In a falling market, the more a coin's trading volume over the past 30 days has shrunk against the 30 days before, the further it falls over the next 5 days, and the deeper its 20-day fall short of a crash, the larger that further fall.
Consequence: A trader leans short on volume dry-ups in falling coins and weights the bet by how far the last 20 days have already fallen, instead of treating every moderate decline alike.
Why:
- F1: Inside the tested situation (falling market, 20-day return between -20 % and 0 %), the volume signal works only in the deeper half of the fall: the slope of the 5-day standardised excess return on the 30-day log volume ratio has t 3.61 (84 cases) when the 20-day return is -20 % to -10 %, against t -0.67 (68 cases) when it is -10 % to 0 %; the volume ratio times the depth of the 20-day fall has t 3.53 (152 cases).
- F2: On the book's active grid bars, the signal's signed 5-day excess return rises steadily with the depth of the 20-day fall: +0.6 % (t 0.54, 70 cases) at 0 to -5 %, +2.8 % (t 2.16, 47) at -5 to -10 %, +3.3 % (t 2.99, 53) at -10 to -15 %, +5.7 % (t 3.90, 66) at -15 to -21 %. Taken together, a 20-day fall of -10 % or deeper gives +4.6 % per case (t 3.40 with each day's cases together, 119 cases on 57 days), against +1.5 % (t 1.21, 117 cases on 64 days) for a shallower fall. It is positive in every year of 2018 to 2021 and on all six coins, from +2.5 % on BTC to +8.5 % on LTC.
- F3: The book earns mainly on its short side, when volume shrinks. Active short cases have a 5-day excess return of -2.4 % (t -1.91, 192 cases on 82 days); active long cases have +2.3 % (t 1.32, 87 cases on 50 days). The lowest forecast quintile alone (forecast -20 to -14.5) gives -5.3 % (t -3.44, 56 cases).
- F5: When the 20-day fall is a crash (below -20 %), volume does not grade the next 5 days. By terciles of the volume ratio the excess return is -1.7 %, +2.0 % and +1.5 % (each |t| ≤ 1.19, about 48 cases each), and the bars in that bucket of the falling market average +0.6 % (t 0.54, 146 cases). The dry-up continuation stops at a crash.
