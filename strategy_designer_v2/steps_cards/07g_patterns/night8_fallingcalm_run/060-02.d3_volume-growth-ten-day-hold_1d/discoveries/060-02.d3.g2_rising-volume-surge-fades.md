A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a rising market, the more the coin's trading volume over the past 30 days has grown against the 30 days before, the smaller its rise, or the larger its fall, over the next 5 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In a rising coin, a surge of volume over the past month is a reason to cut or short it, and a dry-up a reason to stay long, which fills the thin short side of the rising cells.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a rising market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 060-02.d3_volume-growth-ten-day-hold_1d (idea 060-02.d3_volume-growth-ten-day-hold, brief 060_volume-dry-up-extends-downtrends).
What it rests on:
- F4: Outside the falling profile the volume ratio's sign flips. In a rising market (60-day return above +10 %) the rho is −0.168 over 5 days (318 cells, p 0.0026) and −0.154 over 10 days (158 cells, p 0.054). The lowest volume-ratio quartile is followed by a +7.1 % excess 10-day return (t 1.71), the highest by +0.4 %.
- F5: In non-falling agitated cells the flip is graded. The rho is −0.232 over 10 days (84 cells, p 0.033), and the highest volume-ratio quartile is followed by a −7.4 % excess 10-day return (33 cells, t −2.19).
