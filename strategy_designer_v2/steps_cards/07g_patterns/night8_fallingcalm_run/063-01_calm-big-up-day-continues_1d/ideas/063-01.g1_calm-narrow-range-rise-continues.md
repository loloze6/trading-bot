Idea: In a calm market, the larger a coin's one-day rise against its usual daily swing (the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days), and the narrower that day's high-low range against its average range over the past 30 days, the larger its rise over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader in a calm market should back a large up day most when the coin got there with little intraday swinging, and less when the day was wide and choppy.
Why:
- F1: The continuation is real only on up days in calm markets. On calm 5-day grid cases, a regression of the excess 5-day return on the day's swing-scaled return, split by side, gives t 4.05 for the rise part and t 1.26 for the fall part (773 cases). In agitated markets the same terms give t 0.44 and -0.40 (591 cases).
- F2: The gain sits in the largest calm up days. Calm days of +1 to +2 usual swings are followed by +2.41 % excess over 5 days (t 1.98, 76 cases), and days above +2 swings by +6.83 % (t 2.08, 28 cases). Days of -0.3 to +1 swings earn about +0.3 % (t under 0.6). This explains why the mean unusual move over all 1,765 band cases is +0.01 % (t 0.02), below the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F3: Among calm up days, a wider day predicts less continuation. Given the scaled rise (t 3.83), the day's high-low range against its own 30-day average range has a negative slope, t -2.76 (364 calm up days). In agitated up days the range term is t -0.45 (246 cases).
