A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling market, the larger a coin's one-day rise against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the larger its fall over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader should not chase a sharp up day in a coin that has been falling for two months, and may sell it for the next day instead.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO, in a falling market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 059-01.b1.b1.d2_choppy-calm-rise-up-month_1d (idea 059-01.b1.b1.d2_choppy-calm-rise-up-month, brief 059_choppy-calm-weekly-rise-continues).
What it rests on:
- F1: In a falling market (the coin's 60-day return below -10 %), a day in the top fifth of one-day moves scaled by the coin's 30-day daily swing is followed by a next-day return 0.85 % below the coin's normal daily drift (t -3.34, 346 cases). In a rising market the same days are followed by +0.42 % (t 1.63, 530 cases) and in a choppy market by -0.22 % (t -0.62, 162 cases), so the fade appears only in falling markets.
- F2: The fade in F1 lasts one day only. On a 5-day grid in falling markets, the top fifth of scaled one-day moves is followed by +1.30 % over 5 days (t 1.13, 67 cases), so the move is spent by the next close.
