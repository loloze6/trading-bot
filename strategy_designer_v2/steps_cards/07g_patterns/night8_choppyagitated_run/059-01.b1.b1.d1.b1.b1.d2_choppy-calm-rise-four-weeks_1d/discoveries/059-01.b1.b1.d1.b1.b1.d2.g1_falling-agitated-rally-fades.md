A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day rise in multiples of its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the larger its fall over the next 3 days.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader should treat a sharp up day in a falling, swinging coin as a rally to sell into rather than a turn, and refuse to buy it.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 059-01.b1.b1.d1.b1.b1.d2_choppy-calm-rise-four-weeks_1d (idea 059-01.b1.b1.d1.b1.b1.d2_choppy-calm-rise-four-weeks, brief 059_choppy-calm-weekly-rise-continues).
What it rests on:
- F1: In a falling, agitated market, a large up day is followed by a fall rather than a further rise, and the fall grows with the size of the day. A day in the top fifth of rises (the day's return over its 30-day daily standard deviation) has a next-day excess return of -1.18 % (t -2.06, day-clustered, 216 cases on 87 days) and a 3-day excess return of -4.04 % (t -2.20, 76 cases on 29 days). Within that profile's rise days, the 3-day excess goes from +1.16 % to -0.54 % to -4.12 % across terciles of the day's scaled size (top tercile t -2.19, 74 cases on 29 days). The next-day excess also falls step by step, -0.44 %, -0.71 %, -1.19 %. The profile map has no strategy in the falling agitated short cell. The 2-day grid is weaker (-0.34 %, t -0.37), and I looked at many angles, so this rests on t near 2.2.
