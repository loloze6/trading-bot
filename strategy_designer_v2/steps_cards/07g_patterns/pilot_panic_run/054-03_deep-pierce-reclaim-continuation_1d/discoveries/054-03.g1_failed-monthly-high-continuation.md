A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: When a coin's day trades above its highest price of the past 30 days but closes back below that high, the coin rises over the next 10 days, and the rise is larger the further the day's high went above the 30-day high.
What it would mean for a trader: A failed monthly-high break is not a bull trap to short but a reason to be long, or to refuse a short, for the next 10 days.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 054-03_deep-pierce-reclaim-continuation_1d (idea 054-03_deep-pierce-reclaim-continuation, brief 054_reclaimed-monthly-low-in-falling-agitated).
What it rests on:
- F1: A day whose high goes above the coin's highest high of the prior 30 days but closes back at or below that high is followed by an unusual 5-day rise of +3.12 % (t 3.01, 283 cases on 170 days), +2.39 % over 3 days (t 2.80) and +5.17 % over 10 days (t 3.27, 267 cases on 158 days); 10 days is about 27 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F2: The failed-high-pierce rise holds across the sample. It is the same in the first and second half of the windows (+3.10 %, t 2.08 and +3.15 %, t 2.17), positive on 5 of 6 coins (ETH +3.54 % t 3.02, XMR +4.07 % t 2.97, BTC +2.23 % t 2.11; LTC −0.74 %), and positive in every 60-day trend (rising +5.29 % t 2.99, choppy +3.34 % t 2.54, falling +5.58 % t 1.86).
- F3: The failed-high-pierce rise is graded only weakly by how far the high went above the 30-day high. By depth tercile it is +2.68 % (t 2.78), +3.91 % (t 2.47) and +2.79 % (t 1.56): positive throughout but not rising with depth. It is larger in calm than in agitated markets (+2.89 % t 2.07 vs +2.33 % t 0.92), with the largest cell in rising calm (+6.30 %, t 3.76, 73 cases).
