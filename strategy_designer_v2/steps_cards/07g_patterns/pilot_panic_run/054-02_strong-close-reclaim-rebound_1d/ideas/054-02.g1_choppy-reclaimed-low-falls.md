Idea: In a choppy market, when a coin's day trades below its lowest price of the past 30 days but closes back above that low, the coin falls over the next 5 days, and the fall is larger the higher the close sits above that low, measured against the day's range.
Consequence: Outside a falling market a reclaimed monthly low is a trap for buyers rather than a floor, so a trader should refuse the rebound long there and lean short, more so after a strong reclaim.
Why:
- F1: Outside the falling agitated profile, a day that pierces the prior 30-day low and closes back above it is followed by a 5-day return 4.12 % below the coin's normal drift (113 cases, 75 days, t −1.98). Inside the profile the same situation is followed by +3.89 % (30 cases, 15 days, t 1.29). The sign of the brief's effect flips with the market.
- F2: Outside the profile, the fall after a reclaim is graded the wrong way for the tested idea. Reclaims whose close sits higher above the prior low, against the day's range, are followed by −5.57 % excess over 5 days (63 cases, 44 days, t −2.32), against −1.95 % for the lower half (60 cases, t −0.83). The Spearman correlation of that reclaim measure with the excess return is −0.196 on about 113 cases.
- F3: Outside the profile, the post-reclaim fall shows on most coins and years. The excess is negative on 4 of 6 coins over all reclaims (ETH −6.45 % with t −2.02, XRP −5.07 % with t −1.55). By year it is negative in 3 of 4 years (2018 −2.79 %, 2020 −12.89 % with t −1.87, 2021 −2.82 %; 2019 +0.28 %).
- F4: Split by the 30-day trend and the swing, the fall is largest in a choppy, agitated market. There, reclaims are followed by −7.74 % excess over 5 days (18 cases, 17 days, t −2.31), against −0.48 % in a choppy, calm market (26 cases) and −0.43 % in a falling, agitated one (57 cases).
