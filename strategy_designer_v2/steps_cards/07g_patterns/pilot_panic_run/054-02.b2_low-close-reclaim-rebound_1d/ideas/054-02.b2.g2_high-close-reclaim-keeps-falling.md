Idea: When a coin's day trades below its lowest price of the past 30 days and closes back above it, the coin falls over the next 5 days, and the fall is larger the nearer the close sits to the day's high.
Consequence: A strong-looking reclaim that closes near the day's high is a warning of further weakness, not a sign of a bottom.
Why:
- F1: Outside the falling-agitated profile, a day that pierces the prior 30-day low and closes back above it is followed by a fall, not a rebound. The unusual 5-day move is -4.1 % (t -2.00 with same-day cases pooled; 113 cases on 75 days; hit rate 40 %), and the unusual 10-day move is -5.8 % (t -2.92; 93 cases on 62 days). All six coins are negative at 5 days, from -0.5 % for ZEC to -8.7 % for ETH.
- F4: Outside the profile, the post-reclaim fall is graded by where the close sits in the day's range. The Spearman correlation between distance-from-high and the unusual 5-day move is +0.20 (p 0.03, 113 cases). By tercile of close location, the closes nearest the high give -6.0 %, the middle tercile -6.9 %, and the closes nearest the low +0.5 % (38, 37 and 38 cases).
