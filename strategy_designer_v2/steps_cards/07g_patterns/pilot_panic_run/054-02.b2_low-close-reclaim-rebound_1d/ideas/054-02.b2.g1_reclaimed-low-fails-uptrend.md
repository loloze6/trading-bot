Idea: When a coin's day trades below its lowest price of the past 30 days but closes back above it, the coin falls over the next 10 days, and the fall is larger the higher the coin's 60-day return.
Consequence: A trader should refuse to buy a reclaimed monthly-low pierce unless the coin has already fallen hard over the past two months, and should lean short on it in a coin that has held up.
Why:
- F1: Outside the falling-agitated profile, a day that pierces the prior 30-day low and closes back above it is followed by a fall, not a rebound. The unusual 5-day move is -4.1 % (t -2.00 with same-day cases pooled; 113 cases on 75 days; hit rate 40 %), and the unusual 10-day move is -5.8 % (t -2.92; 93 cases on 62 days). All six coins are negative at 5 days, from -0.5 % for ZEC to -8.7 % for ETH.
- F2: After a reclaim outside the profile, the fall is graded by the coin's 60-day return. In the deepest tercile (mean 60-day return -42 %) the unusual 5-day move is +1.6 %; in the middle tercile (-18 %) it is -6.4 %; in the highest tercile (+15 %) it is -12.2 % (27 cases each). Inside the falling-agitated profile, reclaims give +3.9 % (t 1.74, 30 cases), which fits the same gradient.
- F3: When the coin's 60-day return is above -10 % (a choppy or rising market), a reclaim is followed by an unusual move of -11.6 % over 5 days (t -2.28; 31 cases on 21 days) and -12.9 % over 10 days (t -3.82; 26 cases on 17 days). The rising subset gives -14.5 % (13 cases) and the choppy subset -9.7 % (19 cases) at 5 days. These samples are small and I looked at many angles, so this is weaker evidence than F1.
