A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, calm market, the larger a coin's one-day rise is against its usual daily swing over the last 20 days, the larger its fall over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: In a coin that has fallen for two months without unusual swings, a trader has a reason to sell into a sharp one-day rally for one day, and not to buy the dips.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO, in a falling calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 058-02.b1.g1.b1.b2_falling-market-next-day-reversal_1d (idea 058-02.b1.g1.b1.b2_falling-market-next-day-reversal, brief 058_choppy-calm-range-edge-reversion).
What it rests on:
- F2: In falling calm markets the next day is lopsided: down days are not followed by rises, while large up days are followed by falls. After falls the next-day excess return is -0.14 % (t -0.16, 49 days) for -2 to -1 swings and -0.40 % (t -0.19, 14 days) for falls beyond -2 swings. After rises it is -1.42 % (t -1.71, 95 cases, 53 days) for 1 to 2 swings and -2.18 % (t -1.91, 21 cases, 16 days) beyond 2 swings, against +0.39 % after flat days (t 0.95, 131 days).
- F3: Across the whole falling profile, the short leg after up days is mostly the falling coin's own drift, and the down-day rebound carries the edge. Flat days (|z| < 0.3) are followed by -0.45 % (t -3.04, 1185 cases), about the same as up days of 0.3 to 1, 1 to 2 and over 2 swings (-0.48 %, -0.47 %, -0.69 %). Falls of -2 to -1 swings are followed by +0.81 % (t 2.65, 430 cases).
