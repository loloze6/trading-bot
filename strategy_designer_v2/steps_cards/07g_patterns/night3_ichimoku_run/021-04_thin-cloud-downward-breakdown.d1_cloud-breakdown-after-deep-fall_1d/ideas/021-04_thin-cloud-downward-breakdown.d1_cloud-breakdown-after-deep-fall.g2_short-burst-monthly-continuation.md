Idea: The larger a coin's 5-day return at a daily close, the larger its move in the same direction over the next 26 days.
Consequence: A trader would take a short burst as the start of a month-long move and hold it rather than fade it, mainly on the long side.
Why:
- F4: The 5-day return predicts the 26-day unusual move in the same direction. The correlation is 0.137 (t 2.86, 429 cases), positive in 6 of 6 coins, and rose from 0.002 at 5 days to 0.061 at 10 days. The 26-day return does the same: correlation 0.117 (t 2.44, 5 of 6 coins), with 26-day unusual moves by its quartile rising steadily from -2.3 % to +0.2 %, +2.2 % and +5.1 %. As a sign bet it is mostly a long-side effect (+3.1 % after rises, +0.7 % after falls), with strength 0.054.
- F5: Below the cloud the follow-through is weak: -4.5 % over 26 days (t -1.17, 59 cases), and -9.4 % inside the cloud (t -2.05, 25 cases). The tested downward break added nothing: unusual move +0.70 %, t 0.30, slope on depth -0.003. Its trades lost on all 6 coins (mean -2.7 % to -9.3 % per trade), so the book's Sharpe was -0.30 against coins that rose 0.07 % to 0.16 % a day.
