A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a rising, calm market, a coin's one-day fall, measured against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, is followed by a rise over the next day, and the larger the fall, the larger the rise.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader would buy sharp one-day dips of coins in calm uptrends for one day, sized by the dip's size against usual swing.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO, in a rising calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 058-01.g2.b1.d2.b1.d1_middling-rises-fade-next-day_1d (idea 058-01.g2.b1.d2.b1.d1_middling-rises-fade-next-day, brief 058_falling-agitated-multiday-rallies-fail).
What it rests on:
- F5: Outside the profile, in rising markets with a coin's swing at or below its median (approximated by its rank over the whole sample, not over the past year), one-day falls beyond one 30-day deviation were followed by a next-day unusual rise of +1.59 % (t 3.01). It is the only cell among about 100 inspected with a t above 3. The same falls in rising agitated markets gave +1.2 % (t 1.43). Weekday (all |t| < 1.7), the up day's close position and the day's range showed nothing else.
