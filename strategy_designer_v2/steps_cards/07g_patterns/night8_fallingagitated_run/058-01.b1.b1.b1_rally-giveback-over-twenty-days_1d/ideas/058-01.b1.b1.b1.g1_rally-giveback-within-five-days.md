Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's 3-day rise is against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger the fall in its price over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader would short a scaled rally in a falling, agitated market for about a week, sized by how many usual swings the rally spans, and close it before the profile's multi-week rebound sets in.
Why:
- F1: Inside the falling, agitated profile, a large 3-day rise is given back over the following few days but not over the following weeks. After 3-day rises of more than 2 of the coin's 30-day daily swings, the drift-adjusted return was -3.3 % over 3 days (t -1.58, 29 cases, 17 days) and -4.8 % over 5 days (t -2.22, 16 cases, 11 days), a strength of -0.31 of a usual 5-day move.
- F2: Inside the profile, the 3-day give-back grows with the size of the scaled rise. The drift-adjusted 3-day return was +0.2 % after rises of 0 to 1 swings (104 cases), -2.0 % after 1 to 2 swings (47 cases, t -1.09) and -3.3 % after more than 2 swings (29 cases), against +2.4 % after falls of more than 1 swing (104 cases).
- F3: Over 20 days the profile's general rebound swamps the give-back, which is why the tested book lost. Every 20-day grid cell in the profile averaged +3.9 % above the coin's normal drift (64 cases, t 1.08). Cells after a 3-day fall of 0 to 1 swings gained +17.0 % (t 2.91, 20 cases), and the step-5 size shows the cases' unusual move at +3.74 % against the short (t 2.01).
- F4: The short book's losses come from that 20-day hold, not from how large the forecast was. All 208 trades were shorts averaging -6.2 % net, with no improvement with forecast size (terciles -5.6 %, -6.8 %, -6.1 %). One window, w16, held 37 trades averaging -42 % (ZEC -117 % at worst) and summed -0.30 of daily book return, against +0.04 to +0.09 in w13 to w15.
