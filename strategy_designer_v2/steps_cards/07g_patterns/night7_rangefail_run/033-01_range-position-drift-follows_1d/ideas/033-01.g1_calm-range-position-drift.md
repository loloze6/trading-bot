Idea: In a calm market, the higher a coin's daily close sits within its own 20-day high-to-low range, the more the coin rises over the next 5 days, and the lower it sits, the more it falls, graded through the whole range.
Consequence: A trader follows a close's place in its recent range only while the coin swings less than usual for it, and stands aside from that drift when it is agitated.
Why:
- F1: The tested slope of the drift-removed 5-day return on the 20-day range position lives in calm markets. On the non-overlapping 5-day grid of folds B and C, it is 0.086 (t 8.15, 675 cases) when the coin's 30-day swing is at or below its median, against 0.018 (t 1.23, 675 cases) when it is above.
- F2: The calm-market slope holds everywhere it was cut. It is positive in every year (2018 0.046 t 1.44, 2019 0.059 t 3.74, 2020 0.080 t 3.69, 2021 0.073 t 2.48) and in every coin, from XMR at 0.059 (t 2.68) to XRP at 0.133 (t 3.94). The agitated slope is indistinguishable from 0 in every coin (BTC 0.000, t 0.01) and in every year (t between -0.42 and 1.02).
- F3: The effect fades steadily as the swing rises. By quartile of the 30-day swing, the slope falls from 0.096 (t 6.30) to 0.075 (t 5.10), 0.032 (t 1.80) and 0.002 (t 0.07), about 338 cases each.
- F4: The book earns its money in calm markets too. Its gain per bar is 0.299 % in calm conditions (3,444 bars) against 0.053 % in agitated ones (3,445 bars), which suggests its Calmar failure (0.43) comes mostly from positions held in agitated stretches.
