A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: A coin that falls sharply in one day, measured in its own daily swings over the last 20 days, rises over the next day, more the more swings the day's fall spans.
What it would mean for a trader: A sharp one-day drop in swing units is a reason to buy for a one-day rebound, sized by how many swings the drop covered.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 034-03.b1_choppy-far-below-high-falls_1d (idea 034-03.b1_choppy-far-below-high-falls, brief 034_rising-market-nearness-to-high-drift).
What it rests on:
- F1: A sharp one-day fall, counted in the coin's own 20-day daily swings, is followed by a graded rebound the next day. After a fall of 2 or more swings the next day's drift-adjusted return is +1.48 % (t 2.04 by day, 305 cases on 95 days). After a fall of 1 to 2 swings it is +1.06 % (t 1.68, 924 cases), against −0.27 % after a flat day (t −1.47). On down days the slope of the next day's return on the fall's size is negative in all three trend groups: chop t −2.26, fall t −1.24, rise t −0.75. It is negative in 10 of the 12 windows w7–w18.
