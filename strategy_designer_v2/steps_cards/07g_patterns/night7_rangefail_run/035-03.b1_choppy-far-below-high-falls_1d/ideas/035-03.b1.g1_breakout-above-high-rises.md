Idea: A daily close above the coin's highest close of the prior 20 days is followed by a rise over the next 5 days, larger the more of the coin's 20-day daily swings it stands above that high.
Consequence: A trader would buy closes that break above the 20-day high, sizing by how far above it they close in daily swings, in every market rather than only rising ones.
Why:
- F1: Across all coins and windows w7–w18, closes above the prior 20-day closing high are followed by the largest unusual 5-day rises, and the rise grows with the height above that high. Closes 0 to 1 daily swings above it gain +3.14 % (121 cases, 61 days, t 1.94, strength 0.27, 52 % up). Closes more than 1 swing above it gain +10.03 % (71 cases, 44 days, t 1.66, strength 0.48, 58 % up). Both bands are positive in each half: w7–12 +2.76 % / +14.51 %, w13–18 +3.50 % / +4.58 %.
- F2: The rise after a close at or above the 20-day high holds in every trend profile. Rising: +6.15 % (74 cases, 32 days, t 2.41, strength 0.49). Falling: +4.01 % (19 cases, 8 days, t 3.29). Choppy: +24.24 % (22 cases, 16 days, t 1.36). A close above its prior 20-day range gives +5.57 % (193 cases, t 2.44).
- F5: The book's short side cannot pay. Its total move per case is +0.08 %, 0.4 times the 0.22 % cost, because the coins' normal drift works −0.80 % against the short. The long side above the high has the drift with it and an unusual move of +3 % to +10 %, more than ten times the cost.
