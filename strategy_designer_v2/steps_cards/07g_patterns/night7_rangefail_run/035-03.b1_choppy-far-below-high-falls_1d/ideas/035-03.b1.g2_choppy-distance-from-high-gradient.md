Idea: In a choppy market, the higher a daily close sits against the coin's 20-day high, counted in its 20-day daily swings, the more the coin rises over the next 5 days. This runs from falls far below the high to rises above it.
Consequence: A trader in a flat-trend coin would lean long as the close nears and breaks its 20-day high and lean short as it sinks below it, sized by the distance in swings.
Why:
- F3: Over the whole range, the 5-day unusual move rises with distance from the high (the strength rises by 0.063 per swing, day-clustered t 2.37, 2016 cases). The relation is positive in both halves (t 2.05 and 1.44) and in 9 of 12 coins. It is steepest in choppy markets: slope 0.22 per swing, Spearman 0.28, t 2.70, 189 cases. In rising markets it is 0.12 (t 2.21) and in falling ones 0.04 (t 0.87).
- F4: In choppy markets, closes more than 1.5 swings below the 20-day high fall by an unusual 4.49 % over 5 days (116 cases, 55 days, t −2.31, strength −0.35, 34 % up). Beyond that point the depth does not grade the fall. Across all trends the bands give −1.88 % (beyond −4 swings), −0.45 % (−4 to −3) and −1.49 % (−3 to −2). This flat response is why the supporting check failed.
- F2: The rise after a close at or above the 20-day high holds in every trend profile. Rising: +6.15 % (74 cases, 32 days, t 2.41, strength 0.49). Falling: +4.01 % (19 cases, 8 days, t 3.29). Choppy: +24.24 % (22 cases, 16 days, t 1.36). A close above its prior 20-day range gives +5.57 % (193 cases, t 2.44).
