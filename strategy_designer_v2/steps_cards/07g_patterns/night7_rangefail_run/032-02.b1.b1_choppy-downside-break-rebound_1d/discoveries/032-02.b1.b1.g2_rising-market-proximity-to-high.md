A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a rising market, the closer a coin's daily close sits to the highest high of its prior 20 days, or the further above it, measured against its daily swing over the last 30 days, the larger its rise over the next 5 days.
What it would mean for a trader: In a rising market a trader should lean long in proportion to how near the coin trades to its 20-day high and step aside from coins that have slipped far below it.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO, in a rising market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 032-02.b1.b1_choppy-downside-break-rebound_1d (idea 032-02.b1.b1_choppy-downside-break-rebound, brief 032_choppy-market-breakouts-fail).
What it rests on:
- F3: In a rising market, the 5-day excess return on the grid rises steadily with the close's distance to its 20-day high. It is -2.53 % at 3 to 6 swings below (t -1.61, 131 cases), -0.09 % at 1.5 to 3 below, +2.97 % at 0.5 to 1.5 below, +3.17 % just below, and +2.73 %, +5.77 % and +8.42 % at 0 to 0.5, 0.5 to 1 and over 1 swing above.
- F1: In a rising market (60-day return above +10 %), a daily close above the highest high of the prior 20 days is followed by a 5-day excess return of +4.82 % (t 3.08, 257 cases on 114 days, all bars, overlapping). The same check on the non-overlapping 5-day grid gives +5.62 % (t 1.47, 43 cases on 25 days). Rising bars that do not break average -0.02 % (t -0.02, 441 grid cases).
