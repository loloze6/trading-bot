Idea: In a rising market, when a coin's daily close is above the highest high of its prior 20 days, its price keeps rising over the next 5 days, and the further the close sits above that high (measured against its daily swing over the last 30 days), the larger the rise.
Consequence: An upside break of the 20-day range in a rising market is a continuation to buy for about a week, and larger breaks deserve larger positions, not fades.
Why:
- F1: In a rising market (60-day return above +10 %), a daily close above the highest high of the prior 20 days is followed by a 5-day excess return of +4.82 % (t 3.08, 257 cases on 114 days, all bars, overlapping). The same check on the non-overlapping 5-day grid gives +5.62 % (t 1.47, 43 cases on 25 days). Rising bars that do not break average -0.02 % (t -0.02, 441 grid cases).
- F2: That 5-day continuation grows with the size of the break, measured as the close's distance above the 20-day high in units of the 30-day daily swing. On the non-overlapping grid the terciles give +1.83 %, +5.16 % and +10.98 % (15, 16 and 12 cases), and the slope is +1.59 % per swing unit. On all bars the large tercile gives +10.90 % (t 2.88, 90 cases on 56 days).
- F4: The rising-market upside-break continuation (all bars, 5 days) holds across years and agitation. By year it is +4.88 % in 2019 (t 1.67), +4.89 % in 2020 (t 2.25) and +5.08 % in 2021 (t 1.83); 2018 has only 4 cases. By agitation it is +5.28 % agitated (t 2.68) and +6.48 % calm (t 1.77). It is positive on 9 of 13 coins (XRP +17.8 %, t 3.31; ADA +4.8 %, t 2.22).
