Idea: In an agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall against its usual daily move, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: A long-only trader can buy large down days in any market that is swinging more than usual, not only in declines, and should refuse the same trade when the market is calm.
Why:
- F2: The graded rebound is not limited to falling markets; it follows agitation. On agitated days outside the falling agitated regime, the slope of next-day excess return on fall size is 93 bp (t 2.36, 1214 falls on 557 days), and choppy agitated alone gives 310 bp (t 2.01, 73 falls). On calm days outside the regime it is 17 bp (t 0.49, 1643 falls on 589 days).
- F1: Inside the falling agitated regime, the next-day excess return rises with the size of a one-day fall: the slope is 202 bp per unit of standardised fall (t 2.88 clustered by day, 560 falls on 239 days). In my approximate falling calm group the same slope is -17 bp (t -0.24, 391 falls on 175 days), and in choppy calm it is -85 bp (t -1.79), so the brief's contrast with calm declines holds.
- F3: In the falling agitated regime, every coin's mean next-day excess return after a down day is positive. It ranges from +0.67 % for BTC (t 1.50, 133 days) to +1.86 % for LTC (t 2.82, 110 days), and five of the six coins have t above 2.
- F4: The rebound sits almost entirely in the next day. After falling agitated down days the mean excess return is +1.26 % at 1 day (t 1.68, 675 falls on 289 days) and +0.86 % at 2 days (t 0.93). The 2-day slope on fall size, 120 bp (t 2.21), is below the 1-day slope of 202 bp.
