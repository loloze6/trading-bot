Idea: In an agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall against its 30-day swing and the nearer the close sits to the day's low, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: A trader should size the agitated low-close rebound by how large the day's fall was for the coin, not only by where it closed, and hold it one day.
Why:
- F4: In agitated bars, a large down day against the coin's 30-day swing (lowest tercile) that also closes in the bottom third of its range is followed by +0.95 % next day (t 2.27, 775 cases on 321 days). The same cell in calm bars gives +0.31 % (t 1.11).
- F5: The agitated low-close rebound is positive in every year (2018 to 2021: +0.39 %, +0.72 %, +0.97 %, +0.77 %) and on every coin (+0.41 % ZEC to +1.23 % XMR, t 2.45). It is spent after one day: on the 2-day grid the lowest-close quintile gives -0.00 % (t -0.21).
- F3: In agitated bars, the low-close rebound grows with the day's range against its 60-day usual range. A close in the lower half of the range gives -0.17 % (t -0.89) in the narrowest quartile, +0.44 %, +0.76 % (t 2.34) and +1.46 % (t 1.19) in the widest. In a joint day-clustered regression, the range width keeps t 1.78 while the close's place drops to t -1.09.
