Idea: On a day when a coin rises by an ordinary amount for its recent swing, the nearer the close sits to the day's high, the weaker the coin is over the next day.
Consequence: A trader should lean against ordinary up days that close at their high, by more the closer the close is to the high, and expect the effect to be spent after one day.
Why:
- F1: In agitated bars (folds B, C), a day of middling size against the coin's 30-day swing that closes in the top third of its range is followed by a next-day excess return of -1.05 % (t -2.34, 247 cases on 161 days). The same cell in calm bars gives -0.49 % (t -2.52, 330 cases on 218 days). In both regimes the high-close weakness shows on ordinary up days, not on the largest ones: the top z tercile with a high close gives -0.51 % (t -1.08) agitated and -0.04 % (t -0.61) calm.
- F2: Sorted by the day's own return, agitated bars in the fourth quintile (a moderate rise) are followed by -0.75 % next day (t -3.11, 653 cases on 393 days). The top quintile gives -0.36 % (t -0.07) and the bottom quintile +0.91 % (t 1.88). The weakness sits after ordinary rises, not after the largest ones.
