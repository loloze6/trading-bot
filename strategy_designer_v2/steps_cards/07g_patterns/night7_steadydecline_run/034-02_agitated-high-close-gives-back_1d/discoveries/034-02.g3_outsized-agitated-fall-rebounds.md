A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall relative to its usual daily swing over the past 20 days, the larger its rise over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: In a falling, agitated market, an outsized down day is a reason to buy for one day rather than to chase the fall.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 034-02_agitated-high-close-gives-back_1d (idea 034-02_agitated-high-close-gives-back, brief 034_agitated-close-location-reversal).
What it rests on:
- F5: The size of the day's own fall, apart from where the day closed, predicts a next-day rebound in falling agitated bars: slope 0.216 (t 3.48, 738 cases). It does not in rising agitated bars (−0.043, t −0.79) or in calm bars (0.004, t 0.11).
- F4: Combining a low close with the size of the day's fall (low-close share × the fall in units of the 20-day swing) predicts the next day best. Its slope is 0.229 (t 3.26) in agitated bars and 0.040 (t 0.42) in calm bars. On agitated down days, the largest quarter of falls was followed by +1.53 % (t 2.38, 412 cases); the three smaller quarters were followed by +0.19 % to +0.41 % (t under 0.9).
