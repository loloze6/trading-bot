A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall, the more it rises over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: In a decline that is swinging more than usual, a trader would buy a sharp down day for a one-day rebound rather than short it.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 032-02_calm-fall-below-high-continues_1d (idea 032-02_calm-fall-below-high-continues, brief 032_calm-decline-keeps-falling).
What it rests on:
- F3: Outside the calm case, the continuation turns into reversal. In falling non-calm grid cases, past returns slope negatively on the next 3 days (r7 -0.088, t -0.91; r3 -0.110, t -0.88; 242 cases). The next day's unusual return slopes on today's return at -0.175 (t -2.79, 1,902 bars) over all falling bars, against -0.035 (t -0.76) in falling calm alone. Over all bars the slope is -0.087 (t -2.87, 8,262 bars), and the lowest decile of one-day returns is followed by +0.95 % the next day (t 2.16, 276 days).
