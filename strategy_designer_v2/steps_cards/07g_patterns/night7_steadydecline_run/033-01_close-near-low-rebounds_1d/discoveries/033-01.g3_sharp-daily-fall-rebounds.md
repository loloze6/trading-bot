A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: The larger a coin's one-day fall, measured against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the larger its rise over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader would buy the close of a coin's sharpest down days for a one-day rebound, and refuse to short after them.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 033-01_close-near-low-rebounds_1d (idea 033-01_close-near-low-rebounds, brief 033_close-location-next-day-reversal).
What it rests on:
- F5: A large one-day fall is followed by a rise the next day, and it is the strongest single pattern seen. The quintile of largest down days has a next-day excess of +0.71 % (t 4.56, 1,722 cases). The other quintiles run from -0.05 % to -0.31 % (t -0.36 to -2.59).
