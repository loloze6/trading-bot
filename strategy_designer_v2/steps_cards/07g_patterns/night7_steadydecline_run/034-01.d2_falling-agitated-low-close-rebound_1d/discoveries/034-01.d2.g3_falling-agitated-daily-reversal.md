A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day return measured against its 30-day swing, the weaker its return over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader in a falling, agitated coin should expect a sharp up-day to give back and a sharp down-day to bounce the next day, and should not extend either move.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 034-01.d2_falling-agitated-low-close-rebound_1d (idea 034-01.d2_falling-agitated-low-close-rebound, brief 034_agitated-close-location-reversal).
What it rests on:
- F6: The day's own return, scaled by the 30-day swing, reverses in the same cells.
- F1: In a falling, agitated market (60-day return below -10 %, 30-day swing above its recent median), the day's close location (0 at the high, 1 at the low) tracks the next day's excess return: correlation 0.216, t 2.79 over 266 days (788 cases). In falling calm markets it does not (correlation -0.017, t -0.42, 368 days), nor in rising agitated markets (correlation -0.009, t -0.26, 245 days).
- F4: The effect is spent after the next day. In falling agitated markets the two-day correlation is 0.016, t 0.25 (776 cases), against t 2.79 at one day.
