A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In an agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall, the larger its rise over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader buys a coin after a sharp down day in proportion to the fall's size when its swings are above its usual level, a reversal of the day's return rather than of the close's place in the range.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 033-01.d2_low-close-rebound-long-side_1d (idea 033-01.d2_low-close-rebound-long-side, brief 033_close-location-next-day-reversal).
What it rests on:
- F6: The day's own return carries much of the effect. The lowest quintile of daily returns is followed by +0.71 % (t 4.56, 1722 bars), and the slope of the next day on the day's return is −0.087 (t −2.94, 1437 days). Low-half closes on down days are followed by +0.42 %, against −0.03 % on the 633 low-half up days.
- F2: The rebound after a low close depends on how agitated the coin is. Closes in the bottom fifth of the range are followed by an excess next-day return of −0.37 % (t −1.44, 392 bars) in the calmest volatility tercile, +0.58 % (t 2.06) in the middle tercile and +1.56 % (t 4.92, 396 bars) in the most agitated. The slope on nearness to the low is 0.0259 (t 3.55, 713 days) in the agitated tercile and −0.0043 (t −0.77) in the calm one.
