Idea: In an agitated market, after a down day, the nearer the close sits to the day's very low, the larger the rise over the next day, and the more so the larger that day's fall against the coin's 30-day average daily range.
Consequence: A trader buying forced-selling days in agitated markets would favour the days that close right at their low after a large fall, and pass on large falls that closed off their low.
Why:
- F1: In agitated bars (folds B, C), down days in the largest quarter of falls measured against the prior 30-day mean daily range are followed by a next-day excess return that rises with how near the close sits to the low: closes mid/high +-1.40 % (28 cases, t -0.78), near the low +0.70 % (154 cases, t 0.85), at the low +2.10 % (180 cases, 92 days, t 2.64); the same at-low cell in calm bars gives only +0.53 % (248 cases, t 0.99).
- F3: The signal's gain sits in its upper half: forecast quartiles give +0.21 % (t 0.53), -0.04 % (t -0.09), +1.00 % (t 2.40) and +1.08 % (t 2.03) next-day excess, so weak signals earn nothing.
- F2: The book's signal pays on the next day only: on forecast > 0 bars the next-day excess return is +0.56 % (1804 cases, 660 days, t 1.93), the day after is -0.11 % (t -0.38) and three days is +0.35 % (t 0.76), so the rebound does not continue after the first day.
