Idea: When a coin's past 30 days have gone back and forth with little net progress compared with the sum of its daily moves, its 7-day move counted in 30-day daily swings is followed by a move the same way over the next 7 days. The larger that scaled 7-day move, the larger the move over the following week.
Consequence: A trader taking the choppy-market weekly continuation should expect its payoff over a week, where the move per case is many times a trade's cost, rather than over the next day, where it is smaller than that cost.
Why:
- F1: Measured over 7 days instead of 1, the tested signal is much larger: on its own 7-day grid each active case shows an unusual move of +2.41 %, t 2.34 (512 cases, 172 days), strength 0.15, total move +2.38 %. Over 3 days it is +0.93 %, t 2.48 (1257 cases, 417 days), strength 0.10. Over 14 days it is +1.74 %, t 0.82 (246 cases). Against these, the 1-day size behind the verdict is +0.14 %, t 1.26, below the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F2: On the non-overlapping 7-day grid of all bars, the slope of the next-7-day excess return on the 7-day move scaled by 30-day swings depends on the 30-day efficiency ratio. It is 0.0275 (t 2.73, 290 cases) in the choppiest third, 0.0278 (t 3.23, 283) in the middle third and 0.0018 (t 0.25, 286) in the trending third. So the weekly move continues outside trends; it does not reverse as the brief predicted.
- F5: Among the book's active bars, the signed next-7-day excess return rises with the forecast's size: 0.38 %, 1.94 %, 1.67 % and 2.97 % by |forecast| quartile (about 960 bars each). The signed next-day excess stays flat, at 0.29 %, 0.57 %, 0.53 % and 0.16 %.
