Idea: A coin's 7-day move counted in 30-day daily swings is followed by a move the same way over the next 7 days, and more strongly the lower its 30-day volatility is compared with its 90-day volatility. The larger the scaled 7-day move relative to that volatility ratio, the larger the following week's move.
Consequence: A trader should back a strong scaled weekly move when the coin's recent swings have calmed against their longer level, and refuse it when the recent swings have swollen.
Why:
- F3: The same weekly continuation weakens as the coin's 30-day volatility rises relative to its 90-day volatility. By quintile of that ratio, from calmest to most agitated, the slopes are 0.039 (t 2.30), 0.031 (t 2.10), 0.037 (t 1.29), 0.010 (t 0.60) and −0.014 (t −0.83), about 110 to 123 cases each. Dividing the scaled 7-day move by that ratio raises the t from 1.41 to 2.32 (571 cases, day-clustered).
- F2: On the non-overlapping 7-day grid of all bars, the slope of the next-7-day excess return on the 7-day move scaled by 30-day swings depends on the 30-day efficiency ratio. It is 0.0275 (t 2.73, 290 cases) in the choppiest third, 0.0278 (t 3.23, 283) in the middle third and 0.0018 (t 0.25, 286) in the trending third. So the weekly move continues outside trends; it does not reverse as the brief predicted.
- F4: In the calmer two-thirds of that volatility ratio, the continuation is not uniform across the sample. All six coins have a positive slope (0.004 to 0.049), but none has a t above 1.58. The years 2018 and 2020 are positive (t 0.93, 1.30), while 2019 and 2021 are near 0. 8 of 12 windows are positive, and the three first windows (w7 to w9) are negative.
