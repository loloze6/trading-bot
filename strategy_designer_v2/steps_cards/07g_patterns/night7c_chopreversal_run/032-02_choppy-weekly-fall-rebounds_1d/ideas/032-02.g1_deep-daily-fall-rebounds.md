Idea: When a coin falls sharply in one day, measured against its typical daily swing over the past 30 days, it tends to rise over the next day. The deeper that day's fall in units of its usual daily swing, the larger the next day's rise.
Consequence: A trader can take a one-day long after an unusually deep daily drop, sized by the depth of the drop, instead of waiting a week for a rebound that does not come.
Why:
- F2: A large one-day fall is followed by a one-day rebound. In the bottom decile of the daily return divided by its 30-day standard deviation, the next day's return minus the coin's mean daily return is +0.84 %. That is t 2.01 with the cases of one day taken together, on 627 cases over 204 days. The next decile is +0.38 % (t 1.29), and deciles 5 to 9 are all negative (-0.07 % to -0.44 %).
- F3: That one-day rebound holds in every slice of the data. Its mean is positive in every year, from +0.44 % in 2019 to +1.37 % in 2020, and on every coin, from +0.49 % on BTC to +1.41 % on XMR (t 3.09) and +1.06 % on ZEC (t 1.95). The book held a position on only 43 % of these bottom-decile days.
- F6: The tested idea's size cannot pay for its trades. Its total move per case is +0.13 % over 7 days, against a cost of 0.19 % per trade, and its unusual move is -0.84 % (t -1.23). By contrast, the one-day rebound after the deepest decile of falls (+0.84 % excess) is about 4.4 times that cost.
