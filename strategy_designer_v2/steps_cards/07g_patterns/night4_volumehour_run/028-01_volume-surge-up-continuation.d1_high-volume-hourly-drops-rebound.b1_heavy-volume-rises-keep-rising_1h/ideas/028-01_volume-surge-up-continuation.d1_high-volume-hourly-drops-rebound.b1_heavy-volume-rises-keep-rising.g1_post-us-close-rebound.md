Idea: Over the 2 hours after the 19:00 UTC close, the coin rises, and the more its 6-hour return into that close has fallen against its typical 6-hour move, the larger the rise over those 2 hours.
Consequence: A trader would buy the coin at the 19:00 UTC close after a weak US session, and refuse to short it into the following two hours.
Why:
- F1: The two hours from 20:00 to 22:00 UTC carry the coins' clearest hourly drift. Hour 20 averages +5.08 bp with t 4.52 and hour 21 averages +4.62 bp with t 4.21, on about 5,836 non-overlapping 1-hour returns each, while every other hour lies between -2.73 and +2.36 in t, so the pair stands out even after looking at 24 hours. The pair is stronger when US daylight time runs (April to October): BTC +5.52 bp t 2.58 (n 1712) and ETH +10.12 bp t 3.62 (n 1712), against +3.31 bp t 0.94 and +1.08 bp t 0.25 in the other months. That points to the US equity close.
- F2: The 20:00 to 22:00 UTC rise is graded by the coin's 6-hour return into 19:00 UTC, scaled by its typical move. By quintile of that return, lowest first, the two hours return +9.63 bp (t 2.15), +8.25 bp (t 2.34), +1.45 bp, +6.36 bp and -2.49 bp (t -0.66), with about 1,071 cases per quintile. It is also larger in the highest-volatility tercile, +11.38 bp t 2.95, against +3.43 bp and -0.89 bp. It is positive in 2/3 of coin-windows and in 13 of 16 coin-years.
