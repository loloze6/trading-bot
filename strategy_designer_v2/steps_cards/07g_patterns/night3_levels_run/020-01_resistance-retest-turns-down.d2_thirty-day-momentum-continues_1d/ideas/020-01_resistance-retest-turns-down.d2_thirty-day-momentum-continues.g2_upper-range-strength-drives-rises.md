Idea: The nearer a coin's close is to the top of its 30-day high-low range, the more it rises over the next 10 days, beyond its normal drift; the move is carried by the upper part of the range.
Consequence: A trader should take the long side of range strength and not rely on its mirror image (shorting the range bottom) for the same gain.
Why:
- F2: The range position takes over the 30-day momentum: in a joint regression on the 10-day de-drifted return, the 30-day range position keeps its slope (+1.17 %, t 2.67) while the 30-day return loses its own (-0.03 %, t -0.06).
- F3: The effect sits at the top of the range. In the top fifth of the 30-day range position, a coin beats its normal 10-day drift by +2.67 % (t 2.95, 485 cases), about 14 times the 0.19 % cost of a round trip. The second-lowest fifth falls -1.41 % (t -2.24), but the lowest fifth falls only -0.13 % (t -0.19).
- F4: The book's gain comes from its long side alone. Before costs, the long side earns +0.090 % per coin-bar (t 5.27) and the short side -0.007 % (t -0.48). On the 5-day grid, long cells earn +0.93 % and short cells -0.11 %.
