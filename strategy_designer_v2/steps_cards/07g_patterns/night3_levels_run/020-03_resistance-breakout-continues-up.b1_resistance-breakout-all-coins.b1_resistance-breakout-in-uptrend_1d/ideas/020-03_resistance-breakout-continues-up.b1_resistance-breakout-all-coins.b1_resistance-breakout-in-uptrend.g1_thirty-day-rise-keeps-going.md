Idea: The larger a coin's 30-day rise, the larger its rise over the next 10 days, while its 30-day return is positive.
Consequence: A trader would lean longer on a coin the further it has risen over the past month, and would refuse longs on a coin whose month is only mildly up.
Why:
- F1: Outside the breakout signal, a coin's own 30-day return grades what follows. On non-overlapping 10-day cells, the top fifth of 30-day returns per coin (median +40 %) is followed by a strength of +0.17 (t 3.2, 528 cases). The fourth fifth (median +12 %) gives +0.05 (t 1.1). The second fifth (median -13 %) gives -0.13 (t -3.9, 524 cases). The same order holds on 20-day cells: top +0.22 (t 3.0, 241 cases), second fifth -0.12 (t -2.5). It also holds on 5-day cells: top +0.12 (t 3.3, 1067 cases), second fifth -0.11 (t -4.7). The deepest falls (bottom fifth, median -27 %) are flat (-0.02 to -0.07), so the grading holds on the rising side and does not extend to the bottom.
- F2: The 30-day effect of F1 is not one period's. The 10-day strength of the top fifth minus the second fifth is positive in 7 of 8 years (+0.17 to +0.82). The exception is 2018, at -1.06, on few cases. The coins share dates, so the pooled t's overstate the independent evidence.
- F3: Within the tested breakout entries, the 30-day rise does more of the grading than the break measure. The 5-day strength rises from +0.04 to +0.16 to +0.20 across 30-day-return terciles (683 to 684 entries each). Across break-measure terciles it goes +0.13, +0.10, +0.18, with no order.
