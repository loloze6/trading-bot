Idea: When the swing lows bunched just above a coin's daily close are unusually many and near compared with how bunched they have been over the past 90 days, the coin tends to fall over the next 5 days, and the more unusual the bunching against its own recent history, the larger the fall.
Consequence: A short on a broken support is worth taking only at the coin's own rare extremes of bunched lows, where the fall exceeds the coin's drift and the cost; elsewhere the short only pays the drift.
Why:
- F1: The tested short loses because of the coins' drift, not its timing. The book's daily returns correlate -0.80 with the coins' and average -4.7 bps a day while holding earns +11.8. Its 5,710 short trades gain +0.061 % each before fees and lose -0.039 % after. The idea's unusual move (+0.12 % per case) is 0.6 times the 0.19 % round trip, and the -0.59 % drift against it leaves the total move at -0.47 %.
- F4: The tested short's fall sits almost entirely in its most extreme fifth of forecasts, and it is not graded by the raw cluster count.
