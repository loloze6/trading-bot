Idea: When a coin's daily close sits high in its range of the past 20 days, it tends to rise over the next 5 days, and the nearer the close to the top of that range, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A long taken when the close presses its 20-day high keeps both the coin's drift and the timing, which together are several times what a trade costs, while a short against it pays the drift.
Why:
- F1: The tested short loses because of the coins' drift, not its timing. The book's daily returns correlate -0.80 with the coins' and average -4.7 bps a day while holding earns +11.8. Its 5,710 short trades gain +0.061 % each before fees and lose -0.039 % after. The idea's unusual move (+0.12 % per case) is 0.6 times the 0.19 % round trip, and the -0.59 % drift against it leaves the total move at -0.47 %.
- F2: The opposite situation carries a move larger than a trade's cost. On the 5-day grid, the 1,060 cases with no past-90-day swing low above the close (forecast zero) end +0.79 % above the coin's drift over 5 days. The 2,359 cases with a low above end -0.35 %, a gap of about 1.1 % (Welch t about 3.1, pooled). For a long, that is a total move of about +1.4 %, roughly 7 times the 0.19 % cost.
- F3: The higher the close sits in its 20-day high-low range, the larger the 5-day excess return, but the pattern weakens under date clustering and reverses in recent years.
