Idea: When a coin's daily close sits just below prices where it turned up several times in the past 90 days, it rises over the next 10 days by less than usual, and the more such past lows lie just above the close, the smaller its rise relative to its rises after other closes.
Consequence: A close under a support that held several times is a reason to cut or refuse a long for about ten days rather than to go short against the coin's rise.
Why:
- F1: The book loses because it is short against coins that rise. Coins rose 0.142 % a day on average, the book lost 0.085 % a day, and all 5797 trades were shorts (mean net -3.03 %, median -2.90 %, negative on every one of 19 coins). The idea's unusual fall (+0.48 % per case, 2.2 times the 0.22 % cost) is smaller than the coin's drift against it (-1.38 %), so the total move per case is -0.90 %.
- F2: Flat grid bars were followed by a 10-day move of z +0.108 (t 3.24, n 1277) and short bars by z -0.061 (t -3.35, n 2251). But short bars still rose +0.26 % in raw terms, and the weakest-short fifth of cases rose +2.06 %. The forecast separates relative moves, not rises from falls.
