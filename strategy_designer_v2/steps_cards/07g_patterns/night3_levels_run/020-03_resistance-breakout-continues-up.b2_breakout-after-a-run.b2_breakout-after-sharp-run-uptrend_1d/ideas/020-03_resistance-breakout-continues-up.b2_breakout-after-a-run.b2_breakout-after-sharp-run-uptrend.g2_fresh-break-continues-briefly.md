Idea: When a coin closes beyond a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days, the rise over the next 5 days from that close is larger the more often the level held. The rise is concentrated in the days right after the break, and the coin falls back over the following weeks.
Consequence: A close through a well-defended level is a reason to buy at that close and keep the trade short, not to stay long for weeks after the break.
Why:
- F1: On grid bars where the regime marks a close through a repeatedly held level inside a rising quarter, the coin's next 5 days beat its normal drift by +2.05 % (t 3.5, 380 independent cases, strength 0.18), about 10 times the 0.19 % round-trip cost. This is far above the verdict's +0.44 % (t 1.28, 1205 cases), so the edge sits on the break bar itself: 377 of 380 in-position grid cases fall within 2 days of the forecast turning on.
- F2: The edge fades fast after the break. Grid bars 1 to 5 days after a break return −0.55 % (t −1.45, 981 cases), and bars 15 to 40 days after return −1.32 % (t −2.94, 523 cases).
- F8: Trades held longer than their 5-day minimum carry the book's gain. Trades of 5.5 days or less net +0.00 % (1232 trades), 10 to 20 days net +10.5 % (293 trades), and over 20 days net +15.7 % (83 trades). This is consistent with F9 of the earlier runs, but is partly selected by repeated breaks.
