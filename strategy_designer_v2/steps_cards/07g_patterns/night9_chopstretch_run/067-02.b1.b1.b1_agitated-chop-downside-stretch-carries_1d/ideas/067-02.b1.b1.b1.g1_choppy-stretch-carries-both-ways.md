Idea: In a choppy market, the further a coin's close stands from its 20-day average, counted in units of its 20-day standard deviation of daily returns, the more the coin keeps moving the same way over the next 5 days: further above, more rise; further below, more fall.
Consequence: A trader in a coin that has gone nowhere over two months should lean with a close stretched from its 20-day average, on either side and sized by the stretch, for about a week, rather than fade it.
Why:
- F3: In a choppy market (60-day return between -10 % and +10 %, calm or agitated), the |stretch|-weighted signed 5-day continuation is +5.82 % (t 2.04 by day, 125 cases on 65 days), against +1.33 % in falling markets (t 0.78).
- F4: Inside choppy markets the carry is larger below the average than above it. Below, the mean is -5.01 % (t -3.48, 60 cases) and reaches -11.72 % for stretches beyond 2 units (t -1.74 by day, 16 cases). Above, it is +1.78 % (t 1.25, 65 cases) and reaches +4.51 % beyond 2 units (t 1.23, 22 cases).
- F5: The tested book traded the choppy-agitated profile only, which holds 4.8 % of the bars. That left too few cases for its result to be told from chance (24 choppy-agitated cases at +10.19 %, t 1.02). In the calm half of choppy markets, the carry held with 101 cases at +4.63 % (t 2.06).
- F2: The rank correlation between the stretch and the next 5 days' excess return is positive on all 6 coins (0.08 to 0.13). The |stretch|-weighted signed continuation is positive in 11 of the 12 windows; the only exception is w12, at -0.35 %.
