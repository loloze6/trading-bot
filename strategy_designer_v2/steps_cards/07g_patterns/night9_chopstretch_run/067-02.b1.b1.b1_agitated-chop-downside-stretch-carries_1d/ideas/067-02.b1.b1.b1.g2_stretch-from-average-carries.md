Idea: In any market, the further a coin's close stands from its 20-day average, counted in units of its 20-day standard deviation of daily returns, the more the coin keeps moving the same way over the next 10 days, upward above the average and downward below it.
Consequence: A trader can hold a position sized by the stretch for about two weeks, short below the average as well as long above it, a short side the map's strategies acting in every profile barely cover.
Why:
- F1: Across all bars of folds B and C, the stretch carries both ways over 5 days, measured as the close's distance from its 20-day average in units of the 20-day standard deviation of daily returns. Closes at least 2 units below the average are followed by a 5-day excess return of -1.97 % (t -2.43, 276 cases), and closes more than 2 units above by +2.12 % (t 2.77, 284 cases). Signed in the stretch's direction, the largest-stretch cases carry +3.47 % (t 2.39 with the cases of one day taken together, 341 cases on 111 days).
- F2: The rank correlation between the stretch and the next 5 days' excess return is positive on all 6 coins (0.08 to 0.13). The |stretch|-weighted signed continuation is positive in 11 of the 12 windows; the only exception is w12, at -0.35 %.
- F6: The carry also runs over 10 days: closes more than 2 units above the average are followed by +4.38 % (t 2.81, 149 cases), and those at least 2 units below by -2.59 % (t -1.97, 135 cases).
