Idea: In a choppy, calm market, a coin's 14-day return measured against its own daily swing over the past 30 days carries on in the same direction over the next 7 days, and the larger that swing-scaled move, up or down, the larger the further move, with no minimum size.
Consequence: A trader in a choppy, calm coin leans with its recent swing-scaled two-week move, in proportion to its size, rather than waiting for an extreme move that this market almost never produces.
Why:
- F1: In the choppy calm profile, the 14-day return scaled by the 30-day swing never reached -2 in folds B and C. The z-bin for -2 or lower holds 0 cases out of 117 in-profile 7-day grid cases. The tested idea's 2-sigma regime therefore left only 2 cases on 2 days in the whole record, which is why step 5 found it not observable, not because the effect is absent.
- F2: Over the full range of that measure inside choppy calm, the 7-day unusual move rises with the swing-scaled 14-day return. The slope is +0.0435 per unit, with a day-clustered t of 3.17 over 117 cases on 60 days. Outside the profile the slope is +0.0122 (t 2.45, 708 cases). This is graded continuation, about 3.5 times stronger in the brief's profile.
- F3: The same in-profile continuation holds on 30-day and 7-day horizons of the past move. The 30-day slope is +0.044 (clustered t 2.49) and the 7-day slope is +0.018 (t 1.12). It is carried mainly by the 2-to-4-week move, with 14 days the strongest.
- F5: Across windows, the in-profile slope is positive in w10 (+0.049, 23 cases) and w12 (+0.061, 17 cases). It is negative in w8 (-0.028, 22 cases), w14 (-0.046, 14 cases) and w17 (-0.005, 12 cases). The continuation is concentrated in a few windows and must be confirmed on folds A and D.
