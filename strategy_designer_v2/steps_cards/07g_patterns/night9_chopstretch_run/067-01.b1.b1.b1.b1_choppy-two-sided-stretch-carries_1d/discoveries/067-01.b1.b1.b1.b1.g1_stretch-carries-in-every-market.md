A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In any market, a coin whose close stands above or below its 20-day average, measured in its 20-day standard deviation of daily returns, keeps moving the same way over the next 10 days. The further the close stands from the average on either side, the larger that move.
What it would mean for a trader: A close stretched far from its 20-day average for the coin's usual swing is a reason to trade with the stretch for about ten days, short as well as long, in any market rather than only in a range.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 067-01.b1.b1.b1.b1_choppy-two-sided-stretch-carries_1d (idea 067-01.b1.b1.b1.b1_choppy-two-sided-stretch-carries, brief 067_choppy-average-stretch-carries-forward).
What it rests on:
- F1: On folds B and C, across every market (all trends together), the coin's 10-day unusual move rises steadily with the close's stretch from its 20-day average in 20-day daily swings, from -2.68 % (t -2.04, 141 cases) in the lowest fifth through -1.97 %, -0.24 % and +2.02 % to +3.74 % (t 2.69, 141 cases) in the highest. Both sides carry, and at that size they are about 14 to 20 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F2: The stretch's carry over 10 days is not confined to the choppy market. Its slope on the unusual move has t 2.33 in rising markets (158 cases, 42 days) and t 3.13 in choppy ones (63 cases, 30 days). The test's own choppy measure over all four folds gave only t 0.73 with a negative long side (-0.35 %), so the choppy-only version is the least stable reading. In falling markets the slope has t 0.37 (193 cases), but the highest stretch fifth still follows with +8.75 % (t 2.39, 13 cases).
- F6: The tested book's mean daily return is negative in 7 of 12 windows. By coin it is negative or about zero on five coins (from -0.032 % to -0.000 % a day), and only XRP is positive (+0.124 % a day). The book's failure comes from confining a real but small stretch carry to the choppy profile, not from the cost.
