Idea: In a choppy, calm market, a coin whose 14-day return is negative against its own daily swing over the past 30 days keeps falling over the next 7 days, and the larger that swing-scaled fall, the larger the further fall.
Consequence: In a choppy calm market a trader can lean short in proportion to a swing-scaled 14-day fall, the mirror of the long carry.
Why:
- F2: The down side of the theme is there too. In-profile, the lowest z14 quintile ends -3.20 % below drift (t -2.22, n 29) and the next -3.51 % (t -1.52). In my choppy-calm rebuild with z14<0, the slope is +13.99 % per unit (t 2.20, 21 cases on 15 days): the larger the swing-scaled fall, the lower the next 7 days.
- F1: The tested carry shows only where the profile is really choppy calm, and only at the top of the forecast. On in-profile grid bars (regime "unknown", 7-day non-overlapping), the top z14 quintile (0.95 to 2.88) ends +7.61 % above drift (t 2.73, n 29), while the middle quintiles give +0.93 % and +0.89 % (t 0.45, 0.41). With my own profile rebuild, the choppy-calm slope of the 7-day unusual move on z14>0 is +7.24 % per unit (t 3.66, clustered by day, 39 cases on 18 days). On all folds, though, the slope's t was 1.0 and the unusual move per case was +0.05 %, so the effect is thin and rests on a few days.
