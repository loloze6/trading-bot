A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: A coin whose daily swing over the past 30 days is small against its daily swing over the past 90 days rises over the next 7 days, and the smaller that ratio, the larger the rise.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader can lean long on coins whose recent swing has compressed against their quarter's swing, with a move per case far above the 0.22 % cost of a trade.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 077-01.b1.b1_graded-swing-rise-carries-on_1d (idea 077-01.b1.b1_graded-swing-rise-carries-on, brief 077_choppy-calm-swing-scaled-moves-carry).
What it rests on:
- F4: A 30-day daily swing that is small against the coin's own 90-day swing is followed by a rise, graded over the lower quintiles. On 7-day grid bars (all profiles), the lowest ratio quintile (0.34 to 0.74) ends +6.62 % above drift (t 3.24, n 65), the next +2.59 % (t 1.40) and the middle -3.35 % (t -2.27); the upper two are -1.58 % and -0.59 %. This t is not clustered by day, and it is one of about a dozen angles I looked at.
