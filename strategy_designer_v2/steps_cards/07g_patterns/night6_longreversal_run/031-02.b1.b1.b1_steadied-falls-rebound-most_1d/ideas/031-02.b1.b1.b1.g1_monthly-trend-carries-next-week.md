Idea: The higher a coin's 30-day return, the more its price rises over the next 7 days. The lower its 30-day return, the more it falls over those 7 days.
Consequence: A trader leans long a coin in proportion to how far it rose over the past month, and refuses to fade a month-long move for the following week, since medium-term moves continue rather than reverse.
Why:
- F2: Across all bars, a coin's past 30-day return predicts its next 7 days in the same direction. The slope of the 7-day excess return (in usual moves) on the 30-day return (in usual moves) has t 3.20 over 2974 non-overlapping cases. The past move's sign times the next week's excess return averages 0.087 of a usual 7-day move, with t 2.52 when the cases of one day are taken together (315 days).
- F3: The 30-day to 7-day continuation is steady across years and coins. Its signed strength is positive in every year: 0.008 in 2018, 0.009 in 2019, 0.137 in 2020, 0.238 in 2021, 0.046 in 2022, 0.077 in 2023, 0.068 in 2024 and 0.087 in 2025. It is positive on 14 of 15 coins. It is graded by 7-day quintile of the past 30 days: the top two quintiles are +0.030 and +0.076 of a usual move, the lower three -0.028 to -0.068.
- F4: At the tested idea's own horizon, the 30-day return leans toward continuation over the next 30 days, not reversal, but the evidence is weak. The slope t is 2.25 over 687 cases. The top quintile is +0.126 of a usual move against -0.181 for the bottom one. With one day's cases together the t is only 1.33 (71 days), and the yearly signs alternate.
- F1: The book's loss comes from its shorts. The 689 short trades lost a mean of -11.36 % net, with 27 % winners. The 92 long trades earned +12.14 %, with 64 % winners. Per bar in a position, shorts made -0.149 % while the coin rose +0.169 %, and longs made +0.096 %.
