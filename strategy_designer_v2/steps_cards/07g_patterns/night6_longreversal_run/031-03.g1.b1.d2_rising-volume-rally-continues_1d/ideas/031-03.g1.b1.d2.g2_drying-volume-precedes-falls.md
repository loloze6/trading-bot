Idea: The further a coin's mean daily volume over the last 14 days has fallen below its mean daily volume over the last 30 days, the more its price falls over the next 14 days.
Consequence: A trader would short coins whose trading dries up over two weeks, sizing with the drying, or refuse longs in them.
Why:
- F1: Across all bars, whether the coin is rising or not, the 14-day/30-day volume ratio ranks the next 14 days' excess return in grade. The rank slope is +0.114 with t 3.10 (1530 non-overlapping cases, 131 days). The top quintile, ratio above +0.21, gains +5.1 % over the coin's drift (t 1.88). The bottom quintile, ratio below −0.21, loses −4.4 % (t −2.59). The usual 14-day move is 22.1 %.
- F3: Drying volume comes before underperformance, and the tested long-only book never uses this side. With the 14-day return up, the lowest volume-ratio quintile returns −5.96 % excess (t −3.33, 153 cases, 49 days). With it down, it returns −4.9 % (t −2.19). The lowest quintile falls short of the other bars in 18 of 19 coins (94.7 %) and has a negative mean in 6 of 8 years.
- F4: The volume effect is not a volatility effect. The volume ratio and 30-day volatility have a rank correlation of −0.05. Within volatility terciles the volume slope stays positive: t 3.59, 2.46 and 1.67 from low to high volatility.
