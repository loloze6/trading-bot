A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a calm market, the larger a coin's one-day rise against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the more the coin rises over the following two days, with the rise showing on the second day.
What it would mean for a trader: In calm markets a trader keeps or adds to a long after a large up day and expects the follow-through a day later.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 044-02_agitated-down-day-range-rebound_1d (idea 044-02_agitated-down-day-range-rebound, brief 044_agitated-wide-range-fall-rebound).
What it rests on:
- F7: In calm bars, a large rise is followed on the second day, not the first, by more rise: the largest-rise third on wide days gives +0.03 % next day (t 0.09) and then +0.75 % the day after (t 2.60, 553 cases); over all calm days, the second-day excess return grows with the day's move in units of usual swing (coefficient 0.0029, t 2.53, 4266 cases).
