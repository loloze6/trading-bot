Idea: In an agitated market, a coin's next-day rise after a one-day fall grows faster than the fall itself, measured against its 30-day daily swing. A fall of a few usual swings is followed by a rise many times larger than a fall of about one swing, which is followed by almost none.
Consequence: In agitated markets a trader should size a rebound bet steeply with the depth of the fall, putting almost all of it on the rare extreme falls whose rebound far exceeds a trade's cost.
Why:
- F1: In agitated markets the next-day rebound after a one-day fall is convex, not linear. Regressing the next-day excess return on the fall (in 30-day swings) and its square, falls only, t clustered by day: fall² +0.0124 (t 5.94), fall −0.0223 (t −2.76).

- F8: The book earned +0.045 % a day over 1449 days, with 1 % tails of −2.05 % and +2.80 %. Window means range from −0.011 % to +0.179 %, so a few windows (w11, w14, w18) carry most of the gain, consistent with a profit concentrated in rare deep-fall days (F2).
