A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: The larger a coin's fall over the day against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the larger its rise over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader should treat an unusually large one-day fall, measured against the coin's own recent swing, as a reason to buy for the next day.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 049-01_wide-day-low-close-rebound_1d (idea 049-01_wide-day-low-close-rebound, brief 049_wide-day-close-location-reversal).
What it rests on:
- F4: The signal is hard to tell apart from a plain large down day. Today's volatility-scaled return, bottom fifth, gives +0.703 % next day (t 2.38, 1,436 bars). In a joint regression, the close-location × width term keeps a coefficient of 0.998 % but its t falls to 1.65, and the scaled return's term goes to 0.029 % (t 0.13). The two carry the same one-day reversal.
- F5: The reversal is gone after one day. On the non-overlapping 3-day grid, the five signal fifths give excess returns from -0.144 % to +0.309 %, all with |t| < 0.5 (about 470 cases each). Holding longer does not enlarge the move against the 0.19 % trade cost.
