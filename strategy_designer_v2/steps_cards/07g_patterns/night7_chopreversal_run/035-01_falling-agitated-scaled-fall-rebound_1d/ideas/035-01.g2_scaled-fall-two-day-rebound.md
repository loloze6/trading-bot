Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall relative to its 30-day daily swing, the larger its rise over the next two days.
Consequence: The discount left by a sharp down day in a falling, agitated market takes two days to recover, so a trader holding the rebound two days captures more of it per trade.
Why:
- F3: In the falling, agitated profile, the fall's size also grades the return over the next two days: the slope of the two-day excess return on the one-day scaled fall is 0.0149 (t 2.43; 520 bars on 212 days). The two-day scaled fall grades the next day: slope 0.0248 (t 2.31; 554 bars on 218 days).
- F5: The book's profit sits in a few days. Its best 5 % of days sum to 158 % of its total gain. On its best 2 % of days the coins rose +8.6 % on average the same day, and on its worst 2 % they fell -6.6 %. Its gain comes from a few sharp rebounds in windows w14, w16 and w18, where 38 to 49 % of bars are in the falling, agitated profile.
