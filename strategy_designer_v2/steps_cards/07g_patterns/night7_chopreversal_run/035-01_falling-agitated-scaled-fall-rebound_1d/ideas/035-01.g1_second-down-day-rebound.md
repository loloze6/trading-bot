Idea: In a falling, agitated market, when a coin's down day follows another down day, the larger that day's fall relative to its 30-day daily swing, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: The rebound to buy is the one after a second consecutive sharp down day in a falling, agitated market, when forced selling has run two days, not after a single down day following an up day.
Why:
- F1: In the falling, agitated profile, the rebound grows with the day's scaled fall only when the day before was also down: the slope of the next-day excess return on the scaled fall is 0.0327 (t 5.08; 219 bars on 123 days) after a down day, against 0.0035 (t 0.36; 303 bars on 127 days) after an up day.
- F2: The same split shows in the largest falls, those beyond 1.5 of the 30-day swing: they are followed by +5.56 % (t 3.64; 23 bars on 13 days) when the previous day was down, against +2.16 % (t 0.94; 28 bars on 15 days) when it was up. For falls of 0.75 to 1.5 swings the figures are +2.14 % (t 2.32) against +0.62 % (t 0.95).
- F4: In the falling, agitated profile, up days carry no graded effect: the next-day slope on the scaled rise is 0.0015 (t 0.23; 500 bars on 195 days). Outside that profile, a fall's size does not grade the next day in rising markets: slope 0.0035 (t 0.72) with the 30-day swing below the 90-day swing, and 0.0080 (t 0.84) with it above.
