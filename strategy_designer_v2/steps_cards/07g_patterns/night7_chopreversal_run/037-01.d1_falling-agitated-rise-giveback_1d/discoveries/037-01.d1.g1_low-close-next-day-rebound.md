A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, the lower a coin closes within its day's high-low range, the larger its rise over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader buys coins that close near the day's low in a falling, agitated market for one day, more the nearer the low, and avoids shorting them there.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 037-01.d1_falling-agitated-rise-giveback_1d (idea 037-01.d1_falling-agitated-rise-giveback, brief 037_agitated-ordinary-rise-next-day-giveback).
What it rests on:
- F1: In the falling agitated profile (bars not sent to chop), the next day's return beyond drift falls steadily as the day's close moves up its own high-low range: +2.33 % (t 5.22, n 201) after closes in the bottom fifth, then +0.38 % (t 1.26), +0.11 %, -0.71 % (t -1.84) and -0.60 % (t -1.55, n 203) in the top fifth. Out of the profile the same ladder is flat, from +0.20 % (t 1.13, n 831) to -0.32 % (t -2.28, n 1324). These t's are not day-pooled, but 5.2 stands well clear of the roughly 40 cells looked at.
- F6: The tested book's total move per case (+0.43 %) is only 2.2 times the 0.19 % trade cost. The low-close rebound in F1 (+2.33 %) is about 12 times that cost, a move large enough to pay.
