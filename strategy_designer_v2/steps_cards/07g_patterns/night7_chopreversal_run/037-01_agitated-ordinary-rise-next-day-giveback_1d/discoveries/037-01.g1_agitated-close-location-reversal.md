A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In an agitated market, the lower a coin closes within its day's high-low range, the higher its return over the next day; closes near the day's high are followed by the weakest next day.
What it would mean for a trader: In agitated markets, the day's close location is a graded one-day signal: lean long after closes near the low and short after closes near the high, and hold for about a day.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 037-01_agitated-ordinary-rise-next-day-giveback_1d (idea 037-01_agitated-ordinary-rise-next-day-giveback, brief 037_agitated-ordinary-rise-next-day-giveback).
What it rests on:
- F1: In agitated markets, the lower a coin closes within its day's high-low range, the higher its next-day return over its drift. The slope on (0.5 − close position) is +1.86 % (t 2.99, cases of one day together, 3,691 bars). In calm markets it is only +0.63 % (t 1.47, 4,639 bars).
- F2: The close-location effect in agitated markets carries more than the tested idea's own signal. With both in one regression on agitated bars, the coefficient on (0.5 − close position) is +1.05 % against −0.33 % for the volatility-scaled return (2,946 bars). In calm bars the two are +0.22 % and −0.14 % (4,005 bars).
- F3: The agitated close-location slope is positive on every coin and in every year shown.
- F4: Across all agitated days, the next-day excess return falls steadily as the close moves up the range.
- F6: The book does not convert that edge into profit.
