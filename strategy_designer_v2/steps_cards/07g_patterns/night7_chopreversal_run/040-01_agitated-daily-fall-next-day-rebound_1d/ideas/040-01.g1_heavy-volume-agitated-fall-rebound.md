Idea: In an agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall multiplied by how heavy that day's volume was against its average daily volume of the last 20 days, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: A trader buying agitated-market falls should size up on falls that came on unusually heavy volume and size down on falls on thin volume, since the forced selling that gets paid back shows in the volume.
Why:
- F2: On agitated down days, the day's relative volume carries the grading. The product of the standardised fall and the log of the day's volume against its 20-day average has a slope of -0.0079 (t -5.71, 1439 cases, 537 days). Fitted together, the product keeps t -2.92 and the fall alone drops to t -0.65, while relative volume alone adds nothing (t 0.10).
- F3: The volume-weighted grading of F2 points the same way on every coin, with t from -2.17 (XMR) to -8.89 (ETH), and in 2018, 2019 and 2020 (t -2.19, -2.27, -5.52). It fades in 2021 (t -0.20, 474 cases).
- F4: The volume-weighted grading lives only in agitated falls. The same product has a t of -0.09 on calm down days (1978 cases), 0.30 on agitated up days and -0.63 on calm up days.
- F1: In agitated markets only falls are reversed in proportion to their size: on agitated down days the next-day excess return falls by 0.0093 per unit of standardised fall (t -2.62, 1439 cases, 537 days). On agitated up days the slope is +0.0026 (t 1.24, 1505 cases). On calm down days it is -0.0022 (t -0.80, 1978 cases). Where the close sits in the day's range adds nothing (t -0.65 once the net change is in).
