Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall measured against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the last 30 days, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: After a sharp scaled fall in a coin that has dropped over the past 60 days and swings more than usual, a trader buys for one day, more the deeper the fall, and does not hold the rebound longer.
Why:
- F1: In a falling, agitated market (60-day return below -10 %, swing above its yearly median), the next-day move scales with the size of a day's fall: the slope of next-day excess return (in 30-day sd units) on the scaled fall is -0.362, t -3.99, over 377 down days. In the other five profiles the same slope is flat: falling calm -0.004 (t -0.03), choppy agitated -0.088 (t -0.95), rising agitated -0.003 (t -0.03).
- F2: The F1 slope holds on every coin and every window with enough cases, so one episode does not carry it:
- F3: In falling, agitated markets the next-day excess return grows with the depth of the fall:
- F4: The F1 rebound lasts one day only. Over two days, on non-overlapping cases, the slope in falling, agitated markets is -0.005 (t -0.33, 157 cases), against -0.36 over one day.
