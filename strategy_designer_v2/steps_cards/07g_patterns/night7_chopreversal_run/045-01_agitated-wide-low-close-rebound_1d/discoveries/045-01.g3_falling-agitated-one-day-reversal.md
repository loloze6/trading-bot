A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall, the larger its rise over the next day, and the larger its one-day rise, the larger its fall over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader in a falling, swinging market fades each day's move for one day, buying after the deepest falls and selling after the sharpest rises.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 045-01_agitated-wide-low-close-rebound_1d (idea 045-01_agitated-wide-low-close-rebound, brief 045_agitated-wide-day-close-location-fade).
What it rests on:
- F8: In the falling, agitated profile the day's own return predicts the next day more strongly than the close location does. The slope is −0.289 (t −4.40, 738 cases). By quintile of the day's return, the lowest fifth is followed by +2.77 % (t 2.71) and the highest fifth by −1.36 % (t −2.06). The rising agitated profile shows nothing (slope +0.052, t 0.93).
- F5: The effect lasts one day only. On a non-overlapping 2-day grid, the falling-agitated slope turns to −0.0079 (t −0.54, 363 cases), against t 3.68 at one day.
