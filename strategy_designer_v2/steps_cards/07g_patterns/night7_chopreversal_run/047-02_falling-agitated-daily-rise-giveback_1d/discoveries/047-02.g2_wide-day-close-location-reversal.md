A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: The lower a coin closes within its day's high-low range, and the wider that range is against the coin's usual daily range, the larger its rise over the next day. The higher it closes in a wide day's range, the larger its fall over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A wide day's close location gives the side of a one-day trade, the bet being larger the wider the day and the closer the close to the day's extreme.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 047-02_falling-agitated-daily-rise-giveback_1d (idea 047-02_falling-agitated-daily-rise-giveback, brief 047_falling-agitated-two-way-daily-fade).
What it rests on:
- F4: Across all bars, the lower a coin closes within its day's range, the higher its next-day excess return: −1.19 % per unit of centred close location (t −3.04, 8,330 cases on 1,448 days).
- F5: The close-location effect grows with how wide the day is against the coin's usual range. In a regression on close location and close location × relative range, the interaction carries the effect (all bars t −2.32, in the profile t −3.59 % per unit with t −2.76), while close location alone becomes insignificant (t 1.01).
- F6: The close-location effect sits in falling and choppy markets and is absent in rising ones.
- F7: The one-day close-location reversal is partly undone on the second day: the day-two slope is +0.87 % (t 2.32) on all bars and +2.45 % (t 2.59) in the profile.
