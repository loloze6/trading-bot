A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a calm market, the larger a coin's 5-day return against its 30-day daily swing, the larger its move the same way over the next three days.
What it would mean for a trader: In calm markets, lean with a coin's recent scaled move for three days rather than against it.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a calm market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 036-01_agitated-scaled-fall-three-day-rebound_1d (idea 036-01_agitated-scaled-fall-three-day-rebound, brief 036_agitated-fall-three-day-recovery).
What it rests on:
- F3: In calm bars, the 3-day excess return grows with the coin's 5-day return scaled by its usual swing (slope +0.94 % per unit, day-clustered t 2.36; 1,298 cases, 327 days) and with its 20-day scaled return (+0.76 %, t 2.13); in agitated bars the same slopes are +0.28 % (t 0.37) and +0.17 % (t 0.28); every coin's 20-day slope is positive (t 0.78 to 1.80), but only 6 of 12 windows are.
- F4: Pooled over all regimes, the 3-day excess return is −1.45 % after the lowest quintile of the 20-day scaled return (t −3.41, 453 cases) and +1.27 % after the fourth quintile (t 3.25), with +0.58 % in the top quintile (t 1.31); these t's are not day-clustered, so they overstate how sure the pattern is.
