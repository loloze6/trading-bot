Idea: A coin's 24-hour return is partly reversed over the next 24 hours: the larger its 24-hour rise against its usual 24-hour move, the larger its fall over the following 24 hours, and the larger its 24-hour fall, the larger its rebound, on any day of the week.
Consequence: After a coin's unusually large one-day move, a trader takes the opposite side for the next day, and refuses to chase the move.
Why:
- F1: Across all coins, a coin's 24-hour move is partly reversed over the next 24 hours, on the non-overlapping 24-hour grid: the slope of the next 24-hour unusual move (in usual moves) on the past 24-hour move is -0.0915 (t -11.74, 16238 cases). In the extreme quintiles, the falls (z -1.24) are followed by +0.468 % (t 4.40, 3251 cases) and the rises (z +1.32) by -0.322 % (t -3.27, 3249 cases).
- F2: The 24-hour reversal holds in every coin: the mean unusual move in the reversal direction on the extreme quintiles is +0.28 % to +0.59 % (t 1.54 to 3.50, about 1090 cases each). That is about 0.05 of a usual 24-hour move (5.5 % to 9.5 %) and about twice the 0.19 % cost of a trade. It is positive in 2018 to 2023 (+0.20 % to +0.68 %, t up to 3.93) and near zero in 2024 (-0.09 %, t -0.48) and 2025 (+0.04 %, t 0.17).
- F3: The reversal does not depend on the weekend or on thin volume. On Sunday day-ends, the slope of the next 48-hour unusual move on the past 48-hour move is -0.024 (t -0.77, 1951 cases), no steeper than on other weekdays (-0.028 to +0.035). The 24-hour extreme-quintile reversal is +0.31 % (t 2.65) in the lowest volume tercile, against +0.44 % (t 3.51) and +0.43 % (t 3.26) in the middle and highest terciles.
