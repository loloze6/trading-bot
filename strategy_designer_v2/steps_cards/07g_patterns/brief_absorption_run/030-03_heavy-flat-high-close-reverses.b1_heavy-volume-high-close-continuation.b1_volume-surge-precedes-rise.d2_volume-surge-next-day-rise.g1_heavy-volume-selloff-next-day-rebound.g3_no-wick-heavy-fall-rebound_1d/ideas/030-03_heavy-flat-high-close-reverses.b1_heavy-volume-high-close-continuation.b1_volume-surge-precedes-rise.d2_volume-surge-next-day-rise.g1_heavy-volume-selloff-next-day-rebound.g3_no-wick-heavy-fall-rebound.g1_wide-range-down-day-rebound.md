Idea: The wider a down day's high-low range is against the coin's 20-day volatility, the more the coin rises over the next day.
Consequence: A trader buys for one day after down days with an unusually wide range for the coin's recent volatility, more the wider it is, and refuses the narrow-range down days.
Why:
- F3: Across all down days, the next-day excess return rises with the day's high-low range against its 20-day volatility: the top quintile gave +0.70 % (t 4.65, 1432 cases), the fourth +0.35 % (t 2.55), and the lower three −0.20 % to +0.06 %; this holds without any volume or close-position condition.
- F6: The rebound lasts one day: trades held about 1 day earned +1.50 % net (2201 trades), while trades kept open 2, 3 and 4 days, which the forecast extended through further heavy down days, lost −2.95 % (513), −6.89 % (119) and −11.74 % (31).
