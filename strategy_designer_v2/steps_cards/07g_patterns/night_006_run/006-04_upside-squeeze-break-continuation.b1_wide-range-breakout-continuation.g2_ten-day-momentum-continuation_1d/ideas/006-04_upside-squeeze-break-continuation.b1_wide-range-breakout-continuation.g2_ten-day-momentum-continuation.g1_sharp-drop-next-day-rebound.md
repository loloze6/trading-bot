Idea: The larger a coin's fall over the last day, measured against its own usual daily move, the more it rises over the next day.
Consequence: A trader would lean toward buying, or would refuse to short, a coin right after its sharpest one-day drops, scaling with the drop's size.
Why:
- F1: The coin's largest daily falls are followed by a one-day rebound. The next-day excess return in the bottom decile of the day's standardised return is +0.0076 (t=5.02, 1724 cases), then +0.0020 (t=1.61) in the second decile, and between -0.0010 and -0.0026 in every decile from the fourth up; at about five standard errors this survives the many angles examined.
