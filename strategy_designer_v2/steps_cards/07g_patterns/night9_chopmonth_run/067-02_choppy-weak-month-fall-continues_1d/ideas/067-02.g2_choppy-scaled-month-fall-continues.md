Idea: In a choppy market, the larger a coin's 30-day fall relative to its own daily swing over the past 30 days, the more it falls over the next 7 days.
Consequence: After a weak month in a trendless coin, a trader refuses longs for the next week and shorts more the deeper the scaled fall, because that move is large enough to outweigh the coin's normal upward drift.
Why:
- F1: Inside the choppy profile, a coin's 30-day return scaled by its 30-day daily swing ranks its next 7-day unusual move with a Spearman of 0.276 (215 non-overlapping cases), against 0.028 outside the choppy profile (1004 cases). In choppy, the quintiles of the scaled 30-day return run from -8.9 % (lowest, 18 days) and -4.5 % (second, t -3.57, 35 days) through -0.4 % to +2.5 % and +1.6 % (top two).
- F2: In choppy, the continuation is stronger after falls than after rises. After a negative 30-day return the 7-day unusual move in the month's direction is +5.1 % (t 2.67, 54 days; graded t 2.12). After a positive one it is +1.7 % (t 0.78, 48 days; graded t 1.08). The ranking inside each side is about the same (Spearman 0.189 after falls, 0.180 after rises).
- F6: The book is short only: 594 SHORT trades with a mean net result of -2.40 %. Most of the losses come from three coins: ZEC -371 % summed, DOGE -297 % and LINK -219 %. Book returns by window swing from -7.3 to +10.1 bp a day. Shorting gives back the coin's normal drift: unusual move +1.13 % against drift -1.13 %, so the total move is -0.01 % per case against a 0.22 % trade cost.
