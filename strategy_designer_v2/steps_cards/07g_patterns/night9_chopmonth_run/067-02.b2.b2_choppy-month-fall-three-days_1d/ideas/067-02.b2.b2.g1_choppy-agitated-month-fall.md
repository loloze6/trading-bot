Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, the larger a coin's 30-day fall below zero (its close under its close of 30 days earlier), the more it falls over the next 3 days.
Consequence: A deeper month-long fall is a stronger reason to short for a few days when a trendless coin is also swinging more than usual, and a weak one when it is calm.
Why:
- F2: Among choppy bars with a 30-day fall, the agitated ones carry the effect. Their 3-day excess is -0.48 of a usual move (t -2.54, 71 cases), against -0.09 for the calm ones (t -1.27, 201 cases). Over 7 days it is -0.59 (t -2.09, 34 cases) against -0.18 (t -1.94, 77 cases). The map holds no strategy in the choppy agitated short cell (0).
- F1: In the choppy market (folds B, C), the 3-day excess return falls only in the two lowest quintiles of the 30-day return: -0.33 of a usual move in the lowest (t -2.10, 104 cases, 60 days) and -0.15 in the second (t -1.90, 104 cases). The middle and upper quintiles sit near 0 (-0.003, -0.026, -0.006, all |t| < 0.4). So the tested effect lives in the deepest month-long falls, and the gradient inside the fall side is weak (slope t 1.06 at 3 days, 272 cases).
