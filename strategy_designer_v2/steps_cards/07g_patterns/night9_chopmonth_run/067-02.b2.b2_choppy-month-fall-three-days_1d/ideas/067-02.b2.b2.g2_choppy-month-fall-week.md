Idea: In a choppy market, the larger a coin's 30-day fall below zero, the more it falls over the next 7 days.
Consequence: In a trendless market, a deeper month-long fall is a reason to hold a short for a week rather than three days, so the move it captures covers a trade's cost.
Why:
- F3: The choppy 30-day-fall effect grows with the horizon. The excess is -0.11 of a usual move at 1 day (t -1.81, 809 cases), -0.19 at 3 days (t -2.50, 272 cases) and -0.31 at 7 days (t -2.64, 111 cases). At 7 days the graded slope on calm bars is +0.26 (t 2.59, 77 cases), the first horizon where the depth of the fall orders the move.
- F1: In the choppy market (folds B, C), the 3-day excess return falls only in the two lowest quintiles of the 30-day return: -0.33 of a usual move in the lowest (t -2.10, 104 cases, 60 days) and -0.15 in the second (t -1.90, 104 cases). The middle and upper quintiles sit near 0 (-0.003, -0.026, -0.006, all |t| < 0.4). So the tested effect lives in the deepest month-long falls, and the gradient inside the fall side is weak (slope t 1.06 at 3 days, 272 cases).
