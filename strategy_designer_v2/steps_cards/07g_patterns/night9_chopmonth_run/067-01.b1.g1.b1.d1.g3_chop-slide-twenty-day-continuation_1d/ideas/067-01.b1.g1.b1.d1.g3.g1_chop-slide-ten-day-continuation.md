Idea: In a choppy market, the larger a coin's percentage fall over the past 30 days, the more it keeps falling over the next 10 days, the continuation growing with the depth of the slide.
Consequence: A short on a deep monthly slide in a flat market pays over about ten days and fades by three weeks, so it should not be held longer.
Why:
- F1: In the choppy profile, the coin's 30-day return points the way of its move over the next 10 days. On the 10-day grid the Spearman correlation of the 30-day return with the unusual move is 0.38 (157 cases, p 1e-6), and 0.27 over 5 days (306 cases, p 1e-6). Outside the choppy profile it is only 0.04 (737 cases, p 0.32), so the effect lives in the choppy market.
- F2: The effect is carried by falls and is graded. Among choppy cases with a negative 30-day return, the 10-day unusual move is +3.7 % (t 1.15, 28 cases) for the shallowest third of falls (mean -3.7 %), -5.9 % (t -1.82, 27 cases) for the middle third, and -13.8 % (t -4.58, 28 cases) for the deepest third (mean -26 %). The Spearman correlation within falls is 0.53 (83 cases, p 3e-7). Over 5 days the same thirds give -0.5 %, -4.0 % and -7.9 % (t -2.59, 54 cases).
- F4: The tested 20-day horizon is too long for this effect. At 20 days the choppy correlation drops to 0.23 (68 cases, p 0.06) and the quintiles are not ordered. This matches step 5's +0.75 % unusual move (t 0.45) at 20 days, against -13.8 % at 10 days for deep falls in F2.
- F7: The book does not capture the move after its own shorts. Its choppy-profile shorts on negative 30-day returns were followed by a 5-day coin move of -3.0 % (151 cases). It also held 378 short cases outside the choppy profile, carried there by the 20-bar minimum holding. Of these, 86 came after positive 30-day returns and were followed by a +4.4 % 5-day rise. This is where its losses (Sharpe -0.20) sit.
