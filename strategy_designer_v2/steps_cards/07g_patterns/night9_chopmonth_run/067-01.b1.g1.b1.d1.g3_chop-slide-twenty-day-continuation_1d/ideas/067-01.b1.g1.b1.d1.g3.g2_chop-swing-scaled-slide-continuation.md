Idea: In a choppy market, the larger a coin's 30-day fall measured against its own 30-day daily swing, the more it keeps falling over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A short in a flat market should be sized by how unusual the month's fall is for that coin, not by its raw percentage.
Why:
- F1: In the choppy profile, the coin's 30-day return points the way of its move over the next 10 days. On the 10-day grid the Spearman correlation of the 30-day return with the unusual move is 0.38 (157 cases, p 1e-6), and 0.27 over 5 days (306 cases, p 1e-6). Outside the choppy profile it is only 0.04 (737 cases, p 0.32), so the effect lives in the choppy market.
- F5: Scaling the 30-day return by the coin's 30-day swing keeps the gradient. In the choppy profile the correlation is 0.33 at 10 days (157 cases, p 2e-5). The two most negative quintiles give 10-day unusual moves of -9.3 % (t -2.59, 32 cases) and -7.4 % (t -2.15, 31 cases).
- F2: The effect is carried by falls and is graded. Among choppy cases with a negative 30-day return, the 10-day unusual move is +3.7 % (t 1.15, 28 cases) for the shallowest third of falls (mean -3.7 %), -5.9 % (t -1.82, 27 cases) for the middle third, and -13.8 % (t -4.58, 28 cases) for the deepest third (mean -26 %). The Spearman correlation within falls is 0.53 (83 cases, p 3e-7). Over 5 days the same thirds give -0.5 %, -4.0 % and -7.9 % (t -2.59, 54 cases).
