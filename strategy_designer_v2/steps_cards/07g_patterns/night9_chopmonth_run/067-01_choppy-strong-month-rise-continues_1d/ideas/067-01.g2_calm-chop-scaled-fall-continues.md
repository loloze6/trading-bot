Idea: In a choppy, calm market, the larger a coin's 30-day fall measured against its own daily swing over those 30 days, the more it keeps falling over the next 7 days.
Consequence: In a quiet, trendless coin, a month's fall that is large for its swing is a reason to be short for the following week.
Why:
- F1: In choppy markets, a coin's 30-day return predicts its next 7 days, but only on the falling side. On the offset-0 7-day grid of folds B, C, the slope of excess return on a negative 30-day return is 0.38 (t 3.93, 111 cases). The slope on a positive 30-day return is -0.07 (t -0.81, 104 cases). This explains why the tested long-only idea was inconclusive (slope t 0.86).
- F5: In choppy markets the continuation lives in the calmer half of each coin's 30-day swing. There the full slope is 0.26 (t 3.86, 160 cases) and the negative-side slope 0.50 (t 2.64, 71 cases). In the more agitated half they are 0.007 (t 0.12, 55 cases) and 0.24 (t 1.56, 40 cases).
- F6: Scaling the 30-day return by the coin's 30-day daily swing (times √30) strengthens the choppy-market slope to t 4.84 on 215 cases, against t 3.81 for the raw 30-day return.
