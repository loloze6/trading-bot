Idea: In a choppy market, the larger a coin's fall over the past 30 days, the more it keeps falling over the next 7 days.
Consequence: After a weak month in a trendless market, a trader takes a short sized by the month's fall, or refuses a long bought as a range bounce.
Why:
- F1: In choppy markets, a coin's 30-day return predicts its next 7 days, but only on the falling side. On the offset-0 7-day grid of folds B, C, the slope of excess return on a negative 30-day return is 0.38 (t 3.93, 111 cases). The slope on a positive 30-day return is -0.07 (t -0.81, 104 cases). This explains why the tested long-only idea was inconclusive (slope t 0.86).
- F2: In choppy markets, after a negative 30-day return the coin falls 5.1 % below its normal 7-day drift on average. The t is -2.53 with the cases of one day summed, over 111 cases on 54 days. Every one of the 12 coins with cases shows a negative mean, from -1.3 % (BTC) to -10.6 % (AAVE, DOGE). Every year is negative too, from -3.1 % (2019) to -7.7 % (2021).
- F3: The graded effect holds at the ends of the choppy 30-day quintiles. The weakest-month fifth is followed by -9.4 % excess over 7 days (t -4.95, 43 cases) and the second fifth by -4.4 % (t -2.46). The three upper fifths sit between +0.5 % and +2.3 % (all t under 1.3).
- F4: The negative-side slope keeps its sign on all 7 grid offsets: t 3.93, 1.13, 0.80, 1.04, 3.36, 2.65 and 2.20, on 97 to 122 cases each. Four of the seven offsets pass t 2. It also survives leaving out 2018: slope 0.41, t 3.97, 95 cases.
