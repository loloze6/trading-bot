Idea: A coin whose day closes near the top of its day's range keeps rising over the next 7 days, and the higher the close sits in the day's range, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader leans long for the following week after days that close near their high, and refuses longs, or leans short, after days that close near their low.
Why:
- F5: Over the next 7 days, close location points the same way. A day closing high in its range is followed by a 7-day move above drift: slope +0.42 usual moves per unit of close location, t 4.20 on 1,317 grid cases over 218 days, not clustered by day. The slope is positive on all 10 coins, with a day-clustered t of 2.03 on BTC (204 days) and 2.04 on ETH (203 days). The same measure over 3 days shows nothing (t 0.13), and the 7-day grid's weekdays are tied to windows (Fridays come from only 2 windows, mean z +0.30). The pattern may therefore be partly window-specific, and it is the only one in this exploration that stands near the bar set by the many angles examined.
- F4: Over the next day, where a coin closes in its day's range points the other way. A lower close is followed by a higher next day: slope -0.21, day-clustered t -3.00, 9,903 cases on 1,448 days. The same holds for the day's return: t -3.13, and t -2.80 in a falling 60-day trend. The effect lives entirely on wider-than-median days (t -3.83 on 1,192 days, against +0.12 on narrow days). This is the wide-low-close next-day rebound that the map already holds in the agitated and falling-agitated long cells, so it is not a discovery.
