A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the more the coin rises over the next day.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader can concentrate rebound buying in a falling, agitated market, where capitulation days unwind most, and refuse it in a falling calm one.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling agitated market.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 031-03_fading-volume-after-rise-reverses_1d (idea 031-03_fading-volume-after-rise-reverses, brief 031_medium-term-overreaction-reversal).
What it rests on:
- F3: The rebound after a fall of at least 2 swings is concentrated in a falling, agitated market, at +7.94 % next day (t 4.11, 19 cases). In a falling calm market it is -0.71 % (t -0.77, 47 cases), and in a rising calm market +2.38 % (t 2.21, 20 cases). The counts are small, and agitation here is the 30-day swing over the 120-day swing, not the profile's one-year percentile.
- F2: After a fall of at least 2 swings, the next day's return is +2.44 % on average (t 4.67, 194 cases). The t counts each coin's fall as a separate case, but the falls land on only 79 distinct days, so the evidence is thinner than the t suggests. The effect is broad: it is positive on every coin (+1.87 % to +3.32 %), in every year from 2018 to 2021 (+0.79 % to +4.95 %), and in 9 of 12 windows. Falls that close in the bottom fifth of the day's range rebound more (+2.69 %, 113 cases) than those closing above the middle (+1.38 %, 8 cases).
