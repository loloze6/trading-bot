A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: The larger a coin's 20-day return against its usual daily swing over those 20 days, the more the coin keeps moving the same way over the next 5 to 10 days.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader should follow a coin's volatility-scaled 20-day move for the following week or so rather than fade it.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 031-03_fading-volume-after-rise-reverses_1d (idea 031-03_fading-volume-after-rise-reverses, brief 031_medium-term-overreaction-reversal).
What it rests on:
- F4: Against the brief's reversal, a coin's volatility-scaled 20-day move goes on in its own direction over the next 5 to 10 days. The slope is 0.0106 at 5 days (t 3.56, 1,345 cases) and 0.0185 at 10 days (t 3.08, 665 cases). The top fifth leads the bottom fifth by 3.3 % (5 days) and 5.7 % (10 days). At the brief's own scale (a 60-day move) the same lean is weaker (t 1.84 to 1.92), and at a 30-day horizon nothing holds (|t| ≤ 1.58 for 20 to 60-day moves).
- F7: The raw 20-day return's lean on the next 5 days changes sign across windows. It is negative in w7 to w9 (t -1.93 to -3.07) and mostly positive later (w15, w16, w18: t 0.88 to 1.21). So the trend in F4 holds only when the move is scaled by volatility, and not stably across time.
