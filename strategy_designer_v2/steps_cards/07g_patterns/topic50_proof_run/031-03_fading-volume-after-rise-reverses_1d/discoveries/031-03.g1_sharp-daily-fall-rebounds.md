A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: The larger a coin's one-day fall against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the more the coin rises over the next one to two days.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader can buy a coin's sharp one-day fall for a short rebound, sized by how extreme the fall was. The rebound is per case many times larger than a trade's cost, where the long-horizon idea had no usable size.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC.
Found by: line 7.g patterns, in backtest 031-03_fading-volume-after-rise-reverses_1d (idea 031-03_fading-volume-after-rise-reverses, brief 031_medium-term-overreaction-reversal).
What it rests on:
- F1: The larger a coin's one-day fall against its 30-day swing, the more it rises the next day: the slope of next-day excess return on the fall's size is 0.0080 (t 6.97, 3,506 down days). It is 0.0058 over the next 2 days (t 2.46, 1,743 non-overlapping cases), and it fades by 3 days (t 1.57) and 5 days (t 0.13). Up days show no such slope next day (t 1.12).
- F2: After a fall of at least 2 swings, the next day's return is +2.44 % on average (t 4.67, 194 cases). The t counts each coin's fall as a separate case, but the falls land on only 79 distinct days, so the evidence is thinner than the t suggests. The effect is broad: it is positive on every coin (+1.87 % to +3.32 %), in every year from 2018 to 2021 (+0.79 % to +4.95 %), and in 9 of 12 windows. Falls that close in the bottom fifth of the day's range rebound more (+2.69 %, 113 cases) than those closing above the middle (+1.38 %, 8 cases).
