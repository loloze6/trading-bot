A pattern found in a backtest, off the theme of the brief that backtest tested: a theory of its own, to be researched from its first ideas.

The pattern: In a falling market, a coin's up day that comes while its 3-day return is still negative is followed by a fall over the next day, and the wider that day's high-to-low range against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger the fall.
What it would mean for a trader: A trader fades wide bounces inside any 60-day decline, calm or agitated, for one day, and refuses the same fade when the coin's 60-day trend is rising.
Seen on: 1d bars of BTC, ETH, LTC, XMR, XRP, ZEC, in a falling market.
Found by: line 7.e profitable but not proven, in backtest 058-01.g2.g1.d1.d2_wide-range-bounce-fade-in-decline_1d (idea 058-01.g2.g1.d1.d2_wide-range-bounce-fade-in-decline, brief 058_falling-agitated-multiday-rallies-fail).
What it rests on:
- F2: Inside the book, the gain comes only from the widest bounces. The top range quartile of the cases earns 0.943 of the 1.197 book gain. Its cases fall by 2.72 % beyond drift the next day (t 3.43, 53 cases), against −0.76 % and −0.25 % for the two narrowest quartiles. The Spearman correlation of range ratio with the next-day unusual move is 0.26 (p 0.0001).
- F3: The effect is not specific to the falling agitated profile; it lives in a falling 60-day trend.
- F4: In the falling trend, the two widest quartiles hold up without the dominant window and across all coins.
- F5: Outside the falling agitated profile, the same situation still gives back 0.47 % the next day (t 3.09, 978 cases). Inside the profile the figure is 0.68 % (t 1.67, 209 cases), so the profile filter shrinks the sample more than it sharpens the effect. This is why the plan's t stayed at 1.93.
