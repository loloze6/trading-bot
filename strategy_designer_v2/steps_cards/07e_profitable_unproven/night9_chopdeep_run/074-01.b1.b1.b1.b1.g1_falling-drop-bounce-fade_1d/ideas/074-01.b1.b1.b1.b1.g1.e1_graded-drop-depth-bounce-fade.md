Idea: In a falling market, a wide up day is followed by a fall over the next day, and the deeper the coin's fall over the 5 days before that up day, measured in its usual 5-day moves, the larger that next-day fall.
Consequence: A trader should sell for one day into wide bounces in a downtrend in proportion to how deep the preceding 5-day drop was, with the largest short after the steepest drops and little or none after a flat or rising week.
Why:
- F1: The supporting check failed because the forecast saturates, not because the grading is absent. 76 % of the 99 active falling-market cases sit at the cap of -20, with only 25 distinct forecast values, so the larger-size half came out empty and its difference was None. - F2: Over all 390 wide up days in falling markets, with no threshold on the prior fall, the next-day short unusual move grows with the depth of the prior 5-day fall (Spearman -0.117 between the scaled fall and the move):
- F5: The fade lives in falling markets.
- F6: Within falling markets, the 32 qualifying cases the book left flat show -0.25 %, while the 99 it shorted show +2.37 % (t 4.47). These flat cases are the ones whose fall was shallow against its own 60-day history, where the standardised forecast was clipped to 0. The sizing does pick the stronger cases; the failure is that it cannot rank among them once capped.
