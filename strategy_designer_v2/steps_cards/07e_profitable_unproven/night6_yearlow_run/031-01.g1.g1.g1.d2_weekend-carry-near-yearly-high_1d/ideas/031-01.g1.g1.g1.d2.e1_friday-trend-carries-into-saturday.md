Idea: The stronger a coin's 14-day return at its Friday close (UTC), the more it rises over the following Saturday (the next 24 hours), beyond its normal drift.
Consequence: A trader would hold a Saturday long sized by the coin's two-week gain into Friday's close, and refuse the weekend long after a two-week fall.
Why:
- F1: The book's gain is the 14-day trend at Friday's close, not nearness to the yearly high: on all Fridays, the slope of the next-day excess return on the 14-day return is 0.0405 (t 3.35, 2136 cases, 366 days), while on every other weekday it lies between -0.012 and +0.014 (|t| ≤ 1.13), and over all days together it is 0.0023 (t 0.47).
- F3: The weekend move is all Saturday: after up-trend Fridays the Saturday-only excess is +0.69 % (t 3.19, 1055 cases, 281 days) against a two-day excess of +0.81 % (t 3.08), and the Sunday return's slope on the 14-day return is 0.0004 (t 0.04) against 0.0405 for Saturday. The book's mean daily return is +0.245 % on Saturday-dated days and +0.078 % on Sunday-dated days.
- F4: The grading by trend strength is monotone in its upper part: Friday two-day excess by 14-day-return quintile is -0.58 %, -0.59 %, +0.61 %, +0.54 %, +1.16 % (top quintile t 2.50). Down-trend Fridays give -0.35 % (t -1.20). The 7-day return grades less cleanly (top quintile +0.90 %, t 2.05; others between -0.29 % and +0.25 %).
- F5: The gain is not the market's own rise, one coin or a few trades. The market part of the book's mean bar gain is 0.0115 % of 0.0508 % (about 23 %). All six coins have positive case excess (+0.23 % BTC to +1.22 % XRP). The top 10 of 1777 trades carry 17.5 % of the summed net profit; 62 % of trades win, with a median of +0.67 %.
