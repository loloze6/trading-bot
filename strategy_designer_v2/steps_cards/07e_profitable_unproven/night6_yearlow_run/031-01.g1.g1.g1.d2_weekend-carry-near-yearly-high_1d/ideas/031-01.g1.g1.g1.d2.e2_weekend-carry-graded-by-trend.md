Idea: Among coins that closed Friday (UTC) after a 14-day rise, the larger that rise, the more the coin rises over the next two days (Saturday and Sunday together), beyond its normal drift, whatever its distance from the yearly high.
Consequence: A trader would size the weekend carry by the two-week trend rather than by nearness to the yearly high.
Why:
- F1: The book's gain is the 14-day trend at Friday's close, not nearness to the yearly high: on all Fridays, the slope of the next-day excess return on the 14-day return is 0.0405 (t 3.35, 2136 cases, 366 days), while on every other weekday it lies between -0.012 and +0.014 (|t| ≤ 1.13), and over all days together it is 0.0023 (t 0.47).
- F2: Nearness to the yearly high adds little inside the regime: the tested slope has t 0.99, and the recomputed distance-below-high slope among up-trend Fridays is -0.0015 (t -0.06, 290 cases); the nearness quintiles of the cases run +0.40 %, +0.30 %, +1.02 %, +1.16 %, +1.16 % with t from 0.70 to 2.72.
- F4: The grading by trend strength is monotone in its upper part: Friday two-day excess by 14-day-return quintile is -0.58 %, -0.59 %, +0.61 %, +0.54 %, +1.16 % (top quintile t 2.50). Down-trend Fridays give -0.35 % (t -1.20). The 7-day return grades less cleanly (top quintile +0.90 %, t 2.05; others between -0.29 % and +0.25 %).
