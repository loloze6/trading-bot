Idea: In a choppy, calm market, the nearer a coin's close sits to the top of its 20-day high-low range, the larger its rise over the next 5 days, and the nearer to the bottom, the larger its fall.
Consequence: In a coin that has neither trended nor swung for weeks, a close near a 20-day edge is a reason to trade in that edge's direction, not against it as range traders expect.
Why:
- F3: In the calm profile, the next 5 days follow the close's place in its 20-day range on the continuation side, rising and graded at the edges: the mean excess 5-day return is −2.41 % (t −3.14, 163 cases) for the bottom fifth of the range, −2.38 % (t −3.78, 193) for the next, −0.17 % (t −0.24, 173) at the middle, +0.68 % (t 1.13, 198) and +4.40 % (t 4.40, 177) at the top edge, so the brief's reversion is not there and its mirror, continuation, is.
- F5: The edge continuation is stronger in calm bars than in the other bars of the same coins: the top-edge excess 5-day return is +4.40 % in calm against +1.78 % otherwise, and the bottom edge −2.41 % in calm against +0.28 % otherwise.
- F6: Inside calm, the grading is steepest in a choppy 60-day trend, the brief's own choppy calm profile: excess 5-day return −6.38 % (11 cases) at the bottom edge, −3.33 % (28), −1.80 % (36) at the middle, +2.82 % (26), +7.76 % (25) at the top edge; the forecast-signed move there is +4.43 % on longs (t 2.72, 60) and +3.44 % on shorts (t 3.29, 66). In a falling trend the top half and the middle earn alike (+2.3 % each), so there is no grading.
