Idea: In a coin whose price has gone nowhere over the past 60 days, a daily close above its prior 60-day high is followed by a further rise over the next 10 days. The narrower the prior 60-day high-low range is as a share of the price, the larger that rise.
Consequence: A trader should join upside breaks out of a quiet two-month range over about ten days, and more so the tighter that range is against the price.
Why:
- F3: Recomputed on folds B and C, upside breaks out of flat 60-day ranges peak at 10 days, not the plan's 20. The mean unusual move is +9.2 % at 10 days (t 2.00, 58 cases), +5.7 % at 20 days (t 1.32, 39 cases) and −1.7 % at 40 days (19 cases).
- F4: The idea's comparison holds. Breaks above the 60-day high in flat coins (ER ≤ 0.3) beat the same breaks in trending coins: at 10 days +9.2 % (t 2.00, 58 cases) against +2.7 % (t 0.58, 31 cases); at 20 days +5.7 % against +2.0 % (t 0.32, 22 cases).
- F5: The width of the prior 60-day high-low range as a share of price grades the rise; this width is not the tested compression against 180 days. The 20-day unusual move is +19.4 % in the narrowest tercile, −0.6 % in the middle and −1.6 % in the widest (Spearman −0.36, 39 cases). The book's forecast grades it only weakly (Spearman 0.12, terciles +3.3 %, +5.3 %, +8.6 %), consistent with step 5's failed supporting check (−0.034).
