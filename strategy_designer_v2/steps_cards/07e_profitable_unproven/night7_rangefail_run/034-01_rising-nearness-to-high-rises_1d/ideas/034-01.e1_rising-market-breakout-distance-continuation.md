Idea: In a rising market, the further a coin's daily close stands above its 20-day high, measured in units of its daily swing over the last 20 days, the more it rises over the next 3 days.
Consequence: In a rising market, a trader buys strength on closes above the recent high, sizes up the further above it they are, and does not expect much from coins sitting below their high.
Why:
- F1: Almost all of the book's gain comes from its long side, and the shorts lose money. Long bars sum to +8.19 in summed daily gains (108 % of the total, mean +0.19 % a bar over 4296 bars). Short bars sum to −0.60 (−8 %). Long trades net +14,564 % in total with a median of +1.6 %. Short trades net −2,942 % with a median of −5.9 %.
- F2: Inside the long side, the gain sits mostly on bars where the close stands above its 20-day high. Measured in units of the 20-day daily swing, those bars carry 46 % of the total gain (mean +0.42 % a bar over 818 bars). Bars more than 2 swings below the high carry 9 % (mean +0.10 % a bar over 711 bars). Shorts more than 2 swings below the high lose −12 % of the total gain.
- F3: In the rising profile, the drift-removed return is not graded below the high. Over the next 5 days it is −0.43 % (t −0.29, 328 cases) beyond 2 swings below, then +1.45 %, +1.24 % and +1.33 % in the bands nearer the high, all with t under 1.4. Above the high it jumps to +4.58 % (t 2.41, 127 cases, 56 days), and to +8.8 % over 10 days (t 2.13, 63 cases).
- F4: The plan measured one straight slope over the whole forecast range at 5 days, and that blurs the effect. Its slope t was 1.64. Inside the rising profile, the slope of the 3-day drift-removed return on the distance above the high is 0.037 per swing (t 2.45, 244 cases). Below the high, the slope at 3 days is flat (t 0.45, 1236 cases).
- F5: The market's own rise explains little of the gain, so most of it is timing. The position times the coin's mean daily return is 0.0108 % a bar, against a mean gain of 0.0616 % a bar; that leaves 0.0508 % (82 %) as timing.
