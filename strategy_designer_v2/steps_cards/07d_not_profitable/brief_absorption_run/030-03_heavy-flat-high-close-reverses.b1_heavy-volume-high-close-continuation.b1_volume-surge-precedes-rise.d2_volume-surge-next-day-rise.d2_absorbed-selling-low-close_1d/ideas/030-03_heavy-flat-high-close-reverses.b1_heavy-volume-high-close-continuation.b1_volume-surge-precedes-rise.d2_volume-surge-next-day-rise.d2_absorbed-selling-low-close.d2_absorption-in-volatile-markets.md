Idea: The heavier a coin's volume on a day relative to its own prior 20-day average, the more the coin rises over the next day, the lower the day's close sits within its high-low range. This holds only while the coin's own 20-day volatility is high against its history, and the move is larger the higher that volatility.
Consequence: A trader buys heavy-volume low-close days only when the coin's 20-day volatility is elevated, and stays out in calm stretches where the signal earns nothing but carries the coin's falls.
Why:
- F1: The book failed on its falls, not on its gain. Its deepest fall is -48.1 %, over 310 days in w1-w3 (holding the coins lost -66.4 % then), against a yearly return of +24.2 %. Its daily returns correlate 0.82 with holding the coins, because a position is open on 97.9 % of coin-bars (the forecast is above 0 on 99.1 % of them).
- F2: The gain sits in the top fifth of the forecast, which earns +0.44 % next-day excess return per bar (n 3474), and partly in the fourth fifth (+0.01 %). The bottom three fifths earn -0.23 %, -0.09 % and -0.14 %, yet still hold allocations of 0.07, 0.20 and 0.36. The book is long the coin on weak-signal days that lose against its average.
- F6: The signal works in the coin's high-volatility tercile, with a top-two-fifths excess of +0.71 % (t 5.02, n 1710), and fails in the low-volatility tercile (-0.04 %, t 0.93, n 2148). High volatility alone, without the signal, earns -0.17 % excess (n 3139), so the condition is not an effect of its own. It fits the theory: absorbing a large order matters when the day's flow is large against the book.
- F7: The effect lives in the next day only. The top-two-fifths excess is +0.23 % at 1 day (t 3.68, n 6952), +0.16 % over 3 days (t 0.89, n 2289) and -0.04 % over 5 days (t -0.13, n 1368), so a longer holding gives nothing.
