Idea: In a falling, calm market, a coin that has risen over the last 7 days keeps rising over the next 3 days, and rises further the larger its 7-day rise is in units of its 30-day daily-return swing, so that the largest scaled rises carry the most.
Consequence: A trader sizes the long by the scaled weekly rise, so that the many small rises carry little weight in the book.
Why:
- F3: The weakest scaled rises lose. On the 3-day grid in profile, signal cases in the lowest third of the scaled 7-day rise (0.00 to 0.34 usual moves) had an unusual move of -1.1 % (27 cases). The middle third had +1.8 % (27) and the top third +2.2 % (28). In the book, held bars with a scaled rise of 0 to 0.4 summed -0.070 (66 bars, -0.11 % a bar), while those above 1.0 summed +0.319 (77 bars, +0.41 % a bar).
- F4: The signal is the effect, not the profile. In the falling calm profile, grid bars with no signal had an unusual 3-day move of -1.37 % (149 cases), against +1.24 % with the signal (106 cases, strength 0.134, t 1.96). So staying in the profile without the rise loses.
- F1: The Calmar failure (0.20 against 0.75) comes from one long, slow fall: the book's deepest drop was -19.9 %, lasting 612 days from a peak in w7 to a trough in w13, through w8 (book -11.0 % summed, while the coins summed -73.8 %), w9 (-0.8 %), w11 (-0.3 %) and w12 (-0.8 %); w8's trades summed -38.6 % net, and its worst single trade lost -14.2 %.
- F2: The fall is made of short, weak trades. The 110 trades held up to 3.5 days lost -0.41 % net on average (-44.7 % summed), and the 60 held 3.5 to 7 days earned only +0.34 %. The 30 trades held 7 to 14 days earned +14.4 % each, and the 5 held over 14 days earned +41.6 % each. A trade lasts as long as the 7-day rise persists, so the gain sits in the rises that keep going.
