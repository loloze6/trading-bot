Idea: In a falling market, the more the coin's trading volume over the past 30 days has grown against the 30 days before, and the deeper its 10-day fall, the larger its rise over the next 5 days. That rise comes in the first 5 days after volume growth meets the fall and is spent by then, held for 5 days from the signal only.
Consequence: A trader buys the capitulation rebound for 5 days and closes it, instead of staying long while volume stays high in a fall that goes on.
Why:
- F1: The failing measure is Calmar (0.33 against 0.75). Most of the book's deepest fall, -19.9 % over 56 days, sits inside window w14, where the book lost 6.4 % in sum while every coin lost: BTC -24.8 %, ETH -21.8 %, XMR -28.9 % and ZEC -23.8 % of book return summed over that fall. No other window's deepest fall exceeds -6.7 %.
- F2: The losses come from positions held long after the signal. Trades of up to 5.5 days earn +7.03 % net (275 trades) and trades of 5.5 to 10.5 days earn +3.07 % (135). Trades of 10.5 to 20.5 days lose -6.90 % (69, worst -30.2 %) and trades over 20.5 days lose -18.58 % (18). The trades average 6.7 days although the plan's horizon is 5.
- F3: The rebound is spent in the first 5 days of a position. On the 5-day grid, in-position cells aged up to 5 days gain +3.19 % over the next 5 days (122 cells). Cells aged 5 to 10 days gain +1.02 % (69), and cells aged 10 to 20 days lose -1.25 % with 60 % negative (40). The over-20-day group (+10.5 %, 14 cells) rests on one +100.8 % case in w14.
- F4: The forecast keeps the book long for weeks in deep falls. In w14 the in-position cells had a 60-day return of -46 % and a volume log-ratio of 0.96, against -26 % and 0.27 in the other windows. Their median 5-day return was -4.3 % against +2.3 %, and 39 of w14's 62 in-position cells were older than 5 days.
