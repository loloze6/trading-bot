Idea: In a falling market, the more the coin's trading volume over the past 30 days has grown against the 30 days before, the larger its rise over the next 5 days. The deeper its 60-day fall has been, the larger that rise, held for 5 days from the signal.
Consequence: A trader sizes the capitulation rebound by how deep the 60-day fall has gone, and takes only small bets when the fall is shallow.
Why:
- F3: The rebound is spent in the first 5 days of a position. On the 5-day grid, in-position cells aged up to 5 days gain +3.19 % over the next 5 days (122 cells). Cells aged 5 to 10 days gain +1.02 % (69), and cells aged 10 to 20 days lose -1.25 % with 60 % negative (40). The over-20-day group (+10.5 %, 14 cells) rests on one +100.8 % case in w14.
- F5: The effect is graded by fall depth. Fresh positions (age up to 5 days) in the deepest 60-day-fall tertile (-62 % to -37 %) gain +4.22 % over 5 days, against 0.00 % and -0.26 % in the other two tertiles (22 cells each). Across all in-position cells, the deepest 10-day-fall tertile gains +4.68 %, against +1.72 % and +0.64 %.
- F6: A deep 60-day fall is not an effect of its own. In the falling profile, cells with a 60-day fall below -30 % but without the signal (volume grown and 10-day return negative) earn -0.60 % over 5 days (145 cells). With the signal they earn +3.97 % (67 cells). The depth belongs to the idea's capitulation theory: a deeper fall clears more sellers.
