Idea: In a choppy, calm market, the larger a coin's one-day rise against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, when that rise closes the coin at its highest close of the past 20 days, the further it keeps rising over the next 5 days. A rise of the same size that ends well below the 20-day high continues much less.
Consequence: A trader sizes a calm-market breakout by the day's rise in swing units times its closeness to the 20-day high, so a near-high day with a small rise, or a large bounce far below the high, gets little or nothing.
Why:
- F3: Closeness to the 20-day high works as a multiplier of a large rise, not as a separate effect. In choppy markets, a rise above one swing that closes within 1 % of the 20-day closing high earns +14.97 % in excess over 5 days (n 8, t 2.39, strength 1.21). The same large rise further below the high earns +2.78 % (n 10, t 1.16). Being at the high with a rise of at most one swing earns -0.14 % (n 14, t -0.06), so the closeness condition alone is not an effect. Inside the profile, large rises at the high give +10.39 % (n 11, t 2.04). The equal-weight mean of the two components lets a near-high small rise trade on its own.
- F1: Inside the choppy-calm profile, the 5-day move after an up day is not graded the way the forecast assumes. On the 5-day grid, the excess return is -0.88 % after rises of 0 to 0.5 swing units (n 43), -2.67 % after 0.5 to 1 unit (n 24, t -1.64), +8.72 % after 1 to 1.5 units (n 11) and +3.72 % above 1.5 units (n 11, t 1.98). Rises above one 30-day swing earn +6.22 % in excess (total +6.92 %, n 22, t 2.17, strength 0.50). Rises below one swing lose. This is why the idea's size per case is -0.43 % (t -1.15, 651 cases) and its total move, +0.19 %, only equals the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F4: The book's falls come from these frequent weak trades, not from one bad window. The deepest fall of the chained daily book is -15.2 %, with its trough in w10. Falls of -14.0 % in w14, -14.4 % in w15 and -12.1 % in w9 show the same bleed spread across windows. Against a yearly return of +1.3 %, this gives a Calmar of 0.04. Large in-profile rises paid on every coin: their mean 5-day excess was +3.4 % to +19.1 % on all six coins (BTC +5.7 % n 4, XMR +3.4 % n 5, XRP +19.1 % n 3). XMR's trades still lost -1.46 % net on average, so its losses come from the small-rise entries.
