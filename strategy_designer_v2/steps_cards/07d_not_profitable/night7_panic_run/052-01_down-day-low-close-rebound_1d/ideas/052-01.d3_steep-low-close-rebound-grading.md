Idea: After a down day, a coin rises over the next day, the rise growing more than in proportion as that day's close nears its low, as a share of the day's high-low range, so that closes at the very low carry most of the rebound.
Consequence: A trader weights low-close down days far more heavily than mid-range ones, and puts little at risk on down days that recovered toward their high.
Why:
- F4: In a simple book (cost 0.19 % per trade), trading only agitated down days cuts the deepest fall and lifts Calmar:
- F7: The grading is steep at the top. Across all down days the unusual move is -0.07 % (t -0.47) in the bottom tercile, +0.35 % (t 2.34) in the middle and +0.53 % (t 3.61) in the top. Yet the book held a 0.41 allocation in the bottom tercile, where it earned 0.05 % a day. Squaring the size shrank the deepest fall from -42.4 % to -36.0 % at about the same Sharpe (0.73 vs 0.71).
- F8: Volatility targeting works against the effect. The top tercile earns more in high-volatility days (+0.88 %, t 3.05) than in low-volatility days (+0.27 %, t 1.10), but the book's allocation there was 0.52 against 1.01. A volatility-scaled agitated book drops to a mean of 0.013 % a day and Calmar 0.52.
