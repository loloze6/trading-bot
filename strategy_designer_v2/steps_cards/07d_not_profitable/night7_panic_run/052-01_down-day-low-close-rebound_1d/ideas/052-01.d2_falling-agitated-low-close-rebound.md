Idea: In a falling, agitated market, after a down day, a coin rises over the next day, more so the nearer that day's close sits to its low, as a share of the day's high-low range.
Consequence: Forced selling into a falling, agitated coin leaves its largest rebound owed when the day closes at its low, so that is where the long pays most for its risk.
Why:
- F2: The effect lives in agitated markets (30-day swing above the past year's median). Among agitated down days, the top tercile (close nearest the low) is followed by an unusual next-day move of +1.43 % (t 4.09, 321 cases). Among calm down days the top tercile gives -0.11 % (t -0.44, 406 cases), and the calm middle tercile gives +0.11 % (t 0.54).
- F3: Agitation alone does not earn and is not the effect. On all agitated bars the unusual next-day move is -0.09 % (t -0.64, 1965 bars). On agitated up days it is -0.71 % (t -4.13, 1003 bars). Against these, agitated down days closing nearest the low give +1.43 %. Agitation belongs to the idea's own theory: forced selling, from stops and margin calls, is what an agitated market produces, so more of the rebound is left owed.
- F5: The strongest case is a falling, agitated market (60-day return below -10 %). Its top-tercile down days give an unusual move of +2.10 % (t 4.22, 181 cases), against +0.03 % (t 0.09, 239 cases) for falling calm ones. A book restricted to it has Calmar 1.34, deepest fall -12.6 % and Sharpe 1.02. The falling trend alone does not earn: all falling bars give -0.14 % (t -1.28), and falling up days give -0.58 % (t -3.55).
- F9: Size against cost: the agitated top-tercile case moves +1.56 % over the next day, about 8 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade. The unrestricted idea's total move is +0.31 %, only 1.6 times that cost.
