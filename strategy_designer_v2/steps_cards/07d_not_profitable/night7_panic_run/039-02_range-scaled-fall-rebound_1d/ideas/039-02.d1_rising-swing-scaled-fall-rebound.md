Idea: After a down day, a coin rises over the next day, more so the larger that day's fall measured in units of its 30-day average daily high-to-low range, only while its daily swing over the last 10 days is larger than its swing over the last 60 days, the larger the fall the larger the rebound.
Consequence: A trader buys large range-scaled one-day falls only when the coin's short-term swing has risen above its usual level, and stays out of falls that come in a quiet stretch, which is where the book's deepest losses came from.

- F1: The Calmar failure comes from one fall. The book's deepest fall was -27.9 % over 120 days in windows w8 to w9 (w9 alone lost 18.8 %, with its worst day at -17.7 %).
