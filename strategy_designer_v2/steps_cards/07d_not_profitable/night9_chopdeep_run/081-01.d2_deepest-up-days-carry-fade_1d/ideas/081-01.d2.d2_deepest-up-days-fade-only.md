Idea: Except in a rising market, a day that closes up while sitting far below the coin's 20-day high, in its own 20-day daily swings, is followed by a fall over the next day. The fall is carried by the up days furthest below the high and grows with that depth, while up days near the high are followed by no fall.
Consequence: A trader keeps the short to the deepest up days outside rising markets and takes no position on shallow ones, whose expected move is below the cost of a trade.
Why:
- F1: The book loses in a rising market, and that is where the failed Calmar and the failed edge over holding come from. Its position bars earn -0.045 % per bar in a rising 60-day trend (766 bars), against +0.46 % in a falling trend (951 bars) and +0.50 % in a choppy one (312 bars). The deepest up days carry no fade in a rising market: the short-signed excess is +0.02 % (t 0.06, 238 cases) in rising calm and -0.14 % (t -0.25, 84 cases) in rising agitated.

- F6: The book's small positions lose to fees.
