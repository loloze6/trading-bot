Idea: In a falling market, a day that closes up while sitting deeper below the coin's 20-day high, measured in the coin's own usual daily swing over the last 20 days, is followed by a larger fall over the next day, the fall growing faster than the depth.
Consequence: A trader shorts deep up days only while the coin has been falling over the past two months, where the relief selling is strongest and the book's falls are shallowest.
Why:
- F1: The book loses in a rising market, and that is where the failed Calmar and the failed edge over holding come from. Its position bars earn -0.045 % per bar in a rising 60-day trend (766 bars), against +0.46 % in a falling trend (951 bars) and +0.50 % in a choppy one (312 bars). The deepest up days carry no fade in a rising market: the short-signed excess is +0.02 % (t 0.06, 238 cases) in rising calm and -0.14 % (t -0.25, 84 cases) in rising agitated.
