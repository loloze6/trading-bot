Idea: In a falling market, an up day on which the coin's 5-day return including that day is still negative is followed by a fall over the next day. The fall is larger the wider that day's high-to-low range is against the coin's average daily range over the past 20 days, and the further the coin is still down over those 5 days.
Consequence: A trader should sell for one day into every downtrend up day that has not recovered the past week's loss, betting more the wider the day and the deeper the week's loss, instead of waiting for the rare wide bounce that closes at its high.
Why:
- F1: The book fails on its rarity, not on its falls: the recorded situation fired on 35 coin-days in folds B and C (19 distinct days), the book held a position on 0.26 % of coin-bars, and it made 0.0034 trades per day. Its deepest fall was only -0.71 %, and its worst day was -0.52 %, so the Calmar of 0.71 is low because the yearly return is only +1.7 %, not because the falls are deep.
- F2: Five conditions in the situation make it rare. When the width and close-near-high thresholds are dropped, the situation becomes a falling market (60-day return below -10 %), an up day, and a 5-day return still negative. It then gives 598 cases on 191 days, with a next-day short-side unusual move of +1.19 % (t 2.66) and a 62 % hit rate. A rough book of it (a 1-day short, sized by depth against swing, 0.22 % cost) would trade on 4.9 % of coin-days, with a yearly return of +10.5 %, a deepest fall of -4.9 %, a Calmar of 2.15 and a Sharpe of 1.21.
