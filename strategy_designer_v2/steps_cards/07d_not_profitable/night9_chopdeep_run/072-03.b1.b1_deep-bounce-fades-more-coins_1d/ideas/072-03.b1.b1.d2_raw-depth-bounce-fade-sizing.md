Idea: In a choppy market, on a day that closed up, the further the close sits below the coin's 20-day high, in units of its 20-day daily-return standard deviation and not against its own recent depths, the larger its fall over the next 10 days, the fall growing from nothing just under the high to largest several swings below it.
Consequence: A trader should size the short on how many daily swings the bounce sits below the high, so that a bounce just under the top carries almost no short and a bounce several swings down carries the largest.
Why:
- F1: The book loses on shallow cases, and the deep cases do pay. On the 10-day grid of choppy up days, the coin's 10-day return minus its drift falls steadily with raw depth below the 20-day high: -11.5 % in the deepest quarter (t -4.95, 22 cases, mean depth -4.4 swings), -6.6 % in the second (t -1.91, 22), +2.9 % in the third (t 1.12, 21, mean depth -0.85 swings) and +19.1 % in the shallowest (22). Across all 87 cases the Spearman correlation of depth with the move is 0.48 (p 3e-6).
- F2: On the book's own position bars, the gain (position × next-day return) is +0.38 % a bar in the deepest raw-depth quarter (sum +2.62 over 686 bars). It is -0.60 % a bar in the shallowest quarter (sum -4.14 over 685 bars) and slightly negative in the two middle quarters. The shallow shorts erase more than the deep shorts earn, which gives the failing Sharpe of -0.30 and the mean on position days of -0.05 %.
- F3: The shallow shorts come from the forecast being standardised over its own past 60 values. A close only 0.85 daily swings below the high still gets a forecast of -4. The signal fires on 100 % of the third depth quarter, and those cases then rise (+2.9 %). Across signal bars, the forecast's correlation with raw depth is only 0.90.
