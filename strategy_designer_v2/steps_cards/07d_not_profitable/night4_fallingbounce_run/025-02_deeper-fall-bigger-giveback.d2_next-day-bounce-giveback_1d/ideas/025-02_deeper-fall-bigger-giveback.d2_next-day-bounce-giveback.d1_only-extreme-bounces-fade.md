Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is negative and that has just had a one-day rise of several usual daily moves, the larger that rise in usual daily moves, the larger its fall over the next day, with the fall growing much faster than the rise, so that rises of about one usual daily move are followed by almost no fall and only rises of well beyond one usual move give back a large part of themselves.
Consequence: A trader should fade only the rare, very large bounces in falling coins, for one day, and leave ordinary up days alone, because their fade does not cover a trade's cost.
Why:
- F1: The book loses because its fees exceed its gross gain. The position's summed gain before costs is 0.628 and its summed fees are 1.514, 2.4 times as much, over a turnover of 1588 (0.095 % fee per unit traded). The 1883 short trades average -1.12 % before fees and -1.22 % after.
- F2: The gain sits only in the largest bounces, and the small ones carry the fees.
- F3: In coins with a negative 90-day return, the excess next-day fall is close to zero after ordinary rises.
- F5: The book stays short after the effect is gone, which inflates fees and losses.
