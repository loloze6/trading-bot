Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is negative, after a one-day rise of several usual daily moves followed by a falling day, the larger that falling day in usual daily moves, the larger the coin's rise over the following day.
Consequence: After the giveback of a sharp bounce in a falling coin, a trader should expect a rebound the next day rather than a continued fall, and refuse to stay short.
Why:
- F6: After a sharp bounce in a falling coin, a next day that falls is followed by a rise the day after. When that day's move is below -1 usual move the coin then rises 2.23 % (n 41); when it is between -1 and 0 usual moves it rises 1.41 % (n 122). The book is flat on those days.
- F5: The book stays short after the effect is gone, which inflates fees and losses.
