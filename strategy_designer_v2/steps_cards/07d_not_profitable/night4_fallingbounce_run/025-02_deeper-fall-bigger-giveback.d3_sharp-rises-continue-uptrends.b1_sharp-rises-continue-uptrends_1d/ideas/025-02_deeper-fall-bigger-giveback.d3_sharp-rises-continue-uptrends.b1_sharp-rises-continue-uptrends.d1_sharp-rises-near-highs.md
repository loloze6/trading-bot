Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is positive, the larger the day's rise measured against the standard deviation of its last 20 daily returns, the larger its rise over the next 10 days, only while the coin closes near its highest close of the past 60 days. The closer it closes to that high, measured in its usual 20-day moves, the larger the rise that follows; the further below it, the more the rise is given back.
Consequence: A trader should take the continuation of a sharp rise only in a coin trading near its 60-day high, and refuse it in a coin still deep below its recent high, where the book's falls came from.
Why:
- F1: The book fails on its falls, not on its gain. Its deepest fall is -51.0 % against a yearly return of +25.4 %, which gives a Calmar of 0.50 against the required 0.75. The next deepest falls are -44.8 %, -36.2 %, -32.4 % and -29.5 %, and in each one the coins themselves fell 42 %, 46 %, 76 %, 40 % and 39 % (w15–w17, w1–w6, w12–w15, w18–w19, w21–w22).
- F3: How far the coin sits below its 60-day high, in usual 20-day moves, separates winning long bars from losing ones.
- F5: The book loses in recovering-but-damaged coins, mainly by holding positive 90-day-return coins that are already deep below their recent high.
