Idea: In a coin closing far below its highest close of the past 60 days, the larger a one-day rise in usual daily moves, the larger its fall over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A sharp bounce in a coin deep below its 60-day high is a reason to be short, or to refuse a long.
Why:
- F2: Over 10 days, the cases sort steadily by the coin's distance below its 60-day high (correlation of unusual move with that distance 0.124, 819 cases).
- F3: The size of the rise works in opposite directions depending on the distance from the 60-day high.
- F6: The book's worst 5 % of days fall slightly nearer the high than its average held bar (distance below the 60-day high −10.9 % against −14.9 %; 24.5 % of those days below −16 % against 35.4 % of all held bars). The falls are not confined to deep drawdowns, and removing far-from-high exposure is a gain-per-risk repair rather than a certain fix of the deepest fall.
