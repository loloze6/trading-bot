Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is positive, the larger a one-day rise in usual daily moves, the larger its rise over the next 10 days, and the closer the coin closes to its highest close of the past 60 days, the larger that continuation.
Consequence: A sharp up day is a reason to be long when the coin is near its 60-day high, and gives less reason the further below that high it closes.
Why:
- F2: Over 10 days, the cases sort steadily by the coin's distance below its 60-day high (correlation of unusual move with that distance 0.124, 819 cases).
- F3: The size of the rise works in opposite directions depending on the distance from the 60-day high.
- F4: The book's held bars earn more, steadily, the closer the coin sits to its 60-day high.
- F1: The book fails on Calmar alone (0.39 = a yearly return of 23.0 % over a deepest fall of 58.5 %, Sharpe 0.68). The deepest fall ran 747 days across w15 to w21 while the coins rose 31 %. The next two falls were 46.9 % over w1 to w10 (coins −62 %) and 44.7 % over w12 to w15 (coins −55 %).
