Idea: When a coin's 24-hour return, read at an hourly close, is a drop of several times its 30-day average absolute daily return, the price rises over the next 1 to 3 days by more than the coin's normal drift, only while its 7-day return is a large rise; the larger the 7-day rise and the deeper the drop, the larger the rebound.
Consequence: A trader should buy a deep hourly dip inside a strong week-long advance and hold it for a few days, because the dip in a rising coin rebounds by several times the cost of a trade.
Why:
- F5: Most cases are too weak to pay. 4477 of 8426 cases are drops of 1 to 1.5 multiples, with +0.08 % excess (t 1.1), below the 0.19 % cost. Drops of 3 to 5 multiples give +1.56 % (t 4.3, 332 cases), so the pooled unusual move of +0.11 % is 0.6 times the cost.
- F8: The rebound is also larger after a strong 7-day rise. Drops in the top quarter of 7-day returns give +0.68 % (t 6.2, 2146 cases) and +1.12 % over 3 days (t 5.1). The two middle quarters give -0.16 % and -0.17 % (t -1.7 and -2.1).
