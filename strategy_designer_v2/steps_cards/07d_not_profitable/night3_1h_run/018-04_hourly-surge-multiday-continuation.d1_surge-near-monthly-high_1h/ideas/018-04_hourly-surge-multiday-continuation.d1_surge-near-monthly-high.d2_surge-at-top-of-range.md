Idea: When a coin's 24-hour return, read at an hourly close, first reaches several times its 30-day average absolute daily return on the upside, the price keeps rising over the next 5 days by more than the coin's usual 5-day return, only while the close stands in the top of its 30-day range: the higher the close within its 30-day low-to-high range at that hour, the larger the rise, whatever the size of the surge.
Consequence: A trader would size the surge by where it leaves the close in the month's range, not by how large the surge was, and would refuse surges that end below the month's top.
Why:
- F5: The effect is not graded by the size of the surge. Cases split by the 24-hour return in usual daily moves all have unusual moves between +0.25 % and +0.41 % (t below 0.9). The highest 24-hour-return quarter even has the worst average drawdown inside the hold (−8.7 %) and the most losses beyond 10 % (14.6 %).
- F6: The effect is concentrated at the very top of the 30-day range. Cases in the top 9 % of the range (545 cases) have an unusual move of +1.44 % (t 2.24). The other three quarters have −0.06 % to +0.32 %.
- F8: The idea is small but its trades pay. The unusual move is +0.38 % per case, 0.036 of the coin's usual 5-day move, which is 1.9 times the 0.19 % round-trip cost. The total move is +1.02 %, 5.3 times the cost. The book clears its costs; what it lacks is protection from the coins' shared bear spans.
