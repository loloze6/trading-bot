Idea: After an unbroken streak of rising four-hour periods whose gain is large relative to the coin's typical four-hour move over the past 30 days, the coin falls over the next three days, the more so the more the volume declined from the start of the streak to its end, only while the coin's 30-day return lies between a moderate fall and a slight rise.
Consequence: Short a fading-volume up-streak only when the coin's past month has been flat to mildly falling. Size the short by how much the volume faded, and refuse it when the past month rose strongly or collapsed.
Why:
- F1: The book was short on 99.4 % of bars, with a mean position of -1.09 of the book. It stayed short on 99.2 % of the bars where the forecast was 0, which is 49.9 % of all bars. Of its mean daily return of -0.22 %, -0.165 % is the market's own rise against a near-permanent short (the coin's mean daily return is +0.15 %), and only -0.058 % is timing.
- F2: The book's falls are the coin's rises. On its worst tenth of days the book lost 10.9 % while the coin rose 9.1 %, with the book short at -1.42. On its best tenth it gained 8.9 % while the coin fell 7.7 %.
- F4: The give-back depends on the coin's 30-day trend.
- F5: In the half of cases with the lower 30-day return, the volume fade grades the give-back. The unusual move rises from +0.28 % (t 1.09) in the least-faded quartile to +1.30 % (t 5.71, 1,136 cases) in the most-faded quartile. That top figure is about six times the cost of a trade.
- F7: The years follow the trend. The unusual move per case was positive in 2018 (+1.49 %, t 3.74), 2022 (+1.26 %, t 5.94) and 2025 (+0.63 %, t 3.42). It was negative in 2020 (-0.82 %, t -2.64), 2021 (-0.96 %, t -2.37) and 2023 (-0.42 %, t -2.71).
