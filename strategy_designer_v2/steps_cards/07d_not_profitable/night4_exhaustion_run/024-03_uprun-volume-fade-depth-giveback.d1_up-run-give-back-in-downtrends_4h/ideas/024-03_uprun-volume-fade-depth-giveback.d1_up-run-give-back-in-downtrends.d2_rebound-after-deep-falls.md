Idea: After a coin has fallen far over the past 30 days, an unbroken streak of rising four-hour periods is followed by a further rise over the next twelve days, the larger the coin's 30-day fall the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader would buy, not short, a strong four-hour up-streak that comes after a deep 30-day fall.
Why:
- F6: After the steepest 30-day falls, the rally keeps going rather than giving back. For cases in the quartile with the lowest 30-day return (mean −33.6 %, 626 cases), the coin rises +0.78 % over the next 18 bars and +2.94 % over the next 72 bars. In the next quartile (−15.4 %), the coin falls −0.58 % over 18 bars.
- F8: Across all cases, the short's total return over 6, 18, 36 and 72 bars is −0.12 %, +0.04 %, −0.12 % and −0.58 % (3,395 to 3,108 cases). In every case the move before the drift is removed is far below the cost of a trade.
- F4: The 2,928 short trades lose −1.18 % gross and −1.28 % net on average, with 34 % winners. Only the longest quarter of trades (about 734 h) gains (+10.4 % gross); the other three quarters lose −3.4 %, −7.0 % and −4.7 %.
