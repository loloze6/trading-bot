Idea: In a choppy, calm market, the larger a coin's rise over the past 7 days against its usual daily swing over the past 30 days, the larger its further rise over the next 14 days, only while its 30-day return is also positive.
Consequence: A trader should hold a quiet coin's weekly breakout for two weeks when the week extends a month that is already up, and pass on weekly rises that are only rebounds within a falling month.
Why:
- F5: The book's deep falls are market-wide losing spells, and every coin loses in them. In w14 the book drawdown was -32.8 % (book Sharpe -3.52), with 40 trades averaging -18.9 % net at a 7.5 % hit rate; BTC lost -11.6 % of book, ETH -6.7 % and LTC -6.8 %. w7 fell -19.9 % and w9 -19.1 %. The 15 worst trades were entered at a mean forecast of 16.2 but after a mean 30-day return of -3.1 %.
- F6: Among strong-rise trades (raw scaled rise above 0.5), a positive 30-day return separates winners from losers. Trades with a positive 30-day return average +8.84 % net and carry +1214 of the book's P&L (125 trades); those without average -1.42 % and lose -99 (84 trades). On the grid, a positive 30-day return without the signal earns only +1.2 % excess (t 0.16, 14 cases), so it is not an effect of its own. Strong cells with a positive 30-day return earn +13.5 % (t 2.26, 17 cases). The strong cells with a non-positive 30-day return are only 5 cases on 2 days, so the grid evidence on that side is thin.
- F2: Inside the profile, the excess return lives only in rises that are large against the coin's own usual weekly swing. On the 14-day grid, cells with a positive forecast and a raw scaled 7-day rise above 0.5 earn +22.1 % excess (t 2.20, 22 cases, 9 days). Cells with a positive forecast and a smaller raw rise give back -11.0 % (t -2.55, 20 cases, 17 days), and cells with no signal earn -3.2 % (t -0.81, 38 cases).
