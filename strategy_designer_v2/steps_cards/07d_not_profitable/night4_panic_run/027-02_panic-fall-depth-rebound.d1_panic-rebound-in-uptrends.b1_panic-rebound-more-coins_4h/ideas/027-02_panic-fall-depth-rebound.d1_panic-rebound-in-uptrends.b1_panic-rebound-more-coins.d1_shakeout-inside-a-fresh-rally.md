Idea: Among 24-hour stretches where a coin's range is several times its 30-day average and the coin falls, the larger the coin's 7-day return ending at that stretch, the higher its return over the next 14 days, only while the coin's 90-day return is positive.
Consequence: A trader buys a sharp shakeout that interrupts a fresh 7-day rise in a coin trending up over three months, and refuses one that follows a falling week.
Why:
- F2: The idea's grading by fall depth runs backwards: on 24-hour stretches with a range of at least 4 usual bar ranges and a fall, the shallowest quarter of falls (depth up to 1.05 usual 24-hour moves) is followed by a +3.06 % unusual 14-day move (t 2.76, 402 cases), and the two deepest quarters by -2.10 % (t -2.38, 401) and -1.96 % (t -1.52, 401); the book's trades show the same, +11.2 % for the shallowest quarter of 24-hour falls against +5.9 % for the deepest.
- F3: The coin's 7-day return before the stretch sorts the outcome sharply: in panic stretches the top quarter of 7-day returns (above +9 %) is followed by a +6.54 % unusual 14-day move (t 3.59, 410 cases), the two falling quarters by -3.37 % (t -4.52, 410) and -2.35 % (t -2.80, 411); the book's trades in the top quarter of 7-day return (above +23.8 %) earned +20.0 %, against +3.3 % to +7.1 % in the other three quarters.
- F6: Across the book's trades, the size of the 90-day rise grades the gain strongly: the top quarter of 90-day returns (above +110 %) earned +17.96 % per trade, against +2.3 % to +3.9 % in the three lower quarters (about 30 trades each), so the uptrend part of the idea carries the profit and the fall-depth part does not.
