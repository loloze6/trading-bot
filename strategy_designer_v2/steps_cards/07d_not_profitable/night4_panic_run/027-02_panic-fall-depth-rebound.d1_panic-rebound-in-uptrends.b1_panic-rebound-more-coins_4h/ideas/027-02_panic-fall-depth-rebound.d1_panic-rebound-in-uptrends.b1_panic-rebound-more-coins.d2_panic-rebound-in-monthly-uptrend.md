Idea: Among 24-hour stretches where a coin's range is several times its 30-day average, the deeper the coin's fall over that stretch, the higher its return over the next 14 days, only while the coin's 90-day return is positive and the more so the larger that 90-day rise, and only while the coin's 30-day return is also positive, the more so the larger that 30-day rise.
Consequence: A trader buys panics only when the coin's last month as well as its last three months rose, and refuses them once the month has turned down, which is where the book's year-long fall came from.
Why:
- F1: The book's deepest fall, -20.3 % over 364 days from window w12 to window w15, falls in windows w12–w18, where the book's 141 trades lost on average (-0.72 % with the 30-day return down, 80 trades; -1.93 % with it up, 61 trades), while the 396 trades in the other windows earned +10.9 % and +11.2 %; this is the stretch that holds Calmar at 0.748, just under its 0.75 bar.
- F4: The rebound comes with a rising 30-day trend at every range level: with a 30-day rise, stretches of range 4 to 5.5 usual bar ranges give +2.56 % (t 2.20, 459 cases) and 5.5 to 7.5 give +6.25 % (t 1.77, 149); with a 30-day fall, the same stretches give -2.48 % (t -4.06, 744) and -0.56 % (t -0.38, 156), and inside windows w12–w18 the 30-day-falling cases lose -4.97 % (t -3.39, 167).
- F6: Across the book's trades, the size of the 90-day rise grades the gain strongly: the top quarter of 90-day returns (above +110 %) earned +17.96 % per trade, against +2.3 % to +3.9 % in the three lower quarters (about 30 trades each), so the uptrend part of the idea carries the profit and the fall-depth part does not.
