Idea: Among 24-hour stretches where a coin's range is two or more times its 30-day average and the coin falls, the larger the coin's 30-day return before that stretch, the higher its return over the next 14 days.
Consequence: A trader buys the milder and more frequent volatility shakeouts in coins rising over the last month, which gives many more trades than buying extreme panics only.
Why:
- F4: The rebound comes with a rising 30-day trend at every range level: with a 30-day rise, stretches of range 4 to 5.5 usual bar ranges give +2.56 % (t 2.20, 459 cases) and 5.5 to 7.5 give +6.25 % (t 1.77, 149); with a 30-day fall, the same stretches give -2.48 % (t -4.06, 744) and -0.56 % (t -0.38, 156), and inside windows w12–w18 the 30-day-falling cases lose -4.97 % (t -3.39, 167).
- F5: The book misses the mean daily return bar (0.000428 against 0.0005) mainly because it is rarely in: it is long on only 10.6 % of bars, at a mean allocation of 0.94, because its trigger of 7.5 usual bar ranges leaves out the stretches of range 4 to 7.5 in rising coins (608 cases, F4), which carry the same rebound.
