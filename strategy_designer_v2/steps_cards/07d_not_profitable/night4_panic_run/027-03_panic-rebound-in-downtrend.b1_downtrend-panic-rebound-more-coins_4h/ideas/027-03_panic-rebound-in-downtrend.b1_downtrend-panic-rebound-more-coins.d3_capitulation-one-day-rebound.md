Idea: In coins whose 30-day return before the panic was negative, a 24-hour stretch with a range several times its 30-day average and a hard fall is followed by higher than usual returns over the next 1 day, and the weaker the coin's 7-day return before the stretch, the higher that return.
Consequence: A panic that ends a week-long slide offers the largest one-day rebound, which a trader should take and then leave, since the slide resumes over the following two weeks.
Why:
- F5: Panics coming after a weak 7 days give the largest 1-day premium but the worst 14 days. In the bottom tercile of the 7-day return, the excess is +0.77 % over 1 day (t 2.74) and -3.10 % over 14 days (t -4.07). The top tercile gives +0.04 % over 1 day and -3.01 % over 14 days.
- F1: The panic premium is short-lived. Over all 1975 cases, the excess return over the coin's normal drift is +0.35 % over 6 bars (24 h, t 2.98). It is +0.02 % over 18 bars (3 days, t 0.12), then turns negative: -1.59 % over 42 bars (t -5.75) and -2.17 % over 84 bars (t -4.88). The 14-day horizon the book bets on sits where the effect has already reversed.
- F6: The book's losses come in falling markets. On days in the lowest quartile of the 20-day coin return, the book loses -0.75 % a day while the coins lose -1.12 %. In the top quartile it earns +0.50 % against the coins' +1.33 %. This leaves its Sharpe 0.27 below holding's.
