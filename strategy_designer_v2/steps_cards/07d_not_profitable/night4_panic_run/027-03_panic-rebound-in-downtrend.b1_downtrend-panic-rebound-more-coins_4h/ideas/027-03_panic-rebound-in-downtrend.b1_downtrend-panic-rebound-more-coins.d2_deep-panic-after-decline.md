Idea: In coins whose 60-day return before the panic was negative, a 24-hour stretch with a range several times its 30-day average and a hard fall is followed by higher than usual returns over the next 7 days, and the more extreme the panic, the higher the return, the premium lasting longer the deeper the panic.
Consequence: Only the deepest panics after a 60-day decline justify holding the rebound for a week; shallower drops in such coins keep falling and are a reason to refuse the trade.
Why:
- F3: The premium is large and lasting only in the most extreme panics of coins whose 60-day return was negative. In that group's top tercile of panic depth (196 cases), the excess is +1.84 % over 1 day (t 4.14, strength 0.36), +2.07 % over 2 days (t 3.75), +1.74 % over 7 days (t 2.35) and +2.13 % over 14 days (t 1.54). The bottom tercile of the same group shows -1.59 % over 3 days (t -3.02) and -2.91 % over 7 days (t -3.41).
- F2: The book holds far past the premium. It made 3400 trades lasting 34.8 days on average, with a minimum holding of 84 bars. Its daily mean return of 0.047 % therefore mostly carries the coin's later fall, giving a deepest fall of -64.6 % on the chained book and a Calmar of 0.059.
