Idea: When a coin falls over a 24-hour stretch and its high-low range over that stretch is a large multiple of its average 24-hour range over the past 30 days, it earns more than its usual return over the next 24 hours, and the larger that range multiple, the higher the return. That return rises faster than in proportion to the depth of the 24-hour fall measured in the coin's usual 24-hour moves.
Consequence: A trader would put almost all of the book into the deepest panic falls, where the rebound is about five times the cost of a trade, and stay out of ordinary falls whose rebound fees consume.
Why:
- F3: The effect sits only in the strongest panics.
- F2: Fees ate 7.19 of the book's 17.14 summed bar gains, which is 42 %. In the forecast 5–20 bucket alone, fees were 3.56 against a gain of 9.95. This matches the size check: the unusual move per case, +0.19 %, is only 1.0 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F6: Positions held after the signal had faded added no gain but still carried market exposure.
