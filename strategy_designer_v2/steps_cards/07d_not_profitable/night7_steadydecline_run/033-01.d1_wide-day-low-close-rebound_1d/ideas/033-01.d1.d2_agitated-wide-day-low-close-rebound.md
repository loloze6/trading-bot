Idea: In an agitated market, the nearer a coin's daily close sits to that day's low, and the wider that day's high-low range against the coin's usual daily swing over the past 20 days, the larger the rise in its price over the next day.
Consequence: A trader buys wide-day closes pinned near the low when the coin has been swinging more than usual, where the rebound is several times a trade's cost.
Why:
- F5: The strong case is large enough to pay a trade. The idea's size over all cases is +0.09 % unusual move (t 0.81), half the 0.19 % cost of a trade. The top fifth's excess next-day return is +0.60 %, about 3 times that cost.
- F6: The effect lives in agitated markets. Here agitated means a 20-day swing above the coin's median, not the profile's exact definition. In agitated markets the top fifth returns +1.23 % the next day against +0.001 % for the other bars (t 4.25, 655 top cases). In calm markets it is +0.29 % against +0.03 % (t 1.47, 781 cases).
- F7: Agitation is not an effect of its own. Agitated bars outside the top fifth return +0.001 % a day, against +0.03 % for calm bars outside it. Agitation fits the idea's own theory: forced selling into the close pushes the price further from fair value when the coin is swinging hard.
