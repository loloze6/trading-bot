Idea: The nearer a coin's daily close sits to that day's high, and the wider that day's high-low range against the coin's usual daily swing over the past 20 days, the larger the fall in its price over the next day relative to its usual daily drift.
Consequence: A trader stops holding a coin long after a wide day closed near its high, and shorts it there, the mirror of buying the close pinned near the low.
Why:
- F1: The book stays long even where the idea says nothing, and those bars lose; this drives its falls. The forecast is never negative, so the allocation is 0.07, 0.17 and 0.29 in the coin's lowest three fifths of the forecast. On those bars the next day returns -0.21 %, -0.03 % and -0.22 % (excess -0.35 %, -0.16 %, -0.36 %; about 1,740 cases each). Only the top two fifths gain: +0.40 % and +0.74 % (excess +0.26 % and +0.60 %).
- F9: The mirror side is the brief's own and loses when held long. A low forecast (close near the high) is followed by an excess next-day return of -0.35 % in the lowest fifth. A proxy book that is short below the coin's median forecast and long above it has the highest Calmar of the proxies: 1.76, yearly 74.7 %, deepest fall -42.5 %.
