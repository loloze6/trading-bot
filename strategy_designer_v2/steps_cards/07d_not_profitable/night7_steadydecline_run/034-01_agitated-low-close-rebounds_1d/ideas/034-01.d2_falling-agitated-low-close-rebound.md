Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the nearer a coin's daily close sits to the day's low within its high-low range, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: A trader should take the close-location rebound only while the coin is in a falling, agitated stretch, and avoid it in rising markets where it loses.
Why:
- F5: By the coin's 60-day trend, the signed next-day excess return in agitated bars is strongest in falling markets: +1.17 % (n 745), with low-close long +1.33 % (t 4.50) and high-close short +1.00 % (t 3.74).
- F6: In rising markets the same reading gives only +0.09 % (n 769), and the book lost there, with a gross of -0.017 % per bar and -0.050 % on its shorts.
- F7: A falling trend alone is not the effect: in falling agitated bars the next-day excess return whatever the close is +0.20 % (t 0.99, n 738). This fits the theory that stop-outs and margin unwinds are concentrated in falling, agitated stretches.
- F9: The book's deepest falls came in w11-w14 (deepest -3.4 % to -5.0 %, three of those four windows also losing or flat), while it made only +3.0 % a year.
