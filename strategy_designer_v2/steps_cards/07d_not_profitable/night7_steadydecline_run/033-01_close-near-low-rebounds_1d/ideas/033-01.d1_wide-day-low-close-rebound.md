Idea: The nearer a coin's daily close sits to that day's low, and the wider that day's high-low range against the coin's usual daily swing over the past 20 days, the larger the rise in its price over the next day.
Consequence: A trader buys a close pinned near the low mainly after a wide day, when forced selling pushed the price furthest below fair value, and gives little weight to the same close after a narrow day.
Why:
- F3: The effect is about four times stronger after a wide day. Range is measured against the coin's 20-day swing, in terciles. The graded measure (close-from-high minus 0.5) × next-day excess is 0.00188 (t 5.39, 2656 bars) in the widest third, against 0.00044 (t 1.79) in the narrowest. Extreme low closes on wide days are followed by +1.01 % (t 3.25, 351 cases), about 5 times the cost of a trade.
- F4: Wide range alone carries only part of that move. Wide-range days that close away from either extreme are followed by +0.36 % (1870 bars), against +1.01 % when the close also sits near the low. A wide day is in the idea's own theory: the forced late selling pushed the price further from fair value. The high-close shorts barely change with range: +0.34 % narrow and +0.44 % wide.
- F2: The effect is graded and real on both sides. Next-day excess return rises from -0.42 % (t -3.04, 1554 bars) for closes 10-25 % from the high to +0.46 % (t 2.74, 1160 bars) at 75-90 % and +0.68 % (t 2.82, 526 bars) at 90-100 %. Closes near the low carry the larger move: the extreme low-close cases earn a total move of +0.68 % (848 cases), 3.6 times the 0.19 % trade cost. The extreme high-close shorts earn only +0.25 % (1329 cases), 1.3 times the cost.
- F1: The book loses its gain to sizing and to its short side. It held only 0.107 of the book on average and turned over 0.141 a bar, so it grossed 0.0343 % a bar against about 0.0134 % of costs a bar. Its long bars grossed 0.0555 % each (4324 bars) and its short bars only 0.0135 % (4312 bars), which is about the cost of turning them over. This is why the mean daily return on days in a position (4.7e-05) and the Calmar (0.078) fail.
