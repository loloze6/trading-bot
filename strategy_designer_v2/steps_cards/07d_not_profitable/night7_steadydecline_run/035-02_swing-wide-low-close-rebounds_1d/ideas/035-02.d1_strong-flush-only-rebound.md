Idea: The larger a day's high-to-low range is as a multiple of the standard deviation of the coin's daily returns over the past 20 days, and the nearer its close sits to the day's low, the larger the coin's rise over the next day, traded only on days that are both wide against that swing and closing in the lower part of their range, and out of the market on ordinary days.
Consequence: A trader holds the one-day rebound only after a real overshoot, sized by how far the day exceeds a typical one, and stays flat through the ordinary days that carried the book's falls.
Why:
- F1: The failing measure is Calmar: 0.58 against the 0.75 required, from a deepest fall of -39.7 %. The fall sits in w14, where the book lost -0.13 % a day while holding lost -0.43 %. The book is in a position on 100 % of the coins' days, with a forecast above 0 on 98.9 % of bars. On the market's worst fifth of days the book lost -2.2 % a day against holding's -6.5 %, so the falls are the market's own falls carried by a book that is almost always long.
