Idea: In an agitated market, the wider a coin's day is against its usual daily range, and the nearer its close sits to that day's low, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: A trader would buy the close of wide days ending near their low only while the coin swings more than usual, when forced late selling is largest and the rebound is about four times what it is in calm markets.
Why:
- F2: The effect lives in the strong cases on the long side. Bars in the top fifth of |forecast| (forecast at or above 4.1) on the long side returned +0.96 % the next day (t 2.79 with cases of one day together, 891 cases on 344 days), which is +0.77 % after the 0.19 % trade cost. The top twentieth returned +1.40 % (t 3.02, 208 cases on 117 days).
- F3: The short side, the mirror case of a near-high close, does not carry the effect. In the top fifth, short bets returned +0.30 % (t 1.02, 847 cases), below the 0.19 % cost once slippage is counted. Over two days they lost (-0.45 % signed excess) while the long side kept +0.72 %. In a rising 60-day trend the short side lost -0.29 % (t -1.46, 836 cases).
- F6: The strong long cases pay in an agitated market and not in a calm one. Agitated means a 30-day swing above the coin's median. Strong long cases returned +1.19 % excess there (t 2.45, 332 cases) against +0.32 % in calm markets (t 0.81, 340 cases). The agitated state alone does not earn: all agitated bars returned -0.006 % (t -0.03), and weak-forecast long bets in it returned +0.30 % (t 1.10). Agitation fits the brief's theory, since forced late-day selling such as stops, liquidations and risk cuts is largest when the coin swings more than usual.
