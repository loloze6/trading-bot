Idea: When a coin's past 30 days have gone back and forth with little net progress compared with the sum of its daily moves, its 7-day move counted in 30-day daily swings tends to be followed by a move the same way over the next 7 days, only while that 7-day move points the same way as the coin's 30-day net move. The calmer the coin's last 30 days of daily swings compared with its last 90 days, the larger the following move.
Consequence: A trader should hold chop-continuation positions largest when the coin's recent swings are subdued against its longer past, and smallest when they have just flared up.
Why:
- F2: Long positions caused the fall. In w12 to w14, held long bars lost −0.19 % per day (683 bars, sum −1.31), while short bars gained +0.10 % per day (1026 bars, sum +1.01). Outside those windows, longs earned +0.35 % per day (2684 bars).
- F7: The signal works best when the coin's recent swings are calm compared with its longer past. By tercile of the ratio of 30-day to 90-day daily standard deviation, signal cases give +6.9 %, +3.8 % and +3.9 % (80, 83 and 54 cases). Without the signal the same terciles give +0.6 %, +2.9 % and −2.3 %, so a low ratio alone does not earn. Held bars by quartile of the ratio fall from +0.35 % to +0.18 % per day.
