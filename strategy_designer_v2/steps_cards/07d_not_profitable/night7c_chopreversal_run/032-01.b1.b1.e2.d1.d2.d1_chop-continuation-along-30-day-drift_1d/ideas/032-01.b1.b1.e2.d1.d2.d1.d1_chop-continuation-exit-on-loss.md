Idea: When a coin's past 30 days have gone back and forth with little net progress compared with the sum of its daily moves, its 7-day move counted in 30-day daily swings tends to be followed by a move the same way over the next 7 days, only while that 7-day move points the same way as the coin's 30-day net move and the past 30 days are still going back and forth at each day's close, held only as long as all three still hold at each day's close rather than for a fixed 7 days. The larger that scaled 7-day move, the larger the following move.
Consequence: A trader should close a chop-continuation position on the first close where the back-and-forth or the agreement of the 7-day and 30-day moves is gone, instead of carrying it through the following days.
Why:
- F1: Calmar is what failed (0.59 against 0.75). In folds B and C the book's deepest fall is −26.7 %. It ran 332 days, from w11 to w14, while the coins themselves fell −52.9 %. Windows w12, w13 and w14 hold three of the four deepest window falls (−21.7 %, −21.7 %, −26.7 %), and the book's summed daily returns there are −10.1 %, +3.1 % and −0.9 %.
- F2: Long positions caused the fall. In w12 to w14, held long bars lost −0.19 % per day (683 bars, sum −1.31), while short bars gained +0.10 % per day (1026 bars, sum +1.01). Outside those windows, longs earned +0.35 % per day (2684 bars).
- F3: Much of the loss comes from positions held after the chop situation has ended, which the 7-bar minimum holding forces. Held bars outside the chop regime earn about half as much as bars inside it: long +0.17 % against +0.29 % per day, short −0.07 % against +0.13 % per day, the shorts outside chop summing −1.03 over 1451 bars. In the falling windows, bars outside chop lost (−0.07 % per day, 756 bars) while bars inside chop still earned a little (+0.02 % per day, 953 bars).
