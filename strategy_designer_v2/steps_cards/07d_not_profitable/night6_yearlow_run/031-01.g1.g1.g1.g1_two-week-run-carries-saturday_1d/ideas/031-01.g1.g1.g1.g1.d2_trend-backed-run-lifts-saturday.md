Idea: While a coin's 60-day return is positive, the larger its 14-day rise measured in its usual daily moves at Friday's close (UTC), the more it rises over the following Saturday alone (the next 1 day).
Consequence: A trader would carry the strongest Saturday longs when the two-week run sits inside a rising 60-day trend, where the move per case is about six times the cost of a trade.
Why:
- F1: The book's Calmar failure comes from its short side: in a rebuild of the Saturday book by side, the deepest fall lasted 97 Saturdays and lost 12.8 %, of which shorts lost 12.2 % and longs 0.6 %. Over the whole period the short-only part returned −2.2 % a year with a −21.3 % deepest fall, while the long-only part returned +11.3 % a year with a −9.7 % deepest fall (Calmar 1.17 against the rule's 0.75; full book 0.52).
- F2: Only the rise side of the signal carries the effect. After a two-week rise (positive Friday forecast), Saturday moved +0.88 % in the signal's direction (t 6.73, 1221 cases). After a two-week fall (negative forecast), the move in the signal's direction was +0.02 % (t 0.22, 1256 cases). A short therefore captures far less than the 0.19 % a trade costs, while a long captures 4.6 times that cost.
- F5: On the long side, Saturday's rise is largest when the 60-day return is also positive: +1.21 % (404 cases, t 4.97), against +0.58 % when the 60-day return is negative (218 cases). The 60-day condition alone earns nothing: on Fridays with a positive 60-day return but no two-week rise, Saturday's return is −0.00 % (222 cases). The longer trend therefore strengthens the idea's own momentum story rather than being an effect of its own.
