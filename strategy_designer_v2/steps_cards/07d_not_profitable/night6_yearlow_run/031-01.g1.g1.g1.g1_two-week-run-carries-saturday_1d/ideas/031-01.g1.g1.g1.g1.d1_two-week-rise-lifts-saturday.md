Idea: After a two-week rise, the larger a coin's 14-day return measured in its usual daily moves at Friday's close (UTC), the more the coin rises over the following Saturday alone (the next 1 day).
Consequence: A trader would buy only after a two-week rise, hold through Saturday sized by the run's strength, and take no short after two-week falls, which carry the book's deepest losses and earn less than their cost.
Why:
- F1: The book's Calmar failure comes from its short side: in a rebuild of the Saturday book by side, the deepest fall lasted 97 Saturdays and lost 12.8 %, of which shorts lost 12.2 % and longs 0.6 %. Over the whole period the short-only part returned −2.2 % a year with a −21.3 % deepest fall, while the long-only part returned +11.3 % a year with a −9.7 % deepest fall (Calmar 1.17 against the rule's 0.75; full book 0.52).
- F2: Only the rise side of the signal carries the effect. After a two-week rise (positive Friday forecast), Saturday moved +0.88 % in the signal's direction (t 6.73, 1221 cases). After a two-week fall (negative forecast), the move in the signal's direction was +0.02 % (t 0.22, 1256 cases). A short therefore captures far less than the 0.19 % a trade costs, while a long captures 4.6 times that cost.
- F3: The long side is graded. Saturday's return rises across the terciles of the positive forecast: +0.54 %, +0.81 % and +1.29 % (407 cases each). Across the six sextiles of the whole forecast, the move in the signal's direction is +1.35 % (t 5.03) in the top sextile and +0.09 % (t 0.55) in the bottom one.
- F4: The effect is specific to Friday's close. A positive 14-day return in usual moves is followed by +0.90 % the next day when taken on Friday (t 6.19, 1011 cases), against +0.25 % to +0.45 % on Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, and −0.08 % on Monday.
- F6: The long side earned in seven of eight years, with mean Saturday returns of +0.51 % to +1.55 %. The exception was 2023, at −0.25 % (164 cases).
