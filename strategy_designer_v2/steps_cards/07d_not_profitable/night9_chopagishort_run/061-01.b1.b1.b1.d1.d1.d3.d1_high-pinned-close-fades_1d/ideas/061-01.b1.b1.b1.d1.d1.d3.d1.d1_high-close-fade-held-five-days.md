Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, when a coin's daily close sits in the upper part of that day's high-low range after its 5-day return has been negative, its price falls over the full next five days even when the following closes leave the top of the range, and the nearer that close was to the day's high, the larger the fall.
Consequence: A trader keeps the short for the whole five days from the signal instead of covering as soon as the close drops out of the upper range or the 5-day return turns up.
Why:
- F1: The book loses through its early exits. Its 51 trades closed before 4.5 days lose -4.02 % net on average, while its 92 trades held the full 5 days earn +3.26 %. Holding those same 51 early trades for a fixed 5 days would have lost only -1.89 % (47 found), and almost all of them (47 of 51) closed because the forecast fell to 0 when the situation ended, not on a reversal of the forecast. This drain is what keeps the Sharpe 0.086 below holding's and the Calmar at 0.371.
- F2: The fall does not end when the close leaves the top of the range. On all 127 signal bars, the short earns 1.69 % on the first day and another 1.57 % from day 2 to day 5. Even when the first day rose (59 bars), the next four days still gave 1.34 % short, so leaving the position after a day 1 that went the wrong way gives up most of the move.
- F7: A longer horizon does not help. Holding the trades 10 days instead of 5 drops the full trades to +0.35 % and the early ones to -6.50 %, so the move lives in the 5 days after the signal.
- F8: The grading by nearness to the high shows in the trades. Trades in the lowest, middle and highest third of entry forecast size earn -0.39 %, +0.65 % and +1.75 % net (49, 46 and 48 trades).
- F5: The profile carries the effect. The same situation (a negative 5-day return and a close in the upper half of the range) outside the choppy agitated profile has an unusual move of -0.26 % (t -0.37, 317 grid cells), against +12.1 % (t 3.35, 22 cells) inside it.
