Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, when a coin's daily close sits in the upper half of that day's high-low range after its 5-day return has been negative, its price falls over the next five days, and the fall grows steeply with the depth of that 5-day fall measured against the coin's usual 5-day swing, the deepest falls carrying most of the move.
Consequence: A trader shorts the upper-half closes that follow the deepest 5-day falls, sized much more heavily as the fall deepens, so that most of the book's risk sits in the cases that carry the move.
Why:
- F4: The signal's move comes from the deeper 5-day falls. On non-overlapping in-profile bars of the situation, short-signed 5-day moves were about +16.8 %, +17.2 % and +14.5 % in the three deepest 5-day-return quartiles, but −1.8 % in the shallowest (5-day return about −2 %; 5 to 6 cases each). On all situation bars it was +7.9 % below a −15 % 5-day return (25), +8.2 % from −15 % to −8 % (29), +2.1 % from −8 % to −4 % (30) and +1.0 % from −4 % to 0 (40). By trade, entries in the shallowest tercile (5-day return −4.2 %) netted −0.45 %, against +5.0 % and +4.7 % for the deeper two.
- F5: The deep fall is not an effect of its own. In-profile bars with a 5-day return below −8 % but a close in the lower half of the range rose afterwards: short-signed −4.1 % over 5 days (65 bars). With the upper-half close the same bars fell: +8.1 % (54). The other in-profile bars without the signal were also flat to rising: −0.6 %, t −0.46, 63 grid cases. The upper-half close after a deep fall, the idea's own situation, carries the move.
- F6: Cost is not why the book fails. The total move is 7.1 times the 0.22 % cost of a trade, and trades netted +0.62 % on average (139 trades, 36 % winners). The book fails because it is rarely in the market and its falls are deep. It was in a position on 5 % of coin-days at about 0.35 allocation, making +2.6 % a year. Trades shorter than 4.5 days lost −3.66 % net (46), against +2.58 % for 5-day trades (61).
