Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, when a coin's daily close sits near the top of that day's high-low range on an up day, its price falls over the next ten days, and the smaller the day's gain against its usual daily swing, the larger the fall.
Consequence: A trader shorts quiet high closes on up days in a choppy, agitated market and holds the short for about ten days rather than closing it within a few.
Why:
- F2: The effect sits almost entirely in the smaller gains, which is the idea's own grading. Signals whose gain was under one usual daily swing gave an unusual 5-day fall of +5.5 % (t 3.84, 39 cases on 37 days), against +0.8 % for gains of one usual swing or more (t 0.46, 36 cases). The book's 44 trades on smaller gains averaged +1.92 % net (sum +84.6 %), while its 5 trades on larger gains averaged -3.08 % (sum -15.4 %).
- F3: The fall keeps growing well past five days, while the book's trades end early. The unusual move of the tested signal is +0.5 % over 2 days (t 0.47), +3.0 % over 5 days (t 2.43, 75 cases) and +6.0 % over 10 days (t 2.88, 64 cases). On smaller gains it reaches +10.7 % over 10 days (t 4.88, 33 cases on 31 days). Yet the median trade lasted 5 days, and the two worst trades (-23.7 % and -15.5 %) were closed after 1 day.
- F6: The Calmar failure comes from a book too small and too briefly held for its occasional losing trade. Its mean absolute allocation was 0.155, giving a deepest fall of 0.8 % against a yearly return of 0.9 %. Windows w10 and w12 lost (-0.11 % each) on trades averaging -3.1 % and -1.9 %, and the signal's year 2020 averaged -0.7 %.
