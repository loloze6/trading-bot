Idea: A coin whose Sunday close is above its Saturday close falls over the next 2 days, and the more the coin fell over the 7 days before that Sunday, the larger that fall.
Consequence: A Sunday bounce in a coin that fell during the week is a reason to sell into it at Sunday's close, and a reason to refuse a long carried into Monday.
Why:
- F3: The book's deepest fall, which drives Calmar to 0.30, is -18.7 % over 376 days in w3–w6, while the coins rose +0.16 % a day. The Sunday rises in that stretch reversed half as much (+0.59 % against +1.21 % elsewhere, 541 against 2000 cases), on calmer coins (20-day volatility 0.035 against 0.050) that had risen more over the prior week (7-day return +4.1 % against +2.8 %).
- F4: A Sunday rise that follows a falling week reverses far more. With the coin's 7-day return in its lowest fifth, the unusual move is +1.81 % (t 5.10, 477 cases) and the position gain +0.65 %. In the highest fifth it is +0.54 % (t 1.23, 477) and the position gain +0.04 %. The condition alone is not the effect: on no-signal bars a short earns -0.34 % in the lowest fifth and -0.79 % in the highest.
- F5: The worst tenth of cases (by position gain) held larger shorts (allocation -0.83 against -0.48) after stronger 7-day rises (+4.8 % against +2.9 %) and larger Sunday rises (+4.5 % against +3.0 %). The trades that closed on Monday lost -3.79 % before fees (1071 trades), while the 2191 that closed on Tuesday gained +0.98 %.
