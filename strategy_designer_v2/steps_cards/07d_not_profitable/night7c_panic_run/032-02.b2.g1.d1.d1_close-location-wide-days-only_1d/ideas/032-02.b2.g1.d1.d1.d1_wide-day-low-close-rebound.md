Idea: The lower a coin's daily close sits within that day's high-low range, on a day whose range is wider than the coin's average daily range over the previous 20 days, the more the coin rises over the next 1 day, the rise growing with how much wider the day was.
Consequence: A trader would buy the close of an unusually wide day that closes near its low, sized by how low the close sits and how wide the day was, and would not short wide days that close high.
Why:
- F1: The idea's effect sits almost entirely on its long side. On wide days a low close was followed by +0.88 % excess next-day return (t 5.12, 1245 cases), against +0.20 % (t 1.29, 1405 cases) after a high close. The book's long days earned 49.5 bp a day and its short days −0.49 bp, so the short half adds turnover, fees and variance but no gain.
- F4: In my rebuilt daily book (an approximation: one 0.19 % fee per unit of allocation change, coins averaged per day), the as-tested positions gave a Sharpe of 0.21, a Calmar of 0.05 and 4.5 bp a day in position.
- F5: The long effect is graded and lasts two days.
- F6: The effect held in every year, with excess returns of +0.74 %, +0.40 %, +0.43 % and +0.53 % (t 3.17, 2.11, 2.02, 1.92). It was weakest on BTC at +0.15 % (t 0.79) and strongest on XMR at +0.74 % (t 3.13). Long cases that were also a 30-day closing low earned +1.03 % (t 2.26, 243 cases).
