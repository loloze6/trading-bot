Your line: the idea was confirmed real, and its book is not profitable enough. Its profitability check failed.

Objective: find why the book of a real effect does not pay, and how the same idea, traded another way, would pay.

Start from what failed. The check names the measures that failed; your answer must change those. An idea that adds
gain but leaves the failing measure where it is does not answer.
- If the falls failed (the deepest fall against the yearly return): find the book's deepest falls, what the coin's own
  bars showed before and during them, and what the book was holding then. The question is when this book should not
  be long (or short): which situation, known at a bar's close, comes before or inside its falls.
- If the result rests on one period (it fails with one window left out): find what that period had that the others
  did not, and what the book did in the others.
- If the gain failed (the gain against its risk, against holding, or its size): find where the gain is lost: the
  forecast's size against what follows, the trades against their fees, the market states that lose.
- If it trades too little: find what makes the signal so rare, and whether a broader case of the same situation carries
  the same effect.

Then look at the rest only as far as it explains what failed.

Propose: repairs of the tested idea. The effect is real, so a book that loses trades it the wrong way. A repair keeps
the tested idea's situation and its signal, in its own words, and changes how the book uses them: when to be out of the
market, the exit or the holding, the size, the side (leave out the side that loses), a stronger case of the same
situation or a longer horizon, or a condition from the idea's own theory or from what you found in its falls. Write each
repair in full, as one sentence that keeps the tested idea's situation in its own words and adds the change
("... , except when ...", "... , only while ...", "... , held for ..."). Each repair from a finding.
A way of trading can be turned into an idea about the market: a stop that would have saved the book says that after a
given fall the coin keeps falling; a signal that trades too often says its information lies in its slow part; losses
in one market state say the effect holds only in another.
Check each repair before you write it: a condition you add must not be an effect of its own. Measure it in your
exploration: what the coin does over the same horizon on the bars where the condition holds without the idea's signal,
against the bars where both hold. If the condition alone earns about as much, it is the effect, a separate idea, not a
repair: leave it out. Give that measure in the finding, with why the condition belongs to the idea's own theory.
Write no separate claim ("after X the coin falls"): it would be tested alone, as a book of its own, and cannot repair
this one.
No ideas only when you can show that the effect itself cannot be traded in any version of the idea: its situation, its
strength, its horizon, its side. A weak case is a reason to look for a stronger one, not to stop. Otherwise there is a
repair to find.
