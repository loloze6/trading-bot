Idea: In a coin whose price has gone nowhere over the past 60 days, a daily close above its prior 60-day high is followed by a further rise over the next 10 days, and the rise is carried by breaks out of narrow ranges. The rise grows steeply as the prior 60-day high-low range narrows against the price and nearly vanishes in the widest ranges.
Consequence: A trader should join trendless 60-day upside breaks mainly when the prior two-month range was narrow against the price, and refuse those out of wide ranges, which carry the book's deep falls.
Why:
- F1: Calmar fails on the depth of the falls, not on cost. On folds B and C the rebuilt book's deepest fall is -8.7%, running 573 days from w11 to w16, against a yearly return of +8.6%. The worst stretch is w12, where the book summed -2.7% and its 10 entries averaged a 10-day return of -15.7% with a mean worst point of -18.4%. Cost is small beside the move: the total move per case is +6.23%, 27.9 times the 0.22% a trade costs.
- F2: The losses sit in the wide-range breakouts.
- F3: Narrowness amplifies the break more than it acts alone.
