Idea: When a coin closes below its Ichimoku cloud, its return over the next 26 days is higher the thicker the cloud is relative to the price.
Consequence: A coin trading below a very thick cloud is a reason to buy it for the rebound, not to short it.
Why:
- F1: The book loses because the coins rose after the signal: over the 26 days after a grid case, the coin's mean return was +3.90 % (t 2.49, 353 independent cases), against its normal drift of +5.66 %, so the short's total move is -1.86 % per case. The trade cost is 0.22 %, so cost is not the cause. The 1398 short trades lost 3.23 % each on average after fees and won 39 % of the time.
- F5: The thickest cloud quarter (thickness 30 % to 142 % of the price) was followed by a rise of 27.3 % over 26 days (24 cases). The three thinner quarters were followed by -5.6 %, -15.3 % and -3.0 % (23 to 24 cases each).
- F6: Cases far below the cloud whose 26-day return was above its median rose 56.4 % over the next 26 days (11 cases). Far-below cases whose 26-day return was below its median fell 11.5 % (36 cases).
- F8: Higher volatility came with rises. Cases in the two higher quarters of 30-day volatility (above 4.7 % a day) rose 7.5 % and 6.7 % over 26 days. The two lower quarters fell 1.7 % and 0.6 % (49 cases each).
