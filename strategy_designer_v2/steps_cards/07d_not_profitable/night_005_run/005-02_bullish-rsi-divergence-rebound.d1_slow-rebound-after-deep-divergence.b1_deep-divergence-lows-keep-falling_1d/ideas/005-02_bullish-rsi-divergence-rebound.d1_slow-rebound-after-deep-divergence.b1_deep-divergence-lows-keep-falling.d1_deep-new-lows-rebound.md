Idea: When a coin's close makes a new 30-day low, the deeper its 30-day decline into that low, the larger its rise over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A new low reached after a steep 30-day fall is a reason to refuse a short and to consider a long for about ten days.
Why:
- F4: The tested grading runs backwards: at divergence lows, the deeper the 30-day decline, the larger the next rise. The 30-day return correlates −0.37 with the 5-day forward return and −0.41 with the 10-day forward return. The deepest third (30-day return −33%, n 14) rose +2.1% over 5 days and +4.4% over 10 days. The mildest third (−8.6%, n 14) fell −3.6% over 5 days and −3.7% over 10 days.
- F5: The rebound after deep declines does not depend on the divergence. Non-divergence new lows in the deepest third (30-day return −29%, n 22) rose +5.9% over 5 days and +7.4% over 10 days. The middle and mild thirds rose −0.7% to +0.5% over 5 days.
- F1: The book loses on the few bars it acts on: it is short on only 2.3% of bars (136 of 5,842), and on those bars the coin rose +0.41% the next day on average, against +0.12% on all bars, so each short bar lost 0.19% before fees.
