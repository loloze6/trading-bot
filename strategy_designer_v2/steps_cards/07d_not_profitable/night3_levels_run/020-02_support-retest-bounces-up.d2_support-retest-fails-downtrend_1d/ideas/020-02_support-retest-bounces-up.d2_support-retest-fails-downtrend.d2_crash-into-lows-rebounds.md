Idea: When a coin's price falls back to a price where it turned up several times in the past 90 days after a sharp fall over the past 5 days, it tends to rise over the next 5 days, and the steeper that 5-day fall, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A crash into old lows would be a reason to buy, or at least to close a short, rather than to sell.
Why:
- F3: Shorts taken after a steep short fall lose the most.
- F2: The forecast's grading runs the wrong way, so the larger shorts do not fall more. The larger-forecast half minus the smaller half is -0.95 %. Active grid cases in the deepest 90-day-fall quartile end +0.16 % over 5 days, against -0.88 % in the second quartile. The four touch-strength quartiles show no order (-0.39 %, -0.14 %, +0.29 %, -0.34 %).
- F6: Trades lose more when the coin is more volatile: the top 20-day-volatility quartile (daily standard deviation 6.2 %) loses 1.84 % per trade (t -3.1), against +0.02 % to -0.76 % in the lower three.
