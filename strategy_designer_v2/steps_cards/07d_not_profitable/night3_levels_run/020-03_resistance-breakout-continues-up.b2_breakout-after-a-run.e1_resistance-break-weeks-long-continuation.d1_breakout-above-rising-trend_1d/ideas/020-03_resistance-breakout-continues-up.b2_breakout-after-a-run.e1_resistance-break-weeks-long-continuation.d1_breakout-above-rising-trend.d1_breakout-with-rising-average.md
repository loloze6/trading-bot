Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days, it tends to keep rising over the next 20 days, only while its 50-day average has itself risen over the past 10 days; the more times the level held and the faster that average rose over those 10 days, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A break through a repeatedly held level is a reason to stay long for weeks only when the coin's 50-day average is already climbing, and a reason to stay flat when that average is falling, even if the close is above it.
Why:
- F1: The Calmar fails because of one long fall: the book's deepest fall is 50.5 %, from its peak in w12 to its trough in w14, and the book lost 16.5 %, 7.8 % and 33.0 % in w12, w13 and w14 while the coins' average fell 25.0 %, 34.4 % and 49.4 %; the book was long 0.33 of the time in those windows (0.59 elsewhere), so it is the breaks taken inside a falling market that make the fall.
- F2: The entries made inside the falling windows w12 to w14 lost 14.0 % on average over 20 days (25 entries), against +2.1 % for the 189 entries in the other windows, so the breaks fail when the market is falling.
- F3: The test "close above its 50-day average" does not mean the trend is rising: at entry the 50-day average had on average fallen 1.7 % over the previous 10 days. The 193 entries made while that average was falling earned −0.4 % over 20 days, and 32.6 % of them lost more than 10 %. The 21 entries made while it was rising earned +6.0 %, and only 9.5 % of them lost more than 10 %.
- F5: The idea's grading points the wrong way: entries in the top quarter of forecast lost 4.8 % over 20 days, and 38.3 % of them lost more than 10 % (60 entries). Entries in the bottom quarter of distance above the 50-day average gained 6.3 %, against +0.8 % in the top quarter. Step 5's split agrees: upper half minus lower half is −2.8 %.
