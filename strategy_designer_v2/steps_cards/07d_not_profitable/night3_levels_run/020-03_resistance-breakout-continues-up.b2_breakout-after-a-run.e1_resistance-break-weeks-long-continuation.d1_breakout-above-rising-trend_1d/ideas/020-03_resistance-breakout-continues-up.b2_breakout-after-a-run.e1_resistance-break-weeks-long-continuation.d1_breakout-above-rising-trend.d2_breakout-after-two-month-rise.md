Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days, it tends to keep rising over the next 20 days, only while its 60-day return is positive; the more times the level held and the larger its 60-day return, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A break through a repeatedly held level after two months of gains is a reason to stay long for weeks; the same break after two months of losses is a reason to stay flat.
Why:
- F1: The Calmar fails because of one long fall: the book's deepest fall is 50.5 %, from its peak in w12 to its trough in w14, and the book lost 16.5 %, 7.8 % and 33.0 % in w12, w13 and w14 while the coins' average fell 25.0 %, 34.4 % and 49.4 %; the book was long 0.33 of the time in those windows (0.59 elsewhere), so it is the breaks taken inside a falling market that make the fall.
- F2: The entries made inside the falling windows w12 to w14 lost 14.0 % on average over 20 days (25 entries), against +2.1 % for the 189 entries in the other windows, so the breaks fail when the market is falling.
- F4: The coin's 60-day return at entry also grades the outcome: entries in the top quarter of 60-day return (above +4.7 %) gained 14.0 % over 20 days, while those in the bottom quarter (below −16 %) lost 0.5 %, and 42.9 % of the bottom quarter lost more than 10 % (13 to 14 entries per quarter).
