Idea: When a coin has closed above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days and then falls in the 5 days that follow, it tends to keep falling over the next 15 days; the deeper that 5-day fall, the larger the further fall.
Consequence: A break that has fallen back sharply within days is a reason to leave the long, not to hold it for the rest of its horizon.
Why:
- F1: The Calmar fails because of one long fall: the book's deepest fall is 50.5 %, from its peak in w12 to its trough in w14, and the book lost 16.5 %, 7.8 % and 33.0 % in w12, w13 and w14 while the coins' average fell 25.0 %, 34.4 % and 49.4 %; the book was long 0.33 of the time in those windows (0.59 elsewhere), so it is the breaks taken inside a falling market that make the fall.
- F6: A break that fails early keeps failing: when the coin fell more than 8 % in the 5 days after entry, it fell another 6.3 % over the next 15 days (t −2.46, 34 entries). After a 0 to 5 % rise in those 5 days it gained 2.2 % (t 0.76, 47 entries).
