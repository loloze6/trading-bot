Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days after falling over the past 20 days, it tends to fall over the next 10 days, and the deeper its 20-day fall, the larger the decline.
Consequence: A break of resistance during a 20-day decline is a failing bounce, a reason to stay out or sell rather than buy.
Why:
- F4: Breaks that come after a 20-day decline are followed by a fall. In the lowest fifth of the 20-day return, the excess return over the next 10 days is −2.7 % (t −3.3, 377 cases). In the highest fifth it is +4.1 % (t 3.3, 377 cases), and the middle three fifths range from +0.8 % to +1.5 % (t ≤ 1.9).
- F5: Breaks close to the coin's 60-day high are followed by rises. Breaks in the fifth nearest the 60-day highest close are followed by +5.0 % excess over 10 days (t 3.2, 213 cases). The two fifths furthest below it give −0.7 % and −1.3 %. Breaks after a 5-day fall (lowest fifth) give −1.6 % (t −2.25, 444 cases).
- F7: Trade results depend on the market's direction by year. The mean net per trade is −2.7 % in 2018 (207 trades) and 0.0 % in 2022 (215 trades), the two years when holding's mean daily return was negative. In 2020 it is +9.4 % and in 2024 +10.6 %.
