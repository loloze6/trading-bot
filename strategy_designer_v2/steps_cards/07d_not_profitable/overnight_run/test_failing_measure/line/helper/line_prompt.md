Your line: the book is not profitable enough. Its profitability check failed.

Objective: find why the book does not pay, and what idea would make it pay.

Start from what failed. The check names the measures that failed; your answer must change those. An idea that adds
gain but leaves the failing measure where it is does not answer.
- If the falls failed (Calmar, or one window carrying the result): find the book's deepest falls, what the coin's own
  bars showed before and during them, and what the book was holding then. The question is when this book should not
  be long (or short): which situation, known at a bar's close, comes before or inside its falls.
- If the gain failed (Sharpe, the gain against holding, the mean return): find where the gain is lost: the forecast's
  size against what follows, the trades against their fees, the market states that lose.
- If it trades too little: find what makes the signal so rare, and whether a broader situation carries the same effect.

Then look at the rest only as far as it explains what failed.

Propose: ideas about the market that would remove the cause you found, stated as what the forecast should capture.
A way of trading can be turned into an idea about the market: a stop that would have saved the book says that after a
given fall the coin keeps falling; a signal that trades too often says its information lies in its slow part; losses
in one market state say the effect holds only in another. Each idea from a finding.
An idea that repairs this book is the tested idea amended: write it in full, as one sentence that keeps the tested idea's own situation in its own words and adds the change ("... , except when ...", "... , only while ..."). A separate claim ("after X the coin falls") would be tested alone, as a book of its own, and cannot repair this one. You may also propose a new idea, marked as such.
