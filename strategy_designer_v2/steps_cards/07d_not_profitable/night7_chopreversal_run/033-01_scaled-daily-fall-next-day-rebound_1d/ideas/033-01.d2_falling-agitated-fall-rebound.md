Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day fall measured against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: A trader buys outsized one-day falls mainly when the coin has been falling for weeks and swinging hard, where forced selling is heaviest and the rebound largest.
Why:
- F1: The effect lives in agitated markets only. Here "agitated" means the coin's 30-day swing is above its median over folds B and C, which stands in for the past-year median. On signal days in agitated markets, the next-day move beyond drift is +0.57 % (t 3.75, 1795 cases; t 2.06 with each day's cases taken together, 655 days). On signal days in calm markets it is +0.07 % (t 0.71, 2475 cases; t 0.39 by day, 785 days).
- F3: Agitation is not an effect on its own. On agitated bars without the signal, the next day is −0.42 % against drift (t −3.17, 1831 cases), against +0.57 % on agitated bars with the signal. So the condition only selects where the forced-selling rebound is large, which fits the theory: a fall that is outsized in a swinging, leveraged market is the one that triggers liquidations.
- F6: Falling, agitated markets carry the strongest per-case rebound, +0.76 % (t 3.54, 636 cases), against −0.47 % (t −2.35, 453) in falling, calm markets. The book restricted to falling, agitated markets has a Calmar of 1.30 (yearly return +9.9 %, deepest fall −7.6 %) but holds on only 7 % of coin-days.
