Idea: After an hour in which the coin falls far more than its typical hourly move over the past 30 days, on volume several times its average hourly volume over the past 30 days, the price rebounds over the next two hours, more the more extreme the fall and the volume are together.
Consequence: A heavy-volume hourly plunge supports a short long position of about two hours, which catches most of the rebound with less time exposed.
Why:
- F5: The same rebound shows over 2 hours, at +15.5 bp (t 2.24, n 911) in the 95–98 % band and +26.6 bp (t 1.87, n 304) in the top 1 %; down hours with z<-3 and volume ratio>3 give +20.1 bp at 2 hours (t 1.81, n 432) and +47.0 bp at 4 hours (t 1.88, n 230).
- F4: The opposite side carries a graded effect: after down hours, the 4-hour excess return rises with the hour's (fall in usual moves × volume ratio), from -0.7 bp (n 7605) in the bottom half to +16.9 bp (t 1.38, n 456) in the 95–98 % band and +66.2 bp (t 1.91, n 153) in the top 1 %; that last figure is about 0.4 of the 4-hour usual move (1.42 % for BTC, 1.82 % for ETH) and 6 times the cost of a trade.
- F2: The book's loss comes from its fees: it is in position on 49 % of bars at a mean allocation of 0.70 and turns over 2.85 times its value per coin-day; its gross gain is about 5.6 bp a day against about 15.7 bp a day of fees; only 19 % of its in-position bars carry a forecast of 10 or more.
