Idea: The larger a coin's one-day fall measured in its 20-day daily volatility, the larger its rise over the next day, and the more so the larger the coin's 20-day move in either direction before the fall.
Consequence: A trader would size the next-day long after a sharp fall up when the coin has just run far over the past 20 days, and keep it small when the coin was going sideways.
Why:
- F1: The failing measure, Calmar 0.50, comes from long losing stretches in quiet markets. The book's deepest fall, -30.2 % over 119 days in windows 8-9, was held 56 % of its bars on a signal. On those bars the coin's 20-day volatility stood at 0.90 of its 120-day mean, against 1.04 over all bars. The coin's 20-day return was -6.0 % against +2.6 % over all bars, and the coin was below its 50-day mean 65 % of the time against 50 %. The book's position gains summed to -1.18 on those bars.
- F6: The effect is also stronger after a run, as the brief says. On signal bars where the coin's 20-day return was below -15 %, the next day's return beyond the mean was +0.38 % (1627 cases); where it was above +15 %, +0.51 % (1264 cases). Where the 20-day return was between -5 % and +5 % it was -0.03 % (1832 cases). Without the signal the same buckets give -0.83 %, +0.06 % and -0.23 %, so the run alone does not carry the rise.
