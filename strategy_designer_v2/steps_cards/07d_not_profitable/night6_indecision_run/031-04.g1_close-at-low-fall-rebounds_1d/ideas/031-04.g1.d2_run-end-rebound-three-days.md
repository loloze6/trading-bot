Idea: On a day a coin falls sharply against its own 20-day daily volatility after it had already fallen over the previous 5 days, the nearer its close lands to the day's low, the larger its rise over the next 3 days.
Consequence: A trader would hold the rebound after a run-ending sharp close at the low for three days rather than one.
Why:
- F1: The book's whole gain comes from sharp down days that follow a fall of several days. On the 115 cases whose 5-day return up to the day before was below -1 of its 20-day volatility (scaled to 5 days), the next-day unusual move is +2.9 % (t 3.38, 93 cases) and +1.8 % (t 2.40, 22 cases) in the two deepest bands, and the summed position gain is 1.82. The 289 cases without such a prior fall have unusual moves of -0.20 % (t -0.56) and -0.52 % (t -0.91) and a summed gain of only 0.41. This matches the brief's own requirement that a run comes before the day.
- F2: The prior fall is not an effect of its own. Days with the same 5-day prior fall but no sharp down day show a next-day unusual move of -0.21 % (t -1.44, 1540 cases) and -0.71 % (t -2.17, 272 cases). Days after a sharp down day with no new sharp fall show -0.15 % (t -1.27, 1690 cases). The rebound needs both a prior run and today's sharp fall. Across all cases, the deeper the prior run, the larger the rebound (slope -0.0071 per unit of 5-day volatility, t -2.48, 404 cases).
- F5: The effect of the run-down cases is far larger than the cost of a trade and lasts beyond one day. Cases after a sharp previous day return +2.7 % the next day (t 4.06, 94 cases) and +2.2 % over three days. Cases in the lowest quartile of the 5-day return including the day return +3.5 % (t 4.20, 117 cases) and +2.0 % over three days. These moves are more than ten times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
