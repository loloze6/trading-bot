Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, a day closing below the middle of its range is followed by a rise over the next day, larger the further the close sits below the middle, multiplied by the day's range against its 30-day swing.
Consequence: A trader buys the coin after wide days that close near their low in a choppy, agitated market, and does not sell short after high closes, whose sharp up-moves made the book's deepest falls.
Why:
- F1: The Calmar fails because of the short side: 84 % of the 10 worst gross book days come from shorts, and the worst book days are days the coins rose sharply (book −2.6 %, −2.5 %, −2.0 %, −1.4 % on coin days of +10.2 %, +8.3 %, +12.8 %, +8.0 %); on folds B, C before fees, the short-only book has a deepest fall of −5.2 %, a Calmar of 0.61 and a Sharpe of 0.60, while the long-only book has a deepest fall of −0.86 %, a Calmar of 4.95 and a Sharpe of 1.74 (yearly +4.2 %).
- F2: The effect is on the long side: in-position long bars gain +1.10 % the next day in their direction (t 2.58, 186 bars, sd 5.8 %), against +0.69 % for shorts (t 1.34, 231 bars, sd 7.9 %); shorts lose in w11, w12 and w14 (summed gains −0.037, −0.092, −0.151) while longs are positive in 11 of 12 windows.
- F3: The long side is graded and lasts beyond one day: longs in the top third of |forecast| rise +2.93 % the next day (t 3.75, 67 cases), +2.66 % over 2 days and +3.39 % over 3 days, against +0.64 % (lowest third) and −0.20 % (middle third); shorts in the top third gain only +1.19 % (t 1.28), and their gain shrinks to +0.42 % at 2 days.
- F4: The day's own move is not the effect: in-profile bars without a strong forecast earn −1.36 % (88 down days) and +0.21 % (76 up days) when traded against the day's move, while bars with a strong forecast earn +3.96 % (13 big down days) and +3.68 % (33 up days). It is the close's place in the range, not the day's return, that carries the reversal, as the idea's theory of forced flow pushing the close says.
