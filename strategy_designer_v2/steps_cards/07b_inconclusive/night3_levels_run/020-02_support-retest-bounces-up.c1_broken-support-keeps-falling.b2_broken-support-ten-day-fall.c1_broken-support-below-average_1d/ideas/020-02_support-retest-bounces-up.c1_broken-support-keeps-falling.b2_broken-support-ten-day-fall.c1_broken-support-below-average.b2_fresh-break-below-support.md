Idea: When a coin's daily close sits just below prices where it turned up several times in the past 90 days, it tends to fall over the next 10 days, and the more such past turning-up lows lie just above the close, the larger the fall, and the larger still the less the coin has already fallen over its past 20 days, only while the close is below its 50-day average.
Consequence: Below its 50-day average, a close just under a dense cluster of past lows that comes before much of a decline is a reason to be short for ten days; after a deep 20-day fall it is a reason to refuse the short.
