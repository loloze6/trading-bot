Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days, it tends to keep rising over the next 5 days, only while its 60-day return is positive and its 365-day return is positive, and the larger its 10-day return before the break, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader would buy breaks of defended levels inside a rising year and refuse them in a bear year, where a positive quarter is only a rebound.
Why:
- F4: The effect is mixed across periods even with the 60-day return positive. Over all cases, 2018 gives -4.3 % (t -2.48, 69 cases) and 2022 gives -3.8 % (t -4.12, 219 cases). Against that, 2019 gives +2.1 % (t 2.07), 2020 +1.2 % (t 1.88) and 2024 +1.9 % (t 2.77). In the top forecast quartile 2018 and 2022 are still negative (-14.6 % on 8 cases, -3.6 % on 66 cases), so the bear years drown the rest.
- F1: The tested effect is on the predicted side but small and skewed. The 5-day excess return is +0.43 % per case with t 1.42 over 2511 cases, a strength of only 0.031 against F10's usual 0.05. The median case is -1.17 % and only 43 % of cases end above the coin's normal drift, so the mean rests on a minority of large rises.
- F7: Size covers the cost. The total move per case (+1.17 %) is 5.2 times the 0.22 % round trip and the unusual move 1.9 times. In the strongest breaks the unusual move is about +1.8 to +2.1 %, roughly 8 to 10 times the cost. The test already used all 19 coins on all four folds, so no more daily data is available.
