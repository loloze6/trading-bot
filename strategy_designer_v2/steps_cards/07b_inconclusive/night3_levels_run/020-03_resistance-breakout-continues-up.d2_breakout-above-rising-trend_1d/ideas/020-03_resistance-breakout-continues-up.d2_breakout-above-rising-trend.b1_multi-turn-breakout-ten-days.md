Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down in the past 90 days, it tends to keep rising over the next 10 days, and the more times it turned down at that price, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader would buy closes through levels that turned price down several times and hold them for about ten days, giving less weight to breaks of a level that held only once.
Why:
- F1: The breaks carry a positive unusual move that grows with the horizon: on 655 independent cases it is −0.04 % over 1 day (t −0.28), +0.42 % over 3 days (t 1.47), +1.04 % over 5 days (t 2.58), +2.30 % over 10 days (t 3.58, 627 cases) and +3.14 % over 20 days (t 2.46, 597 cases). The 5-day size is 5.4 times the 0.19 % cost of a round trip, so the size of the move does not block this idea.
- F2: The result is unclear because the grading fails, not because the effect is missing. The 5-day excess return does not rise with the distance of the close above its 50-day average.
- F5: The number of past turns at the level grades the 10-day move from a single turn to several. Measured on the bets, with swing highs recomputed from closes:
- F6: How far the close broke beyond the level grades the move only weakly. By break-size quintile the 10-day excess is +1.28 %, +2.36 %, +3.11 %, +3.19 % and +2.42 %, rising over the first four quintiles and easing in the last.
