Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days, it tends to keep rising over the next 20 days, only while its close is above its 50-day average. The more times the level held, the further the close above it and the further the close above its 50-day average, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A break through a repeatedly held level is a reason to stay long for weeks only when the coin already trades above its 50-day average, and a reason to stay flat when it trades below it.
Why:
- F1: The tested result is on the side the idea predicts but far too noisy to show: over 20 days the 507 cases earn a mean unusual move of +1.37 % with t 0.95. The median case loses 2.66 % and only 43 % of cases end above the coin's normal drift, so the positive mean comes from a few large winners against a usual 20-day move of 17 % (BTC) to 46 % (ZEC). With the move per case 7.1 times the cost of a trade, cost is not what stops it (F11 of the runs).
- F2: The effect is drowned by the market's own moves. Cases in 2018 lose 21.7 % per case (t -7.7, 36 cases) and those in 2022 lose 4.8 % (t -2.2, 53 cases). Cases in 2020 gain 8.3 % (t 2.5, 71 cases) and those in 2021 gain 7.3 % (t 1.5, 68 cases). No year or coin decides the sign alone: by coin the means run from -1.2 % (XMR) to +4.5 % (ZEC), none with |t| above 1.1.
