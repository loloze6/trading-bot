Idea: When a coin closes above a price where it turned down several times in the past 90 days, it tends to keep rising over the next 5 days, and the more times the level held before, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A close through a repeatedly held level exhausts the defending orders and triggers stops in the break's direction, accelerating the move, more so the more often it held.
Why:
- F1: The result points the way the idea predicts but is too small for this data to show it: 1,817 cases, mean excess 5-day return +0.31 % with t 1.20, a strength of 0.025 of the coin's usual 5-day move (10.60 %), about half the 0.05 that real ideas usually show (findings F10).
- F3: The effect is mixed across the data. By coin, ZEC carries most of it (+1.52 %, t 1.74), BTC (+0.37 %) and XRP (+0.34 %) are small, and ETH, LTC and XMR are slightly negative (−0.06 % to −0.12 %). By year, 2020 (+1.31 %, t 2.11) and 2024 (+1.49 %, t 2.05) are positive while 2022 (−1.85 %, t −2.86) and 2018 (−1.45 %) are negative. By window, the mean runs from −3.96 % (w18) to +7.33 % (w11, t 3.64).
- F7: The idea is large enough to pay its costs: per case, the total move (+0.90 %) is 4.7 times the 0.19 % cost of a round trip, and the unusual move alone is 1.6 times it. The book beats holding on Sharpe (0.74, 0.19 above holding's) but fails on its falls (Calmar 0.49).
