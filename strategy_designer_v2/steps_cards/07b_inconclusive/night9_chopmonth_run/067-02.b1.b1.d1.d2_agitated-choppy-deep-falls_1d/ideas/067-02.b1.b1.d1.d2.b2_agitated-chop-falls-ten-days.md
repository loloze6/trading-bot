Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, the further a coin's close sits below its close of 30 days earlier, measured as a plain percent of that earlier close, the more it falls over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A trader holds the agitated-chop short on monthly losers for about ten days, sized by the depth of the month's fall.
Why:
- F2: The same situation measured over shorter horizons gives more cases and a clearer effect. On a 5-day grid, the 39 in-profile falls on 25 days have a short-direction unusual move of +9.85 % (t 2.58). On a 10-day grid, 22 cases on 12 days give +10.7 % (t 2.45). On a 3-day grid, 59 cases give +3.6 % (t 1.70).
- F3: The effect is graded with depth, but roughly in proportion rather than cubic. On the 5-day grid, the terciles of the 30-day return give +5.7 % (t 2.16), +10.8 % (t 2.18) and +13.0 % (t 1.96), from shallowest to deepest. The correlation of the 30-day return with the 5-day unusual move is 0.27. Deep cases are not alone in carrying the effect: on the 5-day grid, shallow cases give +9.1 % with t 3.41.
- F4: The effect lives in the profile. Outside the choppy agitated profile, monthly falls are followed by a 5-day short-direction unusual move of only +1.0 % (t 1.13, 678 cases) and a 21-day move of +1.0 % (t 0.16, 117 cases).
- F6: The effect is spread across coins and periods, not carried by one of them, though each piece is small. On the 5-day grid, all 8 coins and all 4 years are positive (2019: +5.8 %, t 3.12; 2021: +4.1 %, t 1.63). Window w12 is the only negative window (-0.6 %, 7 cases).
