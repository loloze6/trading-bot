Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, the further a coin's close sits below its close of 30 days earlier, measured as a plain percent of that earlier close, the more it falls over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader shorts monthly losers in agitated chop for about a week, sized in proportion to the month's fall, rather than holding three weeks on the deepest falls only.
Why:
- F1: The test's own 21-day grid holds very few in-profile cases, so its t cannot reach 2. In folds B and C, only 6 grid cases on 3 days fall in a choppy agitated profile after a monthly fall, and only 3 of them are deep. Step 5's main measure, over all folds, has 22 deep cases on 17 days with mean +5.84 % and t 1.37. It is on the predicted side but short of the bar.
- F2: The same situation measured over shorter horizons gives more cases and a clearer effect. On a 5-day grid, the 39 in-profile falls on 25 days have a short-direction unusual move of +9.85 % (t 2.58). On a 10-day grid, 22 cases on 12 days give +10.7 % (t 2.45). On a 3-day grid, 59 cases give +3.6 % (t 1.70).
- F3: The effect is graded with depth, but roughly in proportion rather than cubic. On the 5-day grid, the terciles of the 30-day return give +5.7 % (t 2.16), +10.8 % (t 2.18) and +13.0 % (t 1.96), from shallowest to deepest. The correlation of the 30-day return with the 5-day unusual move is 0.27. Deep cases are not alone in carrying the effect: on the 5-day grid, shallow cases give +9.1 % with t 3.41.
- F4: The effect lives in the profile. Outside the choppy agitated profile, monthly falls are followed by a 5-day short-direction unusual move of only +1.0 % (t 1.13, 678 cases) and a 21-day move of +1.0 % (t 0.16, 117 cases).
- F7: The move per case is far above the cost of a trade. On the 5-day grid, the total short-direction move after in-profile falls is +9.0 % per case, against a trade cost of 0.22 %.
