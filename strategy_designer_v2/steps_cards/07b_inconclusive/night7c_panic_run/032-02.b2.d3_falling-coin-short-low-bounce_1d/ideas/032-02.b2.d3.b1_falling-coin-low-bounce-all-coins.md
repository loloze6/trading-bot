Idea: In a coin that has fallen over the past 30 days, a close below its lowest low of the prior 10 days is followed by a rise over the next 1 day, larger the further the close sits below that low in percent.
Consequence: A trader would buy deep 10-day lows in any falling coin for one day, sizing up with the depth of the break.
Why:
- F1: In folds B and C the tested situation is followed by a next-day excess on the side the idea predicts: +0.87 % unweighted over 305 cases on 112 days (t 1.41, the cases of one day together) and +1.30 % weighted by the position held (t 0.83). Step 5's own measure is also positive but short of the bar: +0.69 %, t 1.18 over 750 days. The sign is right but the evidence is not sure enough.
- F2: The excess is positive in 5 of the 6 coins but sure in none except ZEC. Next-day excess runs from +1.02 % (BTC, 39 cases, t 1.40) to +1.63 % (ZEC, 59 cases, t 2.27), with LTC flat at −0.07 % (52 cases). Each coin has too few cases to show it clearly, so the six default coins give a small sample of a broadly present effect.
- F3: The gain is graded by depth as the idea says. By depth quintile, the next-day excess is +2.81 % in the deepest quintile (mean depth −13.0 %) against +0.02 % in the shallowest (−0.4 %), with a Pearson correlation of −0.40 between depth and next-day excess. The step-5 slope agreed (+0.0029, passed).
- F4: Much of the deepest-break gain comes from a few days in 2020. The deepest quintile earns +11.5 % on 13 cases over 4 days that year (t 2.34), but only +0.45 % on 48 cases over 20 days outside 2020 (t 0.41). Sizing that piles into the deepest breaks (mean allocation 0.35 in the deepest quintile against 0.01 in the shallowest) lowers the weighted t (0.83) below the unweighted t (1.41).
- F6: The move is large against what a trade costs. The total move per case is +0.57 % and the unusual move +0.45 % (t 0.75 over 167 days), which are 3.0 and 2.3 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade. What is missing is cases, not size per case, and the thirteen other coins in folds A and D (plus part of B and C) are available.
