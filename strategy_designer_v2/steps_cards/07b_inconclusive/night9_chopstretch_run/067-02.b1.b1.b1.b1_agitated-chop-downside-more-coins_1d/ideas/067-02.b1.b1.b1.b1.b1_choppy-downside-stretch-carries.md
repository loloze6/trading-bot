Idea: In a choppy market, the further a coin's close stands below its 20-day average, counted in units of its current 20-day standard deviation of daily returns, the more the coin keeps falling over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader in a range-bound coin, calm or agitated, should lean short on closes stretched below the 20-day average, sized by the stretch, for about a week.
Why:
- F1: In the tested choppy-agitated profile, the evidence is too thin and too uneven to give a clear answer. The profile covers only 4.4 % of the coin-days in folds B and C, which gives 72 five-day grid cells, 48 of them below the average. The two lowest stretch quintiles point opposite ways: the lowest gives -14.1 % (t -2.52, n 15) and the next gives +4.0 % (t +2.11, n 14). That matches step 5's failed supporting check, where the larger half minus the smaller half of |forecast| was -2.7 %.
- F2: The below-average effect in the agitated profile depends on a few windows. w16 gives -33.3 % over 6 cells and w15 gives -6.3 % over 6 cells, while w12 gives +6.8 % (t +4.70, 9 cells), and by year 2021 is +2.2 % (n 22) against 2020 at -15.7 % (n 12). The day-clustered t of 0.84 on 407 cases is this mixture of windows.
- F3: Across the whole choppy profile (60-day return between -10 % and +10 %, calm or agitated, 152 grid cells), the stretch from the 20-day average carries forward in steps over 5 days. By stretch quintile, the 5-day unusual move rises steadily:
- F4: The carry is graded on both sides of the average in the choppy profile. The Spearman correlation between stretch and the 5-day move is 0.27 below the average (p 0.019, n 74) and 0.28 above it (p 0.014, n 78). Below the average it holds at 3 and 10 days too, with rho 0.29 at 3 days (p 0.013) and 0.29 at 10 days (p 0.016).
- F5: The effect is present in choppy calm alone, so the tested agitated slice was the thinnest part of it. In choppy calm, the lowest stretch quintile gives -6.4 % (t -3.14, n 21) and the highest gives +7.3 % (t +2.53, n 29). The agitated part holds only 26 grid cells.
