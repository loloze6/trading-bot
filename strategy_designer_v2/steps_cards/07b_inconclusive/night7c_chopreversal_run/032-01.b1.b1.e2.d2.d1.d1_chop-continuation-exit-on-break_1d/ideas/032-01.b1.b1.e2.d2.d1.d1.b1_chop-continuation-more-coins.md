Idea: When a coin's past 30 days have gone back and forth with little net progress compared with the sum of its daily moves, its 7-day move counted in 30-day daily swings is followed by a move the same way, day by day, only on the days the 30 days are still choppy and the 7-day move still points the same way as the 30-day net move. The larger the scaled 7-day move, the larger the following daily moves, and the follow-through stops on the first day either the chop or the agreement ends.
Consequence: A trader should close the position on the day the month stops being choppy or the week turns against the month, instead of holding a fixed week.
Why:
- F1: The tested next-day effect points the way the idea predicts but is diluted. On folds B and C, step 5's measure recomputes to a t of 1.82 over 1217 days. Read without the forecast weight on a 1-day grid, the chop-and-agreement bars give +0.46 % per case (t 3.00 over 899 days), with both sides positive: long +0.37 % (t 2.85), short +0.41 % (t 3.14).
- F5: The 7-day effect is spread rather than confined to one part of the data, but it varies in strength.
