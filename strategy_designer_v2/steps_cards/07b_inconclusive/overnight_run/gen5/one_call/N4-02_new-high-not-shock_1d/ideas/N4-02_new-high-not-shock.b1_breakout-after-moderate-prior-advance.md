Idea: A coin's new 20-day closing high, made while its close is above the close of 60 days earlier but by less than about 30%, is followed by a 10-day rise larger than the coin's average 10-day rise.
Consequence: A trader would take breakouts that come after a moderate prior advance and skip those that come out of a falling or flat 60-day stretch.
Why:
- F4: Breakouts in coins with a mildly positive 60-day return had the strongest follow-through. Where the 60-day return was 0–30%, excess returns were +3.8 pp (bottom half, n=24) and +6.6 pp (top half, n=31, t=1.79, hit 61%). Where the 60-day return was negative or not yet available, they were about +1.0 pp in both halves (n=114/172, t≈0.75). Where it was above 30%, the top half gave +2.9 pp (n=27) and the bottom half −0.4 pp (n=13).
- F3: The calendar year drowns the breakout effect, and both halves move together. Excess 10-day returns after breakouts were negative in both halves in 2018 (−4.8 / −4.2 pp, n=17/24, hit 24%/25%) and in 2022 (−2.5 / −3.9 pp, n=19/27). They were positive in both halves in 2020 (+7.0 / +5.5 pp, n=22/33, t=2.56/1.94).
