Idea: The larger a coin's fall on its latest day, measured against the size of its own daily moves over the past 60 days, the more it rises over the next 2 days.
Consequence: After an unusually sharp daily drop, a trader can expect a short rebound in the following one to two days, larger the sharper the drop, and should not hold out for a multi-day recovery.
Why:
- F4: What does grow is the size of the last day's fall, scaled by the coin's 60-day daily volatility:
- F5: That fall-size effect over the next day is positive in all six coins, with t from 0.69 (ZEC) to 3.99 (XMR). It is positive in 7 of the 8 years; 2025 is the exception (t -0.24).
- F6: By 5 days the fall-size effect is also gone (down days slope t 0.19, 1381 bars). The rebound after a sharp fall is a 1–2 day move, and the 5-day test diluted it with three or more days of noise.
- F2: The forecast predicts the next day only: its slope is +0.00019 with t 2.47 at 1 day (17,380 grid bars). It fades to t 0.73 at 2 days and t 0.65 at 3 days, then turns negative at 5 days (t -1.27) and 10 days (t -0.58). The test measured the idea at a horizon where the effect has already gone.
- F3: The 1-day effect does not grow with streak length; it only separates "in a down streak" from "not". Being in a down streak at all adds 0.31 % the next day (t 2.54). Among bars already in a streak, a longer streak predicts nothing more (slope t 0.51 over 8531 bars). By streak length of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 or more days, the next-day excess is +0.13 %, +0.05 %, +0.58 %, -0.09 % and +0.11 %, with no order. The idea's graded claim, longer streak means larger rebound, is absent at every horizon.
