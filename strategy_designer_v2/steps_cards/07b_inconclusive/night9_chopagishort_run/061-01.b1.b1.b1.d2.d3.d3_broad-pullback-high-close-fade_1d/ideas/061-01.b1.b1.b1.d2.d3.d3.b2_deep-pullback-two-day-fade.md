Idea: When a coin closes near the top of that day's high-low range on an up day that follows a fall over the previous five days, its price falls over the next two days, and the deeper that five-day fall against the coin's usual daily swing, the larger the fall.
Consequence: A trader takes a short, quick fade of the high-close bounce after a pullback, larger after deeper pullbacks, and holds it two days rather than five.
Why:
- F1: The test's own result points the predicted way but is too weak (unusual move +0.43 %, t 0.91, 647 case-days). In folds B and C, the effect comes almost entirely from the deepest prior falls. Measured in usual daily moves, the deepest fifth of five-day falls is followed by a 5-day unusual move of +4.83 % for the short (t 3.85, 207 cases, 83 days). The other four fifths give +1.32 %, -0.21 %, -0.05 % and +0.23 % (t 1.29, -0.25, -0.05, 0.27). The Spearman correlation between fall size and unusual move is -0.13 (n 1009).
- F3: The high close matters only after a deep fall. Among up days in the deepest fifth of prior falls, a close in the top quarter of the range gives +5.88 % (t 4.17, 164 cases). A lower close gives -0.20 % (t -0.15, 128 cases). In the shallower fifths the high close adds nothing (q3 -0.71 %, q5 +0.35 %).
