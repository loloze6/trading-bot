Idea: In a calm market, the more of its 20-day daily swings a coin's 5-day move spans, the further the coin keeps moving the same way over the next 5 days, after rises and after falls alike.
Consequence: A large move in a quiet coin is a reason to trade in its direction for the following days, on the long side after a rise and on the short side after a fall.
Why:
- F2: The effect is half of what the brief claims. In calm markets on folds B and C, rises alone give a slope t of 1.94 (471 cases, 144 days). Counting falls in their own direction too, the size-ordered continuation clears the bar: slope of the directional excess on |scaled move| t 2.27 (904 cases, 200 days), mean directional excess +1.30 % (day-pooled t 2.44). The signed scaled move on the excess return gives a slope t of 2.79.
- F3: The effect lives in the calm profile only. In agitated markets the same two-sided measure has a slope t of -0.29 and a mean of -0.03 % (718 cases, 186 days), against t 2.27 and +1.30 % in calm markets.
- F4: In calm markets the gain is carried by the largest moves on both sides. The top two quartiles of |scaled move| return +2.4 % and +3.3 % (falls, t 2.11 and 1.87) and +1.0 % and +3.5 % (rises, t 1.78 for the top quartile). The two lowest quartiles are near 0 (-0.06 % to +0.21 %).
