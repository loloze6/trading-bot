Idea: After an unusually large 90-day rise, the more a coin's volume over the last 14 days has risen above its average daily volume during the run, the more its price falls over the next 15 days.
Consequence: A climax of trading on top of an extreme rise marks the overreaction's last buyers, so a trader shorts or trims in proportion to that volume surge rather than to its fading.
Why:
- F1: The tested result is on the opposite side, not merely short of the bar. Step 5's slope of 30-day excess return on the forecast is -0.0076 (t -0.46, 57 cases), so a deeper volume drop came with a higher return. Its 16 short-bet cases have a mean excess return of +6.07 %, against the short the idea predicts.
- F3: The volume relation runs against the idea, and it is the only clear signal in the data. Regressing the 30-day excess return on the raw 14-day/90-day volume ratio inside the regime gives a slope of -0.22 (t -2.19, 28 cases, correlation -0.40). On the 15-day grid it gives -0.14 (t -2.20, 68 cases), and on the 10-day grid -0.069 (t -1.52, 99 cases). So the higher recent volume is against the run's average, the lower the return that follows.
- F4: The fall shows only where recent volume is well above the run's average, which is exactly where the test's clipped forecast was 0. On the 30-day grid, cases with a volume ratio above +0.3 average -5.93 % excess (8 cases, strength -0.13). Every bucket below +0.3 averages +2.6 % to +9.3 %. Cases with a zero forecast average +5.71 % (19 cases) and short-forecast cases +1.39 % (9 cases).
- F6: The effect is mixed across coins and periods.
