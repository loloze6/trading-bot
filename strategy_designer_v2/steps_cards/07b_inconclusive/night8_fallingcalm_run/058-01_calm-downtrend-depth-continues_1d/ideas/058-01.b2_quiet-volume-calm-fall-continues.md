Idea: In a falling, calm market, after a coin has fallen over its last 30 days, the quieter its volume over those 30 days compared with its usual volume, the more it keeps falling over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader in a calm downtrend would short a coin whose fall came on thin volume, and hold off when the same fall came on heavy volume.
Why:
- F3: In the falling calm profile, the continuation sits on the falls made on quiet volume, as the brief says (30-day mean volume over its 180-day mean, in terciles). Quiet: +3.61 % short excess over 5 days (55 cases, 35 days, t 1.59). Mid: +1.64 % (t 0.96). Heavy: -0.29 % (t -0.19). Quiet minus heavy is +3.89 % (Welch t 1.89), and the Spearman correlation of the volume ratio with the short excess is -0.12.
- F4: The volume split is much weaker outside the profile, which places the effect in the calm downtrend. On falls outside it, quiet minus heavy is +1.84 % (Welch t 0.94, 163 cases), against +3.89 % inside.
- F2: The grading the idea claims is not in the data. Step 5's deeper-half minus shallower-half check is -0.05 % and fails. On folds B and C the 30-day-fall quartiles do not rise with depth: deepest +0.28 %, q2 +1.33 %, q3 +4.16 %, shallowest -0.75 %. The Spearman correlation of the 30-day return with the next 5-day return is only +0.06.
- F6: The book cannot pay at this size whatever the result. The total move per case is +0.09 %, 0.4 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade, because the coins' drift in the short direction is -0.65 %. Only an idea with a larger unusual move, such as the quiet-volume cases at +3.6 % (F3), would clear the cost.
