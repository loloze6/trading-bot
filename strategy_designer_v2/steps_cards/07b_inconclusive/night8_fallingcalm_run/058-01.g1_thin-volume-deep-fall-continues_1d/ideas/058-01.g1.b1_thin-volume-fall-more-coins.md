Idea: In a falling, calm market, the deeper a coin's 30-day fall and the more its volume over those 30 days has shrunk against the 30 days before, the more it keeps falling over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A deep calm decline made on thinning volume is a reason to stay short, and the same depth on swelling volume is a reason to refuse the short.
Why:
- F1: Step 5's slope is on the side the idea predicts but short of the bar (slope 0.00084, t 1.22, 444 cells). In folds B and C the slope on the 5-day grid is positive for all 6 coins (t from 0.17 to 3.13, 28 to 60 cells per coin), so the effect is the right sign everywhere but too weak per coin for this many cases.
- F2: The sign of the effect is stable across horizons, and only the 1-day and 3-day slopes come near the bar.
- F3: Inside the active cells, thinning volume carries the effect and the depth of the fall adds nothing.
- F6: The book earns little: Sharpe 0.32 against holding's 0.56, and Calmar 0.18. The total move per case (+0.21 %) is only 1.1 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade, while the unusual move (+0.88 %, t 1.49 over 307 days) is 4.6 times the cost. More cases are needed before the size can be told from 0.
