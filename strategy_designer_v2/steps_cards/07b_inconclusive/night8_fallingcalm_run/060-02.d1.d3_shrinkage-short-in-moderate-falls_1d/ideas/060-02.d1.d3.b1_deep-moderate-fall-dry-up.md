Idea: In a falling market, while the coin's 20-day return is negative but not yet a crash, the deeper that 20-day fall and the more its trading volume over the past 30 days has shrunk against the 30 days before, the larger its fall over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader shorts volume shrinkage in a falling coin, weighted toward falls that are already deep but not crashing. They stand aside from shallow dips, where the dry-up predicts nothing.
Why:
- F1: Inside the falling profile the effect sits in the deeper moderate falls. When the 20-day return is between -20 % and -10 %, active short cases earn a mean short excess 5-day return of +5.10 % (t 5.11, 86 cases). Between -10 % and -5 % they earn +3.39 % (t 2.14, 35 cases). Between -5 % and 0 % they earn only +0.93 % (t 0.50, 39 cases), so the shallow part of the tested range adds cases and no effect.
- F2: Scoring each falling case by depth of fall times volume shrinkage gives a graded result. The short excess 5-day return rises from +1.50 % (t 1.08, 53 cases) in the low tercile to +3.78 % (t 2.42, 53) in the middle tercile and +4.39 % (t 2.86, 54) in the high tercile.
- F3: The dry-up matters within the deeper fall band (60-day return below -10 %, 20-day return from -20 % to -10 %). Shrinking-volume cases give +5.32 % short excess (t 4.11, 59 cases), while growing-volume cases give -2.49 % (t -1.34, 25). In the -5 % to 0 % band the order flips: shrink +1.10 % (t 0.43) against growth +2.91 % (t 1.35).
- F5: The strongest cases carry the measure.
