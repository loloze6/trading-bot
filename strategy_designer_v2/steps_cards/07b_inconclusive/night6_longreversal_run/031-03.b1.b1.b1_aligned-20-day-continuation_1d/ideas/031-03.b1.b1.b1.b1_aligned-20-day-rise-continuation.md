Idea: The larger a coin's 20-day rise, the more its price rises further over the next 10 days, only while its 60-day return is also positive.
Consequence: A trader should lean long on a coin after a strong 20-day rise inside a rising three-month trend, more so the larger that rise, and should not short a coin on its 20-day fall.
Why:
- F1: The main measure falls short only because one side carries no effect: long cases give +0.0314 (t 2.03, 1183 cases on 208 days), while short cases give +0.0029 (t 0.28, 1223 cases on 198 days). Their total move in the forecast's direction is +3.59 % for longs and −0.54 % for shorts.
- F2: The long side is graded the way the idea says. By long forecast quartile the drift-removed 10-day move rises from −0.12 % (q1) to +1.95 % (q2), +2.47 % (q3) and +4.03 % (q4, t 2.13). By the 20-day return itself it rises from +0.28 % (q1) to +7.41 % (q4, t 2.37, strength 0.33).
- F3: The short side is not graded at all. By |forecast| quartile its drift-removed move goes +3.00 %, +1.59 %, −1.54 %, +0.73 % from weakest to strongest. The Spearman of |forecast| with the directional move is −0.094 on shorts against +0.069 on longs.
- F4: The plan's 10-day horizon is where the effect shows best. The main t is 1.32 at 5 days, 1.86 at 10, 0.80 at 20 and 0.29 at 30. On longs it is 1.54, 2.03 and 1.49 at 5, 10 and 20 days.
- F6: The effect is broad across coins rather than carried by one. The main measure is positive on 16 of 19 coins (AVAX +0.044 t 1.99, INJ +0.047, DOGE +0.044, ADA +0.026). Only AAVE is clearly negative (−0.027, t −1.42), and the size per case (+1.28 % unusual move, cost 0.22 %) is well above a trade's cost.
- F7: The test already used every coin and every fold (A–D), so there is no further data to add. The answer has to come from narrowing the idea to where the effect shows: the long side.
