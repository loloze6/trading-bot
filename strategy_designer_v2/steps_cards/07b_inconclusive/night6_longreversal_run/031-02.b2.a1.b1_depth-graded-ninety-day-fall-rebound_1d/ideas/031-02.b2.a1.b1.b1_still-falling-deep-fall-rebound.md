Idea: The further a coin's 90-day return stands below its own usual 90-day move, the more its price rises over the next 30 days than its usual drift, only while it is still falling over the past 20 days, the deeper its 20-day fall the larger the rebound.
Consequence: A trader buying a coin after a long fall buys only falls that are still going, and expects most from those falling hardest in the last 20 days.
Why:
- F2: What happened over the last 20 days before a case splits the result in two. At 30 days, the Spearman correlation between the case's 20-day return and its excess return is -0.40 (-0.25 at 20 days, -0.17 at 10 days). In the top tercile of 20-day returns (a bounce already under way, mean +14.4 %), the excess 30-day return is -12.0 % (t -4.13, 19 cases, 10 days). In the bottom tercile (still falling, mean -20.9 %), it is +6.9 % (t 0.97).
- F3: Split by the sign of the 20-day return, the same pattern holds. Deep-fall cases whose 20-day return was positive returned -10.3 % excess over the next 30 days (t -2.78, 22 cases, 11 days). Cases whose 20-day return was negative returned +1.6 % (t 0.26, 34 cases). The rebound and the failed rebound cancel inside the tested regime.
- F4: The 10-day return shows the same pattern, more weakly. At 30 days, the lowest 10-day-return tercile returned +2.4 %, against -6.2 % and -5.5 % for the two higher terciles. At 20 days the three terciles returned +3.1 %, 0.0 % and -4.6 %.
- F1: The main measure sits at zero. Its mean excess 30-day return is -0.71 % (day-clustered t -0.20, 85 cases on 35 days). On the same grid the measure stays at zero at every horizon: +0.28 % at 10 days (t 0.40, 291 cases), +1.03 % at 20 days (t 0.66), -1.84 % at 60 days (t -0.46). So the test did not miss an effect sitting at another horizon.
