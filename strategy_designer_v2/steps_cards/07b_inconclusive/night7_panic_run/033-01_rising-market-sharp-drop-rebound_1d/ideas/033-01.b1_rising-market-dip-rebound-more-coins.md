Idea: In a rising market, a down day is followed by a rise over the next 3 days, and the rise is larger the more that day's fall exceeds the coin's usual daily swing (the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days).
Consequence: In a rising market, forced or mechanical selling on a sharp down day overshoots, and waiting buyers lift the price back, more so after a bigger overshoot.
Why:
- F1: Step 5's result is on the side the idea predicts but short of the bar. The slope of the 3-day excess return on the forecast is +0.000386 with t 1.11 over 2224 in-profile grid bars, and the supporting difference is +0.42 %. The size is +0.24 % per case, a strength of 0.035 with t 0.65 over 799 days, so the effect is real in sign but too small to separate from noise on six coins.
- F2: In folds B and C, the in-profile 3-day slope is +0.00088 with t 1.75 over 1237 grid bars. All six coins point the same way (slopes +0.0004 to +0.0021), but each is weak on its own (t 0.31 for LTC to 1.16 for XRP). This is a consistent effect that each coin has too few cases to show.
- F4: In-profile, a down day is followed by a mean 3-day excess return of +1.31 % (439 cases), against -0.33 % after an up day (798 cases). This puts the rebound on the side the idea names.
- F5: The grading is uneven. Falls of 1.5 to 2 usual swings are followed by +4.7 % excess (t 2.65, 34 cases) and falls of 1 to 1.5 swings by +3.4 % (t 1.67, 52 cases). Falls of 0.5 to 1 swing are followed by -0.02 % (118 cases) and falls beyond 2 swings by +0.46 % (14 cases). The few large falls carry most of the effect, and there are too few of them on six coins.
- F7: When the 60-day trend is recomputed from the prices, the 3-day slope on the scaled fall is clearest in a rising market (t 2.50, 531 cases), against t 1.48 in a choppy market and t 0.39 in a falling one. This supports the brief's tie to rising markets at this horizon, though only on a recomputed trend over folds B and C.
