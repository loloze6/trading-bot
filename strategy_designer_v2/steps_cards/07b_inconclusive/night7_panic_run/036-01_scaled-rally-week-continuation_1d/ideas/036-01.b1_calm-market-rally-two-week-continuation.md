Idea: In a calm market, a coin whose 3-day return is large against its usual daily swing (the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days) keeps rising over the next 14 days, and the larger that scaled 3-day rise, the larger the further gain.
Consequence: A trader would ride unusually large rallies only when the coin has been swinging less than usual, holding for two weeks, and would refuse the same rally in an agitated market.
Why:
- F4: What continuation there is sits in calm markets (30-day swing at or below its usual level, approximated inside each window) and at a longer horizon. Calm cases earn +1.44 % over 7 days (t 0.61, 39 days) and +7.13 % over 14 days (t 1.42, 35 days; +6.80 %, t 1.50, against the window mean). Agitated cases earn -0.65 % (t -0.23) and +0.10 % (t 0.03).
- F5: In calm markets the 14-day unusual move rises with the scaled 3-day rise across quintiles: -5.06 %, +2.78 %, -0.75 %, +4.17 %, +9.51 % (about 60 bars each). In agitated markets the top quintile is -3.13 % at 14 days. The linear slope in calm markets is still weak (t 0.44, 147 cases), so this is suggestive and short of clear.
- F6: By trend, the 7-day unusual move is positive in rising calm markets (+2.70 %, t 1.61, 75 cases) and in choppy calm ones (+5.96 %, t 1.90, 27). It is negative or flat in agitated choppy (-6.56 %, t -1.82) and agitated falling (-0.72 %) markets. The split again runs along agitation rather than trend.
- F1: Step 5's result points against the idea, not just falls short of the bar: the mean unusual move over 7 days is -0.30 % per case (t -0.67, 854 case days, 1757 cases, strength -0.024 of a 12.44 % usual move), while the 3.1×-cost total move of +0.60 % comes entirely from the coins' normal drift of +0.91 %. On folds B and C the long cases (forecast ≥ 2, 383 cases on 120 days) give -0.39 % (cluster t -0.30) against drift and -0.85 % (t -0.65) against the window's own mean.
