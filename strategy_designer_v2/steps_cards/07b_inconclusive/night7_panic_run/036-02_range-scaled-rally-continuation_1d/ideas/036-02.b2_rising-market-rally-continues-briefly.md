Idea: In a rising market, a coin whose 3-day return is large against its usual daily range (the average of its daily high-to-low range over the past 30 days) keeps rising over the next 3 days, and the larger that range-scaled 3-day rise, the larger the further gain.
Consequence: A trader can ride an unusually large rally for a few days in a coin that has been rising for weeks, but should not hold it for a full week.
Why:
- F2: Over a 3-day horizon the graded effect does show. On the non-overlapping 3-day grid of the up cells in folds B and C, the slope of the excess return on the range-scaled 3-day rise has t 2.22 (1139 cases, 307 days). The mean excess return is +0.53 % (day t 1.23).
- F3: By horizon, the slope's t on up cells falls from 1.93 (2 days) and 2.22 (3 days) to 1.36 (4 days), 0.82 (5 days) and -0.26 (7 days). The test's 7-day horizon is past where the continuation shows.
- F4: The 3-day continuation lives in rising markets, where the slope has t 3.83 and the mean excess is +1.32 % (day t 1.44, 310 cases, 103 days). In choppy markets the slope has t -0.28 (548 cases). In falling markets it reverses, with slope t -2.54 (281 cases).
- F5: In a rising, calm market the 3-day continuation is strongest: mean excess +3.00 %, day t 2.46, slope t 2.88, 128 cases on 54 days. Rising agitated has slope t 2.83 but a mean of only +0.25 % (76 cases).
- F6: The effect is mixed across periods. On the 3-day up cells the slope's t is -2.27 in 2018, +1.85 in 2019, +2.65 in 2020 and +2.01 in 2021. On the 7-day positive-forecast cells the mean excess is -4.88 % (t -3.14) in 2018 and +3.66 % (t 2.65) in 2020. The market's swing between years drowns the 7-day result.
