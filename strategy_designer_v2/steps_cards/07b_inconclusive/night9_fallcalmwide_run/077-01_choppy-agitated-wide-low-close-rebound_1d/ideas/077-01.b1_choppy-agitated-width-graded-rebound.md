Idea: In a choppy, agitated market, a wide day that closes in the lower part of its range is followed by a rise over the next day, larger the wider the day's range against the coin's 30-day swing.
Consequence: In a choppy, agitated market, a trader buying a low close after a wide day should size by how wide the day was against the coin's recent swing, not by how close the close sits to the low.
Why:
- F1: The test's own result points the way the idea predicts but falls just short of the bar: the mean next-day excess return is +0.83 % with a day-clustered t of 1.96 over 213 cases on 164 days, and the slope on the forecast's size is negative (-5.3e-05). So both the t rule and the grading rule failed.
- F3: In the folds shown here (B, C), the rebound lives on the plan's own horizon and is spent after one day: the 32 cases earn +3.54 % the next day (t 2.98), +1.83 % over two days (t 1.11), +1.63 % over three (t 1.14) and -0.01 % over five (t -0.01).
- F4: Nearness to the low does not grade the rebound; it runs the other way. By the close's position among the cases, the next-day excess is +1.70 % for the closes nearest the low, +4.16 % for the middle tercile and +4.83 % for the highest. The forecast's terciles are flat (+3.73 %, +3.00 %, +3.91 %). This is why the slope failed: the forecast multiplies width by nearness to the low.
