Idea: In a rising, calm market, a day whose high-to-low range is wide against the coin's 30-day swing of daily returns and that closes in the lower part of its range is followed by a rise over the next three days; the closer the close sits to the day's low, the larger the rise.
Consequence: In an orderly uptrend, a rebound buy after a wide day that closed near its low should be held for about three days rather than one, and sized by how near the low it closed.
Why:
- F1: The test's result is on the idea's side but short of the bar: across all four folds the mean next-day excess return is +0.47 % with a day-clustered t of 1.71 (330 days, 641 cases). The test already used every fold and every coin on daily bars, so no more data is available.
- F3: The rebound sits almost entirely in the cases that close nearest their low. In folds B and C, the top quarter of close location (mean location 0.91) gains +2.73 % the next day (day-clustered t 3.95, 25 days). The lower three quarters gain +0.56 %, +1.14 % and +1.34 %, with t 0.56, 1.09 and 1.53. The slope of next-day excess return on close location is +0.060 per unit (t 2.23), and its Spearman correlation is 0.20.
- F5: The rebound keeps building past the plan's one-day horizon. In folds B and C the 3-day excess return is +2.83 % with day-clustered t 3.31 (85 days), against +1.44 % (t 2.53) over 1 day. The 5-day return adds little (+2.95 %, t 2.16). For the cases nearest the low, the 3-day return is +5.18 % (t 3.74, 25 days).
