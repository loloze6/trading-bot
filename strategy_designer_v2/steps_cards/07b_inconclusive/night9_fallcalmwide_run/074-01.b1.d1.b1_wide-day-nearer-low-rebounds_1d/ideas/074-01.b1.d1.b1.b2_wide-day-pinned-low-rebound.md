Idea: In a rising, calm market, a day whose high-to-low range is wide against the coin's 30-day swing of daily returns and that closes at the very bottom of its range, ending lower than it began, is followed by a rise over the next day; the closer the close sits to the day's low, the larger the rise.
Consequence: In an orderly uptrend, only wide down days that end pinned to their low are worth buying for a next-day rebound, and the nearer the low the close, the larger the position.
Why:
- F2: In folds B and C the same cases are clearly positive the next day: +1.44 % excess, day-clustered t 2.53 (161 cases on 85 days). All bars together average −0.01 % (t −0.09), so the rebound is not the market's own drift. Since the four-fold mean is only +0.47 %, folds A and D must carry much less of it.
- F3: The rebound sits almost entirely in the cases that close nearest their low. In folds B and C, the top quarter of close location (mean location 0.91) gains +2.73 % the next day (day-clustered t 3.95, 25 days). The lower three quarters gain +0.56 %, +1.14 % and +1.34 %, with t 0.56, 1.09 and 1.53. The slope of next-day excess return on close location is +0.060 per unit (t 2.23), and its Spearman correlation is 0.20.
- F4: The day's width does not grade the move at all. Range quartiles of 2.1, 2.4, 3.0 and 4.1 usual moves give next-day excess returns of +1.35 %, +1.42 %, +1.71 % and +1.27 %, with a Spearman correlation of −0.002. Close location, not width, carries the effect.
