Idea: In a choppy, calm market, the larger a coin's rise over the past 7 days measured in multiples of its usual 7-day swing over the past 30 days, the larger its further rise over the next 14 days.
Consequence: A trader buying a quiet, trendless coin after a week's rise that is large against its own swing should expect the continuation within two weeks, not three, and should favour the largest scaled rises.
Why:
- F1: Step 5 found the grading the idea predicts but not the rise itself. The 21-day slope t is 2.00, but the mean excess return of its 103 cases (48 days) is -0.61 %. The idea's size is an unusual move of -0.88 % per case (t -0.59, 309 days, strength -0.051), so a larger scaled rise does better than a smaller one without the cases beating the coin's drift.
- F2: On the 21-day grid of folds B and C the effect is absent and the cases are few: 19 cases on 10 days, slope t 0.13, mean excess -0.09 % (t -0.02). The forecast quartiles do not line up: q2 +22.3 %, q3 -1.0 %, q4 -10.8 %. Three weeks is where the test measured, not where the effect shows.
- F4: Over the next 14 days the grading holds and the mean is positive. There are 42 cases on 21 days, slope t 2.47 and mean excess +6.4 % (t 0.85). The core coins alone give 35 cases, slope t 2.13 and mean +3.9 %. By forecast tercile: low -13.0 %, mid +20.5 %, high +11.5 %.
- F5: The weakest rises are what pull the mean below the drift. The lowest forecast tercile is followed by -6.2 % at 7 days and -13.0 % at 14 days, against +6.8 %/+1.7 % (7 days) and +20.5 %/+11.5 % (14 days) for the mid and high terciles. So the slope passes while the mean of all cases fails.
