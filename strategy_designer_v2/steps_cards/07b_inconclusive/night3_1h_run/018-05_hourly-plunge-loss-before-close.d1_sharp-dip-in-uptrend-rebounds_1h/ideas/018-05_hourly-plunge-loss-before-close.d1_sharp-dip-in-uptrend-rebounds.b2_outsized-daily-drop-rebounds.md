Idea: When a coin's 24-hour return, read at an hourly close, falls by several times its 30-day average absolute daily return, the coin rises over the next 3 days more than its normal drift, and the larger the fall in multiples of that average, the larger the rise.
Consequence: An outsized one-day drop is a reason to buy for a few days rather than to sell, more so the larger the drop is next to the coin's usual daily move.
Why:
- F4: What grades the rebound is the depth of the 24-hour fall. On the 72-hour grid with a positive 30-day return, falls of 0.5 to 1, 1 to 1.5 and over 1.5 times the 30-day mean absolute daily return are followed by +0.67 % (t 1.29, n 259), +0.87 % (t 1.42, n 164) and +2.67 % (t 3.58, n 171). The product of fall and rise ranks with the move at Spearman rho 0.13 (p 4e-5, n 950).
- F5: A deep fall rebounds less without a rising month. In a non-positive month, falls over 1.5 times the usual daily move are followed by +0.90 % (t 1.88, n 253). Fall depths from 0.5 to 1.5 times are followed by -1.22 % and -1.00 %.
- F7: The move carried over 72 hours is large next to the 0.19 % cost of a round trip. The total move is 0.90 % per case (4.7 times the cost), and 2.67 % unusual on the deepest falls. The book paid: Sharpe 1.11, 0.56 above holding.
