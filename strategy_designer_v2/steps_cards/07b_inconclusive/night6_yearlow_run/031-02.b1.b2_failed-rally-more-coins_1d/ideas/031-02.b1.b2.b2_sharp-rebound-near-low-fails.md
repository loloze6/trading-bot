Idea: While a coin's daily close sits nearer its lowest close of the past 365 days than it has usually been over that year, the larger its 60-day return, the more it falls over the next 20 days.
Consequence: A sharp two-month rebound that has still not lifted the coin away from its yearly low is the one to fade, more so the sharper the rebound.
Why:
- F1: The idea's own side points the wrong way. At 20 days, cases with a forecast below 0 had an excess return of +1.00 % (257 cases, t 0.31), where the idea predicts a fall. The long cases rose by about as much (+0.94 %, 501 cases), so the main measure's +0.0031 (t 0.54, 116 days) is not graded: the Spearman correlation of forecast with excess return is 0.013.
- F3: Among the 20-day short cases whose 60-day return is known (53 % of them), the larger the 60-day rally, the larger the fall that follows. Excess return is +3.6 % when the 60-day return is up to 9 %, -0.2 % between 9 % and 21 %, and -6.6 % above 21 % (about 46 cases each).
- F4: The short side changes sign from year to year. It is negative in 2018 (-13.6 %), 2020 (-6.0 %), 2021 (-2.0 %) and 2022 (-12.2 %), and positive in 2019 (+12.9 %), 2024 (+7.7 %) and 2025 (+7.1 %). This mix is what holds the overall t near 0.
