Idea: When a four-hour bar closes above the highest high of a coin's preceding two days, only while the coin's 7-day return is positive, the coin rises over the next six days. The farther that close sits above the two-day high, measured in the coin's 30-day average four-hour range, the larger the rise.
Consequence: A trader would hold large upward breaks for about six days rather than three, where the move per case is several times the cost of a trade.
Why:
- F3: The effect sits in the largest breaks. Raw-break quintiles 1 to 3 (mean 0.08 to 0.41 ranges) show unusual 3-day moves of +0.20 %, −0.19 % and −0.33 % (t −0.9 to 0.6). Quintile 4 (0.75 ranges) shows +0.53 % (t 1.4), and quintile 5 (1.9 ranges) shows +1.80 % (t 3.38, 446 cases, strength 0.198).
- F4: The largest breaks move far more than a trade costs. In the top quintile the unusual 3-day move of +1.80 % is about 9 times the 0.19 % round trip, against 2.2 times for all cases.
