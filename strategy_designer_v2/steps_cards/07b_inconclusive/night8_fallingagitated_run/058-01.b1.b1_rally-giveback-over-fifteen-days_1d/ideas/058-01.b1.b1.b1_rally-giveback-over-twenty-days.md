Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's 3-day rise is against the standard deviation of its daily returns over the past 30 days, the larger the fall in its price over the next 20 days, taking back a larger share of that rise.
Consequence: A trader would size a short after a rally in a falling, agitated market by how large the scaled rally was, and hold it for about three weeks rather than two.
Why:
- F1: In folds B and C, the plan's own 15-day grading is flat. The OLS slope of the 15-day unusual move on the forecast is 0.0009 with t 0.29 on 58 grid cases, matching step 5's t 1.21 on 177 cases. Yet the mean unusual move after a rise is strongly on the short side: 11.2 % over 15 days, t 6.9. So at 15 days the profile carries a give-back after every rise, but its size does not grow with the rise.
- F2: Across forecast quartiles at 15 days, the unusual fall is 13.0 % for the largest quarter, 12.2 %, 11.0 % and 9.1 % for the smallest (12 to 16 cases each). On the scaled rise itself the four quarters give 9.3 %, 12.1 %, 16.0 % and 12.7 %, with a slope t of 0.41 on 42 cases. The small rises give back nearly as much as the large ones, which is why the 15-day slope is unclear.
- F3: The grading shows at 20 days instead. The slope there is 0.0098 with t 2.42 on 58 cases, or t 2.36 with drift included. The 20-day unusual fall drops in step with the forecast quartile: 16.7 %, 14.2 %, 7.8 % and -0.3 %.
- F6: The tested horizon hides a large ungraded effect. Step 5's size is an unusual move of +3.72 % per case (t 2.49, 217 days, strength 0.167) against a cost of 0.22 % a trade. The book still lost 1.5 % a year (Sharpe -0.03), because its 15-day short is held after small rises that give back without the grading the sizing assumes.
