Idea: When a coin closes below its Ichimoku cloud and the cloud is falling, its return over the next 26 days is negative, and more negative the slower the cloud has fallen over the past 26 days.
Consequence: A trader would short a coin below a falling cloud mainly while the cloud's decline is gentle, and size down as the decline steepens, whatever the coin's recent calm.
Why:
- F3: The cloud's half of the grading works. Cases with the gentlest 26-day cloud fall (raw slope mean −4.0) gained +9.6 % drift-adjusted (t 2.94, 52 cases, 75 % positive). The middle third gained −0.9 % (t −0.20) and the steepest third (raw slope mean −19.2) −4.1 % (t −1.34). The Spearman correlation of cloud steepness with gain is −0.27. The gentle third is positive on all six coins (+3.2 % XRP to +16.3 % ZEC), and in the bear-year group it gains +15.0 % (t 4.96) against +0.8 % for the steep third.
- F4: The calm half of the grading does not work. The Spearman correlation of the standardised 30-day volatility with the gain is +0.04. The calmest third gained −4.7 % (t −1.09) against +6.1 % for the middle third and +3.2 % for the most volatile. Inside the gentle-fall third, calm and volatile cases gain about the same (+10.0 %, t 2.27, 32 cases, against +9.0 %, t 1.85, 20 cases).
- F2: The market's own year drowns the effect. The drift-adjusted short gain was +20.0 % in 2018 (t 4.86, 34 cases), +5.5 % in 2019 (t 2.44, 30 cases) and +9.1 % in 2025 (t 1.92, 14 cases). It was −32.8 % in 2020 (t −3.00, 7 cases), −35.3 % in 2021 (t −2.91, 12 cases) and −10.9 % in 2023 (t −2.15, 11 cases).
- F7: More data is available than the test used. Thirteen more coins have daily bars: in folds A and D, and partly in B and C. The test ran on the six default coins only.
