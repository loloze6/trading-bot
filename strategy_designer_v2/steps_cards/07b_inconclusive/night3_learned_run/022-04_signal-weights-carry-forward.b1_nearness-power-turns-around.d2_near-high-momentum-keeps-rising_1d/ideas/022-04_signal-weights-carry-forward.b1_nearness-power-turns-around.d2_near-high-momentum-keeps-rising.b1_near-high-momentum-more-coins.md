Idea: The nearer a coin's close sits to its 90-day high and the larger its 20-day return, the higher its return over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader should hold a coin longer, and refuse shorts, the closer it is to its 90-day high after a strong 20-day rise.
Why:
- F1: The result sits on the side the idea predicts but just short of the bar: the pooled 5-day slope is +0.00067 with t 1.90 on 3,419 non-overlapping cases. The quintiles rise from -0.35 % and -0.37 % excess return in the two lowest to +0.27 % and +0.61 % in the two highest, against a per-case standard deviation of about 10 to 12 %, so the data are too noisy to show an effect of this size clearly.
- F3: The effect is mixed across coins and years rather than absent. Over 5 days the slope is positive on 4 of 6 coins (ETH t 1.52, XRP 1.47, ZEC 1.39, BTC 0.84) and negative on LTC (-0.24) and XMR (-0.72). It is positive in 2019, 2020 (t 2.14), 2021 and 2025, and negative in 2018, 2022 (t -1.99) and 2023 (t -1.98), so each coin and year holds about 570 or 430 cases and too little to decide alone.
- F5: The idea's size covers the cost of a trade but does not make the book beat holding. Per case the unusual move is +0.43 % (strength 0.034) and the total move +0.47 %, 2.2 and 2.5 times the 0.19 % round trip. Yet the book's Sharpe is 0.42, 0.13 below holding's. The idea is too weak for this sample to confirm, not too small to pay.
