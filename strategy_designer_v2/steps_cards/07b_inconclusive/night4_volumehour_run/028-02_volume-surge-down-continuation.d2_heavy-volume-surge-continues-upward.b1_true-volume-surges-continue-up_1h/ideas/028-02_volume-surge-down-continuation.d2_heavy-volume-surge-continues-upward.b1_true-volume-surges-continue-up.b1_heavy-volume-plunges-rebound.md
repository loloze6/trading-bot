Idea: After an hour in which the coin falls far more than its typical hourly move over the past 30 days, on volume several times its average hourly volume over the past 30 days, the price rises back over the next four hours. The larger that hour's fall relative to its typical 30-day move times its volume relative to its 30-day average, the larger the rebound.
Consequence: A heavy-volume hourly plunge is a reason to buy for about four hours, or to refuse a short that chases it, since its strongest cases rebound by several times the cost of a trade.
Why:
- F5: The mirror situation is a reversal, not a continuation. The top 10 % of heavy-volume falls by fall-in-usual-moves times volume ratio are followed by rises above drift:
- F6: That rebound after heavy-volume falls is graded across the strongest cases. Over 4 hours it rises from +15.6 bp at the top 10 % to +30.8 bp (t 2.00, 478 cases) at the top 3 % and +65.5 bp (t 1.97, 160 cases, strength 0.39) at the top 1 %. The top 1 % move is about six times the 0.11 % cost. Within the top 10 %, the rank correlation of score with excess return is only 0.041 (p 0.098).
- F7: The rebound is present on both coins but uneven across years. At 12 hours, the top 3 % give +81.5 bp on BTC and +105.9 bp on ETH. By year, 2021 carries +532 bp (t 2.75), while 2019, 2022 and 2025 are negative (-45, -32 and -40 bp), so part of the effect is one regime.
- F2: Heavy-volume up hours show no continuation at 4 hours, even among the largest surges. Ranked by rise-in-usual-moves times volume ratio, the top 10 % give -4.4 bp (t -0.80, 1,611 cases) and the top 3 % give -6.3 bp (t -0.52). Weighted by the score, the means are -19 bp and -26 bp, so larger surges do slightly worse.
