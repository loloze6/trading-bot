Idea: In a choppy market, on a day that closed up, the further the close sits below the coin's 20-day high, in units of its 20-day daily-return standard deviation, the larger its fall over the next 10 days.
Consequence: A bounce that still closes deep below the range top is a day to sell into rather than to buy, and the deeper it sits, the larger the fall that follows over the next ten days.
Why:
- F1: The main measure is on the side the idea predicts but just short of the bar: after up-day closes deep below the 20-day high, the coin fell more than its drift by +1.16 % over 10 days (t 1.99, 628 cases on 463 days, strength 0.084). That is 6.0 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade, but it only offsets a normal drift of -1.14 % in the short direction, so the total move is +0.02 %.
- F3: Five of the six coins point the predicted way: BTC +2.1 %, ETH +2.3 %, XMR +3.0 %, XRP +2.9 %, LTC +0.8 %, each with 37 to 50 cases. ZEC alone points the other way at -4.1 % (t -1.15, 32 cases), so the effect is broad across coins rather than carried by one.
- F5: The effect is on the predicted side at 1, 5 and 10 days (+0.60 % t 1.72, +0.92 % t 1.40, +1.44 % t 1.32 in folds B and C) and gone at 20 days (-0.39 %, t -0.18). The plan's 10-day horizon is where it shows, and the shortfall is one of precision, not of horizon.
- F6: More data is available than the test used: 13 further coins have daily bars in the folds (ADA, DOGE, LINK, TRX, UNI, AAVE, AVAX, SOL, INJ, NEAR, SUI, TAO, ONDO). The test ran on six coins only, and its t of 1.99 needs only a modest gain in cases to be told clearly from 0.
