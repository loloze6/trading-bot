Idea: For a coin's nearness to its 90-day high, the larger both the excess of its past 365-day predictive power for 5-day returns and today's deviation of nearness from its usual level, when the reading points against a past relation that implies a fall, the larger the coin's rise over the next 5 days. This rise is larger the lower the coin's own 5-day return before the reading.
Consequence: A trader should take the long nearness-power reversal on coins that have not just risen over the last 5 days, and skip it after a 5-day run-up, where its edge turns negative.
Why:
- F1: The test's slope is on the predicted side but small: t 1.33 on 2489 positive-forecast grid cases. Within those cases the forecast is not graded: mean excess 5-day returns by forecast quintile are -0.11 %, +0.52 %, +0.01 %, +0.63 % and +0.72 %, each with t ≤ 1.41. What separates is a positive forecast against a zero one: +0.35 % against -0.38 % excess, with the top 40 % of the forecast minus flat at Welch t 2.50.
- F4: The slope changes sign across periods and coins.
