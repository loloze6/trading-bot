Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is negative and that has just had a one-day rise of several usual daily moves, only while its 30-day return is also negative, the deeper that 30-day fall, the larger its fall over the next 5 days.
Consequence: A trader would short a bounce inside a falling coin with more conviction the deeper the coin's fall over the past month, and would hold the short for about a week, not judge it on the rise's size.
Why:
- F2: The size of the one-day rise in usual daily moves does not grade the giveback: its correlation with the 2-day excess return is 0.024. By rise quintile the means are U-shaped: +0.84 % (t 2.49) at 0.77 usual moves, +0.24 % to +0.55 % in the middle, +1.03 % (t 2.60) at 2.19 usual moves.
- F3: The depth of the 30-day fall does grade it: by 30-day-return quartile the 2-day excess return falls steadily from +1.17 % (t 2.69, mean 30-day return −34 %) to +0.63 %, +0.35 % and +0.12 % (t 0.38, mean −4 %). The deeper half minus the shallower half is +0.67 % (Welch t 1.90, 702/703 cases), and the deepest quartile minus the shallowest is +1.05 % (t 1.95).
- F4: The fade is larger and clearer over 5 days than over the plan's 2. On the same cases the excess return is +0.51 % (t 4.33) at 1 day, +0.50 % (t 3.26) at 2, +0.70 % (t 3.59) at 3, +1.43 % (t 5.78, 1803 cases) at 5 and +1.95 % (t 5.48) at 10. The total move at 5 days is +0.67 %, against +0.21 % at 2 days and a trade cost of 0.22 %.
