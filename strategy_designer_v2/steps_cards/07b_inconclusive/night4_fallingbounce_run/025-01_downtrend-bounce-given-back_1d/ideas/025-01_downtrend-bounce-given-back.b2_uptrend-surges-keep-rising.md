Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is not negative, a one-day rise that is large relative to its usual daily move (its 20-day standard deviation of daily returns) is followed by a further rise over the next 5 days, and the larger that rise in units of the usual daily move, the larger the rise that follows.
Consequence: A sharp up-day in a coin that is not in a long fall is a reason to go long for the following days rather than to fade it.

- F4: The contrast the brief names does show. On the non-overlapping 5-day grid, rises above 1 usual daily move are followed by a short-direction unusual move 2.63 % larger in falling coins than in coins whose 90-day return is not negative (Welch t 2.35; 170 against 244 cases).
