Idea: In a coin whose 90-day return is positive and that has just had a one-day rise of several usual daily moves, the larger that rise in the coin's usual daily moves over the past 20 days, the larger its further rise over the next 5 days.
Consequence: In a coin that is rising over the past months, a trader should not short a sharp up day: it tends to be followed by more rise, and the stronger the day, the more.
Why:
- F5: In coins whose 90-day return is not negative, sharp rises are followed by further rises, the opposite of falling coins, as the brief's falsification test requires. In the short direction, rises of 1.5 to 2 usual moves are followed next day by -0.68 % (t -2.35, n 626) and rises of 2 to 3 by -0.81 % (t -2.65, n 485). Over 5 days these deepen to -1.33 % and -4.43 % (overlapping 5-day returns). Rises above 1.5 usual moves in falling coins minus those in non-falling coins give +1.46 % next day (Welch t 5.04, 831 vs 1,246 cases).
- F7: The test already used every coin and every fold available, so more data cannot sharpen the answer. Per case, the total move (+0.19 %) is 0.8 times the 0.22 % cost of a trade. Rises above 2 usual moves (F3) give back +0.64 % to +2.26 % next day, about 3 to 10 times that cost.
