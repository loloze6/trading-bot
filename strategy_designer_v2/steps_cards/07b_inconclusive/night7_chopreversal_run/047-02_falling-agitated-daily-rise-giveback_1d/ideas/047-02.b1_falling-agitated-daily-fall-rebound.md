Idea: In a falling, agitated market, the larger a coin's one-day percent fall, the larger its rise over the next day.
Consequence: In a falling, agitated market, a trader can buy a coin after a sharp down day, sized by the fall, and hold it for one day, because forced selling's discount is repaid the next day.
Why:
- F3: The mirror side carries the graded reversal the brief describes. On down days in the profile, the next-day excess return by fall quintile runs from +3.49 % (t 4.99) for the largest falls to +1.13 %, +1.12 %, +0.60 % and -0.06 % for the smallest. The day-clustered slope of next-day excess on the day's return is -0.394 (t -4.10, 675 cases, 289 days), and on the standardised fall it is -0.0106 (t -3.76).
- F4: The graded rebound after falls holds coin by coin and year by year. The slope is negative for all six coins (BTC t -3.78, ETH -2.98, LTC -2.64, XMR -2.45, ZEC -2.61, XRP -1.30) and in all four years (2018 -0.18, 2019 -0.23, 2020 -0.45, 2021 -0.50).
- F5: The rebound after falls is spent within the next day. The day-after-next slope on the standardised fall is +0.0046 (t 1.95), a slight giveback rather than a continuation. The largest-fall quintile's next-day rebound of +3.49 % is about 18 times the 0.19 % cost of a trade.
- F1: In the falling, agitated profile, the size of an up day does not grade the next day. The next-day excess return by rise quintile runs -1.60 %, -0.60 %, -1.57 %, -0.80 %, -0.32 % for mean rises of 0.44 % to 11.25 %. The Spearman correlation of rise and next-day excess is 0.01 (p 0.79), and the day-clustered slope on the rise is 0.061 (t 0.79, 613 cases, 258 days). The test's slope t of 0.25 is therefore a missing gradient, not a weak one.
