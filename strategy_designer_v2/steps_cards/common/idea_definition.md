An idea is one thing we would see in the market if a theory were true, which a trader could act on, and which the data
can confirm or reject. Every idea:
- is a statement with one direction: what happens, and which way. Never "either this or that";
- says that something happens, never that something does not happen;
- says what we would see, not how to check it: no condition on the check, such as what to control for or account for,
  or which coins it must hold for;
- is concrete enough that someone looking at the bars could tell whether it is there;
- is about one coin at a time: what we would see in one coin's own bars. Each coin is tested on its own bars only,
  never alongside another coin, the market or an average of coins;
- uses only what is known at a bar's close;
- is graded (the stronger the situation, the larger the move that follows) or a market condition in which an effect
  holds (a regime); never a fixed threshold that turns a position on or off;
- is something a trader could act on: if confirmed, a reason to take a trade; if rejected, a reason to refuse one;
- names no setting value (no lengths, no thresholds), no trading rule (no entry, exit or size), no result and no price.
