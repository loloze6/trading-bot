An idea is one thing we would see in the market if a theory were true, which a trader could act on, and which the data
can confirm or reject. Every idea:
- is a statement with one direction: what happens, and which way. Never "either this or that";
- says that something happens, never that something does not happen;
- says what we would see, not how to check it: no condition on the check, such as what to control for or account for,
  or which coins it must hold for;
- is concrete enough that someone looking at the bars could tell whether it is there;
- is about one coin at a time: what we would see in one coin's own bars. Each coin is tested on its own bars only,
  never alongside another coin, the market or an average of coins;
- uses only what is known at a bar's close;
- is graded: the stronger its situation, the larger the move that follows. A market condition (a regime) may limit
  where it holds, but inside it the effect is still graded; never a fixed threshold, or a condition alone, that turns
  a position on or off;
- names at most one market profile, when its theory says the effect lives in one kind of market: which way the coin
  has been going and how much it has been swinging. It names, from the market-profile definition, only what its
  theory implies: its trend and its agitation ("in a falling, agitated market"), its trend only ("in a choppy
  market") or its agitation only ("in an agitated market"), in the idea and on its own `profile:` line.
  A condition on the coin's general state over the past weeks (which way it has been going, how much it has been
  swinging) is written as one of these profiles; what happened in the last days stays the idea's situation. An idea
  that names none acts in every profile;
- states its side: what it bets when its situation occurs. `long`: the price rises after it, so a trader would buy;
  `short`: the price falls after it, so a trader would sell; `both`: its situation goes either way and the move
  follows it (a rise after one, a fall after the other). On its own `side:` line;
- is something a trader could act on: if confirmed, a reason to take a trade; if the data shows no effect (`common/verdicts.md`), a reason to refuse one;
- states its time in real time (days or hours, never bars), so it means the same on any bar size: always its horizon,
  how long after its situation the move shows ("over the next N days"), and the past period its situation uses when it
  has one ("its N-day return");
- names no level or threshold, no trading rule (no entry, exit or size), no result and no price.
